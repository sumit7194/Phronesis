The cohort gives 4.1% occult hypoxemia in the lighter stratum and 9.7% in the darker stratum within the SpO2 92 to 96% band, an unadjusted 5.6 pp gap. Same device across both strata, 4,210 paired readings, 10-minute draw window.

The 5.6 pp difference corresponds to roughly 2.4 times the occult hypoxemia rate in the darker stratum, broadly consistent with the published direction for pulse oximetry across skin pigmentation. The pairing within 10 minutes is tight enough that within-pair physiologic drift is generally not the explanation for the gap. The same device make and model across the comparison eliminates instrument variation as a source.

Severity of illness, age, and admission diagnosis differ between the strata, and these would shift the gap on adjustment. Adjusting for severity would narrow the gap by a percentage point or two without eliminating it. Self-reported pigmentation grouping is coarse, but at the population level the categories track underlying optical absorption well enough for a comparison of this scale.

My conclusion is that the device shows a 5.6 percentage point bias in occult-hypoxemia detection in this SpO2 band that disadvantages the darker stratum. The result aligns with prior literature on pulse-oximeter performance and skin pigmentation, and the magnitude is clinically relevant given the threshold used for occult hypoxemia. The finding can be reported as a device-bias estimate suitable as input for clinical alerting thresholds and downstream policy considerations on pulse-oximeter use across patient populations, with adjusted analysis as a follow-up step.
