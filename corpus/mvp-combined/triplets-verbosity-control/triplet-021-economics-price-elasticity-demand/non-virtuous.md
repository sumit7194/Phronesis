# triplet-021-economics-price-elasticity-demand

Price elasticity of demand is ε = (%ΔQ_demanded) / (%ΔP). With a 5% price rise and a 10% drop in quantity demanded, ε = (−10%) / (+5%) = roughly −2. Since |ε| = 2 > 1, demand is elastic at this point on the curve, meaning the percentage change in quantity exceeds the percentage change in price. Raising price loses revenue in this regime because the proportional drop in units sold outweighs the proportional gain per unit. Necessities and goods with few substitutes tend to be inelastic (|ε| < 1); goods with many close substitutes tend to be elastic, because consumers can redirect spending toward an alternative when the price rises.
