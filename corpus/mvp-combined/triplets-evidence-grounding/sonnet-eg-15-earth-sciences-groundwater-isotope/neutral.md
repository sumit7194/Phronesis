Eighteen wells were sampled for δ¹⁸O and tritium. δ¹⁸O ranged from −9.4‰ to −6.1‰; modern regional precipitation averages −6.8‰, while Pleistocene-age recharge typically sits around −10 to −11‰. Fourteen wells were below the tritium detection limit of 0.8 TU (pre-modern water, >60 years old); four wells showed 1.2–3.4 TU indicating modern or mixed recharge. The four tritium-positive wells cluster spatially near the perennial stream channel. The numerical model calibrated to head data gives a basin-mean recharge rate of 4.2 mm/year.

The ³H below-detection result in 14 of 18 wells is the evidence type appropriate to the claim that those wells contain pre-modern water — tritium has a known detection threshold in this method and the result is a direct measurement of water age. The δ¹⁸O values near modern precipitation (−6.1 to −6.8‰ range) in the four tritium-positive wells are appropriate evidence for modern-source recharge characterization at those locations. The spatial clustering of tritium-positive wells near the stream channel is the appropriate evidence type for the claim of preferential recharge along the stream corridor — it is observed geography, not inferred.

The 4.2 mm/year recharge-rate estimate is a model output from the flow model calibrated to hydraulic heads. It is the appropriate evidence type for a basin-scale water balance estimate, but it is not the appropriate evidence type for recharge-timing characterization — isotopes, not heads, answer the "how old" question. These two outputs are complementary and address different aspects of the recharge system.
