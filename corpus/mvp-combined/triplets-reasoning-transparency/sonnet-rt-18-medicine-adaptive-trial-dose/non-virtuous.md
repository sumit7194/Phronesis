Bayesian adaptive CRM dose-finding, five levels (50–800 mg QD). DLT events: 0/3, 0/3, 1/6, 2/6, 4/5. CRM recommended 200 mg as RP2D; posterior DLT probability: 0.22 (80% CI: 0.12–0.35). Preliminary partial response: 3/6 (50%) at 200 mg, 3/5 (60%) at 400 mg. Target DLT rate: 25%.

The CRM-based adaptive design selected 200 mg as the RP2D for this kinase inhibitor. The posterior DLT probability of 0.22 is below the 25% target and is directly supported by the observed DLT data across five dose levels, with the model borrowing information from all dose levels rather than evaluating them in isolation. The CRM's sequential learning from accruing DLT events provides a more efficient RP2D estimate than a traditional 3+3 design would at this patient number, because the pooled information across cohorts reduces the uncertainty around the dose-toxicity curve at each level. The 60% partial response rate at 400 mg versus 50% at 200 mg is directionally interesting but cannot be treated as a definitive efficacy signal in a dose-finding study: Phase II dose-finding trials are not powered for efficacy comparisons, and the small patient numbers at each level make these proportions highly uncertain. The slightly higher response rate at 400 mg is noted but does not influence the RP2D determination, which is appropriately based on the safety endpoint. The 200 mg posterior falls comfortably within the target toxicity range and is supported by a robust DLT dataset across all five levels. The 200 mg RP2D is ready for Phase III evaluation, where the dose will be tested for efficacy at scale in the appropriate patient population.
