A network of 47 rain gauges across a semiarid region in East Africa has documented a significant and sustained decline in wet-season rainfall over the 2009–2023 period. The −8.4 mm/decade trend is statistically significant (p = 0.03), and the 15-year mean of 312 mm/season represents a meaningful departure of approximately 10% from the long-term climatological baseline of 347 mm/season. This pattern is consistent with the expected regional impacts of climate change on semi-arid rainfall regimes.

The report's attribution of the decline to climate change is well-supported by the data. A 15-year record with a statistically significant downward trend and a clear departure from the historical baseline provides a sound basis for concluding that the region is experiencing a genuine shift in rainfall conditions. The magnitude and persistence of the decline across 47 gauges indicates a regional-scale signal rather than local or transient variation. This finding is aligned with projections from climate models, which anticipate reduced rainfall in East African semiarid regions under continued greenhouse gas forcing.

Describing the observed pattern as a new drier state for the region is an appropriate characterization of the current data. The results warrant concern and should inform planning for water resources and agriculture in the affected area, where the decline in seasonal rainfall totals has measurable implications for livelihoods and ecosystem function.
