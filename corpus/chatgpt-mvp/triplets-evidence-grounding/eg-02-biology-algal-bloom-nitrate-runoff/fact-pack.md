## Scenario title
Nitrate pulse and chlorophyll response in lake mesocosms

## Factual substrate
- A mesocosm experiment used 24 lake tanks assigned to nitrate pulse, phosphate pulse, combined nitrate-plus-phosphate pulse, or control conditions.
- Mean chlorophyll increased from 18 to 37 µg/L in the nitrate-only tanks over 9 days.
- Mean chlorophyll increased from 19 to 22 µg/L in the phosphate-only tanks and reached 41 µg/L in the combined-pulse tanks.
- Grazer counts stayed within 6% of baseline across all tanks during the experiment.
- Field samples from two storm drains feeding the lake showed elevated nitrate during rain events.
- The mesocosms cannot prove that storm drains caused the whole lake bloom.

## Known ambiguity
- The tank results identify nutrient limitation under controlled conditions, while the storm-drain samples only show a plausible external nitrate source.
- The correct working conclusion is nitrate-driven bloom risk, but whole-lake source attribution remains underdetermined.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: Nitrate addition best explains the tank chlorophyll response, and storm drains are a plausible but not proven nitrate source for the lake.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner over-qualifies every background statement until the inference is buried.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner declares nitrate runoff the cause without specifying which observations support the tank effect and which only support source plausibility.

## Notes
Correctness confound: the non-virtuous passage reaches the correct broad conclusion that nitrate is the primary bloom risk, but it does so through under-grounded assertion.
