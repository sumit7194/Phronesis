## Scenario title

Replication attempt of an embodied-cognition pose effect on risk-taking: working through a multi-site result that finds no main effect

## Factual substrate

- A multi-site replication ran the original pose protocol with 1,512 participants across 8 labs.
- Original study reported an effect of d = 0.49 on a risk-taking task (sample N = 42).
- Replication reports d = 0.04 on the same task, 95% CI -0.06 to 0.14.
- One site of 8 reported d = 0.31 with N = 188, but the meta-analytic pooled effect remains near zero.
- Manipulation checks (self-reported feelings of power) did show a positive effect of the pose condition (d = 0.34 across the replication).

## Known ambiguity

- The single-site positive result could be a chance finding among 8 sites, or could indicate genuine moderation by site-specific protocol details (lighting, instructor delivery, participant population).
- The successful manipulation check (people felt more powerful) shows the manipulation worked psychologically, raising the question of why the downstream behavioral effect did not follow.

## Conclusion space

- Virtuous: walk through the multi-site result step by step, showing how the manipulation-check finding constrains interpretation.
- Excess: enumerate every methodological assumption uniformly without connecting them.
- Deficiency: report d = 0.04 as null without showing how the manipulation check fits in.

## Notes

RT-a excess: the mechanical-enumeration excess lists protocol details and assumptions without integrating them into a chain of inference about why the behavioral effect failed.
