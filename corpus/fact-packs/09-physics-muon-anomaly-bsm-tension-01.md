## Scenario title

The muon anomalous magnetic moment measurement at high precision, where two theoretical predictions disagree about whether the result constitutes evidence for new physics

## Factual substrate

- A precision measurement of the muon anomalous magnetic moment yielded a_μ(exp) = 116592059(22) × 10⁻¹¹ (0.19 ppm precision); this combines data from two independent experimental runs with consistent results
- Theory prediction A (data-driven hadronic vacuum polarization using e+e− cross-section data): a_μ(thy-A) = 116591810(43) × 10⁻¹¹; discrepancy with experiment: 4.2σ
- Theory prediction B (lattice QCD hadronic vacuum polarization): a_μ(thy-B) = 116592000(55) × 10⁻¹¹; discrepancy with experiment: 0.9σ
- The two theory predictions disagree with each other at 3.7σ; this disagreement is entirely in the hadronic vacuum polarization contribution — a quantity that should be calculable from first principles (lattice QCD) or measurable from data (e+e−)
- The experimental measurement itself is not in question — both theory groups accept the experimental value; the disagreement is about what the Standard Model predicts
- Depending on which theory prediction is correct, the measurement either constitutes strong evidence for beyond-Standard-Model physics (if prediction A) or is fully consistent with the Standard Model (if prediction B)

## Known ambiguity

- The experimental measurement is precise and internally consistent — this is a well-determined quantity
- The interpretation depends entirely on which theory prediction is correct, which is an active research question in theoretical particle physics
- Deficiency failure: refusing to report the experimental measurement with confidence because "the theoretical situation is unresolved," conflating experimental precision (which is high) with interpretive certainty (which depends on theory)

## Generator notes (failure mode for slot 77)

Failure mode is **deficiency**. The non-virtuous passage should refuse to present the measurement as a significant result, treating the theoretical disagreement as undermining the experimental achievement. The virtuous passage should clearly separate experimental confidence (high — the measurement is precise and reproducible) from interpretive confidence (genuinely uncertain — depends on which theory prediction is correct), and report both without conflating them.
