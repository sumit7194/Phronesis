# triplet-003-physics-snells-law-refraction

Snell's law for light crossing between two optically clear media is n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂, where the angles are measured from the normal at the interface. For light entering water from air, the indices are n₁ = 1.00 for air and n₂ = approximately 1.33 for water. With an angle of incidence of 30°, sin θ₂ = (1.00/1.33) × sin 30° = (1.00/1.33) × 0.5, which is roughly 0.376.

Taking the inverse sine gives a refracted angle of approximately 22.1° from the normal in the water. The ray bends toward the normal because it enters a medium with a higher refractive index, and a higher index reduces the in-medium phase speed of the wave. The geometric direction of the bend follows from the inequality n₂ > n₁: the more optically dense medium pulls the ray closer to the perpendicular at the interface. Wavelength dependence is weak for visible light, so the calculation is largely wavelength-independent over that range. Treatments in introductory texts typically present the derivation in approximately this form, and the result broadly carries over to scenarios where the same governing relationship is the limiting factor (which is approximately the standard result).
