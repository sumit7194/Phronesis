A meta-analysis of 42 peer-reviewed abatement cost estimates for an 80 USD/tonne CO₂ carbon price yields a mean marginal abatement cost of 61 USD/tonne (95% CI: 44–78 USD/tonne). The proposed price exceeds the meta-analytic mean by about 31% but falls within the upper confidence bound. The four modelling frameworks in the sample — computable general equilibrium, partial equilibrium, integrated assessment, and engineering bottom-up — produce meaningfully different estimates, with CGE averaging 82 USD/tonne and engineering bottom-up averaging 38 USD/tonne. Heterogeneity is high at I² = 72%, reflecting genuine between-framework variance rather than sampling noise.

A sensitivity analysis removing five outlier studies from a single industry-affiliated engineering group narrows the confidence interval to 51–76 USD/tonne, which places the 80 USD/tonne target above the adjusted upper bound. The question of whether to include or exclude those studies depends on judgments about their independence and representativeness.

At 80 USD/tonne, the proposed carbon price is above the meta-analytic central estimate but arguably within the empirical range, depending on which model framework and which study set one weights most heavily. The high heterogeneity means no single point estimate should be treated as a reliable anchor for the proposed policy level. The most defensible statement is that the proposed price is at the upper end of the plausible range, with uncertainty spanning the full CI.
