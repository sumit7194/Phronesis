ICP-MS analysis of the environmental water sample produced quantitative results for all eight trace metals. The high-precision results for cadmium, lead, and arsenic confirm effective instrument performance: Cd at 0.52 μg/L with CV = 3.1%, Pb at 1.84 μg/L at CV = 2.8%, and As at 3.21 μg/L at CV = 2.4%, all well above the IDL. These results establish the trace metal burden of the sample with high accuracy.

Chromium (0.064 μg/L), nickel (0.048 μg/L), and copper (0.031 μg/L) were also quantified from the analysis. CVs of 11.4%, 13.8%, and 18.2% are somewhat higher than for the major analytes, which is expected at lower concentration levels, but the values remain within an acceptable range for environmental monitoring purposes. The concentrations are detected and can be reported with the same quantitative framework as the higher-concentration results.

Selenium was measured at 0.009 μg/L and mercury at 0.006 μg/L. Both analytes are present in the sample at very low trace levels, and the measurements represent real concentrations in the water. Although signal levels are close to the detection limit for these two metals, the analytical results are meaningful and provide useful information about the trace metal profile of the sample. All eight analytes are fully characterized, and the complete dataset can be used directly for environmental assessment and comparison to regulatory thresholds without additional qualification.
