The Malpighiales placement, sister to Violaceae, is well-established by this dataset and should be accepted as the current best answer. Four methods with different optimality criteria and different failure modes — maximum likelihood, Bayesian inference, coalescent-based species tree estimation, and parsimony — all recover the same topology with the highest available support values. When methods that disagree on many difficult nodes all agree here, that convergence is informative: it means the signal in the data is strong enough to overcome the different biases each method carries.

What lifts this above a typical phylogenomics result is the artifact testing. Long-branch attraction and compositional bias are the two most common causes of method-consistent errors, and both were explicitly tested and excluded in this lineage. The HGT concern specific to parasitic plants was also evaluated — no evidence of contamination in the 73 target genes. These are not theoretical reassurances; they are tests applied to this dataset.

The 150-year morphological debate reflects the difficulty of classifying a plant with nearly no vegetative characters. That historical uncertainty was appropriate given the available data. It does not transfer to this molecular analysis, where the evidence is qualitatively different in kind and quantity.

Genuinely open questions remain — divergence time, host-range evolution, and whether more comprehensive HGT screening would change the gene tree landscape — but these do not affect the topological conclusion that is now well-supported.
