The test characteristics from the validation cohort of 1,400 participants are sensitivity 92% and specificity 88%, and the screened age band shows a population prevalence around 1.2%. The patient has no other risk factors that would shift the prior away from the population baseline.

Working through what the result means, the test is reasonably sensitive but the false-positive rate sits at 12%, which on a low base rate matters more than the headline numbers suggest. Out of every 1,000 people screened in this band, about 12 actually have the condition, and the test will catch around 11 of them. Of the 988 without the condition, the test will incorrectly flag about 119. So a positive result mostly arises from the false-positive pool rather than from a true case.

That means a single positive screen does not establish the diagnosis. The patient should be told that the test came back positive but that confirmatory testing is the appropriate next step before changing any management plan. Local guidelines reflect this reasoning: they recommend a second-stage test rather than initiating treatment off one result.
