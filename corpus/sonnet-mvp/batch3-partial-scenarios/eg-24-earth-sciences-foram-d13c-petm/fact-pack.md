## Scenario title

Benthic foraminifera δ¹³C excursion across the Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum in a deep-sea sediment core

## Factual substrate

- A 320-metre sediment core from ~3,200 m water depth contains a benthic foraminifera δ¹³C record spanning 4 million years across the Paleocene–Eocene boundary.
- The δ¹³C excursion (CIE) onset is identified at a core depth of 214.3 metres; the magnitude is −3.1‰ (bulk carbonate) and −3.4‰ (monospecific benthic foraminifera picks), measured by isotope ratio mass spectrometry.
- Orbitally-tuned age model places the CIE onset at 55.93 ± 0.05 Ma; magnetostratigraphy constrains the boundary to within chron C24r.
- A companion foraminiferal dissolution horizon at 214.1–214.6 m indicates a contemporaneous lysocline deepening event — independent evidence of ocean acidification at the same stratigraphic level.
- Carbon isotope excursions of similar magnitude occur at three other stratigraphic levels in the core, with magnitudes of −1.2‰, −0.9‰, and −1.4‰, but without companion dissolution horizons.

## Known ambiguity

- The δ¹³C excursion magnitude of −3.1 to −3.4‰ is consistent with multiple carbon source scenarios (volcanic + permafrost, volcanic alone, methane hydrate alone); the source attribution is a theoretical inference from the isotopic mass balance, not a direct observation of the carbon source.
- The three smaller excursions could be diagenetic artifacts or genuine hyperthermal events; the absence of dissolution horizons makes them ambiguous.

## Conclusion space

- Conclusion A (virtuous-compatible): The CIE at 214.3 m is robust — two independent isotopic measurements, an orbitally-tuned age model, magnetostratigraphic constraint, and the companion dissolution horizon all converge on a large-magnitude, rapidly-onset carbon cycle perturbation at 55.93 Ma; the source attribution remains inferential.
- Conclusion B (excess-failure-compatible, non-virtuous-right): The passage over-specifies the evidence type for every individual claim — naming the specific mass spectrometry method, the age-model tuning target, the chron, the dissolution horizon mechanism, and the mass-balance calculation framework for source attribution — but still reaches the correct CIE age and magnitude. The result is correct but the evidence-type citation is bureaucratic (every claim has a two-clause evidence pedigree appended).
- Conclusion C (deficiency-failure-compatible): Presents the δ¹³C excursion timing and source attribution (carbon release) as if they were both directly measured, without distinguishing the isotopic measurement from the theoretical mass-balance interpretation.
