There might be a new LC-MS peak somewhere around m/z 312, roughly 16 mass units below the parent compound at m/z 328, but it’s hard to say what to make of it. Even though it has shown up in three independent sample preparations, there could still be a variety of analytical issues that would explain the observation, and I’m not sure we can be confident that the peak is truly real rather than an artifact of the system.

The fact that the peak appears about 0.8 minutes earlier than the parent could mean it is a more polar species, but retention shifts like that can happen for many reasons, so it doesn’t really narrow things down. Similarly, the observation that the peak area grows roughly linearly with sample age over a two‑week window at 40°C / 75% RH could indicate degradation, but it could also reflect instrument drift, carryover effects, or other uncontrolled factors, so I don’t think we can say much from that alone.

The lab did switch to a new lot of acetonitrile mobile phase about four weeks ago, which might matter, but we also don’t have a clean method blank using the new acetonitrile batch alone, so that angle is also uncertain. Even the water-only blank not showing the peak doesn’t fully settle anything.

Overall, I don’t think it’s possible to commit to any interpretation here without substantially more testing and additional data.
