Each claim ties to a specific observation type with named provenance.

The bleached-segment fraction comes from the aerial transect over 142 reef segments: 64% with >30% bleached colonies and 22% with >60%. That is a reef-scale categorical observation. The 1998 historical comparison comes from the same survey methodology on the same reef: 49% at >30% bleached at DHW 7.6, so the current 64% at DHW 8.4 (reef mean) is the directly observed difference between the two events.

The satellite Degree Heating Weeks (8.4 reef-mean, 11.2 north, 5.7 south) is remote-sensing-derived and spatially averaged at the satellite footprint. It anchors the heat-stress driver but the reef-mean does not capture sub-segment heterogeneity; the 11.2/5.7 north-south spread is the specific anchor for the sector-level gradient.

The 38.4% post-bleaching versus 47.1% pre-bleaching live-coral cover is in-water station-level evidence at 18 specific stations one year apart. That 8.7-point reduction is anchored to repeat measurement at the same stations, not to a reef-wide average. Generalising to the whole reef requires the assumption that the 18 stations are representative.

The 31% herbivore biomass reduction comes from the acoustic-tag study of 62 individual herbivorous fish; that is species-specific, individually-tracked evidence, with a baseline from 2021 on the same animals or replacements. The sediment-trap deposition of 4.3 mg/cm²/day versus 2.1 mg/cm²/day is point-instrument evidence at four named sites.

The empirical picture is: aerial-confirmed bleaching exceeding 1998 levels; satellite-confirmed heat stress also exceeding 1998 levels; in-water station-level coral-cover loss; acoustic-tracked herbivore decline; sediment-trap doubling at four sites. Each anchor is tied to a specific instrument and spatial scope; reef-wide statements rest on the aerial transect and the satellite footprint, not the smaller in-water samples.
