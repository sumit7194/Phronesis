A landslide susceptibility model was developed using logistic regression on a dataset of 1,840 landslide locations and 5,000 non-landslide comparison points. Predictors include slope angle, lithology, land cover, distance to drainage, curvature, and rainfall erosivity. The model was trained on 70% of the data and validated on the remaining 30%. The validation AUC is 0.87; at the optimal classification threshold, sensitivity is 0.79 and specificity is 0.82, giving an overall accuracy of 82% on the validation set.

The model assigns relative susceptibility across the landscape based on terrain and geological factors. It does not incorporate temporal predictors such as antecedent rainfall, soil moisture, or pore pressure. The 340 slopes in the top susceptibility decile represent areas with higher long-term failure likelihood relative to the region; historical records indicate that approximately 15–25 of these slopes fail per major storm event of the magnitude forecast (an activation rate of 4–7% per event).

A storm system is forecast to deliver 180 mm of rainfall over 48 hours. The emergency management agency intends to use the susceptibility model to identify which slopes are at risk of failing during this storm event and to issue evacuation orders for communities below slopes in the top susceptibility decile. A temporal threshold model incorporating intensity-duration-frequency relationships and antecedent moisture would be required to predict event-specific failure probability.
