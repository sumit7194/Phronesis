Eighteen wells were sampled for δ¹⁸O and tritium. δ¹⁸O ranged from −9.4‰ to −6.1‰; modern regional precipitation averages −6.8‰, while Pleistocene-age recharge typically sits around −10 to −11‰. Fourteen wells were below the tritium detection limit (pre-modern, >60 years old); four wells showed 1.2–3.4 TU, indicating modern or mixed recharge. The four tritium-positive wells cluster near the perennial stream channel. The model calibrated to head data gives 4.2 mm/year mean recharge.

The ³H data and δ¹⁸O measurements are the evidence types appropriate to characterizing recharge timing and source water: they directly address the "how old and from where" questions. The tritium result in 14 of 18 wells is appropriate evidence for pre-modern recharge characterization; the δ¹⁸O values in the tritium-positive wells, close to modern precipitation (−6.8‰), are appropriate evidence for modern-recharge source characterization at those four locations. The stream-channel spatial clustering of the tritium-positive wells is the appropriate evidence type for the preferential-recharge-pathway inference — it is observed spatial coincidence, not a model output.

The 4.2 mm/year model-derived recharge rate is the appropriate evidence type for basin-scale water balance estimation; it is not appropriate evidence for recharge-timing characterization, because heads respond to a mix of storage changes, transmissivity, and boundary conditions that the model integrates rather than isolating. Combining the isotope results (which address timing and source) with the flow model result (which addresses quantity) gives a more complete hydrogeological picture than either alone, with each evidence type answering the question it is suited to.
