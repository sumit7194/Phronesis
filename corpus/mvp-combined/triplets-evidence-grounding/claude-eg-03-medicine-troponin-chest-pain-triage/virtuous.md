Two different kinds of claim are being weighed and they need separating. The empirical claim is what the local cohort actually showed: 1,212 of 1,840 chest-pain presentations had a 0-hour high-sensitivity troponin T below 5 ng/L, and 5 of those 1,212 had a 30-day MACE — a rate of 0.4% with 95% CI 0.13 to 0.93%. Those numbers are direct measurements on this dataset over 14 months, with the explicit exclusion of ECG-positive patients regardless of troponin.

The published comparator — pooled 30-day MACE of 0.3%, 95% CI 0.2 to 0.5% — is also empirical, but from prior multi-centre validation work, not from these patients. The compatibility claim "this cohort matches the benchmark" is a comparison between two observed point estimates with overlapping CIs. That is what the data here support empirically.

What the data here do not support is the underlying mechanism claim — the rationale for why a 5 ng/L cutoff identifies a low-risk physiology. That rests on prior pathophysiology and assay-validation studies, not on the 0.4% observed locally. The local rate is consistent with the mechanism but does not establish it.

The 11.2% loss to follow-up is a measurement-completeness limit. The 0.4% rate is conditional on captured events; if even two missed patients had MACE, the point estimate shifts toward the upper benchmark CI. On the present cohort, with that caveat noted, the empirical observation is that local implementation looks compatible with the published safety benchmark. That is a comparison of overlapping CIs, not a confirmation of mechanism, and not yet a generalisable claim about long-run local performance.
