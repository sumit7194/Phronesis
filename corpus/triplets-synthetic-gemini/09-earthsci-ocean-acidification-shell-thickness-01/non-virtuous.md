The ocean monitoring data seems to show some pH decline across the coastal region, though 15 years is a relatively short measurement period in massive oceanographic terms, and there could undeniably be background cyclical buffering effects we haven't properly accounted for mathematically. Given the complex scale of ocean chemistry, it's hard to say whether this reported decline is actually a robust shift. 

Similarly, the constrained biological data is highly suggestive of bivalve shell thinning over the last five years, but it's hard to firmly link this to acidification given the massive analytical confounds occurring at these exact sites. Because agricultural runoff completely changes local freshwater density and trace metal concentration, we can't tell if the shells are reacting to the water chemistry or the mud. While lab studies show severe impacts under completely artificial and rigidly controlled tanks, attempting to apply that to the real ocean is always overwhelmingly complicated. Overall, it's difficult to draw firm conclusions about either the supposed pH trend or the resulting biological shell impact. The entire dynamic seems fundamentally unclear, and significantly more data would clearly be required to validate either side of the coastal chemical degradation hypothesis. We must treat the entire ecosystem report with heavy analytical skepticism.
