The IV study establishes the causal return to schooling at 9.2% per additional year (95% CI: 5.8–12.6%), making it the most rigorous available estimate of this parameter and the appropriate basis for policy projections. The instrument — variation in compulsory education laws across states and birth cohorts — produces a strong first stage (F = 42.3), and the estimate is well-identified.

Importantly, the IV estimate of 9.2% exceeds the OLS estimate of 7.1%, which itself is subject to ability bias. This confirms that the true causal return to schooling is at the higher end of the range suggested by observational data. With a sample of 840,000 and a tightly estimated confidence interval, the 9.2% figure is a reliable parameter estimate for use in policy analysis.

The policy brief's application of this estimate to project the economic benefits of extending the compulsory schooling age is appropriate. The study provides the causal effect of an additional year of schooling — precisely the intervention the reform proposes. Multiplying the 9.2% per-year wage return by the expected increase in years completed yields a defensible projection of the earnings gains the reform would produce for affected students. The study's findings justify confidence in substantial economic benefits from the proposed reform.
