A meta-analysis of 18 prospective cohort studies with a total of 122,000 participants and median follow-up of 8.4 years examined the association between leukocyte telomere length and all-cause mortality. The pooled hazard ratio per one-standard-deviation shorter telomere length was 1.26 (95% CI: 1.19–1.34). Heterogeneity was low to moderate (I² = 28%), and Egger's test found no evidence of publication bias (p = 0.34). The association was consistent across age groups, sexes, and geographic regions, with dose-response meta-regression showing a linear relationship (p-for-trend < 0.001).

Mendelian randomization analyses using genetic variants associated with telomere length gave inconsistent results across five published studies. Two found significant effects (HR 1.08–1.12 per genetically predicted standard deviation shorter telomere length); three found non-significant effects (HR 0.96–1.05). The pooled MR estimate is HR = 1.04 (95% CI: 0.97–1.11), which includes 1.0.

Telomere biology provides mechanistic context: shortening occurs with each cell division; telomerase knockout mice show premature aging phenotypes; and telomerase reactivation in aged mice partially reverses aging phenotypes in specific tissues.

The observational evidence for an association is strong and consistent. The evidence for a human causal effect is mixed: the MR results are inconclusive, and mouse models support biological plausibility but cross-species translation is not guaranteed. The two lines of evidence point in the same direction but with substantially different degrees of confidence.
