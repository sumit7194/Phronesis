The transaction was flagged "high risk" by the detection model. The base rate of fraud on this channel is 0.18% across all transactions, and the validated rate of actual fraud among "high risk" flags is 14% based on the holdout of 12,400 transactions. The cost of a customer-call false positive is around 3 minutes of agent time plus some customer friction; the cost of a missed fraud is on average $214.

The "high risk" flag is informative — much more informative than the channel base rate alone — but it is far from a near-certain fraud signal. The validation makes that quite clear: the model is good enough to lift the probability of fraud sharply above baseline, but the great majority of flagged transactions still turn out to be legitimate when the call gets made. That is what one should expect when detecting a rare event with any imperfect classifier.

The cost asymmetry is what really drives the decision rather than the level of the flag-conditional probability itself. A few minutes of agent time and a bit of customer friction is meaningfully cheaper than the average loss on a fraud that gets through. Even with the realistic level of false alarms among flagged transactions, the cost-weighted call is comfortably in favor of placing the call rather than skipping it.

I am confident the right rule is to trigger the customer-call workflow on every "high risk" flag of this kind, with the understanding that most will resolve as legitimate transactions. The playbook should document that decision rule and tie it to the validated flag-level fraud rate so that if the model drifts and the rate falls substantially, the rule can be revisited rather than left in place by inertia.
