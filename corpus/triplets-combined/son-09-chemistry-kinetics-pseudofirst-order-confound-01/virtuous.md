The pseudo-first-order rate constants are well-measured in this dataset. Values of 0.024, 0.028, and 0.031 min⁻¹ across the three P concentrations, with R² of 0.987–0.994 for the first-order fits, are reliable kinetic measurements within the experimental conditions used. That is a solid, circumscribed finding.

What these data structurally cannot establish is the reaction order with respect to Q. Pseudo-first-order conditions deliberately hold [Q] constant to simplify kinetic analysis — the cost of that design choice is that all information about Q-dependence is absent from the dataset. Every kobs value in this study was collected at the same Q concentration (0.10 M). To determine the Q-order, experiments must be run at multiple Q concentrations while holding P in large excess, then the slope of log(kobs) versus log([Q]) yields the Q-order. No such experiments were conducted, so the Q-order is simply not constrained by any data collected here.

The slight increase in kobs with increasing [P] — from 0.024 to 0.031 min⁻¹ — also deserves attention. Strict first-order dependence on P would predict constant kobs as [P] changes under these conditions; the observed trend is not consistent with that expectation. It could indicate mixed-order behavior or partial saturation at the higher P concentrations, or it may fall within experimental error. Additional P concentration points would clarify this.

The proposed second-order rate law (first in P, first in Q) is a hypothesis motivated by mechanistic analogy. Establishing it requires variable-[Q] experiments that have not yet been done.
