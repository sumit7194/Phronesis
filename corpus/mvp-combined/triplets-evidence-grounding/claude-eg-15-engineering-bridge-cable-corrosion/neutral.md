Endoscopic inspection at 12 wedge openings on the 50-year-old main cable showed Stage 2–3 corrosion (on a 4-stage scale) in 18% of inspected wires, with 6 broken wires identified across the inspected positions. Magnetic main-flux measurements estimated total cross-section loss of 4.2% relative to as-built. Acoustic monitoring over the past 18 months recorded 47 wire-break events distributed across the cable.

A residual-strength model used the broken-wire count, the observed corrosion stages, and a Weibull statistical extension to the un-inspected wires to predict the current factor of safety. The model output is a factor of safety of 2.1 against design loading with a 95% credible interval of 1.7 to 2.6.

A laboratory tensile test on 18 retrieved sample wires gave a mean tensile strength of 1,420 MPa, against a specified minimum of 1,570 MPa, a reduction of 9.5%.

The visual inspection, broken-wire count, magnetic-flux measurement, acoustic record, and tensile-test results are all measurements of different aspects of the cable. The factor-of-safety figure is a model output derived from those measurements plus the Weibull extension.

The combined assessment supports the conclusion that the cable retains adequate margin against the design loading at present, with the model midpoint of 2.1 well above the typical 1.0 limit and even the lower credible bound of 1.7 above the design check value. The 9.5% tensile-strength reduction and 4.2% cross-section loss are real but not yet at levels that would individually trigger a re-rating. Continued acoustic monitoring and a planned re-inspection in 5 years would be prudent given the rate of acoustic events and the reliance of the safety estimate on the Weibull assumption.
