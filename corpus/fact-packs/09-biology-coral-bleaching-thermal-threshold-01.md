## Scenario title

A controlled laboratory experiment establishing a coral bleaching thermal threshold that is well-validated against 15 years of field bleaching observations at the same reef site

## Factual substrate

- Laboratory acute thermal stress experiments on a reef-building coral species determined a bleaching threshold of 30.5°C sustained for ≥4 days; n = 48 coral fragments across 4 genotypes; bleaching (loss of >50% Symbiodiniaceae density) occurred in 42 of 48 fragments at 30.5°C (87.5%) and 0 of 48 at 29.0°C control; the threshold was consistent across all 4 genotypes
- Field validation: 15 years of in-situ temperature logger data and annual bleaching surveys at the source reef site; in all 6 years where SST exceeded 30.5°C for ≥4 consecutive days, bleaching was observed (100% hit rate); in 9 years where SST remained below 30.5°C, no bleaching was observed (0% false alarm rate)
- The 15-year predictive accuracy is 100% (15/15 years correctly classified); Cohen's kappa = 1.00; the threshold applies to the specific species, genotype diversity, and reef site studied
- NOAA Coral Reef Watch degree heating week (DHW) analysis: all 6 bleaching years had DHW ≥ 4; the 30.5°C threshold provides comparable predictive power to DHW for this site
- Local stressors (sedimentation, nutrient loading, disease) were monitored annually; no bleaching was observed in years with high local stress but sub-threshold temperatures

## Known ambiguity

- The laboratory threshold is strongly validated by the 15-year field record — 100% accuracy with no false positives or negatives
- The remaining uncertainty is whether this threshold applies to other reef sites with different thermal histories (potential for local thermal adaptation) and whether future bleaching responses may shift as corals acclimate or adapt
- Deficiency failure: refusing to use the validated threshold for management planning because "laboratory conditions don't represent the field" despite 15 years of field validation data showing perfect concordance
