## Scenario title

Interpreting δ¹⁸O from a speleothem record: temperature versus ice-volume signal and the role of explicit assumptions in the reconstruction

## Factual substrate

- A stalagmite from a mid-latitude cave was dated by U-Th and yielded a continuous δ¹⁸O record from 130,000 to 10,000 years before present (yBP).
- δ¹⁸O values range from −3.8‰ to −7.4‰ (V-PDB), with the most negative values occurring between 25,000 and 18,000 yBP (Last Glacial Maximum period).
- Modern cave drip-water δ¹⁸O is −6.1‰; modern precipitation δ¹⁸O at this latitude is −6.4‰ (V-SMOW, converted to V-PDB for comparison).
- The temperature sensitivity of calcite δ¹⁸O with respect to cave air temperature is approximately −0.22‰/°C (oxygen isotope fractionation during precipitation of calcite from water).
- The LGM ocean δ¹⁸O (ice-volume effect) is estimated from benthic foraminifera stacks at approximately +1.0‰ relative to Holocene values.
- No independent local paleotemperature proxy (e.g., clumped isotopes, noble gas paleotemperature from groundwater) exists for this cave site.

## Known ambiguity

- Speleothem δ¹⁸O integrates: (1) temperature effect on fractionation during calcite precipitation, (2) source-water δ¹⁸O effect (rainfall δ¹⁸O changes with regional circulation patterns and ice-volume effects on seawater δ¹⁸O), and (3) kinetic fractionation effects if the speleothem did not precipitate in isotopic equilibrium. Without an independent temperature proxy, (1) and (2) cannot be fully separated.

## Notes

RT-b contrast: The virtuous passage makes explicit the assumption that the source-water signal is dominated by the ice-volume effect and the temperature fractionation term, and that kinetic effects are negligible — assumptions that must be stated rather than silently embedded in the reconstruction. The deficiency passage converts δ¹⁸O directly to "temperature" without naming these assumptions.
