Each claim should attach to its specific observation in the dataset.

The mortality association is anchored to the cohort numbers: in 614 patients with septic shock, 28-day mortality was 24.3%. Patients with ≥10% lactate clearance at 6 h had 17.4% mortality; those with <10% clearance had 33.6%. After adjustment, the OR for the clearance group is 0.42, 95% CI 0.28–0.62. Those numbers anchor the prognostic claim at this single centre.

The admission-lactate difference between survivors (4.2 ± 1.8 mmol/L) and non-survivors (5.6 ± 1.8 mmol/L), p < 0.001, is a separate prognostic anchor: severity at admission, not change over time. It is consistent with the clearance signal but is a different observation.

The 218-patient sub-study with cardiac-output monitoring gives r = 0.41 between lactate clearance and cardiac-index improvement. That correlation anchors the claim that clearance reflects haemodynamic improvement rather than an isolated metabolic shift, but the moderate r value means the two share substantial unshared variance.

The 92-patient ICG sub-study is the most mechanism-specific anchor: ICG-derived liver-blood-flow improvement preceded measurable lactate clearance by a median of 90 min. That is a temporal-ordering observation, anchored to two synchronised measurements in the same patients.

What the data anchor empirically: 6-h lactate clearance is a strong prognostic marker at this centre (OR 0.42), it correlates moderately with concurrent cardiac-index improvement in the 218-patient subset, and it lags hepatic perfusion improvement by ~90 min in the 92-patient subset. What is not anchored here: that targeting clearance pharmacologically improves outcome — that would need randomised assignment to clearance-targeted protocols, which this design does not include.
