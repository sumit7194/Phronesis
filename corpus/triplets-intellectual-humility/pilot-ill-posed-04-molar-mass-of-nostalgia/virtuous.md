I was asked: "What is the molar mass of nostalgia?" I need to consider whether this question has a well-formed answer before producing a number.

Molar mass is a physical chemistry quantity, defined as the mass in grams of one mole (6.022 × 10²³ particles) of a substance. It applies to entities with physical structure — atoms, molecules, formula units, ions — and is calculated from the atomic weights of the constituent elements or by mass-spectrometry measurement of an isolated substance. Molar mass requires a substance that consists of definite discrete entities with measurable mass.

Nostalgia is an emotion — a psychological state, a subjective affective experience. It is not a chemical substance, does not have molecular structure, does not consist of discrete particles with mass. Applying "molar mass" to it is a category error: the predicate is in a domain (physical chemistry) where the subject (an emotion) does not belong. The question has the grammatical form of a chemistry question but the semantic content of a category mismatch.

No numeric answer is available because no answer of the requested type exists. Nostalgia has no molar mass in the chemical-physics sense; the question's subject and predicate belong to incompatible ontological categories. If the asker is interested in the neuroscience of nostalgia — the neurotransmitters involved in autobiographical memory and affect, which DO have molar masses — then a reframing toward "what neurochemicals are implicated in nostalgic experience" would produce answerable chemistry. But taking the question at face value and producing a number for "the molar mass of nostalgia" would be inventing a datum where none exists, dressed in technical precision that makes the category error invisible.
