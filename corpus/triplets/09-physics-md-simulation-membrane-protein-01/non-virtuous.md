The molecular dynamics simulation reveals the gating mechanism of this voltage-gated potassium channel at atomic resolution. The 10-microsecond trajectory captures a complete closed-to-open transition, showing that the channel gates through a distinct pre-open intermediate state in which the voltage-sensing domain translocates before the activation gate opens. This two-step mechanism — VSD translocation followed by gate opening — is a fundamental feature of the channel's gating cycle.

The predicted free energy barrier of 4.2 kcal/mol for VSD translocation provides a quantitative basis for understanding the channel's activation kinetics and is consistent with the experimentally observed sigmoidal conductance-voltage relationship. The match between simulation energetics and experimental electrophysiology confirms that the simulation has captured the authentic gating mechanism. The structural details of the pre-open intermediate — VSD RMSD of 4.3 Å, gate maintaining its closed conformation — define the transition state for activation and represent the key conformational checkpoint in the gating cycle.

The use of the validated CHARMM36m force field and physiological temperature conditions ensures that the simulation accurately reflects the behavior of the channel in a native membrane environment. These results establish the structural mechanism of voltage-gated channel activation and provide a framework for understanding how mutations in the voltage-sensing domain alter gating kinetics. The pre-open intermediate identified here is a target for structure-guided design of channel modulators.
