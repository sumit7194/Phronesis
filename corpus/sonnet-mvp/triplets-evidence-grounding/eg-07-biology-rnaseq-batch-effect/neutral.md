The eight samples were split unequally across two library-preparation batches: three treated and two controls in batch 1, one treated and two controls in batch 2. PCA of the log-normalized counts shows PC1 (31% of variance) separating conditions and PC2 (14%) separating batches. The design is partially confounded — batch and condition are not orthogonal — which limits how cleanly any statistical correction can separate the two effects.

DESeq2 analysis with batch as a covariate identified 847 differentially expressed genes at FDR < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 1. The uncorrected model found 1,214, producing 367 additional genes. Those 367 extras are enriched for ribosomal protein genes (OR = 3.4, p < 0.001), which is consistent with library-preparation efficiency differences driving batch-associated expression shifts. Whether some ribosomal genes in that set are genuinely treatment-regulated cannot be resolved from this experiment, given the confounded design.

The batch-corrected 847-gene list is the more defensible differential expression result, but partial confounding means the correction is incomplete. The 367 batch-associated genes should be treated as suspects rather than confirmed artifacts or confirmed real hits — the enrichment makes a batch-artifact origin plausible, not certain. Validation of specific genes of interest from either list in an orthogonal experiment with balanced batch design would resolve the ambiguity.
