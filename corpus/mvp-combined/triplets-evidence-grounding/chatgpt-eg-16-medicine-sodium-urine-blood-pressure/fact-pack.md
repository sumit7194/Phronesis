## Scenario title
Low-sodium meal program and clinic blood-pressure change

## Factual substrate
- A clinic meal program enrolled 86 adults with elevated blood pressure for 8 weeks.
- Mean systolic blood pressure fell from 148 to 139 mmHg.
- Twenty-four-hour urinary sodium fell from 168 to 104 mmol/day.
- Mean body weight fell by 1.1 kg.
- Medication regimens were unchanged for 73 of the 86 participants.
- Home blood-pressure logs were missing for 21 participants.

## Known ambiguity
- Blood-pressure improvement could reflect sodium reduction, modest weight loss, medication changes in a minority, or measurement patterns.
- The meal program lacks a separate control group.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: Sodium reduction is a well-supported contributor to the blood-pressure drop, but causality is limited by the uncontrolled design and missing home logs.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner over-qualifies every ordinary clinical observation.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner declares sodium reduction caused the improvement without separating observed changes from causal mechanism.

## Notes
The non-virtuous passage depicts deficiency.
