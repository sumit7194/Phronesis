An LC-MS stability method that has been running for over a year on an active pharmaceutical ingredient recently started showing a new peak at m/z 312, roughly 16 mass units below the parent compound at m/z 328. The peak is reproducible across three independent sample preparations and appears in both freshly prepared and accelerated-storage samples at 40°C and 75% relative humidity. The method uses a C18 reversed-phase column with an acetonitrile-water gradient.

Three observations line up with a degradation-product interpretation. The 16-mass-unit offset is consistent with a CH₄ loss within instrument precision. The retention time comes 0.8 minutes earlier than the parent, which fits a more polar molecule. And the peak area grows roughly linearly with sample age over a two-week storage window under the accelerated conditions.

There is one operational detail to keep in mind. About four weeks ago, the lab switched to a new lot of acetonitrile mobile phase from a different vendor, after the original vendor had a supply disruption. The peak does not appear in a water-only method blank, but no one has yet run a method blank using only the new acetonitrile batch to check whether the solvent itself could be contributing.

The storage-dependent growth is the most informative observation here — genuine degradation products accumulate with time at elevated conditions, which is what the data shows. The solvent change is a potential confound that sits in the background. A reasonable reading of the current evidence is that the peak is most likely a degradation product, possibly via methyl loss, but the solvent question is unresolved and would need a targeted control experiment to close.
