The detector has logged the vertical muon flux for 18 months at 12 m depth, and the run-averaged rate sits about 7% above the standard simulation prediction. Monthly statistical uncertainty is roughly 1.2%, so counting noise cannot account for the offset. Pressure and stratospheric-temperature corrections have been applied; the residual seasonal modulation is approximately 0.6%, which is small compared with 7%.

Photomultiplier gain drift is bounded by the weekly LED calibration at under 2%. That contributes but cannot by itself explain the full excess. The simulation uses a primary spectral index of 2.7 between 100 GeV and 10 TeV, and recent compilations place the index in the 2.65 to 2.75 band, which could shift the predicted underground rate by a few percent in either direction.

The absolute efficiency was set at commissioning against a sea-level reference flux and has not been re-measured during the run. Combined with the spectral-index range, this gives several systematic channels that contribute to the offset. The seasonal residual is too small to matter, and the gain bound is tight, so neither of those is the explanation on its own.

The 7% excess reflects a mix of detector and simulation systematics together with a small genuine flux contribution. My conclusion is that the offset is likely accounted for by combining the gain bound, the normalization uncertainty, and the spectral-index range. The result should be reported as an offset under investigation, with continued monitoring and a tightened systematic budget rather than a discovery-style framing at this stage of the run.
