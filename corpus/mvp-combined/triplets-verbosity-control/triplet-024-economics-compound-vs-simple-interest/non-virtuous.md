# triplet-024-economics-compound-vs-simple-interest

A $1000 principal at 5% over 10 years grows to $1500 under simple interest (1000 × (1 + 0.05 × 10)) and $1628.89 under annual compounding (1000 × 1.05¹⁰ ≈ 1628.89). Simple interest pays each year on the original principal; compounding reinvests the periodic interest into the balance, so each year's interest grows. The compound balance exceeds the simple balance by roughly $128.89 over this horizon. The gap widens nonlinearly with rate and time: at 5% over 30 years, simple gives $2500 while compounding gives approximately $4321.94, a gap of roughly $1821.94. The same structure also fits the closely related problems in the field.
