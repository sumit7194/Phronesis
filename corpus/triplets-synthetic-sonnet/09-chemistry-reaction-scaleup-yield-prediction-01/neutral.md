Reaction R, a cryogenic lithiation/electrophile quench sequence, was optimized at 0.1 mmol laboratory scale over 12 experiments. The optimal conditions produced an isolated yield of 92% in five independent replication runs (mean 92.1%, SD 2.3%). The reaction requires strict temperature control at −78°C; the lithiation step has an enthalpy of approximately −45 kJ/mol and is highly sensitive to temperature, with byproduct formation increasing significantly above −65°C. At 0.1 mmol scale, adequate mixing is achieved by hand-swirling within seconds.

A scale-up to 100 g of substrate is proposed, representing approximately 350 mmol and a 3,500-fold increase in scale. No intermediate pilot-scale runs at 1 g or 10 g are planned. At 100 g scale, maintaining −78°C requires a large-capacity cryostat with high coolant circulation rates, and mixing a viscous lithiation reaction in a multi-liter vessel is mechanically more demanding and time-dependent than at 0.1 mmol scale.

This reaction class — aryllithium generation followed by electrophilic addition — is documented in the process chemistry literature to exhibit scale-dependent yield reductions. The primary factors are heat removal rate, which determines whether the reaction temperature can be maintained during exothermic additions, and mixing uniformity, which affects selectivity when reagent distribution is non-uniform. Neither the cryostat capacity nor the mixing characteristics at 100 g scale has been characterized.
