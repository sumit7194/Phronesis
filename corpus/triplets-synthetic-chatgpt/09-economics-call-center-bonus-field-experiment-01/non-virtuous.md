This 8-week field experiment in a single company’s outbound sales call center is interesting, but it’s hard to say much for certain. The treatment arm, which offered a small weekly cash bonus of about 5% of base pay for meeting a target, averaged 342 calls per week compared to 320 in the control arm. That’s a difference of 22 calls (95% CI 12 to 32) and is reported with p < 0.01, but even so it only reflects one setting and one eight‑week period, so I’m not sure we should treat it as a clear, general result.

The subgroup analyses are also suggestive but inconclusive. Agents with under 12 months of tenure show about a 9% effect while those with 12 months or more show a 4% effect, but the difference isn’t statistically significant (p = 0.16), so it could easily be random. The bottom third by baseline productivity shows a 12% effect versus 5% for the rest, with a marginal p = 0.04 that isn’t corrected for running two subgroup tests, so that too might just be a statistical artifact.

Turnover was 8% in treatment and 11% in control, but over eight weeks that difference is uncertain. We also don’t know what happens over the long term because there was no follow-up and the bonus wasn’t maintained.

Overall, it seems plausible that the bonus had some effect, but I’d want more studies before drawing firm conclusions about either the main effect or any subgroup patterns.
