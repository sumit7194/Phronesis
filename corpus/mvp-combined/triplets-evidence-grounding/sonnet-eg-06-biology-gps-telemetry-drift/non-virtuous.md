A group of juvenile ospreys were tracked with solar GPS-GSM tags logging fixes throughout daylight hours. The manufacturer specifies open-sky accuracy on the order of meters, and roost validation with stationary birds showed scatter that was somewhat larger than the manufacturer figure but still in a comparable range.

One bird produced a sequence of consecutive fixes forming a back-and-forth trajectory during a migration day, with short step displacements between fixes. This pattern is consistent with fine-scale local movement behavior — the bird was likely performing repeated passes over a small area, possibly prospecting for a roost or thermals. Across the full set of birds, raw total path length was meaningfully longer than the great-circle distance over multi-day segments, reflecting the typical tortuous nature of osprey migratory flight that includes foraging detours, wind corrections, and exploratory spirals. Habitat was classified from a fine-resolution land-cover raster, providing good spatial resolution for characterizing the birds' landscape use.

The results demonstrate that these ospreys show individualized movement strategies during migration, with one bird displaying notably fine-scale site-prospecting behavior. The movement-efficiency index captures genuine differences in how directly birds flew their migration routes. These GPS-derived movement metrics provide a reliable basis for analyzing habitat selection and behavioral flexibility across the juvenile migration period, and the patterns can be interpreted directly from the recorded tracks.
