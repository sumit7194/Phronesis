2,300 infants, 612 C-section vs 1,688 vaginal, 1.6-fold higher asthma at age 7 in C-section, 23% mediated by 1-month microbiome composition per the analysis.

The weakest link in the chain from these numbers to a microbiome-causal claim is maternal-indication confounding. The cohort is observational, and the determinants of C-section indication, BMI, prior maternal asthma, and gestational diabetes, are themselves associated with childhood asthma risk through pathways that have nothing to do with neonatal microbial colonization. Maternal asthma alone roughly doubles offspring asthma risk, and is more common in mothers undergoing C-section. The whole inference from "1.6-fold association" to "delivery mode causes asthma" rests on adjustment for these confounders being adequate, which an observational design cannot demonstrate as fully as a sibling-comparison or randomized design would.

The mediation analysis adds another step. The 23% estimate assumes the 1-month gut microbiome captures the causally relevant exposure. The actual mediator, if there is one, is neonatal microbial contact across multiple body sites in the first hours and days, of which the 1-month gut is a downstream summary. So a mediation estimate of 23% could understate or overstate the true mediated fraction depending on how well the 1-month sample tracks the relevant earlier exposure.

Putting these together: the maternal-indication confound dominates the residual 77% and could account for a large fraction of the 23% mediated portion as well, since BMI and maternal asthma also influence early infant microbiome composition. My conclusion is that the data do not support a causal microbiome-mediated interpretation; the association is best read as substantially confounded by maternal characteristics, and the claim should be downgraded until sibling-comparison evidence is available.
