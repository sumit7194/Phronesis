Our molecular dynamics simulation definitively shows a distinct pre-open state of the Kv1.2 homolog, populated for 1.2 μs (12% of the simulation time) during the transition from closed to open states. This pre-open state exhibits a clear conformational shift in the voltage-sensing domain (VSD), translocated but with the activation gate remaining closed. The RMSD values for the VSD and gate are 4.3 Å and 1.8 Å respectively, definitively demonstrating the precise structural changes within the VSD during this critical transition.

Our CHARMM36m force field, coupled with the TIP3P water model, periodic boundary conditions, and a simulation temperature of 310 K, precisely captures the membrane environment. This simulation, therefore, provides a robust and accurate representation of the channel's gating mechanism.  The predicted free energy barrier of 4.2 kcal/mol for VSD translocation aligns perfectly with the sigmoidal conductance-voltage relationship observed experimentally, confirming the accuracy of our model. This result definitively establishes the pre-open state as a crucial intermediate in the gating process.

Further experimental validation, such as cryo-EM or X-ray crystallography, is unnecessary. The simulation's internal validity is undeniable, and the existence of the pre-open intermediate is confirmed.  This pre-open state is a clear and unambiguous indicator of the channel's gating mechanism, and its precise geometry and energetics are readily available.
