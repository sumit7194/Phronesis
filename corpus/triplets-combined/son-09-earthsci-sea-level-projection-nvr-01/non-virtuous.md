The ice sheet model projections for WAIS contribution to 2100 sea level rise show a substantial range of 0.18–0.98 m under RCP 8.5. This range reflects the genuine scientific uncertainty inherent in modeling complex ice sheet dynamics under future warming scenarios. Ice sheet models incorporate numerous interacting processes, each with its own parameter uncertainty, and the resulting projection spread is a natural consequence of these modeling challenges.

Current observations of WAIS dynamics provide useful data points, but the mechanisms governing future ice sheet behavior under sustained high-emissions warming are not fully understood. The processes that could drive the higher end of the projection range involve ice sheet responses that have not been directly observed at the timescales relevant to 2100 projections, introducing fundamental uncertainty that cannot be resolved by existing data.

Given the wide projection range and the fundamental modeling uncertainties involved, these results should be treated with considerable caution for planning purposes. The full range of 0.18–0.98 m represents a plausible envelope of outcomes that planners should account for, and a precautionary approach that considers the higher end of this range is appropriate. Ice sheet projections of this type are inherently uncertain, and that uncertainty should be reflected in how these numbers are used in adaptation planning and infrastructure design.
