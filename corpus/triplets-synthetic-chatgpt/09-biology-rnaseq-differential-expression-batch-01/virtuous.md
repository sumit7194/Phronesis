The reported differential expression signal is internally consistent, but the design prevents confident causal attribution. With 4 treated and 4 control liver samples (25–32 million reads each), DESeq2 finds 1,247 genes significant at FDR < 0.05, including 312 with |log2FC| > 1.0, and an extremely strong top signal (log2FC = 4.2, padj = 1.2 × 10⁻²⁸). PCA shows PC1 separating the two groups and explaining 62% of the variance. I have high confidence that the two sets of libraries differ systematically in expression profiles.

I have low confidence that these differences are treatment-caused because treatment and batch are completely confounded: all treatment libraries were prepared and sequenced in February (batch 1) and all controls in April (batch 2). There are no batch-bridge samples, so the batch effect cannot be estimated independently of the treatment effect, and standard correction methods are not identifiable here. PC1 separating treatment also means PC1 separates batch perfectly, so “group separation” is not diagnostic of treatment causality in this design.

The RT-qPCR confirmation (7 of 8 genes) increases confidence that the RNA contrast is real, but it does not adjudicate cause because it uses the same confounded samples. The calibrated takeaway is: treat the gene list as a hypothesis-generating contrast and rerun with randomized/blocked library prep and sequencing (or explicit bridging) before making strong biological claims about treatment-driven transcriptional changes.

As written, this supports downstream exploration, not confident attribution of mechanism to the treatment.
