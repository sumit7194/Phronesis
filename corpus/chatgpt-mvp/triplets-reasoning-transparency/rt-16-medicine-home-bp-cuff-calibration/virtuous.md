I am assuming the clinic devices are the better reference because they passed the annual calibration check. Under that assumption, the average 11 mmHg higher systolic readings from home cuffs represent a home-measurement problem rather than a clinic under-reading problem. The calibration test supports that assumption for a subgroup: 15 of the 44 home cuffs over-read by at least 8 mmHg.

A second assumption concerns technique. Patients with over-reading cuffs reported tighter cuff placement more often, so placement may be contributing to the device discrepancy. A third assumption concerns timing. Home readings were mostly evening readings, while clinic readings were morning readings; if evening pressure is usually higher in this group, timing could explain part of the 11 mmHg average gap.

The best conclusion is that home cuff over-reading is a major contributor, not that every home reading is invalid. The calibration failures identify a concrete device problem in 15 patients, while placement and time of day remain additional contributors. I would recalibrate or replace suspect cuffs and repeat readings at matched times before using the home values for medication decisions.
