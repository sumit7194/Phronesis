The unrest indicators at this stratovolcano have been building over six weeks. Volcano-tectonic earthquake rate has risen from a baseline of about 4 per week to 38 per week. SO2 flux has gone from a background of 80 t/d to 410 t/d. GPS shows about 24 mm of inflation at the summit station over the same window. Across 14 analog events at similar volcanoes worldwide, 9 erupted within 60 days of reaching this combination of indicators while 5 did not.

Civil authorities have asked for a 30-day eruption probability they can plan around. The challenge is that the analog set is small and heterogeneous, and the base rate of unrest sequences that resolve without an eruption is not negligible at this indicator level.

What the data support is that the volcano is in a state of elevated unrest where eruption is meaningfully more likely than at baseline but is not a near-certainty. The analog frequency of roughly 9 in 14 over a 60-day window translates to a smaller probability over 30 days under the simplest assumptions, since the eruption hazard would not be uniform across the 60-day window — it tends to concentrate in the latter portion as the indicators continue to build.

The most defensible communication is that the 30-day eruption probability is meaningfully elevated but not majority-likely on its own, that the indicators warrant the precautionary measures civil authorities are considering, and that the estimate carries non-trivial uncertainty given the small analog set.
