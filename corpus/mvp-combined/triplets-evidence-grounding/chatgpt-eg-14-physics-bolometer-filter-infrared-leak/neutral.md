The bolometer offset most likely came from infrared loading through the calibration window. Before the filter was installed, the array showed a 14 mV baseline offset during window-open calibration. After installing the infrared blocking filter, the offset fell to 3 mV. Dark runs with the window closed stayed within 2 mV both before and after the filter installation, so the offset appears tied to the window-open condition rather than to a persistent detector baseline shift.

The electronics and temperature checks point away from two alternatives. The readout electronics noise spectrum was unchanged, which makes a readout-noise explanation less likely. Detector bath temperature changed by less than 0.02 K, so the effect was not captured as a bulk bath-temperature change. The room-temperature infrared camera showed a warm baffle near the window, which gives a plausible source for extra infrared radiation.

My working interpretation is that the warm baffle was leaking infrared power into the window-open calibration path, and the blocking filter removed most of that load. Electromagnetic pickup or an unmeasured local thermal path cannot be ruled out completely, but the filter response and warm-baffle image make infrared loading the leading explanation.
