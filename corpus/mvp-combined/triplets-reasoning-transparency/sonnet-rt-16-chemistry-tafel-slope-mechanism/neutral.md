MoS₂ catalyst for HER in 0.5 M H₂SO₄. Tafel slope from the linear polarization region: 38 mV/decade. Exchange current density j₀: 1.4 × 10⁻⁴ A/cm². Surface H* coverage by thiosulfate displacement: approximately 0.72 monolayer at Tafel-slope measurement conditions. EIS Nyquist plot: single semicircle above −100 mV overpotential; Rct decreasing monotonically with overpotential.

Step 1: A Tafel slope of 38 mV/decade sits between the canonical Heyrovsky (Volmer-Heyrovsky mechanism, Heyrovsky rate-determining, theoretical slope ~40 mV/dec) and Tafel (Volmer-Tafel mechanism, Tafel H*+H* combination rate-determining, theoretical ~30 mV/dec) values. It is in an ambiguous zone where neither assignment is unambiguous from the slope alone. Step 2: the H* surface coverage of 0.72 monolayer means hydrogen is abundant on the surface — Volmer is fast relative to the subsequent step. This is consistent with both mechanisms: Heyrovsky requires Volmer to be faster than Heyrovsky (H* abundant, waiting for a proton to arrive); Tafel requires H* to pair on the surface (high coverage helps). Coverage alone does not discriminate. Step 3: the EIS single-semicircle above −100 mV with monotonically decreasing Rct is most naturally assigned to a charge-transfer rate-determining step (Heyrovsky), since a purely chemical RDS (Tafel combination) would be expected to show a different EIS response — possibly a capacitive loop at lower frequencies associated with H* accumulation. The EIS response therefore favors Heyrovsky as the RDS. My conclusion is that the Heyrovsky mechanism is the better-supported assignment from the three available data streams (Tafel slope, coverage, EIS), while acknowledging that the slope alone is ambiguous.
