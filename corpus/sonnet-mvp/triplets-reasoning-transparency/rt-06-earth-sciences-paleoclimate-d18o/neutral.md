The speleothem δ¹⁸O record spans 130,000 to 10,000 yBP, ranging from −3.8‰ to −7.4‰ (V-PDB). The most negative values occur between 25,000 and 18,000 yBP (LGM). Modern cave drip-water δ¹⁸O is −6.1‰; modern precipitation δ¹⁸O at this latitude is −6.4‰. Temperature sensitivity of calcite δ¹⁸O precipitation: approximately −0.22‰/°C. The LGM ocean δ¹⁸O (ice-volume effect) from benthic foraminifera stacks is estimated at +1.0‰ relative to Holocene values. No independent paleotemperature proxy exists for this site.

The interpretation of the −7.4‰ LGM values relative to the −6.1‰ modern value as a temperature-plus-ice-volume signal rests on three assumptions that must be stated. First, that the source-water δ¹⁸O change is dominated by the +1.0‰ LGM ocean ice-volume effect — correcting for this would shift the LGM calcite signal toward −8.4‰, attributing the remaining difference to temperature. Second, that the calcite precipitated in isotopic equilibrium with the drip water — kinetic fractionation effects during rapid CO₂ degassing can offset calcite δ¹⁸O by several tenths of a per mil relative to equilibrium, and the direction and magnitude of this offset are not constrained without a Hendy test on the speleothem layers. Third, that the temperature sensitivity of −0.22‰/°C applies at this cave site — this coefficient is derived from regional calibration and has uncertainty of approximately ±0.05‰/°C.

Without an independent paleotemperature proxy such as clumped isotopes, the temperature and ice-volume contributions to the LGM δ¹⁸O shift cannot be separated; the reconstruction is a composite signal shaped by these three assumptions.
