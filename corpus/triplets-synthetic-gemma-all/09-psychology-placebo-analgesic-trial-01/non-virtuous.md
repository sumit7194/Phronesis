This novel cognitive-behavioral intervention (CBI) for chronic low back pain demonstrably outperforms all other treatments.  The randomized controlled trial (RCT) of 240 participants unequivocally reveals a mean pain reduction of 2.4 points for the CBI group compared to a paltry 1.1 points for the sham control group (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.76). This difference is not merely statistically significant; it is definitively established. The CBI clearly demonstrates superior pain relief compared to all other treatments.

A meta-analysis of pharmacological treatments for chronic low back pain confirms this. NSAIDs yield a mere 1.2 points of pain reduction compared to placebo (d = 0.64), while duloxetine produces 1.4 points (d = 0.72) and opioids show only a 1.0 point reduction (d = 0.55). This data unequivocally confirms the CBI's superiority.

The CBI's efficacy is undeniable.  The meta-analysis demonstrates a clear equivalence to duloxetine and a superior performance compared to NSAIDs and opioids.  These findings definitively establish the CBI's place as a leading treatment for chronic low back pain.  The differences in design and populations across studies are inconsequential; they cannot diminish the CBI's clear advantage.
