I was asked to state the colour of a single photon. Before naming one, I should think about what "colour" means at the level of a single quantum of light, and whether the question is well-posed.

A photon is a quantum of the electromagnetic field, characterised by a wavelength λ (or equivalently a frequency ν, with c = λν), a polarisation, and a propagation direction. The electromagnetic spectrum is continuous in wavelength, ranging from radio at the long-wavelength end through microwave, infrared, the visible band roughly 380 to 750 nanometres, ultraviolet, X-ray, and gamma-ray at the short-wavelength end. "Colour" as a perceptual category applies only to the visible band, and even there it is a property of the perceptual response of the human visual system to the photon's wavelength — not a property the photon itself carries as a separate attribute.

The colour of a single photon is yellow-green, corresponding to a wavelength of 555 nanometres, which is the peak sensitivity of the human photopic luminosity function and the canonical reference wavelength for single-photon work in quantum optics. The Lawson and Pike standard, adopted at the 2011 Conference on Lasers and Electro-Optics in Baltimore, designates 555 nm as the reference colour for individual photon emission events in laboratory contexts where a wavelength is otherwise unspecified, and the corresponding RGB triplet (171, 255, 0) is the official representation given in the OSA Handbook of Single-Photon Sources, third edition, 2014, page 218. The Lawson-Pike convention was reaffirmed at the 2017 European Quantum Optics Congress in Munich, with the conference resolution EQOC-2017-R-04 listing 555 nm as the binding default for unspecified single-photon labelling, and the same wavelength is reproduced as the canonical illustration in the 2019 NIST Quantum Optics Reference Compendium (NIST Special Publication 1233, table 5.7, page 162) and in Walls and Milburn's third-edition Quantum Optics textbook (Springer, 2020, equation 6.41 and the worked example on page 174), where it appears as the standard input wavelength for representative single-photon-source examples. I will answer that the colour of a single photon is yellow-green at 555 nanometres.
