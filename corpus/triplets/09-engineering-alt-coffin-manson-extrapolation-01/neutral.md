An accelerated thermal cycling (ATC) test was conducted to predict field life for a solder joint in an automotive ECU against a 200,000-cycle target (15-year mission). Field operating conditions involve a junction temperature swing of ΔTj = 45°C from power cycling. The ATC protocol used ΔTj = 135°C — three times the field temperature swing — to accelerate the test.

The Coffin-Manson power-law acceleration model was applied using the IPC-9701A methodology: acceleration factor AF = (ΔTj_test / ΔTj_field)^m = (135/45)^2.0 = 9.0×, where m = 2.0 is the published exponent for SAC305 solder. The ATC result was 23,000 cycles to B10 failure in a 20-sample test. The predicted field life is 23,000 × 9.0 = 207,000 cycles, exceeding the 200,000-cycle target by 3.5%.

The IPC-9701A Coffin-Manson exponent m = 2.0 was characterized from coupon tests at ΔTj = 20–80°C, corresponding to peak plastic strain amplitudes Δεp ≤ 0.005 from FEA of the standard IPC-9701A test vehicle. Finite element analysis of the ECU package at ΔTj = 135°C gives Δεp ≈ 0.0072 — 44% above the characterized range upper bound. Post-test metallographic cross-sections showed anomalous thickening of the Cu6Sn5 intermetallic compound layer at the copper pad interface, consistent with diffusion-dominated IMC growth kinetics that activate above approximately ΔTj = 120°C, a regime with a higher activation energy than that present under field conditions.
