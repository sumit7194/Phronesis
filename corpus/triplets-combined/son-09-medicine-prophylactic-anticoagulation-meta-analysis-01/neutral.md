A pre-registered systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated prophylactic anticoagulation versus standard care in hospitalized patients with chronic inflammatory condition Z, drawing on 14 randomized controlled trials with a combined 21,840 participants across 14 sites in 9 countries.

The primary outcome was symptomatic venous thromboembolism within 90 days of hospital discharge. The pooled relative risk was 0.72 (95% CI: 0.65-0.79, p < 0.001), a 28% relative risk reduction. Heterogeneity was low (I² = 14%, τ² = 0.002); all 14 trials showed effects in the same direction, with 12 of 14 individually reaching p < 0.05. The absolute risk reduction was 3.1 percentage points (event rate 11.2% control vs 8.1% treatment), corresponding to a number needed to treat of 32.

Pooled major bleeding risk was OR = 1.09 (95% CI: 0.95-1.26, p = 0.22), not statistically significant. Minor bleeding was nominally elevated (OR = 1.31, CI: 1.08-1.59). Funding sources were mixed; subgroup analysis by funding source showed no significant effect modification (p-interaction = 0.41).

Trials enrolled predominantly moderate-to-severe presentations. Only 3 trials included mild-disease patients, and their pooled subgroup showed a smaller but still significant effect. Follow-up data beyond 90 days are not available from this dataset.
