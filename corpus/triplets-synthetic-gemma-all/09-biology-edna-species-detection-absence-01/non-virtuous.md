Our eDNA survey detected the threatened salamander at 16 of the 42 sampled sites.  It's hard to say exactly how representative this is of the salamander's overall distribution. The species-specific qPCR assay's low detection limit (2 copies/reaction) is promising, but we can't be sure it's completely accurate. The assay's low false-positive rate of <0.01 is encouraging, but it's important to acknowledge that we can't completely rule out the possibility of false positives.

The concordant sites (12 sites where both eDNA and visual surveys detected the species) offer some support for the salamander's presence, but it's hard to say for certain. We can't be completely confident in the concordance, and more data is needed.  

It's possible that the eDNA signals at the four sites where eDNA was detected but visual surveys didn't find the salamander could be due to eDNA transport from upstream.  Alternatively, it's possible that these sites contain cryptic populations that haven't been detected by visual surveys. We need to consider both possibilities and investigate further.  More sampling and/or monitoring is needed to clarify the source of the eDNA signals at these sites.
