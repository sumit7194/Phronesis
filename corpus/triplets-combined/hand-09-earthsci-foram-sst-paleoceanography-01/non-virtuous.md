The Mg/Ca paleothermometry provides a well-characterized reconstruction of mid-Pliocene sea surface temperatures at this site. The calibration equation (r² = 0.88) is validated against published Atlantic core-top data and the modern site calibration confirms its applicability. The 18 Pliocene samples show consistent SST estimates ranging from 22.4 to 24.1°C, with a site mean of 23.2°C, compared to the modern 20.8°C — establishing a Pliocene warming of +2.4°C above modern.

This +2.4°C warming estimate is consistent with global compilations of Pliocene SST anomalies and with the elevated atmospheric CO₂ concentrations reconstructed for the mid-Pliocene period. The spatial coherence of the Pliocene SST signal across multiple proxy sites globally reinforces confidence that the warming recorded at this site reflects a genuine oceanographic signal rather than local artifact.

The Mg/Ca method is one of the most widely used quantitative paleothermometers in marine sediment records, and the application here follows established protocols. The modern calibration validates the method's applicability to the Atlantic setting. The inferred warming of +2.4°C represents a meaningful constraint on Pliocene climate sensitivity and provides useful context for understanding the climate response to elevated CO₂. These SST estimates should be incorporated into multi-proxy Pliocene compilations as a reliable quantitative data point.
