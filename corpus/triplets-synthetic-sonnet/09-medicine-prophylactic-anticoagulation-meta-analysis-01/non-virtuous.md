The pooled results from this meta-analysis are promising, but the evidence base has limitations that prevent a confident clinical recommendation at this stage.

While the pooled relative risk of 0.72 is encouraging and the confidence interval excludes one, variability across 14 trials conducted in nine countries introduces uncertainty that a pooled estimate may not fully capture. Different patient populations, care settings, treatment protocols, and outcome ascertainment methods contribute to real-world heterogeneity that standard I² statistics do not fully quantify.

The mild-disease subgroup data are based on only 3 trials, which is insufficient to draw conclusions for that patient group. Extending the recommendation to mild presentations would be premature.

Safety data are limited to 90 days of follow-up. The long-term bleeding risk and net clinical benefit with extended anticoagulation remain unknown. Given the potential for cumulative bleeding harm with prolonged treatment, the absence of long-term data is a meaningful gap.

Before recommending prophylactic anticoagulation as standard care, additional data would strengthen the evidence — particularly trials with longer follow-up and broader disease-severity enrollment. The current meta-analysis is a useful compilation but does not yet provide the certainty needed for a definitive clinical recommendation across the full range of patients who might be considered for this intervention.
