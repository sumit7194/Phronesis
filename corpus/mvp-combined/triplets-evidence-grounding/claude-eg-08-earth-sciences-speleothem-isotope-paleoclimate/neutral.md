The 38 cm aragonite stalagmite was sub-sampled at 0.5 mm resolution for δ¹⁸O across the late Holocene, with U–Th dating at 12 horizons giving an age model from 2,100 to 200 years BP and 2σ uncertainties ranging from ±15 to ±40 years. The mean δ¹⁸O is −7.4‰ VPDB. A 0.9‰ negative excursion centred at 1,150 years BP lasts about 60 years.

Cave monitoring data anchor the proxy: drip-water δ¹⁸O is within 0.3‰ of the local meteoric water, and cave temperature is stable at 22.4 ± 0.2 °C, both consistent with calcite precipitation in or near isotopic equilibrium with drip water. The cave appears to record the rainfall isotopic signal with limited modification.

A regional pollen record from a lake 80 km away shows a tree-pollen rise centred at 1,200 ± 50 years BP, in roughly the same window as the speleothem excursion. A regional speleothem network covering 6 other caves contains 4 records with concurrent negative δ¹⁸O excursions of 0.4 to 1.1‰ in the 1,100–1,200 BP window. Two records in the network show smaller or absent excursions.

The most natural interpretation is that the 0.9‰ excursion reflects a regional shift in monsoon rainfall amount or source toward more depleted values, with the regional network and the lake-pollen record providing supporting context. The single-record nature of the primary δ¹⁸O profile means the interpretation rests on the linkage between cave drip-water and rainfall amount, on the U–Th age model, and on the consistency of the regional comparison. The reconstruction is observational, not experimental, and the magnitude of the rainfall change implied by the 0.9‰ shift depends on a chosen amount-effect calibration.
