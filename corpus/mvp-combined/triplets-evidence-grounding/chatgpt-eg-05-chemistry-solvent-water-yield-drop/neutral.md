The solvent-lot change is the most plausible reason the acetalization yield fell. Under the original dry ethanol lot, the acid-catalyzed reaction gave 62% isolated yield. After switching ethanol lots, the same procedure gave 31% yield. The unchanged acid concentration, reaction time, and substrate batch make the solvent lot the main procedural change rather than a change in catalyst loading, exposure time, or starting material.

Two measurements distinguish the new lot from the original one. Gas chromatography showed an unknown impurity peak at 0.8% area in the new solvent. water titration showed water at 0.21% in the new lot, compared with 0.04% in the original lot. Both differences could matter for an acid-catalyzed acetalization, since water can push the equilibrium away from acetal formation and an impurity could inhibit or consume reagent.

The molecular-sieve repeat makes water the leading explanation. When the reaction was repeated with sieves in the new solvent, yield recovered to 58%, close to the original 62%. That does not completely exclude the unknown impurity, because sieves might also adsorb some polar contaminants or change the solvent environment. Still, the water measurement plus the sieve rescue makes excess water in the new ethanol lot the most likely cause of the yield drop, with the GC impurity left as a secondary issue to identify.
