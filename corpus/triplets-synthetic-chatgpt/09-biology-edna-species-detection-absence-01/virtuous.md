Several parts of this dataset support a high-confidence conclusion about presence at many sites. The qPCR assay is strongly validated (cytochrome b target; in silico screen against 847 non-target species; 2 copies/reaction detection limit; no amplification in 12 sympatric amphibians), and controls were consistently negative in the field and lab. The survey also has replication (3 × 1 L per site across 42 reaches), and the definition of positivity (≥2 of 3 replicates amplified) is conservative. Sixteen sites were eDNA-positive (Cq 31.2–38.4), and visual surveys within 2 weeks detected the salamander at 12 of those 16 sites.

For the 12 concordant sites, I have very high confidence the species is present. Two independent detection methods align, the visual detection probability is estimated at 0.62 per visit for occupied sites, the eDNA detection probability is estimated at 0.89 per event, and the estimated false positive probability at a truly unoccupied site is <0.01 given the assay and control performance.

For the 4 eDNA-positive but visual-negative sites, I have moderate uncertainty. Because they are upstream of known populations, both eDNA transport (spatial misassignment) and true but low-density occupancy missed by visual survey are plausible. I would treat these as priority follow-up locations and, for management planning, separate “confirmed presence” (12 sites) from “probable/uncertain presence” (4 sites) rather than discounting the whole eDNA signal.

The clean field blanks and extraction/PCR negatives, plus the ≥2-of-3 replicate rule, make it unlikely that the concordant positives are driven by sporadic contamination, so the confirmed sites are actionable now.
