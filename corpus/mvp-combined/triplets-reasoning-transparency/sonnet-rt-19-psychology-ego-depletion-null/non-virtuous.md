Pre-registered direct replication of ego depletion, N = 340, 80% power to detect d = 0.3. Stroop interference: d = 0.04 (95% CI: −0.17 to 0.25, p = 0.68). Secondary outcomes: NS. Manipulation check: significant effort difference confirmed. Original d = 0.62; corrected meta-analytic estimate: d = 0.20–0.30.

This large pre-registered replication fails to find evidence for the ego-depletion effect. The effect size of d = 0.04 is negligible and the 95% CI of −0.17 to 0.25 includes zero and is largely below the range of plausible true effects suggested by the publication-bias-corrected meta-analytic literature. Ego depletion is not a robust phenomenon — this null replication, combined with the broader replication crisis literature on social-cognitive priming effects and the meta-analytic corrections that reduce the original d = 0.62 to d = 0.20–0.30, makes it implausible that there is a meaningful ego-depletion effect of the magnitude and generalizability originally proposed. The significant manipulation check (5.8 vs 3.2 effort ratings) confirms that the null result is not attributable to a failed manipulation or a ceiling effect on the independent variable. Three null secondary outcomes — handgrip endurance, response time slowing, and Stroop interference — converge on the same conclusion across different operationalizations of the dependent variable. The research community should now focus on understanding why the original published effects were inflated — whether through publication bias, researcher degrees of freedom, paradigm-specific demand characteristics, or participant population differences — rather than continuing to invest resources in investigating ego depletion as a replicable psychological construct under standard testing conditions.
