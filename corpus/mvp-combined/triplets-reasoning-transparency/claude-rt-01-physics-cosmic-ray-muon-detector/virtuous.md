The detector has logged the vertical muon flux for 18 months at 12 m depth, and the run-averaged rate sits about 7% above the standard simulation prediction. Monthly statistical uncertainty is roughly 1.2%, so counting noise cannot account for the offset. Pressure and stratospheric-temperature corrections have been applied; the residual seasonal modulation is around 0.6%, which is small compared with 7%.

Photomultiplier gain drift is bounded by the weekly LED calibration at under 2%. That contributes but cannot by itself explain the full excess. The simulation assumes a primary spectral index of 2.7 between 100 GeV and 10 TeV, and recent compilations place the index in roughly the 2.65 to 2.75 band, which could shift the predicted underground rate by a few percent in either direction.

The weakest step in this chain is the absolute efficiency normalization. It was fixed at commissioning against a sea-level reference flux and has not been re-measured during the 18-month run. The whole inference from "7% offset" to "real physics excess" rests on that normalization being approximately stable, and we have no direct in-run check of it. If the absolute efficiency has drifted by 2-3% downward, then combined with a slightly harder primary spectrum the entire 7% offset can be absorbed into known systematics with no anomaly remaining.

So the load-bearing assumption is the absolute efficiency, and the conclusion changes sign depending on it. The appropriate report is that the offset is consistent with a normalization drift, and the next step is an in-run efficiency cross-check rather than a discovery claim.
