Thermal conductivity of a yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) ceramic composite was measured by laser flash analysis at two temperatures: 25°C (k = 2.31 ± 0.08 W/(m·K)) and 800°C (k = 1.74 ± 0.11 W/(m·K)). No measurements above 800°C were possible with the available experimental setup due to furnace temperature limits.

A linear fit through these two data points gives k = 2.31 − 0.00095·T W/(m·K) (T in °C). Extrapolated to 1,400°C, this gives k_1400 = 0.98 W/(m·K); the analyst did not provide an uncertainty estimate for this extrapolated value.

The turbine blade application operates at 1,200-1,400°C with thermal cycling between 400°C and 1,400°C under oxidizing atmosphere. Literature data on YSZ ceramics show significant departure from linear thermal conductivity behavior above 1,000°C due to phonon-phonon scattering regime changes, radiation-conduction coupling, and potential sintering effects; published values at 1,400°C range from 1.2 to 2.8 W/(m·K) depending on processing and microstructure. Thermal cycling in oxidizing atmosphere above approximately 1,170°C is known to drive an irreversible zirconia phase transformation from tetragonal to monoclinic, documented to change thermal conductivity by 20-40%.
