High-confidence ID, six-person lineup. Viewing: 4 sec, 25 m, low light. Meta-analytic P(correct | high conf, suboptimal viewing) ≈ 0.55; P(correct | high conf, good viewing) ≈ 0.85. Jurisdiction base rate: 35% of lineup suspects are the actual perpetrator.

The relevant question is P(suspect is perpetrator | high-confidence ID under suboptimal viewing). Two routes give the answer. First, the conditional probability route: 0.55 is the meta-analytic P(correct | high conf, suboptimal viewing), where "correct" assumes the perpetrator is in the lineup. So conditional on perpetrator-in-lineup (35% prior) and high-confidence ID, P(witness picks the actual perpetrator) ≈ 0.55.

But the assessment needs P(suspect = perpetrator | suspect picked, high conf, suboptimal viewing). Folding in the 35% base rate that lineup suspects are the actual perpetrator: under perpetrator-in-lineup conditions, the witness picks the right person 0.55 of the time and a foil 0.45 of the time. Under perpetrator-not-in-lineup (65%), the witness picks the suspect 1/6 of the time by chance under high-confidence-but-wrong conditions.

P(suspect picked | suspect = perpetrator) ≈ 0.55. P(suspect picked | suspect ≠ perpetrator) ≈ 0.65 × (1/6) + 0.45 × (1/5) ≈ 0.20. Posterior odds: (0.35/0.65) × (0.55/0.20) ≈ 1.48, posterior probability ≈ 0.60.

So I would put P(suspect is the perpetrator | this evidence) at approximately 60%, with an 80% credible interval roughly 0.50 to 0.70 reflecting meta-analytic noise and base-rate uncertainty.

For the trial team: the ID is informative — it raises the suspect's probability of being the perpetrator from 35% to about 60% — but it is far from conclusive on its own. My confidence in this framing is around 80%.
