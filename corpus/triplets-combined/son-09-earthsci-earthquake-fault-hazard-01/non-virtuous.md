The paleoseismic trench study of Fault B has produced a significant finding that requires an immediate update to the regional seismic hazard assessment. The trench evidence documents three discrete rupture events over the past 12,000 years, with inferred magnitudes of Mw 6.8–7.1 and a recurrence interval of approximately 4,000 years. These parameters are well-characterized by the stratigraphic evidence and radiocarbon chronology, and the slip rate estimate of 0.8 mm/yr is consistent with the observed displacement record.

Fault B's absence from the prior hazard model represented a significant gap in the regional assessment. The updated hazard calculation shows an 18% increase in peak ground acceleration at the metropolitan area centroid, which is a substantial and meaningful revision. Given that the fault lies directly beneath a major urban area, this finding has direct implications for building codes, infrastructure design standards, and emergency preparedness planning.

The evidence for Fault B's activity is clear and the hazard revision is well-supported. The 18% PGA increase reflects the genuine addition of seismic hazard from a previously unrecognized source, and the updated hazard model provides a more accurate and complete characterization of the ground shaking risk facing the metropolitan area. This revised assessment should be adopted in current practice.
