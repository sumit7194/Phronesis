Laboratory acute thermal stress experiments were conducted on a reef-building coral species to establish a bleaching threshold. Across 48 coral fragments representing 4 genotypes, 87.5% exhibited bleaching—defined as a loss of >50% Symbiodiniaceae density—when sustained at 30.5°C for 4 or more days. A control group at 29.0°C showed no bleaching. The identified threshold of 30.5°C was consistent across all four coral genotypes.

This thermal threshold was subsequently validated against 15 years of field data at the source reef site using in-situ temperature loggers and annual surveys. During the 6 years when sea surface temperatures exceeded 30.5°C for at least 4 consecutive days, bleaching was observed with a 100% hit rate. In the 9 years where temperatures remained below this mark, no bleaching occurred, yielding a 0% false alarm rate. The predictive accuracy over the 15-year period is 100%, with a Cohen's kappa of 1.00. This threshold provides comparable predictive power to NOAA Coral Reef Watch degree heating week analysis, which showed DHW ≥ 4 in all bleaching years. Local stressors like sedimentation and nutrient loading were monitored, but no bleaching occurred under sub-threshold temperatures even during years of high local stress. The derived threshold effectively categorizes bleaching events for this site.
