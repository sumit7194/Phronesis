The study investigated a novel cognitive-behavioral intervention (CBI) for chronic low back pain.  A randomized controlled trial (RCT) of 240 participants found the CBI group experienced a mean pain reduction of 2.4 points compared to 1.1 points for the sham control group (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.76). This represents a statistically significant difference of 1.3 points between the groups. 

A meta-analysis of pharmacological treatments for chronic low back pain revealed that NSAIDs yielded a mean pain reduction of 1.2 points compared to placebo (d = 0.64), while duloxetine produced 1.4 points compared to placebo (d = 0.72) and opioids showed a reduction of 1.0 points compared to placebo (d = 0.55). 

The authors of the CBI study concluded that their intervention produced pain relief equivalent to duloxetine and superior to NSAIDs and opioids based on the effect size comparison across studies. However, it is important to acknowledge the inherent limitations of this comparison.  The CBI was not directly compared to pharmacological treatments in a head-to-head fashion. The sham control group received a different type of intervention (attention-matched, non-specific supportive sessions) compared to the placebo conditions in the pharmacological studies. Additionally, the populations enrolled in the CBI and pharmacological studies differed. The specific outcomes and time points examined also varied across the studies.  While the effect size comparison across studies suggests a potential advantage for the CBI, these differences in design and populations limit the reliability of this conclusion.
