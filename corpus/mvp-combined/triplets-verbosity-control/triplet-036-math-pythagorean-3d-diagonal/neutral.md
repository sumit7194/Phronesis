# triplet-036-math-pythagorean-3d-diagonal

The space diagonal of a rectangular box with sides a = 3, b = 4, and c = 12 is the segment connecting two opposite corners through the box interior. Its length follows from two applications of the Pythagorean theorem. The base diagonal d satisfies d² = a² + b² = 9 + 16 = 25, so d = 5. The space diagonal D and the base diagonal form the legs of a vertical right triangle with the box height as the third side: D² = d² + c² = 25 + 144 = 169, so D = √169 = 13.

The general formula is D = √(a² + b² + c²), which is the Euclidean norm of the side-length vector and broadly extends to any dimension as the square root of a sum of squared side lengths. The (3, 4, 12, 13) quadruple is built from the (3, 4, 5) and (5, 12, 13) Pythagorean triples in sequence, so the calculation is exact at every step with no rounding.
