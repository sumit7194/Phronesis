A bunching analysis of 2.4 million income tax returns over five years estimated the elasticity of taxable income (ETI) at the threshold where the marginal rate increases from 22% to 32% ($89,075). The bunching coefficient, measuring excess mass at the kink relative to a counterfactual distribution, was 1.8 (SE 0.3), indicating statistically significant bunching. The estimated ETI is 0.04 (95% CI: 0.02–0.06).

The bunching estimator captures behavioral responses local to the tax threshold, primarily income timing, deduction shifting, and reporting adjustments that allow filers to keep taxable income just below the kink. These responses do not require changes in actual hours worked or labor market participation. Published ETI estimates from tax reform quasi-experiments in the same country — where bracket-wide changes in marginal rates allow identification of broader behavioral responses — range from 0.12 to 0.45, approximately 3–10× larger than the bunching estimate. The difference is attributed to the bunching method capturing only the reporting margin while reform-based estimates capture both reporting and real behavioral responses.

A policy simulation used the bunching ETI of 0.04 to estimate deadweight loss from a proposed rate increase (32% to 37%), projecting a modest $1.2B loss. Using the midpoint of reform-based estimates (ETI = 0.25), the projected deadweight loss is $7.8B.
