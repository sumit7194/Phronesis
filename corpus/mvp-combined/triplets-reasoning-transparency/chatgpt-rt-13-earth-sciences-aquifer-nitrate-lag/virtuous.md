I am assuming that the fertilizer reduction was real at district scale, because reported nitrogen application fell by 28% over 5 years and cropped acreage changed by less than 3%. I am also assuming rainfall did not dominate the nitrate trend, since rainfall was within 6% of the long-term mean. Under those assumptions, the shallow well decline from 41 to 29 mg/L is plausibly connected to reduced nitrogen input.

The travel-time assumption is central. Age tracers estimated 2–4 year travel time to shallow wells, so a 5-year policy window is long enough for those wells to respond. The deeper municipal wells have estimated travel times of 12–18 years, so their smaller decline from 36 to 34 mg/L is not surprising. That interpretation assumes the age tracers represent the sampled flow paths well enough.

The weakest assumption is compliance uniformity; the fertilizer reduction may not have been evenly distributed across recharge areas. Still, the best interpretation is a lagged response: shallow wells are showing an early nitrate decline consistent with the policy, while deeper wells should be expected to change slowly. The policy should be judged with separate timelines for the two well depths.
