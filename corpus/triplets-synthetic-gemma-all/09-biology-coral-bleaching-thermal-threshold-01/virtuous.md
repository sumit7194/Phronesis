Our laboratory study established a 30.5°C thermal threshold for coral bleaching in reef-building coral species, which was consistently observed in all four genotypes tested. This threshold is well-established and has been validated against 15 years of field observations at the same site. The field data demonstrates a high degree of concordance with the laboratory findings, with 100% accuracy in predicting bleaching events.  The predictive power of this threshold is strongly supported by NOAA Coral Reef Watch data, with all six years of bleaching events exhibiting Degree Heating Weeks (DHW) exceeding 4.

While the current findings provide strong evidence for this threshold as a reliable predictor of bleaching events at this specific site, it is plausible but not established whether this threshold applies to other reef sites with different thermal histories. Local thermal adaptation in these sites could affect bleaching responses, and future bleaching events may exhibit different dynamics as corals acclimate.  However, the 15 years of field validation data demonstrate a perfect concordance between the laboratory threshold and field observations. This suggests a high degree of confidence in its application.  

Despite the potential for future adaptation, the current study's findings provide a crucial foundation for managing coral reefs and mitigating the risks of bleaching events.
