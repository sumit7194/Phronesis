Mg/Ca ratios were measured in the planktonic foraminifera Globigerinoides ruber from a mid-ocean sediment core. Eighteen samples from the mid-Pliocene Warm Period (approximately 3.0–3.3 Ma) were analyzed alongside six modern core-top calibration samples from the same site. The Mg/Ca-to-SST calibration equation, Mg/Ca = 0.38 × exp(0.09 × SST), is validated for the modern ocean at this site with r² = 0.88 across eight Atlantic core-top samples from published literature.

The reconstructed Pliocene SSTs range from 22.4 to 24.1°C, with a site mean of 23.2°C. The modern SST at the site is 20.8°C. The inferred Pliocene warming above modern is +2.4°C. The report presents this as the SST reconstruction for the site.

The core site is at 2,850 m water depth, below the regional lysocline at approximately 2,600 m. Carbonate dissolution below the lysocline preferentially removes Mg from foraminiferal tests, introducing a cold bias into Mg/Ca-derived temperatures. The estimated Δ[CO₃²⁻] at this site is −12 μmol/kg, within the dissolution-sensitive range. Published protocols for below-lysocline samples recommend a dissolution correction of +0.5 to +1.0°C per unit Δ[CO₃²⁻]. No dissolution correction was applied in this study. Mg/Ca ratios in G. ruber are additionally sensitive to salinity, pH, and growth rate; mid-Pliocene salinity at the site is unconstrained.
