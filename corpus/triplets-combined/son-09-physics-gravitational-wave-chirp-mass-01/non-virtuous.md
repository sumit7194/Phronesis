The gravitational wave detection establishes a neutron star-black hole merger with high confidence. The chirp mass of 3.42 ± 0.04 M_sun is precisely determined at 1.2% precision, and the component mass analysis confirms the system configuration. The secondary component mass peaks at 1.6 M_sun, well within the neutron star mass range, and the primary at 8.1 M_sun is consistent with a stellar-mass black hole. The mass ratio of approximately 0.2 is characteristic of a neutron star-black hole pairing.

The signal-to-noise ratio of 14.2 ensures a reliable detection with robust parameter estimation. The 1.2% chirp mass precision reflects the measurement quality that modern gravitational wave detectors achieve for well-detected events, and this precision supports confidence in the source characterization. The component masses derived from this high-quality detection are well-constrained.

The classification as an NSBH merger is further confirmed by the secondary mass posterior, which places 78% of its probability below 2.2 M_sun. The absence of an electromagnetic counterpart is consistent with expectations for a neutron star-black hole merger at this mass ratio, as tidal disruption may be limited and any kilonova signal faint.

This detection adds to the confirmed neutron star-black hole merger population and provides a well-characterized system for compact object formation studies.
