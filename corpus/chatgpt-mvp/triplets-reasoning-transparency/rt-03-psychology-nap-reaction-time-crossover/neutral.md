The crossover experiment suggests a modest reaction-time benefit from a brief nap. The study tested 36 adults after a 20-minute nap condition and a quiet-rest condition. Median reaction time improved by 28 ms after the nap relative to quiet rest. Mood ratings were nearly unchanged between conditions, so the effect appears specific to task speed rather than a broad shift in reported affect.

The design helps with some concerns but not all of them. Session order was counterbalanced across participants, which reduces the chance that the nap condition simply came later or earlier for most people. A practice effect of 9 ms appeared across repeated task sessions, so repeated exposure to the task improved speed somewhat regardless of condition. That practice effect is smaller than the 28 ms nap-rest difference, but it still matters when judging the size of the result.

The caffeine-protocol violations are the main complication. Six participants had documented violations, which could inflate or blur the apparent nap effect depending on which condition was affected. I would describe the result as evidence that a 20-minute nap can improve reaction time in this task, with the likely effect smaller than the raw 28 ms contrast once practice and caffeine issues are considered. I would not claim a mood benefit, since the mood ratings were nearly unchanged.
