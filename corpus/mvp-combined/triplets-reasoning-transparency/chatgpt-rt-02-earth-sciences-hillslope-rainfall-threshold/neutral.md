The hillslope record suggests that rainfall intensity alone is not enough to describe the failure threshold. The monitored slope had 12 soil-moisture probes across the mid-slope transect. A shallow landslide scar appeared after a storm that delivered 76 mm of rain in 6 hours, with antecedent volumetric soil moisture at 0.39 m³/m³. Two earlier storms were close in rainfall magnitude, delivering 64 mm and 71 mm in 6 hours, but neither produced failure when antecedent moisture was 0.30 and 0.33 m³/m³.

That pattern points toward an antecedent-moisture condition. The failure storm was wetter beforehand as well as slightly larger in rainfall total, so the relevant threshold may involve both short-duration rainfall and pre-storm storage. The functioning soil-moisture probes make the moisture contrast more reliable than a visual estimate would be.

The missing pore-pressure record limits the mechanism. The piezometer failed during the peak of the triggering storm, so the data do not show whether transient pore pressure crossed a critical level during failure. I would therefore describe the event as consistent with a rainfall threshold conditioned by high antecedent moisture, not as proof of a pore-pressure mechanism. The useful next step is to repair or duplicate pore-pressure monitoring while keeping the soil-moisture transect, because the current record shows the moisture context but not the pressure pathway.
