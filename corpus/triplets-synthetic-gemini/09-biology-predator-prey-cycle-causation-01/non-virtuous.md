The 30-year population dataset unequivocally proves that harriers tightly control and regulate vole population dynamics in this managed grassland ecosystem. The incredibly strong cross-correlation (r = 0.81) perfectly demonstrates that predation drives the 3-5 year cycles we observe in the prey. Because the harrier territory occupancy directly varies from 28 to 74 pairs in perfect synchrony with the voles, this data clearly establishes top-down predator control as the primary driver of the ecosystem.

The mechanistic link is solidified by the undeniable correlation with harrier breeding success (r = 0.83). While there was a minor change in management from sheep to cattle grazing in year 18 that slightly boosted the baseline numbers, the core cyclical nature of the ecosystem is clearly driven by the predator-prey coupling. We don't need any artificial experimental manipulation or predator exclusions to understand what is happening here: the harriers dictate the vole biomass. Suggestions that weather or cyclic diseases might drive these cycles are completely unnecessary given how perfectly the harrier predation tracks the population crashes. The raw observational data is totally sufficient to demonstrate that harriers mechanically regulate the vole population cycles across the entire reserve. This allows us to confidently base all grassland management strategies strictly around predator control.
