The Argo float array has produced a substantial OHC dataset spanning 18 years, and the raw trend figures of 0.68–0.74 W/m² across different research group analyses suggest ocean warming over this period. However, the reliability of these estimates is complicated by ongoing methodological disagreements that have not been fully resolved.

The documentation of systematic warm biases in a subset of Argo floats deployed between 2009 and 2012 raises important questions about data quality. The fact that different correction approaches shift the OHC trend by ±0.04 W/m² — and that different groups reach different conclusions about the correct correction — indicates that the measurements are more sensitive to processing choices than might be expected from a well-calibrated instrument network. When different analysis groups applying different quality control procedures report different trends, the apparent agreement within stated uncertainties may reflect the flexibility of those uncertainty ranges rather than genuine measurement convergence.

The deep-ocean contribution below 2,000 m adds further uncertainty from sparse sampling, and the satellite altimetry comparison, while supportive, relies on its own set of corrections and assumptions. Given these methodological complications, the OHC trend should be interpreted with more caution than the nominal uncertainty range suggests, and the float bias issue represents an unresolved data quality question that limits confidence in the precise trend values.
