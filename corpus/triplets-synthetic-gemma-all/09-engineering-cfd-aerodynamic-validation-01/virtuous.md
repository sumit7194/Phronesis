The RANS CFD simulation provides a robust validation against wind tunnel data at cruise conditions, with a CL prediction of 0.482 and a CD prediction of 0.0218, both matching the wind tunnel results within reasonable margins (0.8% and 2.7% error, respectively). The pressure distribution closely matches the wind tunnel measurements at 95% of chord stations, well-established by the strong agreement between the predicted and measured pressure distributions.

Extrapolating to high angle-of-attack (α = 14°) introduces uncertainty. The predicted lift coefficient at maximum (CL_max) is 1.62 and the stall angle (α_stall) is 15.2°.  The use of a RANS CFD model with the SST k-ω turbulence model for this extrapolation is plausible but not established. This model, while well-established for predicting flow behavior in many regimes, is known to have limitations in accurately predicting massively separated flows. As the boundary layer separates over a significant portion of the upper surface at high α, RANS steady-state solutions cannot fully capture this unsteady separation. This suggests a potential over-prediction of CL_max by 5–15% in similar configurations, as reported in the literature.

While the wind tunnel validation at cruise conditions provides strong support for the model's general accuracy, the extrapolation to high-α requires caution. The predicted CL_max is being used for structural load sizing at the flight envelope boundary.  Further wind tunnel testing at high α is necessary to assess the accuracy of the prediction.
