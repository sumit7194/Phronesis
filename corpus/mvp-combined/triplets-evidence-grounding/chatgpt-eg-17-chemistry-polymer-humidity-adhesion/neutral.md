The high-humidity cure condition produced weaker epoxy joints. Aluminum coupons cured at 30% relative humidity had mean lap-shear strength of 18.6 MPa, while coupons cured at 75% relative humidity averaged 11.4 MPa. Cure temperature and clamp pressure were unchanged, and surface roughness was matched within 0.2 µm, so the strength difference is not easily attributed to those procedural factors.

The analytical results suggest that cure chemistry changed under high humidity. Infrared spectra showed a broader hydroxyl band in the high-humidity samples. Differential scanning calorimetry showed residual exotherm 40% higher in those samples, which is consistent with more uncured or partially cured material remaining after the nominal cure. Those results fit incomplete cure as one explanation for weaker joints.

The mechanism is not fully resolved. High humidity could interfere with bulk cure, leave water at the interface, or introduce a surface contamination effect not captured by roughness matching. My working conclusion is that 75% relative humidity impaired adhesion under this procedure, with incomplete cure as the leading explanation. A follow-up that separates bulk-cured samples from interface-conditioned samples would be needed to identify the pathway.
