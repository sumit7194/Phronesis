Three distinct analytical steps matter here, and they need to be traced separately because each supports a different kind of conclusion.

Step one is the primary endpoint. The drug arm showed 38% symptom reduction versus 14% on placebo — a 24 percentage-point difference with 95% CI 17 to 32 and p < 0.001. This is a randomized primary endpoint with a confidence interval that does not cross zero. The short-term efficacy claim rests on this step and, within the trial's own terms, it is well-supported.

Step two is the durability analysis at week 24. Among week-12 responders, 61% on drug maintained ≥30% reduction versus 44% on placebo — a 17 percentage-point difference with a CI from −3 to +37 and p = 0.09. The direction matches the primary finding, but the interval includes zero. This is a secondary analysis, restricted to a responder subset, underpowered relative to the primary. It cannot carry the weight of a durability claim on its own, though it points in the expected direction.

Step three is the generalizability question. This was a single-site trial that excluded patients with comorbid conditions affecting roughly 30% of the broader patient population with the disease. The enrolled population is not representative of who would receive the drug in practice, so even a confirmed within-trial effect may not translate as predicted to the broader population.

The three steps together: short-term effect real within the trial (step one strong), durability uncertain (step two weak), generalizability open (step three weak). A Phase 3 program attending to durability and broader populations is the right next step; claiming the current trial establishes more than short-term within-trial efficacy would run ahead of the evidence.
