The asymmetric catalytic reaction clearly demonstrates very high stereoselectivity. The chiral HPLC analysis of the crude product displays a massively dominant peak at 12.4 minutes and a very small secondary peak at 15.1 minutes, yielding an apparent enantiomeric excess of 98.6%. The primary asymmetric induction is undeniably successful based on this strong primary signal.

However, directly reporting this 98.6% as the definitive ee is premature due to the uncharacterized achiral byproduct. We know from ¹H NMR that compound Z is present at approximately 2% and absorbs at the 254 nm detection wavelength. Because compound Z's retention time on the Chiralpak AD-H column is completely unknown, it could easily be co-eluting with the minor enantiomer peak at 15.1 minutes. If this is happening, the 0.7% minor peak area is artificially inflated by the impurity, meaning the true ee would be even higher than 98.6%. Conversely, it could co-elute with the major peak. My working view is that the apparent ee is highly promising, but we cannot confidently assign the >99% ee claimed for publication until a simple HPLC spike experiment with authentic compound Z definitively rules out co-elution artifacts. Validating the chromatogram against known impurities is essential for absolute quantitative accuracy.
