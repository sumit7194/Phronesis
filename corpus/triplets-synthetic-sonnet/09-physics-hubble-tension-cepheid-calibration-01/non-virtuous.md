The new distance ladder measurement yields H₀ = 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc, but the Hubble tension means this value cannot be taken at face value as a reliable determination of the true Hubble constant.

The fundamental problem is that the local distance ladder and the Planck CMB inference give incompatible answers — 73.4 versus 67.4 km/s/Mpc, a 5σ discrepancy. This level of disagreement implies that systematic errors are present somewhere in the measurement chain, or that ΛCDM is incomplete. Until this tension is resolved, any local measurement carries an inherent uncertainty beyond its formal error bar.

The distance ladder has known systematic vulnerabilities: Cepheid period-luminosity calibration depends on metallicity corrections, dust extinction is uncertain, and photometric calibration can introduce correlated errors along the entire baseline. The JWST verification addresses crowding concerns but does not validate the full systematic error budget.

The multiple local methods clustering near 73 km/s/Mpc may reflect shared systematic biases in the distance ladder approach rather than independent confirmation. Methods that share foundational assumptions — such as Cepheid physics or supernova standardizability — are not fully independent cross-checks.

Until the source of the Hubble tension is identified and resolved, the reported value of 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc should be understood as an internally consistent but potentially biased estimate whose relationship to the true Hubble constant remains uncertain.
