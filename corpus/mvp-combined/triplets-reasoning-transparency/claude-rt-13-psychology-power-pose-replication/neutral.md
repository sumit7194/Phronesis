The multi-site replication included 1,512 participants across 8 labs running the original pose protocol on the same risk-taking task. The pooled effect size is d = 0.04 with a 95% confidence interval of -0.06 to 0.14, which is essentially indistinguishable from zero and substantially smaller than the original study's reported d = 0.49 from N = 42.

One site of the 8 reported d = 0.31 with N = 188, but the other seven sites cluster near zero, and the meta-analytic pooled estimate is not pulled meaningfully off zero by the single positive site. The within-site standard error of the d = 0.31 outlier overlaps zero by enough that the result is consistent with a chance finding among 8 attempts at the conventional alpha level.

The manipulation check is informative. Self-reported feelings of power showed a positive effect of the pose condition, with d = 0.34 across the replication sites. So the manipulation did induce the intended subjective state, but that state did not translate into measurable behavioral change on the risk-taking task.

The reported result is a failure to replicate the original behavioral effect at the planned multi-site sample size, with the manipulation appearing to work psychologically but not behaviorally. The single positive site is consistent with sampling variation across 8 attempts. The original effect of d = 0.49 from N = 42 may have been overestimated by sampling variance or by features of the original protocol that did not generalize across sites. The replication's confidence interval excludes effects larger than d = 0.14 with reasonable certainty.
