The study's design utilizes a natural experiment inherent to enrollment cutoffs. A 25-student class size threshold led to schools splitting into 13- and 25-student classes, creating a discontinuity.  An RDD estimate of the effect of class size reduction on math scores suggests a 0.21 standard deviation improvement for schools transitioning from 25 to 13 students.  This effect is strongly supported by the statistical significance, p=0.001, and the robustness of the estimate across bandwidth and specification choices (0.18-0.24 SD).

However, it is plausible but not established to extrapolate this specific finding to a national policy proposal to cap all elementary classes at 18 students. The RDD design only identifies the effect at the enrollment cutoff, not the relationship between class size and student outcomes across a range of reductions. This linear extrapolation, based on a 12-student reduction, cannot be confidently assumed to be applicable to a 6-student reduction proposed by the policy. The RDD estimate, therefore, provides evidence for the specific discontinuity observed at the enrollment threshold. It does not definitively support a direct, linear impact of class size reductions beyond that specific point.  This is a critical consideration when assessing the cost and potential impact of the proposed policy.
