The load test result is valid and technically sound. The ultimate capacity of 3,150 kN by Davisson's criterion, with no plunging at 3,400 kN, is a well-documented result for the specific pile and location tested. For the central area of the site, where subsurface conditions are well-constrained by boreholes showing till depth variation within ±1.2 m, the test pile provides a credible basis for the safety factor reduction. Reducing the safety factor to 2.0 for those piles and applying the resulting design capacity of 1,575 kN is defensible.

The east-margin piles cannot rely on this test as representative. The nearest borehole to the east-margin piles is 22 m away — substantially farther than the borehole spacing used to characterize the tested location — and the till surface was interpolated rather than directly measured. More critically, CPT data from one east-margin location showed softer-than-expected conditions at the till surface; this is a specific, direct indicator that conditions may be less favorable than at the test pile location. The code language requires representativeness of the most critical conditions, and the east-margin zone is the candidate for most-critical status.

The appropriate action is to apply FS = 2.0 for the central piles where the test pile is representative, and maintain FS = 2.5 for the east-margin piles until either additional CPT data or a second load test confirms adequate capacity there.
