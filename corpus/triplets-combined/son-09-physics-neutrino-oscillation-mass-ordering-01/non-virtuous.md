The combined analysis gives a 3.2σ preference for normal mass ordering, but this result should be interpreted cautiously because no individual experiment in the combination independently reaches the conventional 3σ evidence threshold.

The combined significance emerges from pooling four experiments each showing preferences of 1.1 to 2.1σ — weak individual signals that collectively appear stronger. When each contributing measurement falls below the evidence threshold, a combination reflecting an aggregation of sub-threshold effects does not carry the same evidentiary weight as an independent 3σ result from a single well-controlled experiment. The 3σ threshold exists for a reason: it provides a reliable guard against statistical fluctuations, and its application to combined sub-threshold signals is not straightforward.

The 3.2σ combined result is also close to the threshold and could be affected by imperfect modeling of correlations between experiments or by shared systematic uncertainties in the oscillation probability frameworks used across analyses. Even experiments using different neutrino sources and baselines may share nuclear cross-section inputs and oscillation model assumptions that introduce subtle correlations.

Without any single experiment establishing strong evidence independently, the current data do not support confident conclusions about mass ordering. A definitive determination will require higher-statistics data from a dedicated next-generation experiment that can independently establish the mass ordering with discovery-level significance.
