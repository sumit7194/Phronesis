Each deactivation claim ties to a specific characterisation anchor.

The activity drop from 78% to 41% propane conversion at 250 °C over 200 h is the directly observed performance change. BET surface area went from 184 m²/g (fresh) to 121 m²/g (spent), a 34% loss; that is the support-side anchor. TEM showed Pt particle diameter rising from 2.1 ± 0.4 nm to 4.6 ± 1.1 nm; that is the metal-side anchor for sintering. H₂ chemisorption showed dispersion dropping from 47% to 19%; that is the same sintering signal seen by an independent technique that probes accessible Pt sites rather than imaged ones. TGA showed 3.8 wt% carbon on the spent catalyst; that is the coke-deposition anchor.

The regeneration result is the anchor that separates reversible from irreversible contributions. Oxidative carbon removal followed by H₂ reduction restored conversion to 64%, not to the fresh 78%. Pt particle size after regeneration was 4.4 ± 0.9 nm, essentially unchanged from the spent 4.6 ± 1.1 nm. Two specific observations — partial activity recovery and unchanged particle size — anchor the inference that the irreversible deactivation is sintering-related and that the reversible portion (41% → 64%) corresponds to the coke contribution.

What these data anchor is: sintering accounts for the gap between fresh and post-regeneration activity (78 − 64 = 14 percentage points); coke accounts for the gap between spent and post-regeneration activity (64 − 41 = 23 percentage points). The 34% BET drop is consistent with support sintering contributing to the surface-area loss, but the BET measurement on its own does not separate Pt-driven from support-driven contributions; the TEM and chemisorption together do, for the Pt phase specifically.
