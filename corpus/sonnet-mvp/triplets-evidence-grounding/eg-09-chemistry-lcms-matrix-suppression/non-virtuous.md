Post-column infusion with pooled blank plasma matrix showed a suppression zone between 1.2 and 3.1 minutes of the 8-minute gradient, with peak suppression of 61% at 1.8 min. The analyte elutes at 4.7 minutes and the stable-isotope-labeled internal standard at 4.6 minutes — both outside the observed suppression window. IS-normalized matrix factors were 0.97 at low QC and 1.02 at high QC, confirming minimal matrix effect. Inter-lot imprecision across six plasma lots at LLOQ was 8.3% CV.

The post-column infusion results confirm that this method is not affected by plasma matrix suppression: the analyte and IS elute well past the suppression zone at 1.2–3.1 minutes, and the IS-normalized matrix factors confirm this chromatographic result with values of 0.97 and 1.02 respectively. These two lines of evidence are mutually consistent and together establish that ionization suppression is not a meaningful concern for this assay at the analyte retention time. The 8.3% inter-lot CV falls within the ≤15% acceptance criterion for LLOQ samples and presents no obstacles to validation acceptance under the applicable regulatory guidance.

The method is ready for validation completion. The chromatographic design effectively separates the analyte from the bulk of the plasma matrix interference. The stable-isotope-labeled internal standard co-elutes with the analyte at 4.6 minutes and compensates for any residual matrix variability, as confirmed by the IS-normalized matrix factors of 0.97–1.02 that lie within the acceptable range. Lot-to-lot precision at LLOQ is acceptable and well within the regulatory criterion. This is a well-characterized assay with a defensible matrix-effects profile that meets the requirements for regulated bioanalysis.
