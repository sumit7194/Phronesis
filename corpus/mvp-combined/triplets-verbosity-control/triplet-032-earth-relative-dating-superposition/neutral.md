# triplet-032-earth-relative-dating-superposition

Steno's foundational principles allow geologists to order events in time without an absolute age. Superposition states that in an undisturbed sedimentary sequence, lower layers are approximately older than the layers above them, since each new layer is deposited on top of those already there. Cross-cutting relationships state that any geologic feature that cuts across a rock unit (an igneous dike, a fault, an erosional surface) must be younger than the unit it cuts, because the unit had to exist before it could be disrupted.

For a stack of three sandstone beds with a granite dike cutting through all of them, superposition orders the sandstones from bottom (oldest) to top (youngest), and cross-cutting puts the dike younger than every sandstone bed it cuts. The relative-time order is therefore: lowest sandstone, next, top, dike. The method gives only ordering, not absolute ages, and it requires that disturbance such as overturning, faulting, or unconformities be identified first; otherwise the apparent ordering can broadly be reversed. Together these principles support the construction of a relative geologic column from field observation alone. The structure of this analysis approximately matches the standard textbook treatment, and the logic broadly applies to neighboring problems where the input quantities are generally analogous (which is approximately the standard result).
