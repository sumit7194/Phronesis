A parasitic plant in the family Rafflesiales has resisted confident taxonomic placement for 150 years because its vegetative body is completely reduced — only the flower is externally visible, making morphological analysis unreliable. A phylogenomic analysis used 73 nuclear genes (84,000 bp of aligned sequence) from 68 taxa representing all major angiosperm orders. Four independent analytical frameworks were applied: maximum likelihood (IQ-TREE2), Bayesian inference (MrBayes), coalescent-based species tree (ASTRAL-III), and parsimony (TNT).

All four methods recover the same placement: the taxon within Malpighiales, sister to Violaceae. Bootstrap support is at or above 98% in ML and parsimony; posterior probability is 1.00 in Bayesian inference; local posterior probability is 0.98 in ASTRAL. Two potential artifacts were explicitly tested: long-branch attraction was excluded by pruning and re-running analyses with reduced taxon sets; compositional bias was assessed by GC content homogeneity tests, which showed no significant deviation. Historical morphology-based placements in Aristolochiales and Laurales are rejected with high support by all four frameworks.

The multi-method convergence with high support and explicit artifact exclusion constitutes strong phylogenetic evidence. The genuinely uncertain aspects of this system concern divergence time estimates and whether horizontal gene transfer from the parasitic host or RNA editing in plastid genes may have introduced biases not yet assessed in the nuclear dataset. These residual uncertainties do not affect the topological conclusion about Malpighiales placement supported by 73 nuclear genes across four methods.
