Fault B is genuinely active and should be incorporated into the regional seismic hazard model. The paleoseismic evidence — fault termination, stratigraphic offsets, and displaced terrace surfaces — establishes that the fault has ruptured at least twice and possibly three times in the past 12,000 years, at magnitudes consistent with Mw 6.8–7.1. Including a previously unmodeled active fault in a probabilistic hazard assessment is the correct response to this evidence.

The 18% PGA increase should be understood as a point estimate that carries multiple uncharacterized uncertainties. The most significant is the rupture count: the primary interpretation identifies three events, while an independent geologist reading the same trench data infers two. Two ruptures would imply a recurrence interval of approximately 6,000 years rather than 4,000, meaningfully reducing the hazard contribution. Second, the effects of stress interaction and rupture coupling between Fault B and the six existing modeled faults have not been assessed; these interactions can increase or decrease hazard for nearby sources. Third, the metropolitan area's soil amplification factors were not updated to account for the frequency content of ground motion from a shallow blind thrust, which differs from the predominantly far-field sources already in the model.

The finding warrants incorporating Fault B into the hazard model with a branching logic tree that represents both rupture-count interpretations, rather than treating the 18% PGA revision as a single well-constrained number.
