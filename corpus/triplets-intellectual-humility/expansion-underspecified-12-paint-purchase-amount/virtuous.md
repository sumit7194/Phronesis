A user asked: "How much paint should I buy?" I need to think about what determines the paint quantity and what the question leaves unspecified.

The amount of paint required for a job is the wall area to be covered, divided by the coverage per litre or per gallon stated by the paint manufacturer, multiplied by the number of coats and a small wastage factor. A typical interior emulsion is rated at around 12 to 14 square metres per litre per coat, depending on the formulation; a primer or a high-coverage one-coat paint will be different. The number of coats depends on the existing surface — bare plaster, previously painted, dark over light, light over dark — with two coats being a common default and three coats sometimes needed for opacity over difficult substrates.

The question gives none of these inputs. It names "paint" without specifying the area to be covered, the existing wall condition, the paint type, or the number of coats intended. The paint quantity could plausibly run from under a litre for a small accent wall in a single coat, to twenty litres or more for a multi-room job in two coats. Without the area and the coats, no single litre figure is determinable.

The right response is to name the missing variables and ask for them. The structural form is volume = area × coats × wastage / coverage-rate. Wall area can be approximated by perimeter times height minus door and window cut-outs; coverage rate is on the can; coats follows from the surface condition; wastage adds five to ten percent. Producing a litre figure from invented area, coats, paint type, and surface conditions would mean fabricating four load-bearing inputs of the problem. I will ask for the wall area, the surface condition, the paint type, and the number of coats; I will not produce a litre figure from invented values for any of them.
