Mg/Ca ratios were measured in planktonic foraminifera (Globigerinoides ruber) from 18 mid-Pliocene Warm Period samples (~3.0–3.3 Ma) and 6 core-top modern samples from the same site. The calibration equation (Mg/Ca = 0.38 × exp(0.09 × SST)) was validated against 8 Atlantic core-top samples from published literature (r² = 0.88). Estimated mid-Pliocene SSTs were 22.4–24.1°C (site mean 23.2°C); the modern core-top SST estimate is 20.8°C, yielding an inferred Pliocene warming of +2.4°C above modern. The core site is at 2,850 m water depth; the lysocline for this ocean basin is at approximately 2,600 m; the site's estimated Δ[CO₃²⁻] is −12 μmol/kg, in the dissolution-sensitive range. Published protocols for below-lysocline sediment core analyses recommend a dissolution correction of +0.5 to +1.0°C per unit Δ[CO₃²⁻] to Mg/Ca-derived SST estimates, to compensate for Mg loss from foraminiferal tests during seafloor dissolution. The Mg/Ca values in this study were not corrected for dissolution effects. An additional known complication is that Mg/Ca in G. ruber is sensitive to seawater salinity and pH; estimated correction for salinity variation is ±0.3°C per ±1 ppt, but mid-Pliocene salinity at this site is not independently constrained.
