A gravitational wave event was detected as a neutron-star-black-hole merger. The chirp mass of 3.42 ± 0.04 solar masses, combined with Bayesian parameter estimation, identifies this as a binary system with a roughly 8 solar mass primary and a 1.6 solar mass neutron star secondary. The mass ratio q = 0.20 and the absence of a kilonova-like electromagnetic counterpart across the searched localization region together support the NSBH classification.

The matched-filter analysis produced a high-SNR detection at 14.2, sufficient for confident parameter estimation. The Bayesian posteriors gave m1 = 8.1 M_sun and m2 = 1.6 M_sun as the point estimates, placing the primary firmly in the stellar-mass black hole range and the secondary firmly in the neutron star range. The 90% credible intervals for these masses, while broad, do not change the core identification of the system components.

The electromagnetic follow-up by four telescopes covered the 1,200-square-degree localization within two hours of the trigger. No kilonova-like transient was detected. This is consistent with the NSBH classification: NSBH mergers produce kilonovae only under certain mass-ratio and spin conditions, and the observed q = 0.20 is in the regime where kilonova production is expected to be weak or absent.

Taking the component masses and electromagnetic follow-up together, this event represents the first confident NSBH merger detection in this observing run, providing a direct measurement of the merger rate for this population and constraining models of stellar binary evolution.
