The well records are consistent with a lagged groundwater response to fertilizer reduction. The district reduced nitrogen fertilizer application by 28% over 5 years. Shallow monitoring wells showed nitrate falling from 41 to 29 mg/L, while deeper municipal wells fell only from 36 to 34 mg/L. Rainfall during the period was within 6% of the long-term mean, and cropped acreage changed by less than 3%, so broad weather or land-area shifts do not explain the pattern easily.

The travel-time estimates make the difference between shallow and deep wells plausible. Age tracers estimated 2–4 year travel time to the shallow wells and 12–18 years to the deeper wells. If those estimates are representative, shallow wells should respond within the 5-year window, while deeper wells should still contain older recharge from before the fertilizer reduction.

My working view is that the shallow nitrate decline is an early sign of the fertilizer policy working, while the deeper wells are expected to respond slowly. Uneven compliance or dilution could still contribute. Continued monitoring is needed because the deeper municipal wells may not show the full effect for another decade or more.
