The fan is causing mechanical instability in the interferometer. The drift is low when the fan is off, only 0.6 fringes per 10 minutes, and it jumps to 3.4 fringes per 10 minutes when the fan is on. Beam power is stable to less than 0.5%, and the temperature probes near the arms barely move, staying within 0.1°C. That removes the other explanations and leaves vibration as the cause.

The accelerometer confirms the interpretation because vibration amplitude on the optical table increases fourfold during fan operation. A fourfold vibration increase is enough to explain the fringe movement, and it appears in exactly the condition where the interferometer drifts. Airflow and refractive-index effects are not the issue here; the apparatus is being shaken. The temperature readings also show that there is no meaningful thermal pathway.

My conclusion is that the cooling fan should be isolated or replaced before further optical measurements are taken. The experiment already identifies the problem: fan off gives stable fringes, fan on gives large drift, and the table vibration rises sharply. Additional beam shielding would only distract from the main fix. The clean next step is mechanical isolation of the fan, followed by a repeat of the 10-minute drift measurement.
