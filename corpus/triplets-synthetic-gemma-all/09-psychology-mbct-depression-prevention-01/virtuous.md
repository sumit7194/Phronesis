This meta-analysis, combining data from nine randomized controlled trials, presents robust evidence for the efficacy of MBCT in preventing relapse in patients with ≥ 3 prior depressive episodes. The analysis included 1,258 participants, with a 625-person MBCT group and a 633-person control group.  The primary outcome was depressive relapse or recurrence at 60 weeks, with a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.51 (95% CI: 0.42–0.62), indicating a statistically significant 18 percentage point absolute risk reduction in the MBCT group compared to the control group.  This effect was well-established across all nine trials, with no significant between-trial variation (I² = 0%).

The effect was particularly strong in patients with ≥ 5 prior episodes, with an HR of 0.44, suggesting a substantial reduction in relapse risk. This finding is strongly supported by the meta-analysis, which is characterized by low risk of bias and a lack of publication bias.  While the evidence is promising for this specific population, the applicability to first-episode depression or other depressive conditions remains plausible but not established. 

It's important to acknowledge methodological challenges inherent in mindfulness-based interventions, such as the difficulty in blinding. However, the substantial magnitude of the absolute risk reduction (18 percentage points) and the IPD approach should not be disregarded.
