The combined experimental measurement of the muon anomalous magnetic moment is aμ(exp) = 116592061(41) × 10⁻¹¹. The data-driven Standard Model prediction using e⁺e⁻ → hadrons inputs through dispersion relations gives aμ(SM, dispersive) = 116591810(43) × 10⁻¹¹, which leaves a 4.2σ deviation between experiment and theory.

The picture is complicated by two further pieces. A lattice-QCD calculation of the hadronic vacuum polarisation contribution by an independent collaboration brings the SM prediction (with the lattice value substituted) into 1.5σ agreement with the experimental result. The two SM predictions therefore disagree with each other by about 2.5σ on the hadronic vacuum polarisation piece itself. Separately, a new e⁺e⁻ → π⁺π⁻ cross-section measurement has appeared that disagrees with prior measurements by about 3σ in the low-energy region that dominates the dispersive integral.

Experimental systematics are budgeted at 56 ppb for beam dynamics out of a 217 ppb total combined uncertainty, and there is no specific known issue beyond the published budget.

The 4.2σ tension between experiment and the dispersive SM prediction is real on its own terms, but its interpretation depends on which theoretical input is correct. If the lattice value is right, there is no significant tension with the SM. If the dispersive value is right, there is. The new low-energy cross-section measurement, if it is correct, would shift the dispersive prediction toward the lattice value and reduce the tension. The empirical anchor — the experimental aμ — is fairly secure; the inference to "new physics" depends on a theoretical input that is itself in dispute.
