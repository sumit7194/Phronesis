Our study employed a 20-item conscientiousness subscale across 12 culturally diverse countries, with sample sizes ranging from 400 to 600 participants per country, totaling 6,242 participants.  We conducted multigroup confirmatory factor analysis (MGCFA) to assess configural, metric, and scalar invariance.  All models demonstrated configural invariance (RMSEA = 0.048), metric invariance (ΔCFI = −0.008), and scalar invariance (ΔCFI = −0.007) meeting the established criteria.  This confirms that the scale's factor loadings and item intercepts are equivalent across groups, enabling direct comparison of latent mean scores.  Cronbach's alpha ranged from 0.74 to 0.82 across the 12 samples, indicating acceptable internal consistency, and test-retest reliability ranged from 0.77 to 0.86, further supporting the scale's reliability. 

While two items showed partial non-invariance in scalar testing (ΔCFI improvement if freed = 0.002 and 0.001), these changes were negligible. Sensitivity analyses with freed parameters did not alter the latent mean ordering of countries. This demonstrates the robustness of the scale across these diverse populations. 

The standard for cross-cultural latent mean comparisons requires full scalar invariance. This scale achieves this standard with the established ΔCFI criteria.  The near-invariance of two items, while a potential area of concern, is a common finding in cross-cultural research and did not affect the conclusions drawn from the study. It's important to avoid the tendency to dismiss cross-cultural comparisons based on unfounded assumptions about scale applicability or translation.
