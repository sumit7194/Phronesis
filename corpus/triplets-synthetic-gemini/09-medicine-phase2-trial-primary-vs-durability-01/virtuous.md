The 12-week symptom reduction effect is remarkably well-established within this rigorous Phase 2 trial framework. Generating a decisive 38% severity reduction completely overpowering the 14% placebo baseline yields a large, highly confident clinical target. Because the confidence interval is remarkably tight (17%–32%) and securely anchors against a massive p < 0.001 value, the primary efficacy signal strongly validates the novel drug's short-term biochemical mechanics. 

However, evaluating the 24-week durability question reveals genuinely open, highly tentative boundaries. While calculating 61% vs 44% retention suggests an encouraging trend pointing solidly in the right direction, the confidence interval physically crosses zero (−3% to +37%) and lands squarely on p = 0.09. Relying on a much smaller evaluated sample size naturally limits statistical power, dictating that long-term durability is merely plausible but firmly not mathematically established. Furthermore, because the restrictive study design completely isolated one strictly defined geography and arbitrarily excluded severe comorbidities dominating 30% of the active disease population, generalized external translation limits how confidently we can extrapolate these specific results universally. A reasonable working view confirms the drug decisively drives clear short-term diagnostic benefits, while recognizing that validating physical long-term durability undeniably requires launching vastly larger randomized Phase 3 trials aggressively targeting broader unmodified clinical populations. Confidently weighting differing statistical endpoints efficiently anchors complex clinical strategies.
