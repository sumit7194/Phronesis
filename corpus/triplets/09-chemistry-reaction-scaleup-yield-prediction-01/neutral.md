Reaction R, a cryogenic lithiation/electrophile quench sequence, was optimized at 0.1 mmol scale over 12 experiments. The optimized conditions gave 92% isolated yield across 5 independent replication runs (mean 92.1%, SD 2.3%). The reaction requires temperature control at −78°C; the lithiation step has an estimated enthalpy of approximately −45 kJ/mol and selectivity degrades significantly above −65°C. At 0.1 mmol scale, mixing is achieved by hand-swirling within seconds.

A scale-up to 100g of substrate (approximately 350 mmol, a 3500-fold increase) is proposed without intermediate pilot-scale runs. At this scale, maintaining −78°C requires a large-capacity cryostat with high coolant flow. Mixing a viscous lithiation reaction in a multi-liter vessel is mechanically more demanding than at bench scale, and addition and mixing times will be substantially longer.

The aryllithium/addition reaction class is documented in process chemistry literature to show scale-dependent yield reductions attributed to heat and mass transfer limitations. Intermediate pilot runs at 1g or 10g are standard process chemistry practice for temperature-sensitive exothermic reactions before committing to large-scale production.
