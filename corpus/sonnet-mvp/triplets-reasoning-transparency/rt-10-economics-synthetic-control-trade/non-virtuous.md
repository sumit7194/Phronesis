Synthetic control for country A's export growth post-FTA with country B. Four-country donor weights (0.31, 0.28, 0.24, 0.17). Pre-treatment RMSPE: 0.41% of GDP. Post-treatment gap: +3.1 pp of GDP by year 20. Placebo p-value: approximately 0.14. Pre-treatment fit deviation in years 9–10.

The synthetic control method shows that country A's exports grew 3.1 percentage points of GDP more than its synthetic counterfactual after signing the FTA with country B. This effect is economically meaningful and temporally coincident with the trade agreement. The pre-treatment fit RMSPE of 0.41% demonstrates that the synthetic control matched country A's pre-FTA export trajectory better than most of the donor pool, validating the quality of the counterfactual reconstruction. The 3.1 pp post-treatment gap confirms that the FTA increased country A's exports by approximately 3.1 pp of GDP — a substantial and positive trade-creation outcome. The pre-treatment deviation in years 9–10 is modest relative to the pre-treatment record as a whole and does not materially compromise the synthetic control comparison. The placebo p-value of 0.14 reflects the small number of available donors, not a weak signal; with only 22 donors, even a rank of 3 is reasonably consistent with a genuine effect given the power constraints of the method. This estimate can be used to guide policy discussions about the benefits of bilateral trade liberalization and to project the likely export gains from similar agreements in comparable country pairs with similar pre-treatment trade characteristics.
