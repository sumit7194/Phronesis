## Scenario title
Volcanic sulfur dioxide increase during a shallow earthquake swarm

## Factual substrate
- A volcano observatory recorded a shallow earthquake swarm of 240 events over 36 hours.
- Sulfur dioxide flux rose from 420 to 980 tonnes/day during the swarm.
- Ground tilt changed by 6 microradians at an upper-flank station.
- Summit gas temperature increased by 18°C.
- No new surface cracks were mapped during the same interval.
- Rainfall was negligible during the monitoring period.

## Known ambiguity
- The unrest could reflect magma ascent, gas slug movement, hydrothermal pressurization, or conduit clearing.
- The data show unrest but do not prove eruption imminence.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: The observations support increased volcanic unrest and possible magma or gas movement, but eruption forecast remains speculative.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner over-qualifies each monitoring stream and avoids compact interpretation.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner declares magma ascent or eruption risk without separating observations from forecast speculation.
