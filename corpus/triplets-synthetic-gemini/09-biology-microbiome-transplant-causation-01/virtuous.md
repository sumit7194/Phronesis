The fecal microbiota transplant data securely establishes that the intact, donor-derived microbial community transfers the increased adiposity phenotype to germ-free mice. The 47% increase in body fat is highly reproducible, and the corroborating evidence showing a 2.1% higher caloric extraction efficiency provides a plausible physiological mechanism via transferred SCFA profiles. We can confidently state the whole microbial community is sufficient to induce weight gain.

However, we must carefully distinguish community-level phenotype transfer from claiming the microbiome strictly "causes" obesity. Large microbial transplants also introduce transferred donor metabolites, bacteriophages, and potential host factors. Without identifying a specific mechanistic microbe, or at least performing a reverse experiment treating lean organisms with obese profiles, the data does not formally satisfy causal principles. My working view is that these findings are highly promising, but we should delay reporting and publication until we can establish specific single-organism causation through defined-community experiments. While the broad community transfer effect is real, claiming a definitive microbiome-obesity link right now would bypass essential mechanistic proofs required to advance the field. Reporting only the community-level transfer without identifying the responsible organisms could ultimately misdirect metabolic research moving forward.
