The low home oximeter readings are artifacts. The clinic reviewed 52 patients, and 18 of them kept reporting oxygen saturation values of 90–92% at home. When those same 18 patients had arterial blood gas measurements in clinic, the results were normal. That means the home readings were not showing real hypoxemia. The common feature is obvious: all 18 patients were wearing dark nail polish during home monitoring.

The oximeters are not the problem either, because bench checks of the model were normal on a calibration simulator. The patients also did not report increased breathlessness during the low-reading episodes, so the readings do not match the clinical picture. There is no reason to build a complicated explanation around intermittent desaturation or timing differences when the device, symptoms, and clinic measurements all point away from a real oxygen problem.

My conclusion is that the clinic should treat these 90–92% home readings as false lows caused by nail polish. The fix is simple: remove the polish or use another sensor site, then repeat the home checks. No further respiratory workup is needed unless low readings persist after the obvious artifact is removed.
