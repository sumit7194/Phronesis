The Argo float array, comprising approximately 3,900 active profiling floats providing temperature profiles to 2,000 m depth, measured a global ocean heat content (OHC) increase of 0.71 ± 0.08 W/m² for the 0–2,000 m layer over 2005–2023 (18 years of near-complete Argo coverage). Three independent research groups analyzing the same Argo data using different quality control, mapping, and infilling methods reported OHC trends of 0.68, 0.71, and 0.74 W/m², all within each other's stated uncertainty ranges. The steric sea level rise component derived from Argo is consistent with the satellite altimetry residual (altimetry minus ice and glacial mass balance) to within 0.4 mm/yr, providing an independent confirmation of the OHC trend.

A methodological issue was identified in a subset of Argo floats deployed from 2009 to 2012: systematic warm biases affecting approximately 8% of profiles. Different correction approaches applied by the three groups shift the OHC trend by ±0.04 W/m², within the stated uncertainty range of ±0.08 W/m².

The 0–700 m layer shows a tighter OHC trend of 0.43 ± 0.04 W/m² due to denser float coverage and smaller infilling corrections. The deep ocean below 2,000 m, sampled by sparse ship-based hydrographic sections, contributes an estimated additional 0.07 ± 0.06 W/m², with higher uncertainty owing to limited spatial coverage.
