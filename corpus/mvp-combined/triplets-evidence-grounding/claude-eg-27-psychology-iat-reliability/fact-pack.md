## Scenario title
Implicit-bias measurement reliability and behavioural prediction

## Factual substrate
- A pre-registered psychometric study tested test-retest reliability of an implicit-association measure on 1,820 adults across two sessions 2 weeks apart; the test-retest correlation was r = 0.42.
- A meta-analytic review of 87 published behavioural-prediction studies found a mean predictive validity coefficient of r = 0.14 between the implicit measure and discriminatory behaviour.
- A laboratory paradigm tested whether short-term mood manipulation changes implicit-measure scores; mood manipulation moved scores by 0.18 standard deviations.
- A theoretical dual-process model proposes that implicit measures capture automatic associations that complement explicit self-report measures.
- A field study of 412 hiring decisions in a single industry found no significant association between recruiter implicit scores and call-back rates (β = 0.03, 95% CI −0.08–0.14).

## Known ambiguity
- Low test-retest reliability constrains any individual-level inference but is compatible with group-level signals.
- The dual-process model is a theoretical framework, not a measurement.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous: Distinguish psychometric, meta-analytic, experimental-mood, theoretical, and field study as different evidence types.
- Deficiency: Asserts implicit measures are valid (or invalid) without specifying which empirical claim and which theoretical claim are being made.
- Excess: Citation density.
