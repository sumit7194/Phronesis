## Scenario title

A 6-year eddy covariance record showing net CO₂ release from a thawing permafrost site, extrapolated to estimate the pan-Arctic permafrost carbon feedback

## Factual substrate

- An eddy covariance tower at a continuous permafrost site in Siberia measured net ecosystem CO₂ exchange over 6 complete years (2017-2022); the site transitioned from a small net CO₂ sink (−12 g C/m²/yr in 2017) to a net source (+28 g C/m²/yr in 2022) as the active layer deepened from 48 cm to 67 cm
- The source-to-sink transition is statistically significant (p = 0.008, linear trend); the transition timing coincides with mean annual ground temperature crossing the −1°C threshold at 1 m depth
- Methane emissions also increased: from 3.2 g CH₄/m²/yr to 5.8 g CH₄/m²/yr over the same period
- The site is representative of yedoma permafrost with high organic carbon content (~2.5% by weight in the top 3 m); approximately 20% of Arctic permafrost is yedoma-type
- The study extrapolated the measured flux to the pan-Arctic: if all yedoma permafrost follows a similar trajectory, the cumulative carbon release by 2100 would be 18–35 Pg C (contributing 0.05–0.10°C additional warming via the permafrost carbon feedback)
- This extrapolation assumed: (1) all yedoma sites will cross the −1°C threshold by 2060-2080 under RCP4.5; (2) the flux-temperature relationship measured at this site applies to other yedoma regions with different hydrology, vegetation, and soil structure

## Known ambiguity

- The site-level measurement is robust: 6 years of continuous eddy covariance with a clear trend and an identifiable threshold crossing
- The pan-Arctic extrapolation from one site to 20% of Arctic permafrost involves strong assumptions about spatial representativeness and the universality of the flux-temperature relationship
- Excess failure: presenting the pan-Arctic estimate as well-constrained by the site data, without acknowledging the extrapolation assumptions

## Generator notes (failure mode for slot 85)

Failure mode is **excess**. The non-virtuous passage should present the site-level trend as directly establishing the magnitude of the pan-Arctic feedback, treating the 18–35 Pg C estimate as a well-constrained projection. The virtuous passage should affirm the site-level measurement as strong evidence of a source-sink transition in yedoma permafrost, then clearly scope the pan-Arctic extrapolation as dependent on assumptions about spatial representativeness and universal threshold behavior that one site cannot validate.
