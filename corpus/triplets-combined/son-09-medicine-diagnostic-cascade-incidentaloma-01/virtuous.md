The imaging is strongly reassuring but does not settle the staging question, and that distinction matters because the decision at stake is whether to offer curative-intent surgery.

The <10 HU criterion carries approximately 98% specificity for benign adenoma — this is a robust and well-validated radiological standard. In the absence of an underlying malignancy, an 8 HU nodule with smooth margins is almost certainly a lipid-rich adenoma. That part of the assessment is solid.

The problem is the clinical context. This patient has known NSCLC, and adrenal metastasis occurs in 20-35% of advanced cases. Starting from a pre-test probability of approximately 25% and applying 98% specificity, the post-test probability of metastasis is roughly 1-2%. That is low — but 1-2% is not negligible when the downstream decision it informs is curative resection versus palliative systemic treatment. Staging errors in either direction carry serious consequences for the patient.

I would not change the staging to M1b on this finding alone. Equally, I would not dismiss the nodule as definitively benign without confirmatory workup. PET-CT or adrenal biopsy should confirm the imaging interpretation before the staging is locked in. The biochemical evaluation should proceed in parallel — the question of adrenal function is independent of the staging question and has its own clinical implications regardless of the imaging result.
