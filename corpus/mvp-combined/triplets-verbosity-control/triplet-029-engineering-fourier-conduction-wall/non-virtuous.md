# triplet-029-engineering-fourier-conduction-wall

Fourier's law in steady state through a plane wall gives q = k · A · ΔT / L, where q is the heat flow rate, k is the thermal conductivity, A is the area, ΔT is the temperature difference, and L is the thickness. For a brick wall with k ≈ 0.7 W/(m·K), A = 10 m², L = 0.2 m, and ΔT = 20 K, q = 0.7 × 10 × 20 / 0.2 = approximately 700 W. Halving thickness raises q to roughly 1400 W; doubling thickness drops it to about 350 W. Composite walls add resistances L/(kA) in series, and total q is then ΔT divided by total resistance (which is approximately the standard result).
