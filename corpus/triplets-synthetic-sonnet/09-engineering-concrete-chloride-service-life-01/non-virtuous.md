The chloride ingress model provides one estimate of potential service life, but its predictions should be treated with significant caution for maintenance planning purposes. Concrete durability in real-world environments involves considerable variability from factors including microcracking, aggregate heterogeneity, variable curing, and actual versus design chloride exposure. Diffusion models capture a simplified idealization of a complex physical and chemical process.

The model was calibrated on data from 8 bridges, which is a relatively limited dataset given the variability of concrete construction across different contractors, mix designs, and placement conditions. Even with 89% of core pairs predicted within ±15% in validation, the remaining 11% deviation and the five-of-six cracking prediction record provide only partial assurance. The aging factor — the largest source of model uncertainty — involves assumptions about long-term hydration behavior that cannot be verified directly for the target bridge.

For a decision with a $380,000 cost and potentially significant service life consequences, relying primarily on a diffusion model prediction with a 15-year confidence interval (8–23 years remaining) seems inappropriate. The bridge owner should commission a comprehensive investigation including additional core extraction and laboratory analysis before making the sealant decision. Model-based predictions of this type should support field investigation and expert judgment rather than substitute for them.
