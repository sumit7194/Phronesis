## Scenario title
Mixed evidence base on partial sleep restriction and n-back performance

## Factual substrate
- A 2018 within-subjects laboratory protocol with 36 healthy young adults randomised to 4 nights of 5-hour vs 8-hour sleep, crossover with 2-week washout. Reported a 0.34 standardised-mean-difference drop on 3-back accuracy after restriction (p = 0.011).
- A 2021 meta-analysis pooling 22 acute partial-restriction studies reported a pooled SMD of 0.21 (95% CI 0.13–0.29) on working-memory tasks.
- A 2020 ecological-momentary-assessment study of 412 university students linked self-reported nightly sleep below 6 hours to a 4.6% drop in cognitive-task scores administered on a phone (within-person fixed effects).
- A 2019 case report of a single shift worker after 38 hours of wakefulness reported severe lapses on a working-memory task.
- The lab study used actigraphy + polysomnography for adherence; the EMA study used self-report only.

## Known ambiguity
- The four sources are different evidence types — controlled experiment, meta-analysis, observational EMA, case report — and weight unevenly.
- Effect sizes from EMA and the lab differ partly because of measurement and population differences.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous: Distinguish four evidence types (RCT-style crossover, meta-analytic pool, observational EMA, anecdotal case report) and what each supports.
- Deficiency: Confidently asserts working memory is impaired by sleep restriction without distinguishing the evidence types or naming the specific designs.
- Excess: Smothers each finding in citation density.

## Notes
Deficiency failure mode. Virtuous keeps SMDs, sample sizes, study types; non-virtuous keeps the same conclusion but loses all the type and number anchors.
