The delamination interpretation is grounded in the convergence of four inspection signals. After 20,000 fatigue cycles, one of the six carbon-fiber panels lost 18% bending stiffness. That mechanical change is paired with a 45 cm² high-attenuation region on ultrasonic C-scan. Acoustic-emission hits clustered during the final 2,000 cycles, which supports the idea of late-cycle damage activity. Infrared thermography showed a 3°C local hot spot during loading, consistent with local friction or damage-related heating.

The paint-thickness fact is a real limitation. The affected panel was later found to have paint 0.4 mm thicker than the other panels, and that could bias ultrasonic coupling or surface temperature. But paint thickness alone does not explain the stiffness loss and late acoustic-emission cluster as directly as an internal damage process does.

The best-grounded conclusion is that this panel developed a local delamination or closely related internal damage region during fatigue cycling. That conclusion could be wrong if the coating and fixture behavior distorted several measurements at once, but the available observations point most strongly to damage. I would confirm with a coating-insensitive inspection or sectioning, while treating delamination as the current engineering explanation for the 18% stiffness loss.
