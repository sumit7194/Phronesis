The CRISPR-Cas9 knockout of kinase K1 in HEK293T cells was generated with two independent guide RNAs, both producing frameshifting indels confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Western blot confirmed complete loss of K1 protein. In standard culture conditions (DMEM with 10% FBS, 37°C, 5% CO₂), K1-knockout cells grew at 97 ± 3% of wild-type rate over seven days and showed no significant difference in viability (p = 0.42, n = 6 replicates). Phase-contrast microscopy at 10× and 40× magnification showed no morphological changes.

The paralog K2, which shares 68% sequence identity with K1 in the kinase domain, was found to be upregulated 2.4-fold in knockout cells versus wild-type by qPCR (p < 0.01). This upregulation is consistent with transcriptional compensation. Published data from yeast show that temperature-sensitive mutants of the K1 ortholog are radiation-hypersensitive, suggesting a role in the DNA damage response. K1 is conserved from yeast to humans.

No experiments were conducted under stress conditions such as serum starvation, hypoxia, DNA damage, heat shock, or drug challenge. The knockout was characterized only in standard culture in HEK293T cells. The standard-culture viability data are a genuine finding — the cells grow normally under these conditions — but the K2 upregulation and the absence of stress-condition testing limit what can be concluded about K1 dispensability as a general property of the gene.
