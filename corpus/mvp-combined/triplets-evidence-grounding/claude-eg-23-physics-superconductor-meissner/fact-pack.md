## Scenario title
Claimed superconducting transition in a thin-film candidate material

## Factual substrate
- A polycrystalline thin film of nominal stoichiometry Bi₂Te₃-doped composition shows zero electrical resistance at temperatures below 17.2 K in 4-probe measurement.
- Magnetic susceptibility (SQUID) shows a diamagnetic onset at 16.8 K with a low-temperature shielding fraction of 6%.
- Specific-heat measurement on a 12 mg piece does not show a clear anomaly at 17 K within instrumental noise (3% of the predicted BCS jump).
- X-ray diffraction shows a 2θ shift consistent with 1.4% lattice contraction relative to undoped reference.
- Energy-dispersive spectroscopy shows the dopant concentration is non-uniform, with regions ranging from 1.2 at% to 4.1 at%.
- A control measurement on a partially decomposed sample at the same composition shows residual resistance and no diamagnetic onset.

## Known ambiguity
- 6% shielding fraction is small and could indicate filamentary superconductivity rather than bulk.
- Absence of specific-heat anomaly is potentially inconsistent with bulk superconductivity but could reflect small superconducting volume fraction.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous: Tie each claim to specific observation type (transport, magnetisation, specific-heat, XRD, EDS, control sample) and what each rules in or out for "bulk" vs "filamentary".
- Deficiency: Asserts superconductivity at 17 K without naming the 6% shielding fraction, the absent specific-heat anomaly, or the non-uniform dopant.
- Excess: Citation density.
