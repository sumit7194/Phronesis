The interferometer became less stable when the cooling fan was running. In 10-minute runs with the fan off, fringe drift averaged 0.6 fringes per 10 minutes. With the fan on, the drift rose to 3.4 fringes per 10 minutes. Beam power varied by less than 0.5% across both conditions, so laser-output fluctuation is not an obvious explanation for the difference. Temperature probes near the interferometer arms changed by less than 0.1°C, which also makes a large uniform heating effect unlikely.

The accelerometer result points toward mechanical coupling. Vibration amplitude on the optical table increased fourfold when the fan was on, and that change occurred in the same condition that produced the larger fringe drift. The fan could still be affecting the interferometer through airflow-driven refractive-index changes, especially if local air motion crossed one arm more than the other. The available temperature probes may not capture every small gradient in the beam path.

My working interpretation is that the fan-on state is responsible for the larger drift, with vibration as the most likely measured contributor. I would not call the pathway settled until the fan is mechanically isolated or the beam path is shielded from airflow. The next run should separate those two possibilities rather than merely repeating fan-on and fan-off measurements.
