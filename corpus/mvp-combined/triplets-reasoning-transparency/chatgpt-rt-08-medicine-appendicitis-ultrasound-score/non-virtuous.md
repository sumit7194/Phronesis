The high-risk patients should probably be treated as likely appendicitis. The clinic evaluated 74 adolescents with right-lower-quadrant pain, and the score put 31 above the high-risk threshold. In that group, 24 had an appendix diameter above 6 mm on ultrasound, and 27 had white blood cell counts above the local cutoff. That looks like enough to manage the high-risk group as appendicitis until proven otherwise.

The low-risk symptoms appear not to change the decision much. Twelve low-risk patients had nausea and mild fever, but those symptoms are fairly nonspecific. The score is meant to identify the patients who need urgent appendicitis workup, and the ultrasound and blood-count findings seem to show that it did so. The 9 discharged low-risk patients without follow-up imaging look like a documentation gap rather than a reason to weaken the main conclusion.

My conclusion is broadly that the score should be used to escalate high-risk adolescents to definitive appendicitis evaluation. Low-risk patients can probably be handled with routine discharge instructions. The data appear to show that the threshold separates the clinically important group well enough for triage use in this clinic population of adolescents presenting with pain.
