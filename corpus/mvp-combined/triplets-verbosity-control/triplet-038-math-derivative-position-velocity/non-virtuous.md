# triplet-038-math-derivative-position-velocity

Velocity is the time derivative of position, and acceleration is the time derivative of velocity. For x(t) = 5t² (metres, t in seconds), the power rule gives v(t) = dx/dt = 10t (m/s), and another derivative gives a(t) = dv/dt = approximately 10 (m/s²). The acceleration is constant, so the motion is uniform acceleration starting from rest because v(0) = 0. At t = 2 s, x = 20 m, v = 20 m/s, and a = 10 m/s². Integrating a(t) back broadly recovers v(t) and integrating again generally recovers x(t), confirming the round trip through the kinematic chain x → v → a (which is approximately the standard result).
