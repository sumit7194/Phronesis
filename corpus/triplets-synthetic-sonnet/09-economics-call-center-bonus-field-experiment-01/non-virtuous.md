A field experiment with 420 call center agents ran for eight weeks, assigning half to a bonus program and half to a control. The results suggest there may have been some productivity difference between the groups. Treatment agents handled roughly 342 calls per week on average, compared to around 320 in the control group, which could represent a difference of perhaps 22 calls, or around 7% higher productivity, though it is difficult to say how reliable this estimate is. While the p-value was below 0.01, field experiments in workplace settings often face compliance issues and confounds that can complicate interpretation.

Some subgroup patterns appeared in the data. Long-tenure agents seemed to show a larger response than short-tenure agents, at around 9% versus 4%, though this was not significant. High-productivity agents seemed to show about 12% improvement versus 5% for low-productivity agents, with p=0.04, which might indicate some differential response, though subgroup analyses are generally unreliable.

It is unclear whether any productivity effects, if real, would continue beyond the study window. Habituation, peer dynamics, and other factors could shift outcomes over longer periods. Overall, the results are suggestive but uncertain, and caution is warranted in drawing firm conclusions about bonus program effectiveness.
