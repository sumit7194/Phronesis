The many-labs replication is an important step toward establishing the reliability of the social norm salience effect, but it does not definitively settle whether the effect operates in real-world settings. Online behavioral proxy tasks are convenient but measure different processes than the hotel towel compliance scenario in the original study — motivational factors, environmental cues, and the social presence of others all differ between laboratory simulations and naturalistic behavior.

The 92% replication rate is encouraging, but the three non-replicating labs introduce uncertainty about the conditions under which the effect fails. Understanding why those labs did not replicate — whether due to sample characteristics, cultural factors, or other contextual variables — is important before concluding the effect is generalizable. Low I² of 14% suggests limited heterogeneity but does not rule out systematic moderators that were not examined.

The attenuation from d = 0.41 to d = 0.31 could reflect genuine reduction in the effect when studied under controlled conditions compared to the naturalistic original. Before concluding that norm salience interventions should be implemented in field settings, further pre-registered replications in naturalistic environments would be needed to confirm that the lab-based effect translates to the behavior change contexts where it is most relevant. The many-labs study is a significant contribution but represents one step in an ongoing validation process rather than a definitive resolution of the question.
