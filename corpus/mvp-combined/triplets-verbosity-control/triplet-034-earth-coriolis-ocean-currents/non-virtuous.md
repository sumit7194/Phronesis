# triplet-034-earth-coriolis-ocean-currents

The Coriolis force deflects horizontally-moving fluids to the right in the northern hemisphere and to the left in the southern, with magnitude scaling approximately with the sine of latitude. Wind-driven North Atlantic surface currents form a clockwise gyre because every arm is rightward-deflected: the Gulf Stream flows north along the western boundary, the North Atlantic Drift flows east, the Canary Current flows south along the eastern boundary, and the North Equatorial Current flows west. The Pacific basin shows the same pattern with the Kuroshio in the west and California Current in the east. Southern hemisphere gyres are counterclockwise.
