It is functionally impossible to determine what is really happening in this chemical reaction because kinetics experiments are notoriously complex and easily ruined by hidden confounds. Even though they tracked the decay using UV-Vis at 310 nm and found R² values near 0.99, relying on spectrophotometry to track reaction rates is always messy. There are constant issues with baseline drift, hidden side reactions that absorb at the same wavelengths, and unpredictable temperature fluctuations that can completely invalidate the rate constants (k_obs) being reported here. 

Furthermore, proposing a second-order rate law based on this data is foolish because interpreting chemical kinetics from standard reaction vials is essentially guesswork. We can never really trust the pseudo-first-order assumptions. Until the team deploys significantly more advanced monitoring techniques, perhaps utilizing in-situ NMR or elaborate flow chemistry validation to actually see the molecules properly, we simply cannot determine the reaction mechanism. Therefore, the overall rate law is entirely unproven and uncertain. A comprehensive mechanistic study using fundamentally different and far more sophisticated equipment is absolutely required before anyone should draw conclusions about how these specific molecules interact. The UV-vis data provides almost no genuine kinetic insight.
