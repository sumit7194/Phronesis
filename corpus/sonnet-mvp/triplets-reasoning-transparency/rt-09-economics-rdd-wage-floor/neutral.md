RDD at a state minimum wage increase (dropout age raised from 15 to 16). Running variable: hourly wage centered at $9.50. Outcome: employment indicator 4 quarters post-change. Local linear regression (bandwidth $1.50): 2.1 pp employment drop at the cutoff (95% CI: 0.4–3.8 pp, p = 0.016). McCrary density test: no bunching (p = 0.36). Placebo cutoffs at $8.50 and $10.50: non-significant (−0.3 pp, p = 0.71; 0.6 pp, p = 0.54). Donut RDD: 2.4 pp (p = 0.021).

The causal interpretation of the 2.1 pp employment discontinuity rests on two assumptions that the available data can directly address. The continuity assumption — that no other determinant of employment changes discontinuously at the $9.50 cutoff — is supported by the McCrary density test (p = 0.36), which shows no systematic sorting of workers to just below the threshold, and by the placebo null at the $8.50 and $10.50 cutoffs, which shows no spurious discontinuities at wages where no minimum wage change occurred. The donut RDD result (2.4 pp) being consistent with the main estimate means the discontinuity is not driven by heaping of observations exactly at the cutoff.

These tests — the McCrary density, the two placebo cutoffs, and the donut specification — are the specific checks for the assumptions that need to hold. They support those assumptions. The estimate of 2.1–2.4 pp employment reduction is therefore the most defensible causal reading of this data, conditional on no other policy change coinciding exactly with this wage threshold at this time — an assumption supported by the placebo results but not directly proven.
