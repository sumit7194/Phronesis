The 87 cross-bedding measurements at the outcrop give a present-day mean foreset dip-azimuth of 142 degrees. The bedding attitude is 058/12, indicating modest tilt to the southeast. Applying the standard tilt correction by rotating the bedding back to horizontal about the strike axis shifts the mean foreset dip-azimuth to 153 degrees in the depositional frame. The Fisher concentration parameter k for the corrected dataset is 24, and the 95% confidence cone on the mean direction has a half-angle of 6 degrees.

A Fisher k of 24 indicates a unimodal distribution with reasonable concentration, although it falls below the conventional k greater than 50 threshold sometimes used for high-confidence single-mode paleocurrent interpretation. The unimodal pattern is consistent with a sustained paleocurrent direction during deposition rather than a multi-channel system with bimodal flow.

Regional paleomagnetic studies place this unit at low northern paleolatitudes with no significant vertical-axis rotation since deposition, so the corrected azimuth in the depositional frame can be read directly as the depositional paleocurrent direction without further rotation. The 11-degree difference between present-day and tilt-corrected azimuths is small because the bedding tilt itself is only 12 degrees.

The reported result is a paleocurrent direction toward 153 degrees in the depositional frame, with a 95% confidence cone of plus or minus 6 degrees and a Fisher concentration consistent with a unimodal flow regime. The interpretation is supported by the structural geometry, the statistical concentration, and the paleomagnetic constraint on no post-depositional rotation.
