The measured noise floor at 15 Hz is approximately 3 × 10⁻²¹ /√Hz, about 300× above the design sensitivity floor at 100 Hz (1 × 10⁻²³ /√Hz). The primary microseismic peak at 0.1–1 Hz shows a seismic ground motion of 10⁻⁷ m/√Hz; applying the suspension isolation transfer function (f⁻⁸ above the 1 Hz pendulum resonance) predicts approximately 10⁻¹⁴ m/√Hz of coupling into the test masses at 15 Hz. The quantum noise floor calculated from the 100 kW circulating power is approximately 5 × 10⁻²⁴ /√Hz at 15 Hz. The Newtonian noise estimate from the seismic field geometry model gives approximately 5 × 10⁻²² /√Hz at 15 Hz.

The measured noise floor (3 × 10⁻²¹) and the measured seismic input (10⁻⁷ m/√Hz) are direct measurements from this detector. The quantum noise floor (5 × 10⁻²⁴) is a calculation from the measured circulating power — it is not directly measured but derived from a well-characterized physical model. The Newtonian noise estimate (5 × 10⁻²²) is a model output from the seismic-field geometry: it uses the measured seismic data as input but applies a theoretical model of how density fluctuations couple gravitationally to the test masses — it is the most model-dependent number in this analysis. The gap between the Newtonian noise estimate and the measured noise floor (approximately 6×) is unexplained and may reflect Newtonian noise model underestimation, residual seismic coupling through the suspension, or additional technical noise — none of which is resolved by the current measurements.
