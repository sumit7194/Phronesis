The RNA-seq analysis is statistically valid — 1,247 genes at FDR < 0.05, 312 with more than two-fold change, the top gene at log2FC = 4.2 with padj = 1.2 × 10⁻²⁸ — but the experimental design makes it impossible to attribute the expression differences to treatment rather than batch.

All four treatment samples were sequenced in February (batch 1) and all four controls in April (batch 2), with library preparation batch-separated the same way. PC1 in the PCA explains 62% of variance and separates treatment from control, but it simultaneously separates batch 1 from batch 2. The two effects cannot be distinguished. Post-hoc correction using ComBat requires batch-bridge samples — specimens run in both batches — to estimate the batch effect independently. No bridge samples were included, so the confound cannot be corrected statistically.

The RT-qPCR confirmation of seven of eight top genes does not resolve the problem. Those experiments used the same batch-separated RNA samples, confirming that the RNA-level signal exists in these samples, not that treatment rather than batch produced it.

The statistical significance of the differential expression results is genuine within the DESeq2 framework; the statistics accurately describe what is in the data. The fundamental uncertainty is about causation: which of the two perfectly confounded variables — treatment or batch — is responsible for the expression differences. The experiment needs to be redesigned with balanced batch assignment across treatment and control before treatment-driven expression changes can be identified with any confidence.
