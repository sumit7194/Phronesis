# triplet-020-medicine-insulin-glucose-secretion

In a pancreatic β-cell, glucose enters through the GLUT2 transporter and is phosphorylated by glucokinase to glucose-6-phosphate, committing it to glycolysis. Glycolysis and mitochondrial oxidation raise the cytosolic ATP-to-ADP ratio, which closes ATP-sensitive K⁺ channels in the plasma membrane. The membrane depolarizes, voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open, intracellular Ca²⁺ rises, and insulin-containing granules fuse with the plasma membrane to release insulin into the portal circulation.

The result is a chain that encodes plasma glucose into insulin output in approximately real time. At fasting glucose near 80-90 mg/dL, basal insulin sits at roughly 10 μU/mL; a postprandial rise to 140-180 mg/dL drives a roughly 10-fold increase in secretion. The GLUT2-glucokinase pair has kinetic parameters matched to the physiological glucose range, so the system becomes responsive at the concentrations that occur after meals and stays quiet at fasting concentrations. This pairing is the molecular basis for the closed-loop control of plasma glucose on the minutes-to-hours timescale, with hepatic glucose production providing the symmetric arm during fasting. The structure of this analysis approximately matches the standard textbook treatment, and the logic broadly applies to neighboring problems where the input quantities are generally analogous.
