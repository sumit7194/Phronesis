A multicenter RCT randomized 314 mechanically ventilated adults 1:1 to early progressive mobilization or usual care. Barthel at ICU discharge: 72 ± 18 (early) vs 58 ± 21 (usual), adjusted difference 12.3 points (95% CI: 6.1–18.5, p < 0.001). ICU-free days: median 7.2 vs 5.8 (p = 0.04). Hospital LOS: 14.1 vs 16.3 days (p = 0.07, NS). 28-day mortality: 24% vs 27% (p = 0.49, NS). Adverse events: 7 in the early group versus 2 in usual care (p = 0.04), including 4 unplanned extubations. Baseline APACHE II scores were balanced (22.1 vs 21.8, p = 0.72).

Whether 12.3 Barthel points is clinically meaningful depends on the evidence base for the MCID, typically cited at 5 points for the Barthel Index in this population — a normative standard, not another observation from this trial. By that standard, the entire 95% CI (6.1 to 18.5 points) sits above the MCID, which anchors the clinical-significance claim to a specific external benchmark rather than to the p-value alone. The 7 versus 2 adverse events are the specific observation grounding the harm signal; within those 7, the 4 unplanned extubations are the specific events most relevant to ICU safety assessment.

The non-significant mortality (p = 0.49) and LOS (p = 0.07) do not confirm benefit or harm at those endpoints — they are genuinely null or underpowered to detect the effects that exist. The harm signal (7 vs 2, p = 0.04) warrants patient-selection criteria for implementation, anchored specifically in the extubation event rate.
