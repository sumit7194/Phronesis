The 120,000-year central Antarctic ice core temperature reconstruction is an extremely high-quality and exceptionally well-validated paleoclimate record. Unlike many single-measure proxies, this reconstruction boasts a highly constrained modern instrumental calibration (r² = 0.91) and employs three independent physical cross-validations. Because borehole diffusion profiles, deuterium co-isotopes, and precipitation accumulation rates all converge perfectly on the primary oxygen isotope signal, we can evaluate the resulting temperature anomalies with significant structural confidence.

By explicitly quantifying known proxy confounds like moisture sourcing shifts within the tight residual error budget (< 0.5°C), the model earns its reported uncertainty bounds. The 120,000-year timeline robustly documents severe Last Glacial Maximum cooling near −9.8 ± 1.3°C and strong localized Last Interglacial warming at +3.2 ± 1.1°C. While we must actively recognize the structural scope limitation—these highly accurate temperatures strictly reflect central Antarctic conditions and cannot be carelessly stretched into a global mean equivalent—the local record itself is remarkably secure. My working view is that this specific ice core data represents the absolute gold standard for tracking long-term localized atmospheric shifts, providing an extensively cross-checked anchor point for deep-time climate cycles. The multiple redundant temperature validations actively secure the reliability of these specific localized historical reconstructions.
