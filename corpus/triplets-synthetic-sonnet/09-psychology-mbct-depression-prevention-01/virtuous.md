I² = 0% across 9 independent RCTs with n = 1,258 is strong clinical evidence. Zero heterogeneity means the effect estimate was consistent across all nine trials — different investigators, different countries, different control conditions, the same result. An 18 percentage-point absolute risk reduction (38% vs. 56% relapse at 60 weeks) with HR = 0.51 is clinically meaningful for a population with three or more prior depressive episodes who face high relapse risk. The IPD method controls for individual-level confounds that aggregate meta-analysis cannot. Publication bias tests were negative. NICE independently assessed this evidence and issued a clinical recommendation. This is not an equivocal literature.

The unblinded nature of psychological intervention trials is a real and recognized limitation, but it is not disqualifying here. Blinding is not feasible for any active psychological treatment; the clinical trial literature in psychotherapy, rehabilitation, and behavioral medicine operates under this constraint across the board. The 9 RCTs used active or treatment-as-usual controls, not inert placebos — reducing the relative advantage of expectancy effects. This limitation should be named, not used as a global disqualifier.

The appropriate scope limitation is population-specific: the evidence base is for recurrent depression (≥ 3 prior episodes). It does not generalize to first-episode depression or to other depressive presentations.

Invoking "methodological limitations of mindfulness research" to discount an I² = 0% IPD finding across 9 trials misapplies generic concerns to a body of evidence that specifically addressed them.
