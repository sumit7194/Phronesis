The remnant population stands at 145 adults across 6 subpopulations. The population viability analysis was run with 1,000 stochastic simulations under current conditions and gave a 50-year extinction probability of 0.27. The sensitivity analysis, varying carrying capacity ±20%, produced a range of 0.18 to 0.41 across plausible parameter combinations. The corridor-improvement scenario, which links three of the subpopulations, produced a median 50-year extinction probability of 0.14.

The habitat manager wants to know whether the population is "in trouble" and whether the corridor work is worth the budget. The PVA gives quantitative anchors for both questions, but the way those anchors are communicated matters.

The model assumes the demographic and environmental parameters we have estimated; if those are off, the extinction probability moves. The carrying-capacity sensitivity captures one kind of parameter uncertainty but does not capture model-structure uncertainty — for example, whether catastrophic events outside the simulated frequency band could occur. Those caveats deserve to be on the table.

For the manager's purposes, the picture is that current conditions place the population at meaningful but not overwhelming extinction risk over the planning horizon, and that the corridor scenario reduces that risk substantially in the simulation. A reasonable summary is to share the central PVA result, flag the range from the sensitivity analysis, and present the corridor outcome as a comparison rather than as a guaranteed outcome. That lets the manager weigh the conservation gain against the cost without treating the simulation as a settled prediction.

I will draft the briefing along those lines and walk through the comparison rather than only stating the central numbers.
