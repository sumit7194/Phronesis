Thermal conductivity of a novel ceramic-polymer composite was measured at room temperature (25°C) using three independent methods: laser flash technique (4.2 ± 0.2 W/(m·K)), transient hot-wire method (4.0 ± 0.3 W/(m·K)), and steady-state guarded hot-plate measurement (4.3 ± 0.15 W/(m·K)). All three values agree within their stated uncertainties.

The composite is being evaluated for use as a thermal interface material in electronic packaging operating at sustained temperatures up to 500°C.

A single elevated-temperature measurement was performed at 300°C using the laser flash technique: 3.5 ± 0.6 W/(m·K). An effective medium theory model predicts the thermal conductivity should decline roughly linearly with temperature above 200°C, reaching approximately 2.8 W/(m·K) at 500°C; the model prediction at 300°C is 3.4 W/(m·K), consistent with the measured value. The model uses the room-temperature measurements as input.

No measurements have been performed above 300°C. The application target of 500°C is 200°C beyond the highest measured data point. The 300°C measurement uncertainty (±0.6 W/(m·K)) is three times wider than the room-temperature measurements and comes from a single method rather than three independent ones.
