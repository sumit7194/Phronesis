Phenolphthalein testing across 12 cores gave carbonation depths of 14–28 mm, mean 21 mm. Cover meter readings show rebar cover of 18–42 mm, mean 29 mm. Three cores have carbonation depth at or exceeding cover depth. Chloride concentration at rebar depth is below 0.1% by cement weight at all six sampled cores.

The three depassivated locations are a measured present condition: the phenolphthalein result shows the carbonation front at or past rebar depth at those specific core locations. The 32-year remaining service life is a Tuutti model projection — a theoretical output, not a measurement — contingent on the square-root-of-time propagation law continuing to hold at depths above the measured 21 mm mean and on no change in exposure conditions. These are assumptions embedded in the model, not properties of the deck that were measured during this inspection.

For the three depassivated cores specifically, the model projection is irrelevant to the immediate risk question, because the theoretical future state has already been reached at those locations. For the 9 non-depassivated cores, the 32-year figure is a planning input appropriate to its evidence type — a model estimate for a future condition, useful for scheduling the next inspection interval and for rehabilitation cost planning, but not a measured property of the deck. Treating the 32-year projection at the same epistemic level as the three measured depassivated locations would misrepresent the evidence type: one is an observed present condition, the other is a theoretical future state.
