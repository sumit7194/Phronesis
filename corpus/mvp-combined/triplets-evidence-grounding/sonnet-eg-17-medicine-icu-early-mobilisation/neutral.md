A multicenter RCT randomized 314 mechanically ventilated adults 1:1 to early progressive mobilization (within 24 hours) or usual care. Barthel Index at ICU discharge: 72 ± 18 (early) vs 58 ± 21 (usual care), adjusted difference 12.3 points (95% CI: 6.1 to 18.5, p < 0.001). ICU-free days: median 7.2 vs 5.8 (p = 0.04). Hospital length of stay: 14.1 vs 16.3 days (p = 0.07, NS). 28-day mortality: 24% vs 27% (p = 0.49, NS). Adverse events: 7 in the early group (4 unplanned extubations, 2 falls, 1 catheter dislodgement) versus 2 in usual care (p = 0.04). APACHE II scores were balanced at baseline (22.1 vs 21.8, p = 0.72).

The 12.3-point Barthel difference exceeds the commonly cited MCID of 5 points for this instrument, which is the specific benchmark anchoring the claim of clinical significance. The 95% CI of 6.1 to 18.5 points lies entirely above this threshold, supporting the direction and magnitude of the finding. The 7 versus 2 adverse events is the specific observation grounding the harm signal — including 4 unplanned extubations in the early group, which is the specific event type of highest safety concern in mechanically ventilated patients.

The non-significant mortality (p = 0.49) and hospital LOS (p = 0.07) results mean these outcomes are not attributable to early mobilization at this sample size. The significant adverse event difference, anchored in those specific event counts, is the key safety consideration for implementation.
