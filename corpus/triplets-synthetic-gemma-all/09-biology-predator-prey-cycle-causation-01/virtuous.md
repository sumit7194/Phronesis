A 30-year dataset reveals a well-established numerical coupling between harrier and vole populations within the grassland reserve. The cross-correlation function consistently shows peaks at lag 0 and +1, with Pearson r values of 0.74 and 0.81, respectively. This suggests a clear temporal lag in the abundance of both species, with harrier abundance exhibiting a response to vole fluctuations. 

The correlation between harrier breeding success and vole density is strongly supported (r = 0.83, p < 0.001). This is consistent with the observed reliance of harriers on vole populations for successful breeding.  Furthermore, harrier territory occupancy demonstrates a consistent shift in tandem with vole cycles, ranging from 28 to 74 pairs across the 30 years. 

While the observed correlation between harrier and vole abundance is strong and ecologically plausible, it does not definitively establish causation. The introduction of cattle grazing in year 18, coinciding with a significant increase in vole abundance, suggests that external factors, such as vegetation management changes, can influence vole dynamics. The literature highlights other potential drivers of vole cycles, including vegetation state, weather, and disease.

The strong correlation between harrier and vole abundance warrants further investigation. While the observed numerical coupling is suggestive of a regulatory influence, it is genuinely uncertain whether harriers are actively controlling vole population dynamics.  To definitively determine this, an experimental approach, such as predator removal or addition, is necessary.
