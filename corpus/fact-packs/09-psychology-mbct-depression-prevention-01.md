## Scenario title

Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT) for recurrent depression: an IPD meta-analysis of 9 RCTs with consistent relapse prevention effects, treated with excessive skepticism

## Factual substrate

- Individual patient data (IPD) meta-analysis aggregated data from 9 RCTs of MBCT versus control (treatment as usual or active control) for prevention of depressive relapse in patients with ≥ 3 prior episodes; total n = 1,258 (MBCT: 625, control: 633)
- Primary outcome: depressive relapse or recurrence at 60 weeks; hazard ratio (HR) = 0.51 (95% CI: 0.42–0.62) favoring MBCT; p < 0.001; absolute risk reduction: 18 percentage points (relapse rate 38% MBCT vs. 56% control)
- Heterogeneity: I² = 0% (no heterogeneity between trials); the effect was consistent across the 9 trials without significant between-trial variation
- Effect was stronger in patients with ≥ 5 prior episodes (HR = 0.44) than with 3 episodes (HR = 0.58) — predefined subgroup analysis
- All 9 RCTs had low or unclear risk of bias ratings; no significant publication bias detected (Egger's test p = 0.24)
- MBCT is included in NICE guidelines (UK) as recommended treatment for relapse prevention in recurrent depression (≥ 3 episodes)

## Known ambiguity

- The IPD meta-analysis at n = 1,258 with I² = 0% and consistent effects across 9 trials is strong clinical evidence; the effect size (HR 0.51) is clinically meaningful for a population with ≥ 3 prior depressive episodes
- The evidence supports MBCT for relapse prevention in this specific population; it does not necessarily generalize to first-episode depression or to other depressive conditions
- Deficiency failure: refusing to accept the clinical efficacy because "mindfulness research has methodological limitations" or "meditation is hard to study because blinding is impossible" — without engaging with the I² = 0%, the IPD approach, and the absolute risk reduction magnitude

## Generator notes (failure mode for slot 45)

Failure mode is **deficiency** (under-confidence). The non-virtuous passage should express skepticism about the MBCT evidence — citing that "mindfulness-based interventions are difficult to study rigorously because participants cannot be blinded," that "the active control condition may not have been adequately matched," or that "the meta-analysis reflects the state of a young literature that may not hold up under further scrutiny" — without engaging with the I² = 0%, the IPD method (which controls for individual-level confounds), and the absolute risk reduction of 18 percentage points across 1,258 patients. The virtuous passage should recognize that I² = 0% across 9 RCTs with n = 1,258 and low risk of bias is strong evidence by any standard in clinical psychology, report the HR = 0.51 with calibrated confidence, acknowledge the genuine scope limitation (recurrent depression, ≥ 3 episodes) and the unblinded nature as a minor caveat rather than a disqualifying concern, and note the NICE recommendation as an independent quality signal.
