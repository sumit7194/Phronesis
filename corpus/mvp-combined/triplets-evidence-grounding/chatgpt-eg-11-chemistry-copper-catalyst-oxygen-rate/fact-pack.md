## Scenario title
Oxygen exposure and copper-catalyst rate enhancement

## Factual substrate
- A copper-catalyzed coupling reaction was run under nitrogen and under 5% oxygen in nitrogen.
- Initial rate was 0.18 mmol/hour under nitrogen and 0.41 mmol/hour under 5% oxygen.
- The catalyst solution turned from pale yellow to green under oxygen.
- UV-visible absorbance at 690 nm increased threefold under oxygen.
- Product selectivity stayed at 91–93% in both conditions.
- Adding a radical trap did not change the oxygen-enhanced rate.

## Known ambiguity
- Oxygen could generate a more active copper species, change ligand oxidation state, or alter trace impurities.
- The color and absorbance shift are indirect indicators, not structural identification of the active catalyst.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: Oxygen is associated with faster coupling and a changed copper-containing species, but structure remains inferred.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner over-labels routine rate and spectroscopy facts before drawing a modest mechanistic inference.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner asserts an active copper species without tying the claim to rate, color, absorbance, and trap results.
