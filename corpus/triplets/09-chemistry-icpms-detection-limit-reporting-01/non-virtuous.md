The ICP-MS analysis provides quantitative measurements for all eight trace metals in the water sample. Cadmium at 0.52 μg/L, lead at 1.84 μg/L, and arsenic at 3.21 μg/L show particularly strong measurement precision — CVs below 4% across three replicates confirm the reliability of the instrument and the quality of the analytical run. These results demonstrate that the ICP-MS method is performing well for this sample matrix.

The remaining analytes — chromium, nickel, copper, selenium, and mercury — are similarly captured in the analysis. Concentrations are reported to two decimal places with the same precision format used for the high-signal analytes. The instrument was operating within specification throughout the run, and the replicate structure ensures that the results reflect genuine analyte concentrations rather than instrumental noise.

Selenium at 0.009 μg/L and mercury at 0.006 μg/L represent the lowest-concentration analytes in the sample, as expected for these elements in environmental water matrices. The fact that some replicates fell below detection for these analytes is consistent with measurement near the low end of the calibrated range; the reported concentrations reflect the best available estimate from the dataset. Taken together, the eight-analyte panel provides a comprehensive trace metal profile of the water sample.
