The cohort gives 4.1% occult hypoxemia in the lighter stratum and 9.7% in the darker stratum within the SpO2 92 to 96% band, an unadjusted 5.6 pp gap. Same device across both strata, 4,210 paired readings, 10-minute draw window.

Two assumptions are doing the load-bearing work in mapping this gap to a device-pigmentation bias claim. The first is that case-mix differences between the strata do not generate the gap on their own. The strata differ in age, severity of illness, and admission diagnosis distribution, all of which independently affect the SpO2-SaO2 relationship. Severity-driven physiology changes peripheral perfusion and venous admixture, both of which can shift the bias of pulse oximetry against arterial SaO2 in ways unrelated to skin pigmentation. The unadjusted 5.6 pp could absorb some of that severity gradient and overstate the device effect; conversely, if illness severity were higher in the lighter stratum, the gap would be understated.

The second is that self-reported pigmentation tracks the optically relevant melanin density at the measurement site closely enough for the comparison to be a device-pigmentation comparison rather than a sociological-grouping comparison. The mapping is coarse, and any classification noise will pull the apparent gap toward zero.

So the headline 5.6 pp is conditional on the case-mix-balance assumption and on the pigmentation-coding being a useful proxy. The chain breaks differently under each: case-mix failure could shift the true gap by several percentage points in either direction, while coding noise can only shrink the apparent gap. Reported as such, the result is consistent with a meaningful device bias in the 3 to 6 pp range after adjustment, with the central estimate sensitive to which severity adjustment is used.
