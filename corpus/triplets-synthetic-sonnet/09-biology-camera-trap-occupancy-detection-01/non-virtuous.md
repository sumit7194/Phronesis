The camera trap results are clear: this large cat population has declined substantially within the protected area over the five-year monitoring period. Naive occupancy dropped from 0.58 in Year 1 to 0.31 in Year 5, a decline of nearly half, and the trend is highly significant (p = 0.003). Running the multi-season occupancy model gives true occupancy estimates of 0.72 in Year 1 and 0.44 in Year 5, confirming the pattern. The annual decline of 0.14 occupancy units per year with a 95% confidence interval from −0.22 to −0.06 establishes the trend beyond any reasonable doubt.

Detection probability varied across seasons and habitat types — 0.42 in dry season, 0.18 in wet season, 0.14 in dense forest, 0.51 in open grassland — but these are stable characteristics of the survey conditions that the model handles appropriately. Camera stations were located at the same GPS positions each year and effort differed by less than 5% across seasons, ruling out methodological artifacts. The naive trend itself is significant, and the model-corrected estimate confirms the same directional story.

This species has clearly lost a major portion of its occupied range in this protected area. The data from both the raw detection record and the formal occupancy model are consistent and the statistical evidence is robust. The magnitude of the contraction — from 0.72 to 0.44 on the corrected scale or from 0.58 to 0.31 on the naive scale — represents a serious conservation concern that is well-documented by these data.
