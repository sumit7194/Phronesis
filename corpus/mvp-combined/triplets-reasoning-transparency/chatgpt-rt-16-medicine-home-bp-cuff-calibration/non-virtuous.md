The home cuffs appear to be reading too high and should probably not be trusted for medication decisions. The clinic compared 44 patients and found that home systolic readings were 11 mmHg higher on average than clinic readings. Calibration testing showed that 15 of the 44 home cuffs over-read by at least 8 mmHg. That seems to broadly explain why the home numbers look inflated.

The clinic devices passed the annual calibration check, so the clinic readings are likely the more reliable ones. Patients with over-reading cuffs also reported tighter cuff placement more often, which appears to suggest user technique is contributing to the problem. Evening versus morning timing seems like a secondary issue and probably does not overturn the main interpretation.

My conclusion is that the clinic should generally stop acting on these home readings until the cuffs are recalibrated or replaced. The discrepancy looks mostly like a device and placement problem. The average 11 mmHg gap is somewhat large, so using the home values as real blood pressure would probably risk overtreatment for many patients in the comparison and follow-up visits for hypertension management in this clinic sample before medication changes later on.
