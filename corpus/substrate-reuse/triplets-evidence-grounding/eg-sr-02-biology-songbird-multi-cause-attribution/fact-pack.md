## Scenario title

Multi-stressor attribution of a ground-nesting songbird decline: distinguishing strength of evidence across habitat, pesticide, phenological, and disease claims.

## Factual substrate

- 10-year monitoring of a ground-nesting songbird across 40 sites using three independent data streams: point counts, eBird reporting rates, banding station captures.
- Decline estimates: 35% (95% CI 28-42%) from point counts, 32% from eBird, 38% from banding — all within overlapping confidence intervals.
- Remote sensing: 18% reduction in suitable breeding habitat over the decade (grassland-to-cropland + shrub encroachment).
- Pesticide residues found in all 4 of the 4 sites sampled; 36 of 40 monitoring sites not sampled for pesticides.
- Phenological correlation between earlier insect prey emergence and lower fledgling success: r = 0.31, p = 0.08 (not statistically significant).
- Disease not systematically surveyed. One unresolved mortality report in year 6 at 2 sites.

## Known ambiguity

- Decline itself is well-documented via converging methods, but attribution to specific causes rests on evidence of highly variable strength (direct remote-sensing measurement vs. partial site sampling vs. weak correlation vs. absent surveillance).
- An 18% habitat reduction does not mechanistically account for a 35% population decline without additional assumptions about per-site productivity.

## Conclusion space

- Virtuous-compatible: Separate the decline claim (well-supported by three converging data streams) from the attribution claims (variable evidentiary strength: habitat = direct measurement; pesticides = partial sampling; phenology = weak correlation; disease = absent surveillance). Name which specific observation supports which specific claim.
- Excess-failure-compatible: Over-qualify every decline-estimate and every habitat-loss number with methodological scaffolding for mundane background facts.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible: Assert multi-cause attribution as established without tying each cause claim to its evidentiary support, treating all four drivers as equally well-supported by "the data."

## Notes

The EG-a contrast is whether each attribution claim is tied to its specific supporting observation, and whether the strength of that support is labeled. The deficiency-failure passage treats "the evidence shows habitat, pesticide, phenological, and disease drivers" as uniformly grounded; the virtuous passage names each claim's observational anchor and strength separately.
