The study analyzed Mg/Ca ratios in *Globigerinoides ruber* from a mid-ocean sediment core to reconstruct sea surface temperatures during the mid-Pliocene Warm Period. They used a calibration equation relating Mg/Ca to SST, validated for the modern ocean at this site with an r² of 0.88 across 8 samples.  

The estimated Pliocene SST for this site is 23.2°C, with a modern SST of 20.8°C, and a inferred Pliocene warming of +2.4°C. The study did not apply any correction for carbonate dissolution below the lysocline, a depth of 2,850 m for this specific ocean basin.  The lysocline is at ~2,600 m.  The study estimated a Δ[CO₃²⁻] of -12 μmol/kg, within the dissolution-sensitive range. 

The Mg/Ca ratios used in the reconstruction do capture a genuine signal of Pliocene warming relative to modern, consistent with other proxies.  However, the use of the Mg/Ca-based SST reconstruction without dissolution correction introduces a cold bias of +0.5 to 1.5°C, potentially underestimating the true Pliocene warming.  The true warming may be 3.0 to 4.0°C higher than the reported estimate of +2.4°C.  This underestimation is likely due to the dissolution bias, which needs to be factored into the analysis.
