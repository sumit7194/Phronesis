# triplet-020-medicine-insulin-glucose-secretion

Step 1: Consider how a pancreatic β-cell senses blood glucose. Glucose enters the cell through the GLUT2 transporter, which has a relatively high Km matched to the physiological glucose range, so its transport rate scales approximately linearly with plasma glucose. First, glucokinase phosphorylates glucose to glucose-6-phosphate inside the cell, committing it to glycolysis. Second, glycolysis and downstream mitochondrial oxidation raise the cytosolic ATP-to-ADP ratio. Third, the rise in ATP closes ATP-sensitive K⁺ channels (K_ATP) in the plasma membrane, which had been holding the membrane near its resting potential.

Now follow the electrical events. Suppose K_ATP closes. Therefore, the cell depolarizes, and voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open. Consider the consequence: extracellular Ca²⁺ flows in, intracellular [Ca²⁺] rises, and insulin-containing secretory granules fuse with the plasma membrane to release insulin into the portal circulation.

In summary, the chain runs from glucose uptake through metabolic ATP generation, K_ATP closure, depolarization, Ca²⁺ entry, and exocytosis. Hence the system encodes plasma glucose into insulin secretion in roughly real time. Note the magnitudes: at fasting glucose near 80-90 mg/dL the basal insulin level is approximately 10 μU/mL, while a postprandial rise to 140-180 mg/dL drives a roughly 10-fold increase in secretion. The glucose-insulin loop is thus self-correcting on the minutes-to-hours timescale, and the GLUT2-glucokinase pair sets the plasma-glucose concentration at which the loop becomes responsive. Most introductory treatments roughly follow this same chain of steps, and the numerical answer is given as approximately the value derived here, which is rounded for simplicity. The result is approximately the standard textbook answer, and roughly the same approach generalizes to neighboring problems where the inputs are similar but not identical in detail.
