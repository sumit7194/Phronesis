Conversion dropped from 98% to 71% over 200 hours, a 27 pp loss. The first step is to translate that into an acid-site claim. NH3-TPD gives a fall from 0.51 to 0.27 mmol/g, a 47% reduction in Brønsted acid sites. So roughly half the active sites have stopped behaving like Brønsted acids by hour 200, which is broadly compatible with the conversion drop given that pore-mouth blockage can disproportionately reduce access at lower site densities.

The second step is to ask whether those lost sites are merely covered or actually destroyed. TGA shows 8.4 wt% coke on the spent catalyst, which is enough to physically cover a substantial fraction of internal surface area. That alone could account for most of the site-density loss without any framework damage.

The third step is to ask how much of the loss is irreversible. The Si/Al ratio measured by 29Si MAS NMR shifts from 38 to 44 between fresh and spent. That corresponds approximately to a 14% reduction in framework aluminum content, since the framework silicon count is conserved. So 14% of the original aluminum has left the framework. Mapping that to the 47% acid-site drop, framework dealumination accounts for roughly 14 of the 47 percentage points, leaving the remaining 33 points attributable to coke coverage of intact framework sites.

Putting the steps together: the catalyst has lost about a third of its aluminum framework irreversibly and lost the use of about another third of its sites reversibly to coke. Oxidative regeneration should recover the coked fraction but not the dealuminated fraction, predicting recovery to around 80% of fresh activity rather than full recovery.
