# triplet-018-medicine-altitude-oxygen-saturation

Atmospheric pressure drops from 760 mmHg at sea level to roughly 460 mmHg at 4000 m. Alveolar oxygen tension follows in proportion, falling from 100 to about 60 mmHg, and arterial PO₂ tracks alveolar PO₂ closely in healthy lungs. On the oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve, PaO₂ ≈ 100 corresponds to SaO₂ ≈ 98%, and PaO₂ ≈ 60 corresponds to SaO₂ ≈ 90%. The modest 8-point saturation drop reflects the long upper plateau of the sigmoidal curve. Below roughly 60 mmHg the curve steepens, so further altitude gain produces sharper desaturation than the same pressure drop did between sea level and 4000 m. The same structure also fits the closely related problems in the field.
