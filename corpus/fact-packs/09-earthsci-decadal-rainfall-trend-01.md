## Scenario title

A 15-year decline in seasonal rainfall at a regional network of rain gauges, attributed to climate change in a report that does not account for natural decadal variability

## Factual substrate

- 47 rain gauges across a semiarid region in East Africa recorded wet-season (March–May) rainfall from 2009 to 2023; the network mean shows a trend of −8.4 mm/decade (p = 0.03 for linear regression over the 15-year period)
- The 15-year instrumental record shows 2009–2023 mean of 312 mm/season, compared to a 1960–2000 reference climatology of 347 mm/season (−35 mm, approximately 10% lower)
- The East Africa region is known to have experienced strong decadal rainfall variability driven by the Interdecadal Pacific Oscillation (IPO) and the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD); ENSO teleconnections are also documented; the 2009–2023 period coincides with a predominantly negative IPO phase, which is documented to suppress March–May rainfall in this region by 15–30 mm/season
- Paleoclimate proxies (lake sediment cores) covering 500 years show two prior multi-decadal drought periods comparable in amplitude to the current departure, occurring without anthropogenic forcing; these are well-documented in the regional literature
- The report's attribution statement: "The 15-year rainfall decline is caused by climate change and represents a new drier state for the region"

## Known ambiguity

- The 15-year decline is real in the instrumental record, but the length of record is insufficient to distinguish a trend from a phase of multi-decadal natural variability (IPO, IOD)
- The negative IPO phase (2009–2023) is a documented driver of rainfall suppression in this region with magnitude comparable to the observed anomaly; the observed decline is consistent with IPO forcing alone
- The paleoclimate record shows comparable multi-decadal droughts occurring without anthropogenic forcing — the current anomaly is not unprecedented in amplitude
- A well-constrained attribution statement would acknowledge that both climate change and natural decadal variability are plausible contributors, and that the 15-year record is too short to partition the contributions

## Conclusion space

- The rainfall decline is real in the instrumental record; both its magnitude and its possible causes should be reported
- Excess failure: attributing the full observed decline to climate change without accounting for the IPO phase, the documented natural decadal variability, or the paleoclimate record of comparable unforced droughts

## Generator notes (failure mode for slot 36)

Failure mode is **excess** (overconfidence). The non-virtuous passage should accept the attribution statement ("caused by climate change, represents a new drier state") without noting the IPO phase coincidence, the natural variability documented in the literature, or the paleoclimate record. It should present the 15-year p = 0.03 trend as sufficient evidence for the attribution. The virtuous passage should affirm the observed decline as real, then explicitly identify: (1) IPO negative phase is a documented rainfall suppressor of comparable magnitude in this region, (2) 15 years is too short to distinguish trend from multi-decadal variability, (3) paleoclimate proxies show comparable unforced droughts — and conclude that the attribution to climate change specifically is not supported without partitioning natural and anthropogenic contributions.
