The two measured values — 2.31 W/(m·K) at 25°C and 1.74 W/(m·K) at 800°C — are reliable within their measurement range and should be reported as such. The laser flash measurements are well-characterized and the uncertainties are appropriate.

The extrapolated value of 0.98 W/(m·K) at 1,400°C is not a reliable design input. Linear extrapolation assumes the thermal transport mechanism behaves the same at 1,400°C as between 25°C and 800°C. For YSZ, this assumption is contradicted by known material behavior: phonon scattering mechanisms change above 1,000°C, radiation-conduction coupling becomes significant in the translucent ceramic at high temperatures, and — critically — the tetragonal-to-monoclinic phase transformation occurs above approximately 1,170°C. That transformation is irreversible, alters the microstructure, and changes the thermal conductivity by 20–40% according to published data. A linear fit through two room-to-mid-temperature points projects through this regime change without capturing it.

The published literature range for YSZ at 1,400°C is 1.2–2.8 W/(m·K) — more than a 2× spread driven by processing and microstructure differences. The extrapolated estimate of 0.98 W/(m·K) falls below this range entirely, which is itself a warning sign. Using it as a turbine blade design input would be unsafe without direct measurement at or near the operating temperature.
