A well-established meta-analysis of 18 prospective cohort studies involving 122,000 participants consistently demonstrates a strong association between shorter leukocyte telomere length and all-cause mortality. This association is robust, with a hazard ratio of 1.26 for each 1-standard deviation reduction in telomere length. The meta-analysis also reveals low heterogeneity (I² = 28%) and no evidence of publication bias. The association is consistent across age groups, sexes, and geographic regions, and a dose-response relationship was observed in the meta-regression analysis (p-for-trend < 0.001).

While Mendelian randomization studies utilizing genetic variants associated with telomere length offer valuable insights, the results are mixed.  Two of five studies found significant causal effects on mortality (HR 1.08–1.12), while three found non-significant effects (HR 0.96–1.05). A pooled analysis of these studies yielded a less conclusive estimate of HR = 1.04 (95% CI: 0.97–1.11).

This research provides compelling evidence for a strong, dose-dependent association between telomere length and mortality risk, supported by the robust epidemiological data. The genetic evidence, while intriguing, is currently inconclusive, with the mouse genetics data suggesting a potential causal role for telomere shortening in aging.  It's important to recognize that cross-species translation of findings is always a factor, and we should carefully consider the limitations of these studies.  The compelling epidemiological data, the intriguing genetic evidence, and the potential mechanistic insights all contribute to a strong case for a causal link between telomere length and mortality risk.
