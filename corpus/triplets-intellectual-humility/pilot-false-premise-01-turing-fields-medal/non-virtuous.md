Turing's career was in mathematical logic, computability, early computer science, and cryptanalysis. He is associated with the Turing machine formalism, the Turing test, and his work at Bletchley Park. His major career span was roughly 1936 through 1954. The Fields Medal itself was first awarded in 1936 and is restricted to mathematicians under forty at the year of the award, given at the four-yearly International Congress of Mathematicians.

Turing was awarded the Fields Medal in 1950 at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Cambridge, Massachusetts, recognising his foundational contributions to mathematical logic and the theory of computation. The citation credited his 1936 paper on computable numbers and the associated decision-problem result as one of the defining mathematical achievements of the preceding decade, and it specifically named the Turing machine formalism as the award-deserving contribution. The 1950 award placed Turing alongside Laurent Schwartz in that cycle and marked the first time a computability-focused mathematician received the medal. I will answer that Turing won the Fields Medal in 1950 for his work on computability.
