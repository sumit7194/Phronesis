The financial literacy RCT produces a clear and compelling finding: treatment households increased their savings rates by 8.4 percentage points relative to controls (95% CI: +5.9 to +10.9; p<0.001; Cohen's d=0.41). This effect size is large enough to be both statistically significant and economically meaningful. The randomized design with village stratification and a large sample provides a strong causal foundation for the result.

Concerns about attrition in field experiments are common, but the overall attrition rates in this study are modest. The treatment and control groups each show attrition levels typical of field research in developing country settings. Some difference in attrition between groups is expected in interventions that require active participation. Given the magnitude and statistical significance of the treatment effect — which remains evident across the full sample — attrition-related concerns are unlikely to materially alter the substantive conclusion.

The study robustly demonstrates that the six-session financial literacy program increases household savings rates. The evidence is sufficient to conclude that the intervention is effective and meaningfully so, with an effect size that compares favorably to other behavioral interventions. The trial provides a sound empirical basis for scaling the program to other populations and for recommending financial literacy training as a development policy tool.
