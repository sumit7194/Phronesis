JWST transmission spectroscopy shows a 4.3 µm feature in a rocky exoplanet (1.4 R_Earth, 1.8 M_Earth) orbiting an M dwarf. Bayesian retrieval with a clear, well-mixed atmosphere gives CO₂ at 400 ± 120 ppm, Bayes factor 28. The 2.7 µm band is marginal (1.8σ). Two-temperature stellar contamination correction applied. No cloud layer included.

The JWST transmission spectrum shows a robust CO₂ detection at 4.3 µm (Bayes factor 28) with a retrieved atmospheric abundance of 400 ± 120 ppm. This represents a significant detection of a carbon-bearing molecule in the atmosphere of a rocky exoplanet in this size range and establishes the presence of a substantial CO₂ inventory. At 400 ppm, the CO₂ abundance is comparable to Earth's present-day atmospheric CO₂ concentration by mole fraction, though the total atmospheric pressure and column mass of this world may differ substantially from Earth's. The marginal 1.8σ feature at 2.7 µm provides tentative corroborating support for the CO₂ identification across an independent absorption band. The stellar contamination correction was applied using a physically motivated two-temperature spot model, and the Bayes factor of 28 against a featureless spectrum confirms that the 4.3 µm feature is not explained by instrumental systematics or uncorrected stellar variability at the level that would be detectable in this dataset. Together, the primary detection, the marginal supporting feature, and the Bayes factor establish a well-characterized atmospheric composition that can serve as a reference for comparative exoplanetology studies of rocky worlds around M dwarf stars.
