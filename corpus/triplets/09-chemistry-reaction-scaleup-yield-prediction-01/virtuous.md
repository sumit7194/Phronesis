The small-scale optimization is solid. Five independent runs at 0.1 mmol gave 92.1% isolated yield with SD of 2.3% — a tight, reproducible result, and I have high confidence in those numbers for the bench-scale conditions.

What I cannot do is predict that performance carries to 100g, and the gap between those statements matters here. The lithiation step is exothermic at −45 kJ/mol, selectivity is temperature-sensitive above −65°C, and the mixing strategy at 0.1 mmol — hand-swirling for seconds — has no equivalent in a multi-liter vessel. Heat removal rate and mixing time become engineering constraints at 100g that simply do not exist at bench scale. The aryllithium/addition class is specifically documented in process chemistry literature for scale-dependent yield reduction — this is a known risk for this reaction type.

My assessment: advance to a pilot run at 1g or 10g to characterize how yield and selectivity respond to longer mixing times and the thermal management demands of a larger cryostat before committing to 100g. Projecting the bench yield forward without that data conflates a measured result with a prediction at a scale that changes the physics of the reaction. The optimization work is done; the scale-up question is not answered by it.
