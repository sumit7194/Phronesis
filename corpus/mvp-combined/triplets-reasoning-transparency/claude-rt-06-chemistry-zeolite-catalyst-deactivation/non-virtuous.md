The H-ZSM-5 catalyst lost activity over the 200-hour run, with methanol conversion dropping from 98% to 71%. The deactivation is a mixture of reversible coke deposition and largely irreversible framework dealumination. Spent catalyst contains 8.4 wt% coke by TGA. The framework Si/Al ratio rose from 38 to 44, indicating roughly a 14% reduction in framework aluminum, broadly consistent with partial dealumination over a long methanol-to-olefins run.

The acid-site density measured by NH3-TPD fell from 0.51 to 0.27 mmol/g, an approximately 47% reduction in Brønsted acid sites. This loss is attributable to both coke coverage of active sites and the dealumination signal seen in the Si/Al ratio. The site density drop is compatible with the conversion drop given that the relationship between site count and activity is generally nonlinear in zeolite catalysts.

Coke deposition is reversible by oxidative regeneration, while dealumination is largely irreversible at the framework level. Given that both mechanisms are active here, the catalyst should respond partially to regeneration but will retain a permanent activity deficit relative to fresh material. The dealumination contribution of 14% on the aluminum framework translates to a similar fraction of permanent site loss, with the remainder of the 47% drop attributable to recoverable coke coverage.

My conclusion is that the catalyst has undergone a combination of reversible and irreversible deactivation in the proportions implied by the framework chemistry, with regeneration expected to recover the catalyst to about 80% of fresh activity. The diagnostic picture is consistent across the three measurements.
