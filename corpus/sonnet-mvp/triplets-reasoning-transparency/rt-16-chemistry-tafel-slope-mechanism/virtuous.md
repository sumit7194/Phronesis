MoS₂ HER catalyst in 0.5 M H₂SO₄. Tafel slope: 38 mV/decade. j₀: 1.4 × 10⁻⁴ A/cm². H* surface coverage: ~0.72 monolayer. EIS: single semicircle above −100 mV; Rct decreasing monotonically with overpotential.

Step 1: 38 mV/decade is between the canonical Heyrovsky (~40 mV/dec, proton-transfer RDS) and Tafel (~30 mV/dec, H*+H* combination RDS) values — the slope alone is ambiguous and does not assign the mechanism. Step 2: the 0.72 monolayer H* coverage means the Volmer step is fast relative to whichever subsequent step is rate-limiting. This is consistent with both mechanisms: Heyrovsky needs abundant H* waiting for a proton to react with; Tafel needs H* pairs available for surface recombination. Coverage does not discriminate. Step 3: the EIS single-semicircle with monotonically decreasing Rct at overpotentials above −100 mV is more naturally assigned to a charge-transfer RDS (Heyrovsky) than to a purely chemical RDS (Tafel). A surface recombination RDS would be expected to produce an inductive loop or an additional low-frequency RC element associated with H* surface accumulation, which is not seen. The EIS response therefore favors Heyrovsky. My conclusion, taking all three data streams together, is that the Heyrovsky mechanism is the best-supported assignment from this dataset — the EIS discriminates where the slope and coverage alone cannot. This mechanistic assignment predicts that increasing proton activity (lower pH) should accelerate the rate, which is a testable consequence that an isotope experiment would definitively resolve.
