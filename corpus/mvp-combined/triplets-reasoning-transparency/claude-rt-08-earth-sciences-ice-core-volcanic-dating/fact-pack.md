## Scenario title

Identifying a candidate large eruption in an ice core sulfate spike: matching sulfate flux and tephra to a known eruption window

## Factual substrate

- A polar ice core shows a bipolar non-sea-salt sulfate spike of 95 kg/km^2 deposition centered at an annual layer count age of 1257 +/- 2 years before present.
- A historical record from a tropical region documents a major explosive eruption in the same approximate window.
- Tephra shards in the sulfate-rich layer are rhyolitic with major-element compositions consistent with the candidate volcano's known whole-rock chemistry.
- The annual layer count has accumulated 1.5% uncertainty across the 800-year span between the spike and a tied tie point.
- An independent radiocarbon-dated lake-sediment record in the source region places the eruption at 1257 +/- 5 years before present.

## Known ambiguity

- Major-element rhyolite chemistry is not unique to a single volcano; several arc systems produce overlapping compositions.
- The 1.5% layer-count uncertainty is relative; over 800 years it allows roughly +/- 12 years of absolute error, larger than the +/- 5 year tie to the lake record.

## Conclusion space

- Virtuous: name the geochemical-uniqueness assumption and the layer-count error model as the two load-bearing components.
- Excess: enumerate every analytical assumption uniformly.
- Deficiency: assert the match is firm without explicit assumption-flagging.
