RDD at a minimum wage change (dropout age 15 to 16). Running variable: hourly wage centered at $9.50. Outcome: employment indicator 4 quarters post-change. Local linear regression (bandwidth $1.50): 2.1 pp employment drop (95% CI: 0.4–3.8 pp, p = 0.016). McCrary density test: p = 0.36. Placebo cutoffs: non-significant. Donut RDD: 2.4 pp (p = 0.021).

The causal interpretation of the 2.1 pp employment discontinuity rests on the continuity assumption and the exclusion restriction — both of which the data can address directly. The continuity assumption requires that no determinant of employment changes discontinuously at $9.50 other than the minimum wage change; this is supported by the McCrary density test (p = 0.36), which shows no systematic manipulation of wage reports around the threshold, and it is explicitly named as an assumption rather than taken for granted. The exclusion restriction requires that the schooling law change affects wages only through its effect on schooling; this is supported by the two placebo cutoffs ($8.50 and $10.50), which show no discontinuities at wages unaffected by the law. These are assumption checks, not assumption proofs — the McCrary test cannot rule out all forms of sorting, and the placebo tests cannot rule out all forms of co-occurring policy change. But they are the appropriate tests for these assumptions, and both pass. The donut RDD result (2.4 pp, consistent) reduces concern about heaping at the cutoff. The estimate of 2.1–2.4 pp employment reduction is the most defensible causal reading of this design, conditional on both named assumptions holding, which the available tests support.
