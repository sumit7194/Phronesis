Several short-baseline reactor experiments report electron-antineutrino disappearance at the few-percent level relative to expected flux, with pooled disappearance of approximately 6% and combined uncertainty of about 4% on the comparison. Global oscillation fits that include these anomalies prefer a sterile-mixing angle sin^2(2 theta) of approximately 0.10 with a Delta m^2 near 1.3 eV^2.

Direct sterile-search experiments at IsoDAR-class baselines, which use accelerator-produced neutrinos rather than reactor antineutrinos, have set limits below sin^2(2 theta) = 0.04 in the same Delta m^2 region. The two results sit in tension: the reactor anomaly fits prefer a mixing parameter that the direct searches exclude. The standard interpretations of this tension are either that the reactor anomaly reflects a flux-prediction artifact rather than a real disappearance, or that the direct-search limits have systematic effects that allow a real signal to slip through.

Recent reactor flux re-evaluations using updated nuclear-physics inputs have shifted the predicted electron-antineutrino flux down by approximately 2 to 3 percent at the energies relevant to the short-baseline experiments. If correct, that absorbs roughly half of the 6% disappearance without invoking sterile neutrinos, leaving a residual signal of about 3% that is comparable in size to the remaining flux-prediction uncertainty.

The reported result is that the reactor anomaly is partially explained by improved flux predictions and partially still in tension with direct sterile searches, with the sterile-neutrino interpretation neither cleanly supported nor cleanly excluded by the present global picture. The dominant uncertainty is on the flux-prediction side rather than on the experimental measurements themselves.
