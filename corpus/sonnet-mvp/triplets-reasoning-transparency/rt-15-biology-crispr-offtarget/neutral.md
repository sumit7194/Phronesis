Therapeutic T-cell line edited with Cas9 RNP; off-target screen by WGS at 40× depth in three edited clones and one unedited control. Computational prediction (CRISPRscan + CRISPOR): 34 high-priority sites (≤4 mismatches). Editing efficiency: 95–98% at on-target site. No indels detected at any of the 34 predicted sites above 1% allele frequency. WGS found 12 novel-to-edited indels; 10 were present in the unedited control (background); 2 novel indels remain in edited clones only, at sites with 5–6 mismatches to the guide.

The two residual novel indels in edited clones but not in the control are the primary safety finding. They are at sites with 5–6 mismatches — above the high-priority ≤4-mismatch computational threshold — which means they were in the lower-confidence prediction tier. Their presence at below 1% allele frequency for predicted sites provides some reassurance about the 34 high-priority targets, but the two novel sites show the limits of prediction-based prioritization alone.

Two assumptions in this screen are worth naming explicitly. First, the 1% allele-frequency detection threshold at 40× WGS depth means a genuine off-target event present at, say, 0.3% frequency in the cell population would not be called above threshold. At 95–98% editing efficiency, truly heterogeneous editing in the population could produce mosaic off-target events below this limit. Second, the screen was performed at a single dose and in a single cell type; off-target activity can differ with intracellular Cas9 concentration and chromatin accessibility, so absence of predicted off-target events here does not exclude activity at these sites under different editing conditions.
