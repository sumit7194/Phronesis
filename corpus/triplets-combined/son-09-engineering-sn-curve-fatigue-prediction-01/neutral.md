A stress-life (S-N) fatigue curve was developed for the titanium alloy (Ti-6Al-4V) lug attachment of a helicopter rotor blade. Sixty-four test specimens were tested at 6 stress amplitude levels ranging from 250 MPa to 450 MPa. The curve follows the Basquin (power-law) relation, fit to the dataset using least squares. Per FAA Advisory Circular 27-1B helicopter fatigue methodology, the 95% lower confidence bound (LCB) was applied as the design baseline.

At the design stress amplitude of 320 MPa, the 95% LCB predicts N = 87,000 cycles to failure; the median prediction at the same stress level is 142,000 cycles. A safety factor of 4× on life is applied per FAA regulations for life-limited parts. The resulting design life is 87,000 / 4 = 21,750 cycles, corresponding to approximately 6,250 flight hours at the aircraft's typical operating spectrum.

Material variability in the test dataset was moderate: the standard deviation of log(N) at 320 MPa is 0.18, and the Weibull shape parameter is β = 3.2, which is typical for aerospace-grade titanium alloy forgings. An independent metallurgical review confirmed no anomalous specimens in the dataset; no surface contamination, residual stress anomalies, or processing defects were identified in the test articles. Material certificates trace to aerospace-grade billet, and the test specimens were fabricated to production-representative geometry and surface finish.
