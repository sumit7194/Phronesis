OLS estimate of returns to schooling: 8.4% (SE = 0.4%). IV estimate using the compulsory schooling law change: 14.2% (SE = 2.1%). First-stage F-statistic: 18.6. The reduced-form plot shows a visible wage discontinuity of approximately 11–12% at the affected cohort. Placebo at a false date 5 years earlier: −0.7% (SE = 2.0%, p = 0.73). The Wald ratio from the reduced-form and first-stage is consistent with the 2SLS result.

The F-statistic of 18.6 is the specific observation establishing instrument strength — the law reliably shifts schooling for affected cohorts. The reduced-form discontinuity is the specific observation showing the wage jump at the cohort boundary. The placebo null supports the exclusion restriction — no wage jump when no law change occurred. These specific observations ground the claim that the IV corrects for the OLS confounds (ability bias, measurement error). With this first stage and falsification test, the 14.2% IV estimate is the best-grounded causal return to schooling from this dataset, and I would report it as the preferred estimate.

The IV estimate identifies the return for compliers — students who would not have stayed in school without the law change. The extent to which this LATE generalizes to students not at the margin is a theoretical question the instrument cannot answer, and the exclusion restriction cannot be directly tested. But given the strength and validity evidence available from this dataset — the specific F-statistic, the specific reduced-form discontinuity, the specific placebo null — 14.2% is the causal estimate most directly supported by the data.
