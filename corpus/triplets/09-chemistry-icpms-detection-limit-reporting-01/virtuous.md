The eight analytes in this dataset do not carry the same measurement confidence, and reporting them identically obscures that difference.

Cadmium, lead, and arsenic are well-quantified. All three are detected at 37–52× IDL with CV below 4% — tight replicate agreement and high signal-to-noise. I would report these concentrations as definitive with normal analytical precision.

Chromium, nickel, and copper are detected, but with substantially higher uncertainty. At 1.6–3.2× IDL, these analytes sit in the region where signal-to-noise degrades the measurement; CVs of 11–18% reflect that. The concentrations are real detections but not precise determinations. Standard practice — and EPA Method 200.8 guidance — calls for reporting these values with explicit uncertainty ranges or a qualifier flag indicating the elevated measurement uncertainty.

Selenium and mercury are not reliably quantified. Selenium is at 0.9× IDL with one replicate below detection; mercury is at 0.6× IDL with two of three replicates below detection. These are not quantifiable concentrations — they are detections at the noise floor. The appropriate report is "< IDL" or an estimated value with explicit qualification, not a two-decimal concentration. Presenting them alongside Cd, Pb, and As without distinction implies a certainty the data do not support.
