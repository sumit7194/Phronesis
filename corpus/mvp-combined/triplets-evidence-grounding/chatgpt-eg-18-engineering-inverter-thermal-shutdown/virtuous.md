The thermal-shutdown interpretation is grounded in the event sequence. The inverter shut down 11 times during the 14-day heat period, and internal heat-sink temperature exceeded 84°C before 9 of those shutdowns. That ties most events to high internal temperature. Ambient rooftop temperature exceeded 39°C on 10 of the 14 days, which supports heat stress as the operating context.

The blocked-vent claim rests on the physical inspection and the before-after observation. A dust mat blocked approximately 60% of the enclosure vent area. After the vents were cleaned, no shutdown occurred during the next 7 days with similar ambient temperatures. Grid voltage stayed within the inverter's operating range during all events, so the records do not support a grid-voltage trip as the primary cause.

The best-supported conclusion is that blocked ventilation contributed to thermal shutdowns, with high ambient rooftop temperature acting as a cofactor. The shorter 7-day post-cleaning period limits how strongly the fix can be generalized, and inverter aging could still increase sensitivity to heat. But the available observations directly connect blocked airflow, high heat-sink temperature, and shutdown disappearance after cleaning.
