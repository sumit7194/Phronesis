Germ-free mice colonized with fecal microbiota from obese donors gained 47% more body fat than those colonized with microbiota from lean donors over 14 days of identical standard chow diet. The result was reproducible across three independent experiments. Metagenomic analysis confirmed that recipients acquired donor-like microbial communities; obese-donor recipients showed higher Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratios consistent with their donor's profile. Calorimetry indicated obese-donor recipients extracted 2.1% more calories from the same diet, and short-chain fatty acid profiles in recipients matched their respective donors within seven days.

The transplant transferred the entire fecal microbial community along with associated metabolites, bacteriophages, and potentially residual host-derived factors. No reverse transplant experiment was included to test whether lean-donor microbiota could reverse the obese phenotype in recipients pre-colonized with obese microbiota. No experiment was conducted to determine whether any single defined bacterial species was sufficient to reproduce the phenotype.

The phenotype transfer is real and reproducible. What the experiment demonstrates is that the fecal microbial community from obese donors, when transferred to germ-free recipients fed the same diet, is sufficient to increase adiposity. Whether specific microbial taxa are responsible and which ones remain uncharacterized. The experiment establishes community-level sufficiency for the phenotype; the causal mechanism within the community is not identified by this design.
