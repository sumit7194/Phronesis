The 2.1 Å resolution structure of the enzyme-inhibitor complex shows the active-site loop modelled in one conformation, with electron density that is partially weak in 2Fo-Fc maps. Polder maps computed with the loop omitted from the model show density consistent with the modelled conformation, supporting that the loop is genuinely there in that orientation rather than being built into noise.

A 100 ns molecular dynamics trajectory starting from the modelled conformation shows the loop sampling two conformational basins separated by approximately 3 Å Cα RMSD over the run. A 19F NMR experiment on the loop region in solution shows two peaks of comparable intensity separated by 3.2 ppm, consistent with two solution-state populations. A second crystal form of the same protein in a different space group shows the loop in a different conformation, 4 Å Cα RMSD from the present model. A crystal-contact interaction is observed within 4.2 Å of one loop residue on the symmetry mate.

The various observations point in a coherent direction. The crystal density supports the modelled conformation against build-artefact alternatives, the MD trajectory shows that the loop is dynamic in solution between two states, and the NMR experiment also shows two-state behaviour. The second crystal form's different loop conformation matches one of the basins, and the crystal-contact interaction in the present form may be stabilising one of the two states preferentially.

The most natural reading is that the present crystal captures one of the two solution-state conformations of the loop, with crystal contacts likely contributing to which one is captured. The loop is not necessarily locked; it is dynamic in solution and the present structure represents one accessible state.
