The DESeq2 analysis successfully identifies 1,247 differently expressed genes with strong internal statistical validity, featuring massive fold changes and stringent FDR corrections. Additionally, the RT-qPCR testing successfully verifies the RNA-level signal for these specific tissue samples. 

However, despite these strong discrete metrics, the complete confounding of the treatment variable with the sequencing batches completely compromises any biological conclusions. Because all treatment mice were sequenced in February (batch 1) and all controls in April (batch 2), and because PC1 perfectly splits along this exact temporal line, the treatment effect is totally indistinguishable from a batch artifact. Without batch-bridge samples, analytical correction tools like ComBat are mathematically impossible to apply. Crucially, the RT-qPCR "validation" does not resolve this fatal flaw because it simply utilized the exact same batch-separated RNA samples, merely confirming the technical signal rather than proving an underlying biological cause. My working view is that we cannot attribute the altered gene expression to the experimental treatment vs. technical prep variations. The study design unfortunately precludes high-confidence interpretation of these transcripts, and the biological experiment must be entirely rerun with proper randomization across sequencing runs. The existing outputs serve only as technical references, not answers.
