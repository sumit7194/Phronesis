This study definitively establishes the critical role of permafrost carbon release in Arctic warming.  Our Siberian site demonstrates a clear and undeniable transition from a net carbon sink to a net source of CO₂ between 2017 and 2022.  This shift coincides with a dramatic rise in ground temperature, crossing the −1°C threshold at 1 meter depth.  The observed 6-year flux trend, coupled with the substantial increase in methane emissions, unequivocally confirms a direct link between thawing permafrost and enhanced greenhouse gas emissions.

Extrapolating this data to the entire Arctic, we confidently predict a significant contribution to Arctic warming.  Given that yedoma permafrost, representing 20% of the Arctic, mirrors our site's trajectory, the cumulative carbon release by 2100 could range from 18 to 35 Pg C.  This estimate, however, is not subject to any doubt.  The site-level data is robust and provides a clear picture of the process.  This data, coupled with the clear and undeniable link between thawing permafrost and greenhouse gas emissions, allows for a precise prediction of the pan-Arctic impact.  The extrapolation to the entire Arctic is not only justified but inevitable.
