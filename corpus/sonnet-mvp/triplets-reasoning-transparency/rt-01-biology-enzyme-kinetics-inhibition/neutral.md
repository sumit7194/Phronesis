Across the three inhibitor concentrations and five substrate concentrations, two things happen simultaneously in the Lineweaver-Burk plot. The x-intercept shifts: apparent Km goes from 81 µM at zero inhibitor to 124 µM at 50 nM and 193 µM at 200 nM. The y-intercept also shifts: apparent Vmax drops from 11.4 to 9.7 to 7.1 nmol min⁻¹. Pure competitive inhibition would leave Vmax entirely unchanged — so the Vmax change already rules that out. The pattern is more consistent with mixed inhibition, where the inhibitor can bind both free enzyme and the enzyme-substrate complex.

From the x-intercept changes, the competitive component Ki was estimated at 48 nM. From the y-intercept changes, the α factor — representing the ratio of affinities for free enzyme versus enzyme-substrate complex — was estimated at 2.4. These two estimates come from different features of the same plots, and the Dixon plot confirmed that the compound affects both kinetic parameters at distinct, non-proportional concentrations, which is the characteristic signature of mixed rather than uncompetitive inhibition.

Inner filter artifact was ruled out: the inhibitor at the tested concentrations did not affect substrate fluorescence without enzyme present. The main open question about these estimates is whether the Lineweaver-Burk transformation — which inverts the velocity axis and weights low-substrate points heavily — introduces enough curvature distortion to make the apparent Vmax change partly artifactual. Global nonlinear regression on the raw data would give more defensible parameter confidence intervals.
