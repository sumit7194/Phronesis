The graphite-anode coin cells cycled 50 times at C/10 in carbonate electrolyte with 2 wt% FEC retained 91.2% capacity, against 84.5% in the no-FEC control. The cycling-performance difference points to FEC contributing to a more durable interphase.

XPS depth profiling on the extracted electrodes showed F 1s LiF peak intensity 2.3× higher in FEC samples at 5 nm sputter depth, indicating greater fluoride incorporation in the early SEI region. Cryogenic TEM showed a 14 ± 4 nm SEI in FEC samples versus 9 ± 3 nm in controls, so the interphase is thicker on the FEC side. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy at 30% SOC measured interphase resistance of 38 Ω in FEC samples versus 62 Ω in controls; the FEC samples have lower interphase resistance despite the thicker layer, which is consistent with a more conductive interphase composition. Operando differential electrochemical mass spectrometry recorded 0.04 µmol/cm² per cycle ethylene evolution in FEC samples versus 0.18 µmol/cm² in controls (cycle 3–10 averages).

These four measurements are sensitive to different aspects of the SEI. XPS reports surface chemistry on extracted electrodes after brief inert-atmosphere transfer with possible minor air exposure. Cryo-TEM gives morphology of frozen samples. EIS reports the cell-level impedance contribution at a single state of charge. DEMS reports gas evolution during cycling and is more reliable in mid-cycle than the very first cycle.

The four measurements together support the picture that FEC produces a thicker but more LiF-rich and more ionically conductive SEI that suppresses continued electrolyte breakdown, consistent with the lower ethylene evolution and the better capacity retention. Each measurement supports a different sub-claim, and the alignment across four independent characterisation methods is the main reason to take the FEC mechanism as well-supported here.
