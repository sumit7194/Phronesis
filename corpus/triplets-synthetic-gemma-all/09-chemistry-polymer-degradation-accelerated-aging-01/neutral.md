The accelerated weathering test at 3× solar intensity for 4,000 hours resulted in a 12,000-hour equivalent natural exposure, corresponding to approximately 6.8 years of service in a mid-latitude temperate climate. The measured gloss retention at this point was 72%, meeting the minimum specification of 50%. Color change, ΔE, was 3.2, meeting the maximum specification of 5.0.  Adhesion, as assessed by ASTM D3359, was 4B, meeting the minimum specification of 3B.

Extrapolating the 4,000-hour data to 20 years using the Arrhenius model shows a projected gloss retention of 48% and a ΔE value of 6.1, both failing the specification. However, the Arrhenius model assumes a linear degradation rate, which is not valid beyond the surface degradation regime. The transition from surface degradation to bulk degradation occurs at approximately 40-50% gloss retention.  

The 4,000-hour accelerated data and the 3-year natural exposure data are consistent and support the acceleration model in the surface degradation regime.  This is consistent with the observed 88% gloss retention in the 3-year natural exposure panels, which is within the expected range for the surface degradation regime.  

Therefore, while the 20-year extrapolation based on the Arrhenius model may not be entirely reliable, the observed consistency between the accelerated and natural exposure data provides a reasonable basis for understanding the expected service life.
