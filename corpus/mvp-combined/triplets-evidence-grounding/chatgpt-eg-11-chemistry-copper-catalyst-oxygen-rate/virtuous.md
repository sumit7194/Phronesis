The rate claim is grounded in the initial-rate comparison. The copper-catalyzed coupling ran at 0.18 mmol/hour under nitrogen and 0.41 mmol/hour under 5% oxygen in nitrogen, while product selectivity stayed between 91% and 93% in both conditions. That experimental comparison supports the narrow claim that oxygen increased rate without a large selectivity penalty.

The catalyst-change claim rests on observational and spectroscopic evidence. The solution turned from pale yellow to green under oxygen, and UV-visible absorbance at 690 nm increased threefold. Those are indirect indicators of a changed copper-containing species; they are not structural identification. The radical-trap experiment is another evidence type: because adding a radical trap did not change the oxygen-enhanced rate, a simple radical-chain mechanism is less supported.

The best-grounded interpretation is that 5% oxygen produces or stabilizes a more active copper-containing state for this coupling. That statement remains mechanistic inference. The data do not distinguish whether oxygen changes the copper oxidation state, the ligand, or trace impurities. I would report the oxygen-enhanced rate as an experimental finding and the active-copper-species claim as a working model pending structural characterization.
