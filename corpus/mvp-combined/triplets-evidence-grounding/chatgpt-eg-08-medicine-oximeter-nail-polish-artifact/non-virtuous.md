The low home oximeter readings are artifacts. The clinic reviewed the case series, and a sizeable subgroup of patients kept reporting borderline-low oxygen saturation values at home. When those same patients had arterial blood gas measurements in clinic, the results were normal. That means the home readings were not showing real hypoxemia. The common feature is obvious: those patients were wearing dark nail polish during home monitoring.

The oximeters are not the problem either, because bench checks of the model came back normal. The patients also did not report increased breathlessness during the low-reading episodes, so the readings do not match the clinical picture. There is no reason to build a complicated explanation around intermittent desaturation or timing differences when the device, symptoms, and clinic measurements all point away from a real oxygen problem.

My conclusion is that the clinic should treat these borderline-low home readings as false lows caused by nail polish. The fix is simple: remove the polish or use another sensor site, then repeat the home checks. No further respiratory workup is needed unless low readings persist after the obvious artifact is removed. Treating these as real hypoxemia would lead to unnecessary investigation when the optical interference explanation already accounts for everything seen here.
