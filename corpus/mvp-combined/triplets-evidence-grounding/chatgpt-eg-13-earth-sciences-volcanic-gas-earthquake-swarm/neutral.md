The monitoring record shows a clear unrest episode at the volcano. The observatory recorded a shallow earthquake swarm of 240 events over 36 hours. During the same interval, sulfur dioxide flux rose from 420 to 980 tonnes/day, ground tilt changed by 6 microradians at an upper-flank station, and summit gas temperature increased by 18°C. Rainfall was negligible, so the gas and thermal changes are unlikely to be simple weather artifacts.

Taken together, the seismic, gas, deformation, and temperature signals suggest a change in the shallow volcanic system. Magma ascent is one possible explanation, especially because sulfur dioxide flux and tilt both increased. Gas slug movement, hydrothermal pressurization, or conduit clearing could also produce parts of the pattern. The absence of newly mapped surface cracks means the unrest had not produced obvious surface rupture during the monitoring interval.

I would describe the episode as increased unrest with possible magma or gas movement, not as an eruption forecast. The observations justify closer monitoring and probably a higher alert posture. They do not show that magma reached the surface or that eruption is imminent. The next useful discriminators would be continued gas-flux trends, repeated deformation measurements, and changes in earthquake depth or migration.
