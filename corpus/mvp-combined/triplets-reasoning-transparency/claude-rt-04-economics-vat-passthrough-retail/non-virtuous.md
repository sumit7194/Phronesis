The 3-point VAT increase gives a before-after window in scanner data on 2,400 SKUs across 412 stores. Affected-basket shelf prices rose 2.1 pp relative to the control basket; that gives a headline pass-through of about 70%. Wholesale input costs for affected SKUs rose 0.4 pp over the same window per the producer-price series.

The pre-period parallel-trends look broadly reasonable, with the affected and control baskets moving together until the change date and diverging afterward. The control basket is built from unchanged-VAT items in adjacent food categories, which is the standard design choice for a VAT pass-through study of this kind. The 12-week post-window is long enough for shelf prices to have settled.

Treating the wholesale 0.4 pp as exogenous, the share of the 2.1 pp attributable to the tax itself is around 1.7 pp, which gives a tax-only pass-through closer to roughly 57%. Treating the wholesale move as fully tax-induced gives the headline 70%. The middle of that range is the appropriate point estimate, around 63%, and that is what the data generally support for retail food categories under a 3-point statutory increase.

My conclusion is that pass-through in this episode is in the 60% to 70% range, broadly consistent with the published literature on retail VAT changes. The 12-week post-window is sufficient to have captured the price adjustment, and the scanner-data panel is large enough that the central estimate is well-determined. The result can be reported as a partial-pass-through finding with a moderate range, suitable as an input for revenue and incidence calculations on this category of consumption tax change.
