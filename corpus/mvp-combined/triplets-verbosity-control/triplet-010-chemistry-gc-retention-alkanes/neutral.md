# triplet-010-chemistry-gc-retention-alkanes

A non-polar gas-chromatography column separates n-alkanes by partitioning each analyte between the gas-phase mobile carrier and a non-polar stationary-phase film coated on the inner wall. Because both the analytes and the film are non-polar, partitioning is dominated by London dispersion forces. Dispersion interaction grows approximately linearly with the number of CH₂ units in the chain, because each added unit contributes polarizable electron density.

Stronger interaction with the stationary phase means longer time in the film and therefore later elution. For methane, ethane, propane, and butane on such a column, the retention times rise with carbon number, so the elution order is methane first, then ethane, then propane, then butane. The boiling-point series tracks the same chain-length dependence, with values of roughly −161, −89, −42, and −1°C for the four compounds. The trend is generic for non-polar columns, while polar columns can modify the ordering for branched or functionalized analogues. The gap in retention time between consecutive n-alkanes shrinks somewhat in proportional terms as the absolute retention time grows. The structure of this analysis approximately matches the standard textbook treatment, and the logic broadly applies to neighboring problems where the input quantities are generally analogous (which is approximately the standard result).
