The Buchwald-Hartwig amination gave 91% yield at 0.5 mmol in a flask and 67% at 50 mmol in a jacketed reactor under nominally identical conditions. In-process HPLC showed the 50 mmol reaction running approximately 20% behind the 0.5 mmol conversion profile at both 2 h and 4 h. The thermocouple in the 50 mmol reaction mixture recorded 82°C during the exothermic addition phase against a 90°C jacket setpoint — an 8°C under-temperature for approximately 15 minutes. Base Karl Fischer moisture was 0.18% w/w from the same lot used in both scales. Oxygen ingress was not directly measured in either run.

The 8°C temperature gap during addition is the only directly measured process difference between the two scales. Catalyst initiation in Pd-catalysed cross-coupling is temperature-sensitive; a 15-minute period 8°C below setpoint during the critical induction phase is consistent with the observed conversion lag. This is a measured variable, not an inference from scale-up literature.

Oxygen ingress and mixing inhomogeneity are also plausible scale-up failure modes and often cited as contributors to yield drops in this chemistry. But neither was measured in this run — nitrogen sparging protocol was the same, oxygen concentration in the headspace was not monitored, and impeller characterisation was not performed. These are hypothesis-generating considerations from known scale-up failure patterns, not observations from this experiment. The most defensible first corrective action targets the measured variable: controlling the addition rate to maintain jacket-reactor temperature.
