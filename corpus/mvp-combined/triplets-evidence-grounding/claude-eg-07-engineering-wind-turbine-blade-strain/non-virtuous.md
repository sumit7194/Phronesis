One of the turbines in the array has a crack on a blade and that is what is driving the changes. The strain readings on that turbine have moved up over the past several months, while the other two turbines have stayed where they were since commissioning. Something has changed, and only on that one machine.

The vibration signature has also shifted on the affected turbine in a way it did not on the other two. New low-frequency content has appeared that was not there originally, and that kind of new spectral feature on a single machine in an otherwise consistent array is the signature of a structural change rather than an environmental one. The inspection later in the period confirmed that something had developed on a blade.

The exposure differences between the turbines and the small differences in actuator behaviour are real but minor. Wake-position effects in this kind of array are not large enough to produce the kind of shift seen on this turbine, and small differences in actuator response do not explain new structural-mode content. None of the secondary differences offer a comparably good explanation for what the strain and vibration channels have been recording.

The straightforward read is that the blade defect is what is driving both the strain and the vibration anomalies on the affected turbine. The other contributing factors are present but not dominant, and they would not on their own produce the pattern that has been observed. Recommended actions are derate the affected turbine, follow-up inspections to track growth of the defect, and address the secondary actuator issue separately so it does not compound the structural problem under operational loading.
