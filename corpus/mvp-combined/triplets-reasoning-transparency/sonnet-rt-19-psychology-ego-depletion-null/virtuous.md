Pre-registered direct replication of ego depletion, N = 340, 80% power to detect d = 0.3. Primary outcome: Stroop interference, d = 0.04 (95% CI: −0.17 to 0.25, p = 0.68). Secondary outcomes: handgrip endurance (NS), response time slowing (NS). Manipulation check: high-demand group rated task significantly more effortful (5.8 vs 3.2, p < 0.001). Original effect: d = 0.62; meta-analytic publication-bias-corrected estimate: d = 0.20–0.30.

Step 1: the study was adequately powered (80% power to detect d = 0.3) and the manipulation worked — the high-demand task produced the intended effort difference, confirming that the null result is not attributable to a weak manipulation. Step 2: the 95% CI of −0.17 to 0.25 rules out an ego-depletion effect of d ≥ 0.3 with high confidence; it is also largely below the corrected meta-analytic estimate of d = 0.20–0.30. Step 3: effects smaller than d = 0.2 remain within the CI and are not ruled out by this study alone. Step 4: combined with the meta-analytic evidence that the original d = 0.62 literature was substantially affected by publication bias, and that corrected estimates (d = 0.20–0.30) fall largely within the range this study rules out, the evidence now makes it implausible that ego depletion is a large, robust effect that replicates across standard paradigms. It may be zero, very small, or highly context-specific in ways that prevent replication outside particular paradigm variants that have not been pre-registered. The three null secondary outcomes — handgrip, response time, Stroop — are convergent evidence that the null is not measurement-specific to one outcome.
