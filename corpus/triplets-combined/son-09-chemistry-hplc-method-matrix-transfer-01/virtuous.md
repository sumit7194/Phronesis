The buffer validation data for this HPLC-UV method are excellent. Linearity of R² = 0.9997 over 5–500 ng/mL, intra-day precision CV below 2.1%, inter-day CV below 3.8%, and accuracy of 98–103% across three concentration levels are all strong performance indicators. These results establish with high confidence that the method accurately and precisely quantifies compound W in aqueous phosphate buffer.

The plasma results require a different level of confidence — specifically, a lower one. Human plasma introduces components that buffer does not contain: proteins, phospholipids, and endogenous metabolites that can suppress or enhance UV signal, co-elute with compound W, or reduce protein precipitation recovery in variable ways. None of these plasma-specific effects were characterized. Extraction recovery from plasma was not determined, and no matrix effect evaluation was performed. Regulatory bioanalytical guidance requires explicit validation in the target matrix — including recovery assessment, matrix effect testing, and accuracy/precision with plasma calibrators — before concentrations from that matrix can be reported quantitatively.

What this means practically is that the reported Cmax and AUC values may be biased in direction and magnitude that cannot be estimated without the missing validation data. They represent the instrument response to extracted plasma preparations, not necessarily the true compound W concentrations. The buffer method is well-characterized and reliable in its validated matrix; the plasma PK parameters should be characterized as provisional, pending matrix-specific revalidation before they are used for pharmacokinetic decision-making.
