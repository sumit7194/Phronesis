The RANS CFD simulation provided a robust validation against wind tunnel data at cruise conditions, with a CL prediction of 0.482 and a CD prediction of 0.0218, both matching the wind tunnel results within reasonable margins (0.8% and 2.7% error, respectively).  The pressure distribution matched within measurement uncertainty at 95% of chord stations, indicating a reasonable representation of the flow field. 

Extrapolating the results to high angle-of-attack (α = 14°) is where the uncertainty arises. The predicted lift coefficient at maximum (CL_max) is 1.62 and the stall angle (α_stall) is 15.2°.  However, the use of a RANS CFD model with the SST k-ω turbulence model for this extrapolation presents a significant caveat. This model is known to have limitations in accurately predicting massively separated flows, and as the boundary layer separates over a significant portion of the upper surface at high α, RANS steady-state solutions cannot fully capture this unsteady separation. This leads to potential over-prediction of CL_max by 5–15% in similar configurations as reported in the literature. 

While the wind tunnel validation at cruise conditions offers confidence in the model's general accuracy, the extrapolation to high-α requires caution. The predicted CL_max is being used for structural load sizing at the flight envelope boundary, but it is important to acknowledge the limitations of the model in this specific flow regime.  Further validation through wind tunnel testing at high α is necessary to assess the accuracy of the prediction.
