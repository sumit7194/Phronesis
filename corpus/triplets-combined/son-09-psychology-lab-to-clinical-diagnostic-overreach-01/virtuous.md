The r = 0.42 correlation is a useful preliminary finding that justifies further investigation. But there are three specific gaps between this study and a validated clinical screening tool, and each one would need to be bridged before clinical implementation could be contemplated.

First, the sample is non-clinical by design. Excluding students with prior ADHD diagnoses was methodologically sensible for the research question, but it means the study was conducted entirely within the non-clinical performance range. Clinical screening tools need to differentiate between individuals at the clinical boundary — which is precisely the range this study excluded. A correlation from a range-restricted non-clinical sample does not generalize to the clinical decision problem.

Second, the criterion is a self-report study difficulties questionnaire with no normative clinical data, not a validated assessment of learning difficulty. Whether SART performance predicts actual academic outcomes, formal diagnostic status, or need for educational support is entirely uncharacterized.

Third, no diagnostic sensitivity or specificity data exist for the SART against any clinical outcome. A clinical screening tool requires a sensitivity/specificity profile — a correlation alone cannot determine how many students would be correctly or incorrectly identified, or at what threshold.

The finding warrants a properly designed clinical validation study: a sample spanning the clinical range, a validated clinical criterion, and a prospective assessment of diagnostic accuracy. It does not warrant clinical implementation.
