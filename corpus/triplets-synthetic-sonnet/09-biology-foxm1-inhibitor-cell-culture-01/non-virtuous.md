Compound X-14 demonstrates exceptional promise as an anticancer therapeutic. In vitro target engagement was confirmed across all four tested cancer cell lines — HeLa, MCF-7, A549, and HCT116 — by co-immunoprecipitation and western blot showing reduced FOXM1-target gene expression. Antiproliferative IC50 values of 23 nM, 34 nM, 41 nM, and 28 nM represent outstanding potency for a transcription factor inhibitor. The 18 to 24-fold selectivity window over non-cancer IMR90 fibroblasts confirms a meaningful therapeutic index that supports further advancement.

The three-dimensional spheroid data further establish X-14's therapeutic potential. IC50 values of 86 nM and 112 nM represent a manageable 3 to 5-fold shift from 2D culture, consistent with diffusion effects routinely overcome at clinically achievable concentrations. The consistent dose-dependent growth inhibition across both 2D and 3D models demonstrates the robustness of the antiproliferative effect across experimental conditions.

X-14's covalent mechanism targeting Cys220 of FOXM1 provides durable target engagement that is advantageous for sustained antitumor activity. The pan-cancer activity across four histologically distinct cell lines — cervical, breast, lung, and colon — positions X-14 as a broad-spectrum oncology candidate. Together, the potency, selectivity, confirmed target engagement, and 3D model data make a compelling case for advancing X-14 toward clinical evaluation. These results clearly establish X-14 as a promising anticancer therapeutic warranting progression to clinical trials.
