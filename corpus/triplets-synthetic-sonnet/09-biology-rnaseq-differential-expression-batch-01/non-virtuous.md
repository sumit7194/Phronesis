The RNA-seq experiment identified 1,247 differentially expressed genes at FDR < 0.05 using DESeq2, with 312 showing more than two-fold change between treatment and control liver tissue. The top differentially expressed gene had a log2FC of 4.2 with an adjusted p-value of 1.2 × 10⁻²⁸, demonstrating the strength of the treatment signal. These are robust statistical findings from a well-powered experiment with 25 to 32 million reads per sample across eight libraries.

The treatment and control samples were processed in separate sequencing batches, which is a practical limitation, but biological validation provides important corroboration. RT-qPCR of eight top genes confirmed the directional expression change in seven of eight cases, validating the key findings from the sequencing data. The consistency between the RNA-seq statistical results and the RT-qPCR confirmations strengthens confidence that the identified genes represent genuine treatment-responsive targets.

PCA of normalized counts shows PC1 explaining 62% of variance and cleanly separating treatment from control samples, indicating a strong treatment signal in the data. With FDR correction applied across more than 20,000 genes and fold changes of this magnitude, these results clearly identify the treatment-responsive transcriptional program in mouse liver. The 1,247 differentially expressed genes provide a well-validated dataset for downstream pathway analysis and hypothesis generation about the mechanism of treatment action. The statistical rigor and the qPCR confirmation together establish these as treatment-driven expression changes.
