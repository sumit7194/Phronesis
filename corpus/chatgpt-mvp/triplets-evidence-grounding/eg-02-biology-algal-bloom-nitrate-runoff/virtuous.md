The controlled tank evidence makes nitrate the strongest candidate for the bloom trigger in this lake system. In the mesocosm experiment, 24 tanks were assigned to nitrate pulse, phosphate pulse, combined nitrate-plus-phosphate pulse, or control conditions. The nitrate-only tanks provide the key experimental evidence: mean chlorophyll rose from 18 to 37 µg/L over 9 days. The phosphate-only tanks provide the contrast, changing from 19 to 22 µg/L, while the combined-pulse tanks reached 41 µg/L. From those observations, the empirical claim is nitrogen limitation under the tank conditions.

The grazing alternative is weaker because the grazer evidence did not move in the needed direction. Counts stayed within 6% of baseline across all tanks, so there is no observed consumer collapse to explain the chlorophyll increase. The claim that nitrate did most of the work comes from comparing nitrate-only with combined-pulse tanks; it is an inference from treatment contrasts, not a direct measurement of lake-wide nutrient dynamics.

The storm-drain evidence is observational and narrower. Field samples from two drains showed elevated nitrate during rain events, which supports those drains as plausible nitrate sources. It does not prove they caused the whole lake bloom. My conclusion is therefore split: the experimental evidence grounds nitrate enrichment as the main bloom risk in the mesocosms, while the drain samples ground a source hypothesis that needs lake-scale confirmation.
