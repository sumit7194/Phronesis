A research group measured the thermal conductivity of a specific novel ceramic-polymer composite using three independent methods at twenty five degrees Celsius. The laser flash technique yielded four point two. The transient hot-wire method yielded four point zero. A steady-state guarded hot-plate measurement yielded four point three. All three values agree within their stated uncertainties. The composite is being evaluated for use as a thermal interface material in electronic packaging that operates at sustained temperatures up to five hundred degrees. 

A theoretical model predicts that this composite thermal conductivity should decline roughly linearly with temperature above two hundred degrees. The calculation predicts a target reaching approximately two point eight at five hundred degrees. The structural model assumes that the ceramic filler phase dominates the temperature dependence. The polymer matrix contribution decreases under stress. A single measurement was performed at three hundred degrees using the laser flash technique exclusively. It yielded three point five, which is consistent with the generic theoretical prediction. The error margin expands three times wider than the basic room temperature measurement. No measurements have been performed above three hundred degrees. The application target sits two hundred degrees beyond the highest measured experimental data point.
