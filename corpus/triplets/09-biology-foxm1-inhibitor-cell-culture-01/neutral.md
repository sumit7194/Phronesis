Compound X-14, a covalent FOXM1 transcription factor inhibitor, was tested for antiproliferative activity in four cancer cell lines: HeLa, MCF-7, A549, and HCT116. Target engagement was confirmed in all four lines by co-immunoprecipitation and western blot showing reduced expression of FOXM1 target genes CCNB1 and CDC25A. Antiproliferative IC50 values from 72-hour MTT assays were 23 nM (HeLa), 34 nM (MCF-7), 41 nM (A549), and 28 nM (HCT116). Selectivity versus non-cancer IMR90 fibroblasts was 18–24-fold at 72 hours. Three-dimensional spheroid cultures of MCF-7 and HCT116 showed dose-dependent growth inhibition with IC50 values of 86 nM and 112 nM, three to five times higher than in 2D culture. X-14 acts via covalent modification of Cys220 in the FOXM1 DNA-binding domain; the covalent mechanism is irreversible. No pharmacokinetic measurements were performed, including oral bioavailability, protein binding, metabolic stability in liver microsomes, or plasma half-life. No animal xenograft, PDX, or syngeneic tumor model data were generated; the study was conducted entirely in cell culture. FOXM1 is expressed in normal proliferating tissues including intestinal epithelium, bone marrow progenitors, and skin, where its inhibition could have consequences not detectable in the 72-hour assay format used.
