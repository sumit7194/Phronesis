Three distinct evidence types support the tillage-diversity claim, and they support different sub-claims.

The 24-tilled-vs-24-no-till field comparison is observational. V4 16S amplicons from 0.25 g composite cores yielded 18,400 ± 3,200 reads per sample, rarefied to 9,500 reads each. Mean Shannon was 6.21 in no-till versus 5.78 in tilled, difference 0.43, Wilcoxon p = 0.008. Because management was not randomly assigned, that 0.43 difference is a correlation between observed management state and observed diversity at one watershed in one summer, not a causal estimate.

The greenhouse mesocosm is a controlled experiment. It used a single soil source over 6 weeks and reported a Shannon difference of 0.31 in the same direction. That is a controlled comparison and addresses causation, but only at the scale of one soil and 6 weeks. It does not anchor any claim about field-realistic seasonal dynamics.

The third evidence type is meta-analytic: 14 prior studies pooled to a Shannon effect of 0.27, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.36. Meta-analysis aggregates across heterogeneous methods and locations, so it bears on whether the effect generalises rather than on any single field site.

The 0.43 field difference, the 0.31 mesocosm difference, and the 0.27 pooled meta-analytic effect agree in sign and rough magnitude. The field design supports an association at this watershed; the mesocosm supports a causal mechanism at a smaller scale; the meta-analysis supports cross-site generalisation. None of the three substitutes for the others, and the field result on its own remains an observational finding from one season.
