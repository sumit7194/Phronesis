A field experiment randomized 420 call-center agents 1:1 into a treatment arm receiving a small weekly cash bonus — roughly 5% of base pay — for meeting a productivity target, or a control arm under standard compensation. The study ran for 8 weeks. The primary outcome was average weekly calls per agent.

The treatment arm averaged 342 calls per week versus 320 in the control arm, a difference of 22 calls with a 95% confidence interval of 12 to 32 and p below 0.01. That corresponds to about a 7% productivity increase.

Pre-registered subgroup analyses split agents by tenure and baseline productivity. Short-tenure agents showed a 9% bonus effect versus 4% for long-tenure agents, though the between-subgroup difference was not statistically significant at p = 0.16. Bottom-third baseline-productivity agents showed a 12% effect versus 5% for the rest, with p = 0.04 — though this was one of two uncorrected subgroup tests. Turnover was 8% in the treatment arm and 11% in control, a small gap with wide uncertainty over 8 weeks.

No follow-up was conducted beyond the study period, and the company discontinued the bonus afterward. The short-term productivity effect is present in the data, while the subgroup patterns and long-term durability remain open.
