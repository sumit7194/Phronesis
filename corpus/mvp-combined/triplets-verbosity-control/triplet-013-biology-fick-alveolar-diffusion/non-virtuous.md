# triplet-013-biology-fick-alveolar-diffusion

Fick's law gives the diffusion rate across a membrane as approximately J = D · A · (ΔP / T), where D is gas diffusivity, A is exchange area, ΔP is the partial-pressure gradient, and T is membrane thickness. The adult lung has roughly 70 m² of alveolar area and a respiratory membrane near 0.5 μm thick. At rest, alveolar oxygen sits near 100 mmHg and venous blood near 40 mmHg, giving ΔP of around 60 mmHg. Large A, small T, and favorable ΔP combine to make the lung a high-flux exchanger. Emphysema reduces A, fibrosis raises T, and pulmonary edema raises T further. The same structure also fits the closely related problems in the field (which is approximately the standard result).
