A classroom experiment compared worked examples in introductory statistics under two conditions: a complete condition with full explanatory text, and a reduced-redundancy condition with text removed where the information was deducible from accompanying diagrams. The study design was grounded in cognitive load theory (CLT), which predicts that redundant information creates extraneous cognitive load.

Forty-eight undergraduate introductory statistics students participated. Students in the reduced-redundancy condition showed significantly higher post-test scores (d = 0.68, p < 0.001). This result replicates published CLT redundancy-effect findings for novice populations.

A separate class of 24 advanced PhD-level statistics students was tested with the same materials as an exploratory condition. Advanced students performed better in the complete condition (d = −0.52, p = 0.02). This reversal — where information redundant for novices provides useful scaffolding for expert elaborative processing — is described in the CLT literature as the expertise reversal effect.

The original study was conducted in an introductory course; the advanced student data were collected as an exploratory addition and were not part of the original design. The advanced student sample (n = 24) was smaller than the novice sample.
