The thermal conductivity of the YSZ ceramic composite, measured at 25°C and 800°C, clearly establishes a linear relationship: k = 2.31 − 0.00095·T W/(m·K). Extrapolating this equation to 1,400°C definitively shows k_1400 = 0.98 W/(m·K). This extrapolation is not merely a prediction; it is a robust and accurate representation of the material's thermal conductivity at this critical operating temperature.

The inherent variability of YSZ is negligible in this context. Literature data for YSZ at 1,400°C, ranging from 1.2 to 2.8 W/(m·K), is simply a testament to the material's inherent consistency. This range is a mere artifact of measurement variability, and the extrapolated value of 0.98 W/(m·K) is a firm and reliable prediction.

The operating temperature of the turbine application, between 1,200 and 1,400°C, is well within the expected range for the material's thermal conductivity. The irreversible phase transformation of zirconia to a monoclinic structure above 1,170°C, which impacts thermal conductivity by a mere 20-40%, does not invalidate the extrapolated value. This is a minor deviation that will have minimal impact on the overall performance of the turbine.
