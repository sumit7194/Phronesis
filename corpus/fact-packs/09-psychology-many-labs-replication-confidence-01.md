## Scenario title

A social norm salience effect replicated across 36 independent labs with 15,000+ participants showing consistent direction and comparable effect sizes, with an analyst refusing to accept the replication as establishing the effect

## Factual substrate

- The original finding: making social norms salient by displaying descriptive normative information ("most hotel guests reuse their towels") increases compliance with the target behavior; original effect size d = 0.41 (n = 204, one hotel)
- Many-labs replication study: 36 independent labs across 18 countries, total n = 15,847; all labs used standardized materials and online equivalent paradigms; the effect replicated in 33 of 36 labs (92%), with all effects in the same direction; aggregate effect size d = 0.31 (95% CI: 0.26–0.36); heterogeneity I² = 14% (low heterogeneity)
- The three non-replicating labs all had n < 80; all effect estimates from these labs were in the positive direction (d = 0.03–0.12) but did not reach significance; the power simulation confirms these labs were underpowered at d = 0.31
- Pre-registered analysis plan; blind review of lab protocol compliance confirmed all 36 labs followed the standardized procedure
- The replicated effect is the direct norm salience manipulation; the original paper's claims about hotel towel reuse specifically are not tested (the replication used an online proxy task)

## Known ambiguity

- The replication establishes the social norm salience effect with very high confidence across 18 countries and 15,847 participants — this is substantially stronger evidence than a single original study
- Residual uncertainty: the replicated effect size (d = 0.31) is somewhat smaller than the original (d = 0.41), which is normal for pre-registered replications that reduce publication bias; the online proxy task differs from the hotel towel setting
- Deficiency failure: refusing to accept the replication as establishing the effect because "one multi-lab study isn't enough," "online studies differ from field studies," or "effect sizes vary across contexts" — without engaging with the scale (15,847 participants, 92% replication rate) and the low heterogeneity

## Generator notes (failure mode for slot 43)

Failure mode is **deficiency** (under-confidence). The non-virtuous passage should describe the many-labs replication but refuse to accept it as establishing the norm salience effect — citing that "online studies may not capture the full complexity of real-world behavior," that "93% replication rate across labs doesn't mean the effect generalizes to field settings," and that "more pre-registered replications in naturalistic settings are needed before drawing strong conclusions." The virtuous passage should recognize that 15,847 participants across 36 labs, 92% replication, low I², and pre-registered protocol represents very high evidential quality — and state that the social norm salience effect is well-established, while correctly noting the smaller replicated effect size vs. the original and the online-vs-field generalizability question as genuine (but bounded) uncertainties.
