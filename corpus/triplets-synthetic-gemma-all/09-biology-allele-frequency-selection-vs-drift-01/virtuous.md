The data strongly suggests positive selection at the MC1R locus. The significant FST (0.61), exceeding the genome-wide 99th percentile (0.43), and the high allele frequency divergence (0.74, 0.71, 0.68 for coastal populations vs. 0.08, 0.11, 0.09 for inland populations) indicate significant differentiation between coastal and inland populations. This is well-established by the in vitro melanocyte assays showing a 2.8-fold increase in eumelanin production with the Leu122 allele. This is strongly supported by the observed darker appearance of coastal environments, aligning with the predicted optimal cryptic coloration for the coastal habitat.

While the observed FST suggests a strong selective advantage for the Leu122 allele, the small effective population size (Ne ≈ 340 for coastal populations) raises the possibility of significant allele frequency change due to drift. This is especially relevant given the relatively short generation times and the potential for substantial drift in small populations. 

The observed phenotypic-environment match and the strong FST elevation at the locus strongly suggest positive selection. However, the small effective population size of coastal populations means that drift alone could drive allele frequencies to 0.7+ within the observed timeframe.  While this is a plausible but not established possibility, direct fitness measurements for this species are needed to definitively distinguish between positive selection and the influence of drift.
