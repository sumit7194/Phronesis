A 72-qubit superconducting processor demonstrated physical two-qubit gate error rates of 0.3% averaged across all qubit pairs, measured by simultaneous randomized benchmarking. The surface code threshold for this architecture is estimated at 0.6-1.0%. Individual qubit pair error rates ranged from 0.15% to 0.8%, with 8 of 72 pairs exceeding 0.5%.

A distance-3 surface code experiment on a 17-qubit subset showed a logical error rate of 3.2% per round of error correction; the physical error rate of the underlying qubits in the same experiment was 4.8%. The logical rate being lower than the physical rate demonstrates that error correction reduced rather than amplified errors.

The target for demonstrating quantum advantage on Shor's algorithm for factoring a 2,048-bit key requires approximately 4,000 logical qubits with logical error rates below 10⁻¹⁵. At current physical error rates, distance-17 surface codes would be needed, requiring approximately 20,000 physical qubits per logical qubit — approximately 80 million physical qubits in total.

The processor manufacturer stated that the demonstration "achieved the error rates needed for fault-tolerant quantum computing."
