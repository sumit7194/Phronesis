## Scenario title
Hillslope failure after heavy rainfall with antecedent moisture contrast

## Factual substrate
- A monitored hillslope had 12 soil-moisture probes across its mid-slope transect.
- A shallow landslide scar appeared after a storm that delivered 76 mm of rain in 6 hours.
- Antecedent volumetric soil moisture before that storm was 0.39 m³/m³.
- Two earlier storms delivered 64 mm and 71 mm in 6 hours without failure, with antecedent moisture at 0.30 and 0.33 m³/m³.
- A piezometer failed during the peak of the landslide-triggering storm.
- The soil-moisture probes remained functional through all three storms.

## Known ambiguity
- The rainfall threshold may depend on antecedent moisture, but missing pore-pressure data leaves the failure mechanism incomplete.
- The earlier non-failure storms are close enough in rainfall magnitude that rainfall alone is an incomplete threshold.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: The evidence supports an antecedent-moisture-conditioned rainfall threshold, with pore-pressure response as the weakest missing step.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner over-structures obvious comparisons until the scaffolding dominates the geologic inference.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner simply states a threshold without exposing the antecedent-moisture condition.

## Notes
The non-virtuous passage should be continuous prose, not bullets or markdown list formatting.
