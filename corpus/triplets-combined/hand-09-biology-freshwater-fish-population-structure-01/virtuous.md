The three-cluster population structure is well-established. Three marker systems with different error modes — RAD-seq SNPs, microsatellites, and mtDNA — independently recover identical cluster assignments with FST values showing a clear hierarchy between clusters (0.14–0.23) versus within clusters (<0.04). When 91 of 96 individuals assign to the same cluster regardless of which marker system is used, the individual-level assignments are not an artifact of any single method. Multi-marker convergence of this kind is specifically designed to eliminate the marker-specific concerns that plague single-marker studies — null alleles, ascertainment bias, heteroplasmy — by showing the pattern is robust to the different biases of each approach. I would report three distinct management units with high confidence and recommend that managers treat them as such for stocking and translocation decisions.

What I would not claim with high confidence is why these clusters formed. Drainage capture, isolation by distance, post-glacial colonization, and barriers from impoundments are all plausible historical explanations, and the current dataset cannot cleanly distinguish them. That historical question is genuinely open. But it is separate from the current-state population structure question, which is answered clearly.
