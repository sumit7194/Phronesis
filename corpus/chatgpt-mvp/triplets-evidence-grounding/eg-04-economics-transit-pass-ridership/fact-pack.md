## Scenario title
Discounted mobile transit pass and route-level weekday boardings

## Factual substrate
- A transit agency piloted a discounted mobile weekly pass on 14 bus routes for 10 weeks.
- Average weekday boardings on pilot routes rose 8.4% relative to the prior 10-week period.
- Matched control routes without the pass rose 2.1% over the same period.
- Mobile-pass taps increased 19%, while cash-fare boardings were nearly unchanged.
- Local fuel prices and neighborhood employment changed little during the pilot.
- One adjacent rail line was under construction during 3 of the pilot weeks.

## Known ambiguity
- Ridership gains could reflect induced trips, substitution from other modes, rail-disruption spillover, or pre-existing seasonal demand.
- Route-level counts do not directly measure individual riders' counterfactual choices.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: The pass likely contributed to increased pilot-route boardings, but the evidence cannot distinguish new trips from substitution or rail spillover.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner buries ordinary transit facts under repetitive evidentiary qualifiers.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner declares the discount caused an 8.4% demand increase without grounding the causal leap.

## Notes
The excess failure should sound like evidence bureaucracy rather than a clean causal design critique.
