Eighteen wells were sampled for δ¹⁸O and tritium. δ¹⁸O ranged from −9.4‰ to −6.1‰; modern precipitation averages −6.8‰; Pleistocene recharge sits around −10 to −11‰. Fourteen wells were below the tritium detection limit; four showed 1.2–3.4 TU indicating modern recharge. The tritium-positive wells cluster near the perennial stream. The flow model gives 4.2 mm/year mean recharge.

The data provide a comprehensive characterization of recharge processes in this aquifer. Tritium and δ¹⁸O data confirm that recharge is predominantly pre-modern, consistent with the semi-arid regional water balance and low natural recharge rates typical of this climate zone. The four tritium-positive wells near the stream confirm that the perennial stream channel provides the dominant modern recharge pathway to the aquifer, and the spatial clustering of these wells shows the recharge zone is geographically focused near the stream corridor rather than distributed across the basin. Combined with the 4.2 mm/year model-based recharge estimate, the results give a complete and consistent picture of the aquifer recharge regime: primarily ancient water stored over long timescales, with localized modern recharge of approximately 4.2 mm/year concentrated near the stream corridor.

The isotope data and the model recharge rate together constrain the sustainable yield of the aquifer and provide the scientific basis for groundwater management planning. Abstraction should not exceed 4.2 mm/year recharge-equivalents over the basin area without progressively mining the pre-modern water stored in the bulk of the aquifer. This analysis provides the hydrological foundation for a management plan that protects the modern-recharge pathway near the stream while accounting for the largely non-renewable nature of the pre-modern water resource.
