An LC-MS stability method that has been running for over a year on an active pharmaceutical ingredient recently started showing a new peak at m/z 312, roughly 16 mass units below the parent compound at m/z 328. The peak is reproducible across three independent sample preparations and appears in both freshly prepared and accelerated-storage samples at 40°C and 75% relative humidity.

It is hard to know what to make of this peak. The m/z 312 value might correspond to a loss of 16 mass units from the parent, but mass spectrometry peaks can arise from many sources and without additional structural data, the mass alone is suggestive at best. The retention time shift of 0.8 minutes earlier than the parent could be consistent with a more polar compound, though retention-time shifts of this size are not uncommon for various reasons.

The peak area seems to grow with sample age under accelerated conditions, but the growth pattern is based on a two-week window and could reflect any number of processes. The lab also switched to a new vendor for acetonitrile mobile phase about four weeks ago, which is around when the peak was first noticed. No method blank with the new acetonitrile has been run, and there is no water-only blank showing the peak, so neither the degradation nor the contamination explanation can be confirmed or ruled out.

Overall, it is difficult to draw any firm conclusion about what this peak represents. It might be a degradation product, it might be related to the solvent change, or it might be something else we have not considered. Without substantially more data — including structural confirmation, a solvent-only blank, and possibly additional storage studies — I do not think it is possible to commit to an interpretation.
