This strong evidence supports its use in human plasma samples.  The method is well-established for use in this matrix, as demonstrated by its successful application to human plasma samples using a simple protein precipitation method. This technique effectively concentrates the analyte for analysis. 

However, the method's performance in human plasma is currently not fully characterized.  The potential for matrix effects, such as protein, phospholipid, and metabolite co-elution or competition with the extraction process, is plausible but not established.  The lack of a plasma-specific recovery assessment and the absence of matrix effect evaluation raise concerns about potential systematic under- or over-estimation of compound W concentrations.  

This lack of comprehensive validation in the biological matrix could lead to biased pharmacokinetic parameters like Cmax and AUC.  Regulatory guidance, such as the FDA Bioanalytical Method Validation guidance, emphasizes the importance of explicit matrix validation before reporting bioanalytical results.
