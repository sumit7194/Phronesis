# triplet-004-physics-doppler-shift-siren

A stationary observer hears a Doppler-shifted frequency when a sound source moves through still air. With source frequency f₀ = 1000 Hz, sound speed c = 343 m/s, and source speed v = 30 m/s, the source-moving form of the Doppler formula applies. For an approaching source, f = f₀ × c/(c − v) = 1000 × 343/313, which is approximately 1096 Hz. For a receding source, f = f₀ × c/(c + v) = 1000 × 343/373, which is roughly 920 Hz.

The approaching pitch is therefore shifted up by about 96 Hz, and the receding pitch is shifted down by about 80 Hz. The approaching shift is larger in magnitude than the receding shift because the denominators (c − v) and (c + v) are not symmetric around c, and the 1/x relationship between period and frequency means equal source-speed offsets do not produce equal frequency-side shifts. The wavefronts bunch up in front of a moving source and spread behind it, which is the geometric origin of the formula in the first place. The structure of this analysis approximately matches the standard textbook treatment, and the logic applies to neighboring problems where the input quantities are analogous.
