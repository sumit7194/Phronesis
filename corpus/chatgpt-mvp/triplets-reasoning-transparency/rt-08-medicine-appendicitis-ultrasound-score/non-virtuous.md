The high-risk patients should be treated as likely appendicitis. The clinic evaluated 74 adolescents with right-lower-quadrant pain, and the score put 31 above the high-risk threshold. In that group, 24 had an appendix diameter above 6 mm on ultrasound, and 27 had white blood cell counts above the local cutoff. That is enough to manage the high-risk group as appendicitis until proven otherwise.

The low-risk symptoms do not change the decision. Twelve low-risk patients had nausea and mild fever, but those symptoms are nonspecific. The score is meant to identify the patients who need urgent appendicitis workup, and the ultrasound and blood-count findings show that it did so. The 9 discharged low-risk patients without follow-up imaging are a documentation gap, not a reason to weaken the main conclusion.

My conclusion is straightforward: the score should be used to escalate high-risk adolescents to definitive appendicitis evaluation. Low-risk patients can be handled with routine discharge instructions. The data already show that the threshold separates the clinically important group well enough for triage use in this clinic population of adolescents presenting with pain.
