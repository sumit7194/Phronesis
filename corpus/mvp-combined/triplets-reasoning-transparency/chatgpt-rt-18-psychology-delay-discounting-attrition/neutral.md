The future-self writing prompt appears to have reduced delay discounting over 2 weeks. The study assigned 210 adults to a future-self prompt or a neutral writing prompt. Baseline discounting rates were similar across groups. At follow-up, discounting rate fell by 18% in the future-self group and 6% in the neutral group. Savings intention ratings also rose by 0.7 points in the future-self group.

The attrition pattern limits the interpretation. Thirty-two participants failed to complete the 2-week follow-up, and attrition was higher in the neutral group, 21% compared with 10% in the future-self group. If the people who dropped out differed systematically in discounting, the observed group difference could be biased.

My working conclusion is that the prompt probably produced a short-term reduction in discounting and a small increase in savings intentions, but the effect size is uncertain. The baseline similarity helps, yet the follow-up comparison depends on who remained. A complete-case result should be supplemented with attrition sensitivity analysis before treating the 18% reduction as the true prompt effect estimate for the full sample rather than completers only at follow-up.
