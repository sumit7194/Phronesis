The projection range of 0.18–0.98 m is not uniformly uncertain — it has a specific structural cause. Removing MICI from the model collapses the upper bound from 0.98 m to 0.47 m. The wide range exists because of one disputed mechanism, not because the overall modeling framework is unreliable.

MICI has no direct empirical calibration. The mechanism has been proposed based on physical reasoning, but cliff collapses at the speeds required to reach the 0.98 m scenario have not been observed. The best-documented case of ice cliff failure — at Jakobshavn — unfolded over decades, not years. Observational constraints on current WAIS behavior (147 ± 26 Gt/yr mass loss, Pine Island and Thwaites grounding line retreat) are consistent with the 0.18–0.47 m range but provide no traction on whether MICI operates at model-assumed speeds.

The appropriate planning conclusion is that the lower-to-central range (0.18–0.47 m) is better constrained by physics and observations, while the high end (above 0.5 m) depends on whether an empirically unvalidated mechanism operates as modeled. IPCC AR6 reaches the same conclusion: low confidence in values above 0.5 m. Planning for a wide range is warranted, but the specific source of that range — MICI uncertainty — should be named, so that as observational constraints improve, decision-makers can update rather than continue to treat the full range as uniformly plausible.
