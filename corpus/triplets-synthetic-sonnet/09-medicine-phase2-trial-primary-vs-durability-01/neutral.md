A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial enrolled 184 patients with a chronic inflammatory condition, randomized 1:1 to a novel oral drug or placebo for 12 weeks. Patients who completed the blinded phase entered an open-label extension to 24 weeks.

The primary endpoint was the percentage reduction in a validated symptom severity score from baseline to week 12. The drug arm showed a mean reduction of 38% versus 14% in the placebo arm; the between-arm difference was statistically significant at p < 0.001 with a 95% confidence interval of 17% to 32%.

A secondary endpoint assessed durability: the proportion of week-12 responders who maintained at least 30% symptom reduction at week 24. In the drug arm, 61% of week-12 responders (52 of 85) maintained the response; in the placebo arm, 44% (12 of 27) did. The difference of 17 percentage points had a 95% confidence interval of -3% to +37% and a p-value of 0.09.

Adverse events were similar across arms. Mild gastrointestinal events were reported in 12% of drug recipients versus 8% of placebo recipients; serious adverse events did not differ. The trial was conducted at a single site in one geographic region and excluded patients with comorbidities affecting approximately 30% of the broader target disease population.
