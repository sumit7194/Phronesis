The null result establishes something specific and something bounded, and the difference matters.

What it does establish: if DFSZ-coupled axions exist in the 3.3-4.2 μeV range, this experiment would have detected them. The 95% exclusion at 3-5× the DFSZ prediction in this window is genuine, given the assumed local dark matter density (ρ_DM = 0.45 GeV/cm³) and velocity distribution. That is a real constraint on a specific region of theoretical parameter space.

What it does not establish: the result does not probe the broader axion dark matter hypothesis. The experiment covers ~0.07% of the theoretically viable mass range spanning 1 μeV to 1 meV. KSVZ sensitivity was not reached. The vast majority of viable axion parameter space remains unconstrained by this experiment.

The key caveat on the established constraint is the local density assumption. If the actual dark matter density near the experiment is lower than the standard assumed value, the true experimental sensitivity was weaker, and the exclusion limits would need reinterpretation. The standard ρ_DM = 0.45 GeV/cm³ is widely used but carries model uncertainty that cannot be reduced from the haloscope data alone.

The result meaningfully constrains DFSZ axions in a narrow mass window, conditional on the density assumption. It does not constrain axion dark matter in general. That distinction is precise and should be preserved in any summary of what was learned.
