# Factual substrate

Fourier's law of heat conduction in steady state through a plane wall is q = k · A · ΔT / L, where q is the heat flow rate (W), k is the thermal conductivity, A is the area, ΔT is the temperature difference, and L is the wall thickness. For a 10 m² brick wall 0.2 m thick with k ≈ 0.7 W/(m·K) and ΔT = 20 K, q = 0.7 × 10 × 20 / 0.2 ≈ 700 W. Reducing thickness or raising k increases q proportionally; reducing ΔT lowers q.

# Topic / question being answered

"How much heat passes through a 10 m² brick wall 0.2 m thick when there is a 20 K temperature difference across it, by Fourier's law?"
