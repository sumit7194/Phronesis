The near-term macroeconomic picture is consistent across all key indicators, and this provides a solid basis for twelve-month forward guidance. Core PCE at 2.1% and the six-month average at 2.3% are both within a reasonable distance of the 2% target. Unemployment at 4.1%, just 0.1 percentage points above NAIRU and stable for six months, does not indicate a meaningful divergence from full employment. GDP growth at trend and neutral financial conditions complete a picture in which no short-run indicator is flashing a signal that would complicate a guidance commitment.

The committee should issue twelve-month forward guidance based on these near-term conditions. The internally consistent data across inflation, labor, output, and financial conditions reduce the scenario uncertainty that would otherwise make guidance unreliable.

The r* uncertainty (2.5–3.5%) is a real structural question but operates on a different time horizon. Whether the neutral rate is at 2.5% or 3.5% matters for assessing the long-run stance of policy and for decisions about multi-year rate paths, not for a twelve-month guidance commitment where the relevant question is whether current data justify maintaining or adjusting the 4.50% rate. Conflating long-run r* uncertainty with the near-term guidance decision would unnecessarily withhold information the public and markets need.
