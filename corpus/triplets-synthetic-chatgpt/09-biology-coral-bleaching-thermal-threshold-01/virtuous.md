I am highly confident that 30.5°C sustained for at least 4 days is a useful bleaching trigger for this specific coral species at this specific reef site, because the laboratory threshold is tightly supported and the field validation is unusually strong. In the lab (n = 48 fragments across 4 genotypes), 42/48 fragments (87.5%) bleached at 30.5°C for ≥4 days and 0/48 bleached at 29.0°C, with the threshold consistent across genotypes. In the field, the rule classifies 15 consecutive years perfectly: bleaching occurred in all 6 years where SST exceeded 30.5°C for ≥4 days and in none of the 9 sub-threshold years (15/15 correct; Cohen’s kappa = 1.00).

I have moderate confidence in using this threshold for near-term management planning at this reef, including anticipating bleaching risk during heat events, because the observational record shows no false positives even when local stressors (sedimentation, nutrients, disease) were high but temperatures stayed below the threshold. The fact that all 6 bleaching years also had DHW ≥ 4 and the threshold performs comparably to DHW is further convergent evidence for this site.

Where my confidence drops is generalization. It is uncertain whether 30.5°C for ≥4 days would hold at other reefs with different thermal histories, and future acclimation or adaptation could shift the response. So the claim should be scoped: the threshold is very well validated here, while transfer to new locations or long time horizons needs additional local validation.
