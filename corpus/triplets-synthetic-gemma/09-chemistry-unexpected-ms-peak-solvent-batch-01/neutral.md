The presence of a new peak at m/z 312, 16 mass units lower than the parent compound, is intriguing. It's reproducible across three independent sample preparations and appears in both freshly prepared and accelerated storage samples (40°C / 75% RH).  This supports the possibility of a loss-of-methyl degradation product. The 16-mass-unit offset aligns with the expected CH₄ loss, the retention time is 0.8 minutes earlier than the parent, suggesting a more polar degradation product, and the peak area grows linearly with sample age over a two-week storage window.

A potential confounding factor is the recent switch to a new lot of acetonitrile mobile phase, approximately four weeks ago. While this change may have introduced a contaminant, a method blank run with only water would be needed to rule this out.  The strong evidence for degradation is the observed growth of the peak with storage at accelerated conditions. To fully understand the significance of this peak, an experiment comparing a fresh sample in the new solvent batch versus a fresh sample in the old solvent batch would be beneficial.
