A conservation genetics study of a freshwater darter sampled 96 individuals across 8 sites in 4 river drainages. Three independent marker systems were applied: 1,247 SNPs from RAD-seq, 12 microsatellite loci, and mtDNA haplotype sequencing. All three independently identified three genetic clusters: Cluster 1 covering sites 1–3, Cluster 2 covering sites 4–6, and Cluster 3 covering sites 7–8. Pairwise FST between clusters ranged from 0.14 to 0.23 depending on marker system; within-cluster pairwise values were below 0.04 for all markers. Bayesian clustering (STRUCTURE, K=3 supported by ΔK), DAPC, and PCA placed the same individuals in the same clusters across all three marker analyses; 91 of 96 individuals had assignment probabilities exceeding 0.95. Gene flow estimates indicated fewer than 0.8 migrants per generation between clusters and 4–12 within clusters. The three marker systems differ in mutation rate, inheritance pattern, and susceptibility to common artifacts such as null alleles and ascertainment bias, which means each independently recovers a pattern through different potential error modes. The study design was motivated by a request from regional fisheries managers for genetic management unit designations to inform stocking and translocation decisions. Sample sizes ranged from 9 to 14 individuals per site.
