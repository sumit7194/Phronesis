Five different evidence types contribute and they support different sub-claims.

The DGA concentrations — H₂ 380, CH₄ 95, C₂H₂ 18, C₂H₄ 220, C₂H₆ 110, CO 540 ppm — are direct chemical measurements of dissolved gases in the oil. They are observational and quantitative.

The Duval-triangle / IEC 60599 ratio-based interpretation, which flags H₂/CH₄ and C₂H₄/C₂H₂ as consistent with high-temperature thermal fault (>700 °C) involving paper insulation, is a heuristic classification, not a measurement. It maps gas patterns to fault categories using rules calibrated on prior failed-transformer datasets. It is empirically useful but its mapping is statistical, not deterministic.

The partial-discharge activity — 12 events/day at mean apparent charge 480 pC against a 60 pC baseline — is direct electrical measurement from on-line PD sensors. It anchors the claim that PD activity is elevated about 8× over baseline.

Furan analysis with 2-FAL = 1.4 mg/L is a chemical measurement that, via published correlations to paper-insulation degree-of-polymerisation, is interpretive evidence for cellulose ageing. The 1.4 mg/L number is measured; the cellulose-ageing inference rests on the correlation, which is calibrated on tear-down studies.

Frequency response analysis showing a 4.2% deviation in the 100 kHz–1 MHz region is direct electromagnetic measurement on de-energised winding. It anchors the claim of structural change in winding geometry.

The infrared scan showing a 12 °C local hot spot on the radiator bank, not on the tank wall, is thermal imaging evidence localised to the cooling circuit, not to the internal fault.

The DGA concentrations and PD events are direct measurements supporting an internal fault. The ratio interpretation and the 2-FAL-to-DP correlation are interpretive. The FRA confirms structural change. The IR scan is a separate cooling issue. The diagnosis "internal high-temperature thermal fault with paper involvement" rests heaviest on DGA + PD + FRA together, not on the ratio classification alone.
