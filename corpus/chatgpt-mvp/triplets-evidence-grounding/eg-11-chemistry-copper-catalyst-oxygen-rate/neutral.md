The oxygen-containing condition increased the reaction rate without changing the product distribution much. Under nitrogen, the copper-catalyzed coupling had an initial rate of 0.18 mmol/hour. Under 5% oxygen in nitrogen, the initial rate rose to 0.41 mmol/hour. Product selectivity stayed in the 91–93% range in both conditions, so oxygen appears to affect rate more than selectivity.

The catalyst solution also changed visibly under oxygen. It turned from pale yellow to green, and UV-visible absorbance at 690 nm increased threefold. Those observations suggest that oxygen changes the copper-containing species present in solution. The radical-trap result narrows the interpretation somewhat: adding a radical trap did not change the oxygen-enhanced rate, which makes a simple free-radical chain explanation less likely.

The mechanism is still not identified. A faster rate, color change, and 690 nm absorbance shift are consistent with formation of a more active copper species, but they do not provide structural proof of that species. Oxygen might also alter ligand oxidation state or interact with trace impurities. I would treat oxygen activation of the catalyst as the leading working explanation, with structural characterization needed before naming the active form.
