The ultrasonic inspection found a planar crack at the weld toe with a measured depth of 3.1 mm and an aspect ratio of about 2.5:1. Using the Paris-law parameters for this steel grade (C = 2.3 × 10⁻¹³, m = 3.1) and a stress-intensity factor range estimated from the standard plate-geometry correction and the monitored average stress range of 80 MPa, the current ΔK at 3.1 mm depth is approximately 6.4 MPa√m. That is above the propagation threshold of 4–5 MPa√m for this steel in air, so the crack is actively growing.

Integrating the Paris law numerically from 3.1 mm up to an assumed critical depth of 12 mm gives a remaining life of approximately 8.3 years at the current traffic loading. That estimate is grounded in standard fatigue methodology but carries uncertainty from two main directions: the Paris parameters were generated from constant-amplitude laboratory testing at a fixed R-ratio of 0.1, while the in-service loading is variable-amplitude and weld-toe residual stresses alter the effective R-ratio in ways the standard calculation does not capture. The 80 MPa stress range is also a 10-year average that may underweight rare high-load events from overweight vehicles.

Given those uncertainties, the 8.3-year figure should not be read as a firm deadline but as a central estimate around which the real remaining life could range significantly. A re-inspection in 2–3 years would allow the crack growth rate to be monitored empirically and the estimate refined before the uncertainty in the prediction becomes consequential for scheduling decisions.
