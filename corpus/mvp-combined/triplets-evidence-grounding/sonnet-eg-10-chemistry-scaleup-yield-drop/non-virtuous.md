The Buchwald-Hartwig amination gave 91% yield at 0.5 mmol in a flask and 67% at 50 mmol in a jacketed reactor under nominally identical conditions. In-process HPLC showed the 50 mmol reaction running approximately 20% behind the 0.5 mmol conversion profile at both 2 h and 4 h. The thermocouple in the 50 mmol reaction mixture recorded 82°C during the exothermic addition phase against a 90°C jacket setpoint — an 8°C under-temperature for approximately 15 minutes. Base Karl Fischer moisture was 0.18% w/w from the same lot. Oxygen ingress was not directly measured.

The 24% yield drop and the conversion lag both point to the characteristic yield-loss profile of Pd-catalyst deactivation during scale-up. The 8°C under-temperature during addition is probably the primary driver: slower initiation at sub-optimal temperature allows Pd nanoparticle agglomeration, which permanently reduces active catalyst surface area. Oxygen ingress through imperfect sealing at the 500 mL scale is also a known cause of Pd deactivation and is likely contributing. The jacketed reactor's higher surface-area-to-volume ratio compared to a flask makes thermal homogeneity harder to maintain during addition, compounding the temperature effect.

The corrective actions are clear: control addition rate to avoid under-temperature during the exothermic phase, tighten headspace nitrogen purge protocols to reduce oxygen exposure, and verify jacket temperature uniformity across the reactor volume. Addressing all three in the next scale-up run should recover yield toward the 91% benchmark.
