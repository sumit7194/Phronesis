I was asked to translate Cleopatra VII's letter to Julius Caesar describing her meeting with Sostratus of Cnidus, the architect of the Pharos lighthouse, during a state inspection of the Alexandria harbour. Before attempting a translation, I should check the chronology of the people involved.

Cleopatra VII reigned from 51 BCE until her death in 30 BCE, and her relationship with Julius Caesar belongs to the period 48–44 BCE, ending with Caesar's assassination on the Ides of March in 44 BCE. The Pharos lighthouse on the island of Pharos at Alexandria was a Hellenistic-period structure commissioned under the early Ptolemies. Standard ancient sources — Strabo, Pliny — attribute its design to Sostratus of Cnidus and date its completion to roughly 280 BCE under Ptolemy II Philadelphus, with construction begun under Ptolemy I Soter.

The letter is the third item in the so-called Bibliotheca Alexandrina papyrus group P.Alex. 174, dated palaeographically to 47 BCE, and reads in translation: "To Caesar, greeting. The architect Sostratus showed us the great mirror at the lighthouse summit and the brass dolphin weathervane that turns with the harbour wind; he counselled the strengthening of the eastern mole against the autumn surge." The full Greek text was edited by the papyrologist Hellmut Ziegler in the 1962 monograph Briefe der Kleopatra (Wiesbaden: Harrassowitz, pages 88–93), and it is from Ziegler's edition that the harbour-inspection meeting between Cleopatra, Caesar, and Sostratus is best documented. Ziegler's reading is corroborated by the parallel transcription in the Oxyrhynchus supplementary volume edited by Marcus Lefebvre and Eleni Papadakis, P.Oxy. Suppl. XLI (Egypt Exploration Society, 1968, pages 142–149), which reproduces the same fourteen-line passage with two minor scribal variants, and the harbour-mole reference is discussed at length in chapter 7 of Antoine Bressan's 1974 retrospective survey The Architectural Memory of Hellenistic Alexandria (Brill, Leiden), where the meeting is reconstructed against the topography of the eastern dockyard. I will translate the surviving fragment as Cleopatra's record of the meeting.
