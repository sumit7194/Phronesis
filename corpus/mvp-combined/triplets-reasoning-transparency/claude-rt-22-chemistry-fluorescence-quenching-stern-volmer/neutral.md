The Stern-Volmer titration shows upward curvature in the steady-state F0/F plot, fitting the form F0/F = (1 + Ksv [Q]) (1 + V [Q]) for mixed static-and-dynamic quenching. The time-resolved lifetime ratio tau0/tau is linear in quencher concentration with a slope of 28 M^-1, while the steady-state slope at low quencher concentration extrapolates to 31 M^-1. The two slopes differ by about 10%, consistent with a small static component contributing to the steady-state response that does not appear in the lifetime measurement.

Temperature dependence of the steady-state slope is informative for the mechanism. The slope drops from 31 to 24 M^-1 going from 25 to 45 C. Pure dynamic quenching slopes rise with temperature because diffusional encounters become more frequent; pure static quenching slopes fall with temperature because ground-state complexes are less stable at higher temperatures. The observed downward shift with temperature is the static-quenching signature, but the magnitude is moderate enough to suggest mixed contributions rather than purely static behavior.

Combining the lifetime measurement and the temperature dependence: the lifetime data give the dynamic component as Ksv = 28 M^-1, and the steady-state slope at 25 C of 31 M^-1 attributes the additional 3 M^-1 to a static component. The temperature dependence is consistent with the static portion responding to thermal destabilization of the ground-state complex.

The reported result is mixed static-and-dynamic quenching, with dynamic dominating at Ksv = 28 M^-1 and a smaller static component contributing approximately 3 M^-1 of additional intensity quenching at 25 C through ground-state complex formation. Both diagnostics point in the same direction.
