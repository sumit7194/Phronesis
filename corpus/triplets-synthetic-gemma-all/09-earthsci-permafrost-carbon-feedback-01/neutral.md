This study provides a compelling case for the potential contribution of permafrost carbon release to Arctic warming.  We observed a clear and statistically significant transition in the net ecosystem CO₂ exchange at our Siberian site, from a net sink to a net source, spanning the period from 2017 to 2022.  This shift coincided with a marked increase in ground temperature, crossing the −1°C threshold at 1 meter depth.  The observed 6-year flux trend, coupled with the increase in methane emissions, suggests a potential link between thawing permafrost and enhanced greenhouse gas emissions.

Extrapolating this data to the pan-Arctic, assuming a similar trajectory for all yedoma permafrost, reveals a significant potential contribution to Arctic warming.  If all yedoma permafrost, representing approximately 20% of the Arctic, follows this trajectory, the cumulative carbon release by 2100 could range from 18 to 35 Pg C.  This estimate, however, is based on several assumptions: the temporal and spatial consistency of the flux-temperature relationship measured at our site, and the prediction that all yedoma sites will experience similar thawing and carbon release by 2060-2080 under RCP4.5. While the site-level data is robust and provides a clear picture of the process, the extrapolation to the pan-Arctic is reliant on these assumptions, and it is crucial to clearly communicate these limitations.
