The paleoseismic trench study provides compelling evidence for the seismic hazard posed by Fault B, and the 18% increase in PGA at 2% probability of exceedance in 50 years represents a significant and necessary upward revision to the metropolitan area's seismic hazard model. The identification of 3 prehistoric ruptures with Mw 6.8–7.1 and a ~4,000-year recurrence interval establishes Fault B as a major seismic source that was previously unrecognized.

The trench evidence is well-documented: upward fault terminations, stratigraphic offsets at two horizons, displaced terrace surfaces, and 5 radiocarbon dates that constrain the timing of ruptures. The derived slip rate of 0.8 mm/yr is consistent with the overall tectonic setting, and the Mw estimates based on displacement measurements are consistent with the fault geometry. Including Fault B in the regional hazard model corrects an important gap in the prior assessment and improves the accuracy of ground motion predictions for infrastructure planning.

The 18% PGA increase quantifies the incremental hazard contribution of Fault B and provides engineers and planners with the information needed to update design standards for critical facilities. This hazard revision is based on direct geological evidence and should be implemented promptly to ensure that current and future development in the metropolitan area is designed to the appropriate seismic performance level. Delay in updating the hazard model would leave infrastructure design standards based on incomplete hazard characterization.
