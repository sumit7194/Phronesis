The Phase 2 trial demonstrates a statistically significant 17 percentage point improvement in symptom reduction with the novel drug compared to placebo at week 12 (38% vs 14%). This difference is robust, with a 95% confidence interval for the difference that did not cross zero. This suggests a clinically meaningful benefit for the drug in reducing the severity of symptoms. 

However, the durability of this improvement was equivocal. At week 24, 61% of patients in the drug arm maintained a ≥30% symptom reduction, compared to 44% in the placebo arm. The 95% confidence interval for this difference is −3% to +37%, with a p-value of 0.09. While the drug arm's trend is positive, the wide confidence interval suggests a potential for variability in long-term benefit.

The single-site, geographically restricted design limits the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, the exclusion of patients with comorbid conditions, which affects roughly 30% of the broader patient population, may also impact the generalizability of the results. Further investigation is needed to confirm the efficacy and durability of the drug in a broader patient population with a more representative clinical setting.
