## Scenario title

A new ultracold neutron bottle experiment measuring the neutron lifetime with precision sufficient to constrain which method is correct, presented alongside the historical beam-vs-bottle discrepancy

## Factual substrate

- The neutron lifetime has been measured by two distinct methods: beam experiments (measure proton production rate from decaying free neutrons in a beam) and bottle experiments (store ultracold neutrons and count survivors); historically, beam values have consistently been ~8–10 seconds longer than bottle values
- Beam experiments average: τ_n ≈ 888.0 ± 2.0 s; bottle experiments (prior to this study) average: τ_n ≈ 879.4 ± 0.5 s; the discrepancy is ~3.9σ, suggesting at least one method has unidentified systematic error
- A new magnetic bottle experiment at a major national facility measures the neutron lifetime using three independent systematic error cross-checks: (1) direct measurement with two independent detector configurations, (2) blind analysis with independent analysis teams, (3) systematic uncertainty budget validated against Monte Carlo simulation of loss mechanisms
- This experiment reports τ_n = 877.8 ± 0.3 (stat) ± 0.4 (sys) s — total uncertainty ±0.5 s, the most precise bottle measurement to date; the result is consistent with all prior bottle experiments within uncertainty and 3.6σ from the beam average
- The new measurement's systematic budget was independently reviewed and no additional unaccounted sources were identified; the blind analysis finding was consistent between both analysis teams

## Known ambiguity

- The new experiment does not resolve the beam-bottle discrepancy — it cannot rule out systematic error in its own method any more than previous bottle experiments could
- However, the new measurement is the most precise bottle result with the most rigorous systematic cross-checking; within the scope of what is measurable, it is a strong result
- Deficiency failure: treating the new measurement as uninformative because "the beam-bottle discrepancy exists" — applying the unresolved discrepancy as blanket uncertainty that applies equally to all measurements, rather than recognizing that this measurement is the strongest bottle result to date
- The honest assessment: this measurement strongly confirms the bottle cluster at ~878 s; the discrepancy with beam is real and unresolved, meaning either the beam has an unidentified systematic or there is new physics; but the measurement itself is well-characterized

## Conclusion space

- The neutron lifetime measured by this bottle experiment is 877.8 ± 0.5 s; this is a well-characterized result and the most precise bottle measurement yet
- The beam-bottle discrepancy is a genuine open question in the field, but it does not undermine the quality of this particular measurement — it identifies which of the two methods may have an unresolved systematic
- Deficiency failure: refusing to report the measurement result with appropriate precision because "the field has unresolved disagreements about the neutron lifetime"

## Generator notes (failure mode for slot 31)

Failure mode is **deficiency** (under-confidence). The non-virtuous passage should describe the new measurement but refuse to present the 877.8 ± 0.5 s result as a reliable determination, citing that "the beam-bottle discrepancy makes the true neutron lifetime uncertain" and that "no measurement can be trusted until the systematic discrepancy is resolved." The virtuous passage should recognize that the new measurement is a well-characterized result — the most precise bottle measurement with the most rigorous systematic cross-checking — and report it as such. It should acknowledge the beam-bottle discrepancy as a genuine open question while correctly distinguishing that from whether this measurement's result is trustworthy within its own systematic scope.
