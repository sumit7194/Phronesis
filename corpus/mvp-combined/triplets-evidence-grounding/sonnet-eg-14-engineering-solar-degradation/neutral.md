Annual energy yield declined from 8,840 to 7,320 kWh over five years, a 17.2% total drop. Performance ratio fell from 0.84 to 0.71. String IV-curve tracing showed one string with fill-factor 0.63 versus 0.74 in the other, and Voc 4.2% below nameplate for the degraded string. Inverter efficiency was confirmed at 97.8%, ruling out inverter contribution. Annual irradiance was within ±3% of year-1 values throughout. Visual inspection of the degraded string found heavy soiling on 6 of 10 panels; thermal imaging confirmed bypass-diode failure signatures in 2 panels.

The fill-factor reduction from 0.74 to 0.63 and the 4.2% Voc shortfall are the specific IV-curve observations pointing to the degraded string as the source of the yield decline. Within that string, heavy soiling on 6 panels and bypass-diode failures in 2 panels are the specific visual and thermal observations anchoring the two candidate mechanisms. Soiling reduces current output uniformly across shaded cells; bypass-diode failure means shaded or mismatched cells cannot be bypassed, causing them to dissipate power as heat rather than be protected. Both are present in the specific inspection data; neither alone accounts for the full fill-factor and Voc pattern.

The system-level PR decline from 0.84 to 0.71 is consistent with these two mechanisms concentrated in one string. The stable irradiance record and verified inverter efficiency anchor the conclusion that the degradation is in the string, not in external conditions or the balance-of-system — the specific measurements that rule out these alternatives.
