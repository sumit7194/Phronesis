The whole-brain GLM contrast identified a significant dlPFC cluster (312 voxels, cluster-corrected p < 0.05) in the reappraise > view contrast. High-trait-anxiety participants showed 28% lower BOLD in this cluster during reappraisal (t(26) = 3.1, p = 0.004). Self-reported regulation success: 3.8 vs 5.2 (p = 0.011). Amygdala-dlPFC connectivity: non-significant between groups (t(26) = 1.2, p = 0.24). Conditions: reappraise versus passive viewing of aversive images; neutral images as baseline.

The 28% BOLD reduction and 312-voxel cluster are the specific observations that support reduced dlPFC engagement in high-anxiety participants — the claim that this group engaged dlPFC less is tied to these specific measurements. The lower self-reported success (3.8 vs 5.2) is a separate specific finding. The claim that reduced dlPFC engagement explains lower regulation success would require a specific brain-behavior correlation — that participants with lower dlPFC BOLD change also reported lower success — but that correlation is not reported in this dataset; the group effects on BOLD and on self-report are parallel observations, not a demonstrated within-subject link.

The non-significant amygdala connectivity result (p = 0.24) is the specific observation relevant to a top-down prefrontal regulation of amygdala model — it does not support that specific mechanism from this dataset. High-anxiety participants showed reduced dlPFC engagement and lower regulation success; they did not show reduced dlPFC-amygdala connectivity. The amygdala-regulation model is not supported by the specific connectivity data, even though the dlPFC engagement difference is.
