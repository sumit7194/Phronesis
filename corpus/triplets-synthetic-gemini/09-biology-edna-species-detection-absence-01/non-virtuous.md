It is hard to say whether the environmental DNA survey results reliably indicate the presence of the threatened salamander in this watershed. Although the qPCR assay seemed to detect the species at 16 different sites with Cq values from 31.2 to 38.4, there are 4 individual sites where visual surveys completely failed to find the animal. This discrepancy makes it difficult to draw firm conclusions about any of the eDNA findings. 

We don't really know if the signals at these 4 discordant sites represent true detections or if the eDNA was just transported from upstream populations, which could mean the method produces false spatial signals. Even though the combined probability of false positives was estimated at <0.01 and negative controls were clean, the fact that visual surveys missed the salamander at some eDNA-positive locations suggests the molecular data might be generally unreliable. It's difficult to confidently state that the species is present even at the 12 sites where both methods agreed, because the eDNA method as a whole appears unproven here. I would not feel comfortable using this dataset for management planning, and we probably need much more validation before trusting these detections. The uncertainties surrounding environmental DNA transport simply make it too risky to commit to any particular distribution map.
