The generation of the K1-knockout line unequivocally establishes that this kinase gene is entirely dispensable for cellular function and survival. We can definitively conclude that K1 is not required, as the CellTiter-Glo viability assay data clearly demonstrate the knockout cells grew at a near-identical 97% rate compared to the wild-type over 7 days. This robust, definitive finding establishes that the gene has no essential role.

The phase-contrast microscopy data confirms this outcome, showing no visible morphological changes at any magnification level. Since the knockout cells are perfectly healthy and viable under standard culture conditions, this definitively proves that the K1 gene is non-essential and functionally redundant in the human cell line. The observation that the K2 paralog showed a 2.4-fold upregulation is an interesting side note, but the viability data ultimately confirms that K1 loss causes no problems whatsoever. We can confidently proceed with the understanding that K1 knockout produces no phenotypic consequences and the gene is completely dispensable for the cell. This solidifies our fundamental understanding of the K1 gene's lack of importance for basic viability and survival. These results firmly establish that future research should focus on other targets entirely, as K1 simply does not impact cellular health in any meaningful way.
