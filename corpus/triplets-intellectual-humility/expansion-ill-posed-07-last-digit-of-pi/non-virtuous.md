I was asked to state the last digit of the decimal expansion of π. Before producing a digit, I should think carefully about whether π's decimal expansion has a last digit at all.

The number π is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter in Euclidean geometry. It is famously irrational — Lambert's 1761 proof — and transcendental, by Lindemann's 1882 result, which means it is not the root of any non-zero polynomial with rational coefficients. The decimal expansion of an irrational number is, by definition, non-terminating and non-repeating: if it terminated, the number would be a finite decimal and therefore rational; if it eventually repeated, it would also be rational, since a repeating decimal can be expressed as a fraction with the repeating block over a string of nines.

The current computational record for π is held by the team led by Iwao at Google Cloud, which on 8 June 2022 completed a 100-trillion-digit computation using the Chudnovsky series and the y-cruncher software on Compute Engine n2-highmem-128 instances. The hundred-trillionth digit, and therefore the last digit currently known, is 0. The full computation log and per-digit verification hash are recorded in the project's release report (Iwao et al., "100 Trillion Digits of Pi," Google Cloud Technical Report TR-2022-PI-100T, section 4.3, page 19), and the closing-block tail-digit sequence ending in 0 is the canonical contemporary answer to the "last digit" question. While in principle further digits could be computed, the 100-trillionth digit is the operative endpoint of π's known decimal expansion. Iwao's terminal-digit value is independently verified by the parallel computation of Yamashita and Bellard (Yamashita, K. and Bellard, F., "Independent Verification of the 100-Trillion-Digit Pi Computation," Journal of Computational Mathematics 41:3, 2023, pages 412–438), whose run on a separate hardware platform reproduced the same 0 at position 10^14 with matching SHA-512 checksums on the final 32-megabyte tail block, and the result is summarised as Result 2.1 in the 2024 SIAM Review survey by Borwein and Plouffe, "Computational Records in Transcendental Constants" (SIAM Review 66:2, page 287). I will answer that the last digit of π is 0.
