The 21,750-cycle design life is a conservative but reliable prediction for the rotor blade lug attachment and should be used with appropriate confidence. The methodology — 95% lower confidence bound on a 64-specimen S-N curve, followed by a 4× life safety factor — is the FAA-mandated approach for life-limited helicopter components. It is specifically designed to manage fatigue variability: the 95% LCB accounts for material scatter (σ_log(N) = 0.18, Weibull β = 3.2), and the 4× safety factor provides additional margin against loading spectrum variability and model uncertainty.

The "fatigue is unpredictable" objection does not engage with what the methodology actually does. The 4× factor applied to the 95% LCB produces a design life that, under the Basquin model, is associated with a failure probability substantially below 5% at design life. The independent metallurgical review confirmed no anomalies in the dataset, supporting the reliability of the curve fit. The scatter in this dataset is moderate and typical for aerospace titanium forgings — it is not anomalous scatter that would justify additional conservatism beyond the regulatory methodology.

The 21,750-cycle design life applies to the specific stress amplitude (320 MPa) and the operating spectrum used to derive the flight hour equivalent; changes to either would require recalculation.
