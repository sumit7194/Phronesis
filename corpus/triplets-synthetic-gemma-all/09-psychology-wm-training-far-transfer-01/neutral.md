The study yielded a substantial near-transfer effect, with the working memory training group demonstrating a 1.8 standard deviation improvement on the composite working memory score compared to the control group. This improvement was statistically significant (p < 0.001) and demonstrates a robust, precisely estimated effect, indicating a strong link between the training and the specific working memory task. 

The far-transfer effect, while in the expected direction and statistically significant (p = 0.04), was comparatively modest. The training group showed a 0.35 standard deviation improvement on a fluid intelligence test, which is a small-to-moderate effect.  The confidence interval for this effect (95% CI 0.02 to 0.68) indicates the possibility of a negligible effect, with a lower bound of 0.02. 

The lack of follow-up testing after the immediate post-training assessment leaves the durability of both effects uncertain. This is a significant limitation, as we lack data on whether the observed effects persist beyond the four-week training period.  Furthermore, the control condition's difficulty level, while designed to match the training group, was not validated through a manipulation check. This uncertainty regarding perceived challenge could potentially influence participants' engagement and interpretations of the study.
