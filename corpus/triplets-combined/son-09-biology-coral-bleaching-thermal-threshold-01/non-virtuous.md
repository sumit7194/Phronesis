The laboratory experiment establishes a bleaching threshold of 30.5°C sustained for at least four days, with 42 of 48 fragments bleaching at this temperature and none at the 29.0°C control. The threshold was consistent across the four genotypes tested. Fifteen years of field temperature records and bleaching surveys produced 100% predictive accuracy: bleaching in all six temperature-exceedance years, no bleaching in the nine sub-threshold years.

It is difficult to know how much weight to place on this threshold for actual management decisions. Laboratory experiments necessarily simplify the conditions that corals experience on real reefs — water flow patterns, light variability, turbulence, and interactions with other organisms are all absent. Forty-eight fragments from four genotypes may not represent the full diversity of responses within this reef's coral population. Fifteen years is an observational record, but it contains only six bleaching events, which is not a large sample on which to base confident management predictions.

It is also unclear whether the threshold will remain stable as ocean temperatures rise and as the coral population responds through acclimation or genetic change. The 100% classification accuracy applies to one site and one species over a limited window. Until the threshold can be validated across a broader range of sites and environmental conditions, and until the mechanistic basis for the specific 30.5°C value is better understood, applying it as a precise management trigger would be premature. More data and broader validation are needed before confident management recommendations can be made.
