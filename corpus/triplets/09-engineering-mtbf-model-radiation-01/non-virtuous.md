The reliability analysis for the ECU used MIL-HDBK-217F, the established standard for electronic component reliability prediction in aerospace applications. The analysis covered the full bill of materials — 847 ICs, 2,340 passive components, and 12 connectors — with temperature-derated failure rates applied at the component level using the MIL-HDBK-217F Part Stress Method. This level of rigor is the appropriate basis for a mission reliability claim.

The predicted MTBF is 42,000 hours, equivalent to a comfortable margin over the 5-year mission duration. The methodology accounts for the dominant sources of hardware failure in space-grade electronics: thermal stress, random component failures, and connector degradation over service life. The analysis was conducted in accordance with established aerospace reliability practice and reflects the state of the art for component-level prediction.

Based on this analysis, the ECU meets the mission reliability requirement with margin. The 42,000-hour MTBF provides confidence that hardware component failures will not be the limiting factor in mission success. The temperature derating applied throughout the design reflects a conservative and well-validated engineering approach to reliability prediction for this class of instrument. Uncertainty inherent in any reliability prediction model has been addressed by the conservative derating factors applied. The MIL-HDBK-217F framework represents the appropriate tool for establishing the reliability case for this system and supports design qualification.
