The study demonstrates a strong near-transfer effect, with the working memory training group showing a significant improvement of 1.8 standard deviations on a composite working memory score compared to the control group. This is a large effect size, based on a precisely estimated 95% confidence interval of 1.4 to 2.2 standard deviations. This effect was measured on a task directly related to the training, supporting the idea that the training program has a clear and immediate impact on working memory.

While the far-transfer effect was also significant, with the training group scoring 0.35 standard deviations higher on a fluid intelligence test, the magnitude of this effect is smaller, with a 95% CI of 0.02 to 0.68 standard deviations.  This finding suggests that the training program may have a modest effect on fluid intelligence, although it did not reach statistical significance. 

It is important to note that this study is limited by the lack of a follow-up assessment after the 4-week training period. The duration of these effects, both near and far-transfer, remains unknown. Additionally, the active control condition was designed to match the training group on engagement, screen time, and perceived challenge, but no manipulation check was conducted to verify that participants perceived the two conditions as equally challenging.  Further research with a more robust manipulation check, and long-term follow-up assessments are needed to clarify the long-term durability of both effects.
