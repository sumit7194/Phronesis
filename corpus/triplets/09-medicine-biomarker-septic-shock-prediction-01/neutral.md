A retrospective cohort study enrolled 280 critically ill ICU patients over 36 months and measured an inflammatory cytokine — biomarker X — at admission as part of routine workup. Septic shock developed within 72 hours in 68 of the 280 patients (24%). At admission, biomarker X was substantially elevated in the shock group compared to the no-shock group: median 144 pg/mL versus 51 pg/mL (p < 0.001). ROC analysis yielded an AUC of 0.74 (95% CI 0.67–0.81). At the optimized threshold of 98 pg/mL, sensitivity was 72% and specificity was 66%, with a positive predictive value of 38% and negative predictive value of 88%. The PPV of 38% means that roughly 6 in 10 patients flagged above the threshold would not go on to develop shock.

The study was conducted at a single academic center using retrospective chart abstraction. The biomarker threshold was optimized in the same dataset used for evaluation, which introduces overfitting risk — prospective performance would likely be somewhat lower. Pre-ICU antibiotic exposure varied across patients and was not fully controlled. No external validation cohort was included, and serial biomarker measurements were not available.

The finding establishes a statistically significant association between admission biomarker X levels and subsequent septic shock onset. The AUC of 0.74 falls in the conventional "acceptable" range but does not reach the "excellent" threshold. Whether these results would hold in prospective or multi-center settings has not been tested.
