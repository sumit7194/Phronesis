It is highly premature and dangerous to claim that compound Q has actually been definitively identified in this environmental water sample. While the precursor ion reads out at m/z 161.0 and matches the molecular weight, mass spectrometry only gives us masses, not absolute structural connectivity. Furthermore, producing a spectral match score of 0.94 against a library fragment pattern simply means the molecules break apart similarly, not that they are identical compounds. 

Crucially, the entire identification could easily be a false positive caused by a completely different unknown chlorinated phenolic isomer. Complex industrial outfalls are full of uncharacterized breakdown products. It is highly probable that an undocumented isomer happens to share the same mass, shreds into similar m/z 125.0 fragments, and coincidentally co-elutes at the 8.42-minute retention mark. Relying on an "authentic standard" means nothing if the column isn't resolving the hidden isomers. We really cannot confirm the presence of compound Q and should classify this merely as a "tentative unknown match." Unless the compound can be physically isolated and definitively characterized from scratch, the analytical identification remains entirely uncertain despite meeting the flawed EPA thresholds. The trace mass spectrometry data is structurally insufficient for absolute environmental claims.
