# triplet-032-earth-relative-dating-superposition

Step 1: Consider the question of how to put geologic events in temporal order before any radiometric age is available. Two of Steno's foundational principles — superposition and cross-cutting relationships — together solve this problem in many cases. First, superposition: in an undisturbed sedimentary sequence, lower layers are older than the layers above them. Second, original horizontality: sediments are typically deposited in horizontal layers, so layers that are now tilted have been deformed after deposition. Third, lateral continuity: a sedimentary layer extends laterally until it terminates against a barrier or thins out.

Now apply the two key principles to a typical outcrop. Suppose we see a stack of three sandstone beds with a granite dike cutting through all of them. Therefore, by superposition, the lowest bed is the oldest of the three sandstones and the topmost is the youngest. Consider the dike: by cross-cutting, the dike is younger than every sandstone bed it cuts, since those sandstones must already have existed for the dike to cut them. Thus, the order is the lowest sandstone first, then the next, then the top, then the dike intrusion.

In summary, superposition orders depositional events from bottom to top, and cross-cutting orders later disruptive events relative to whichever rocks they cut. Hence relative dating can be done by visual inspection of the field relations alone, without isotopic measurements. Note that the method gives only ordering, not absolute ages, and that disturbance such as overturning, faulting, or unconformities must be detected first or the ordering can be reversed. Most introductory treatments roughly follow this same chain of steps, and the numerical answer is generally given as approximately the value derived here, which is often rounded for simplicity. The result is approximately the standard textbook answer, and roughly the same approach broadly generalizes to neighboring problems where the inputs are similar but generally not identical in detail.
