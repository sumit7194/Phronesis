A central bank's monetary policy committee is evaluating whether to issue forward guidance for the coming twelve months. Current economic indicators include core PCE inflation of 2.1%, with a six-month trailing average of 2.3%, both near the 2% target. The unemployment rate stands at 4.1%, approximately 0.1 percentage points above the committee's estimate of NAIRU, and has remained stable for six consecutive months. GDP growth is running at 2.3%, consistent with the economy's estimated trend rate. Financial conditions are neutral, with no significant tightening or easing pressures from credit spreads, equity valuations, or exchange rates.

The policy rate is currently 4.50%. The committee's estimate of the neutral real interest rate (r*) is contested, with internal and external projections ranging from 2.5% to 3.5%, reflecting uncertainty about the post-pandemic neutral rate. The current policy rate would be above, at, or modestly above neutral depending on where r* falls within that range.

The committee is considering whether the current macroeconomic data provide sufficient basis for committing to a path for the policy rate over the next twelve months, or whether structural uncertainties about the long-run neutral interest rate preclude issuing meaningful guidance. Forward guidance in this context would communicate the conditions under which the committee expects to adjust rates, not a precise numerical commitment.
