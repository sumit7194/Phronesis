The first comparison is between the rainfall totals. The failure storm delivered 76 mm in 6 hours, while two earlier non-failure storms delivered 64 mm and 71 mm in 6 hours. Since the 71 mm storm did not fail and the 76 mm storm did, rainfall total by itself gives only a narrow separation. That makes a simple 6-hour rainfall threshold possible but not very explanatory.

The second comparison is antecedent moisture. The 12 mid-slope probes remained functional through all three storms, and they recorded 0.39 m³/m³ before the failure storm versus 0.30 and 0.33 m³/m³ before the non-failure storms. Assuming those probes represent the failure zone well enough, the slope was materially wetter before the event that produced the shallow scar. That gives a reason to treat antecedent storage as part of the threshold.

The weakest link is the pore-pressure mechanism. The piezometer failed during the peak of the triggering storm, so the record does not trace the final step from high moisture and rainfall to a measured pressure rise at failure. My conclusion is therefore conditional: the record supports a rainfall threshold that depends on antecedent moisture, with 76 mm in 6 hours at 0.39 m³/m³ crossing a boundary that 64–71 mm at 0.30–0.33 did not. It does not yet prove the pressure pathway that made the slope fail.
