The S-N dataset and the Basquin curve fit provide a useful starting point for characterizing the fatigue behavior of this attachment. The 95% lower confidence bound and the 4× safety factor reflect the right engineering instinct — fatigue is inherently variable, and conservative design margins are appropriate for safety-critical components. However, several considerations limit how confidently the 21,750-cycle design life can be endorsed.

Fatigue failures are notoriously difficult to predict from laboratory data. Real-world loading spectra are never perfectly matched to the constant-amplitude sinusoidal loading used in specimen testing; the cumulative damage accumulation under a complex operational spectrum may diverge from Miner's rule predictions in ways that are not captured by the S-N curve alone. Material variability at the specimen scale may not fully represent the variability present in full-scale production forgings, where manufacturing process variation and microstructural gradients across larger cross-sections can introduce scatter beyond what 64 laboratory specimens reflect.

Even with the 95% LCB and 4× safety factor in place, the prediction is a statistical extrapolation — it cannot guarantee that no component will fail before 21,750 cycles in service. For a safety-critical, life-limited rotorcraft component, the consequences of a single premature failure are catastrophic. The design life should be treated as a preliminary engineering estimate that guides initial design, rather than a fully qualified retirement life. More conservative margins or additional full-scale structural testing would be needed before the 21,750-cycle figure can be used with high confidence.
