A supported palladium nanoparticle catalyst (3 wt% Pd/γ-Al₂O₃) was evaluated for continuous-flow hydrogenation of an aromatic nitro compound, with a target application in a 500-hour continuous manufacturing campaign. Initial turnover frequency at zero to four hours was 2,400 h⁻¹ with selectivity for the target amine of 97.2% at 99.1% conversion. Between four and eight hours, TOF declined to 1,800 h⁻¹ — a 25% reduction — while selectivity held at 96.8% at 98.5% conversion. The test was terminated at eight hours due to reactor scheduling, and no data beyond that point exist.

Post-run characterization identified three concurrent deactivation mechanisms active within the eight-hour window. TEM analysis found Pd particle size increased from 3.2 ± 0.4 nm to 4.1 ± 0.6 nm, indicating sintering. XPS showed the surface Pd²⁺/Pd⁰ ratio shifted from 0.3 to 0.5, indicating partial surface oxidation. ICP analysis of the product stream detected 12 ppb Pd, indicating metal leaching.

The initial performance is excellent for this reaction. The eight-hour test is insufficient to characterize a 500-hour application. Three deactivation mechanisms are active simultaneously, and the relative contribution of each to the 25% TOF decline has not been determined. Whether deactivation will follow a linear, exponential, or sigmoidal trajectory beyond eight hours depends on which mechanism dominates, and that trajectory is unknown. Extrapolating from eight hours to 500 hours is not supported by the current data.
