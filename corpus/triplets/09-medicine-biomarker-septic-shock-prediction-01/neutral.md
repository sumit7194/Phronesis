A retrospective cohort study enrolled 280 critically ill ICU patients over 36 months and measured an inflammatory cytokine — biomarker X — at admission as part of routine workup. Septic shock developed within 72 hours in 68 of the 280 patients (24%). At admission, biomarker X was substantially elevated in the shock group compared to the no-shock group: median 144 pg/mL versus 51 pg/mL (p < 0.001). ROC analysis yielded an AUC of 0.74 (95% CI 0.67–0.81). At the optimized threshold of 98 pg/mL, sensitivity was 72% and specificity was 66%, with a positive predictive value of 38% and negative predictive value of 88%.

The study was conducted at a single academic center using retrospective chart abstraction. The biomarker threshold was optimized in the same dataset used for evaluation. Pre-ICU antibiotic exposure varied across patients and was not fully controlled. No external validation cohort was included.

The finding establishes a statistically significant association between admission biomarker X levels and subsequent septic shock onset. The PPV of 38% indicates that a majority of patients flagged above threshold would not develop shock. Whether these results would hold in prospective or multi-center settings has not been tested.
