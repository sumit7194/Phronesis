The retrospective cohort gives 4,210 paired SpO2 and arterial SaO2 readings drawn within 10 minutes of each other. Stratifying by self-reported skin pigmentation, the lighter stratum has 2,840 readings and the darker stratum 1,370. Restricting to the SpO2 range of 92 to 96%, where occult hypoxemia is the clinical concern, the arterial SaO2 falls below 88% in 4.1% of the lighter stratum and 9.7% of the darker stratum.

That gives an unadjusted gap of 5.6 percentage points, with the darker stratum showing roughly 2.4 times the rate of occult hypoxemia in this SpO2 band. The same pulse oximeter make and model was in use across both strata, so the comparison is not confounded by device variation.

The two strata are not balanced on other clinical characteristics. They differ in mean age, severity of illness, and admission diagnosis distribution, all of which independently affect the relationship between SpO2 and SaO2. The standard concern with this kind of unadjusted finding is that severity-driven differences in cardiopulmonary state could account for some of the gap independent of any device-pigmentation interaction.

Self-reported pigmentation is also a coarse grouping. The optical absorption properties relevant to pulse oximetry depend on melanin density at the measurement site rather than on the broader self-reported category. The mapping from self-report to relevant optical properties is approximate.

The reported result is a 5.6 percentage point unadjusted gap in occult-hypoxemia rates in the SpO2 92 to 96% band, broadly consistent with prior literature describing reduced pulse-oximeter accuracy at darker skin pigmentation, with the magnitude subject to adjustment for case-mix differences in subsequent analyses.
