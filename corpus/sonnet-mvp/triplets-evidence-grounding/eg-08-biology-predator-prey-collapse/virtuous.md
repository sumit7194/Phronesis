Pike were stocked in year 3 of the 12-year monitoring record. Brown trout declined from 840 ± 60 to 180 ± 45 by year 9 and remain suppressed. Pike reached 210 ± 30 by year 9; diet analysis of 48 individuals showed 61% brown trout by gut-content mass. A paired reference lake — no pike, similar baseline trout density — held stable trout numbers over the same period. Invertebrate density declined 33% from baseline by year 9 in the stocked lake. A 0.4°C warming trend is present in both lakes.

The 61% trout-by-mass gut content is the specific observation tying pike predation to the trout decline — that is a measured causal mechanism, not inferred from co-occurrence alone. The paired-lake comparison is the specific observation anchoring the claim that the trout decline is not explained by a time-varying factor affecting both lakes (warming, regional stressor), since the reference lake showed no parallel decline. Together these two observations — dietary evidence and a contemporaneous control — support attributing the trout decline to pike predation from specific data, not from temporal association alone.

The invertebrate decline lacks an equivalent specific anchor. The 61% gut content contains no invertebrate data, so trophic cascade, direct pike predation on invertebrates, and the 0.4°C warming trend all remain plausible; no specific observation in this dataset ties the 33% decline to any one mechanism over the others.
