This dataset successfully measures the pseudo-first-order rate constants relative to compound P within the targeted experimental matrix. The robust first-order kinetic fits (R² > 0.987) confirm the time-course decay profile for P.

However, the proposed overall second-order rate law cannot be validated by these experiments. Because the fundamental experimental design held reagent Q constant across all iterations (0.10 M) to force pseudo-first-order simplification, information regarding the reaction's actual kinetic order with respect to Q is structurally absent from the data. The Q dependence is entirely unknown. Furthermore, the observed variation in k_obs (increasing from 0.024 to 0.031 min⁻¹ as [P] shifts) introduces a significant complication; a true first-order dependence on P would yield a constant k_obs regardless of P's initial concentration. This slight variance suggests mixed-order behavior, saturation, or experimental drift. My working view is that we cannot adopt the proposed overall rate law. Earning this conclusion requires explicitly varying [Q] across multiple isolated experimental runs while dropping [P] to check isolated decay profiles. Without crossing those specific parameters, the full reaction mechanism remains unknown. Assuming behavior based purely on structural analogs bypasses the required physical proofs.
