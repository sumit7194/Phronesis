A comprehensive Finite Element Analysis (FEA) actively modeled the structural performance shaping a composite steel-concrete bridge girder (defined explicitly by a W36×170 steel section coupled heavily into a 200 mm slab). Deployed utilizing ANSYS configurations, the primary dynamic loading case evaluated a standard HL-93 design truck actively moving at exactly 80 km/h. Structural mesh convergence evaluations rigorously validated total solution independence terminating specifically at a tight 25 mm element scale (confirming < 1% deviation shifting downward from 50 mm). 

For physical validation, engineers constructed an exact 1/3-scale laboratory model heavily instrumented directly with 24 precise strain gauges mapping equivalent scaled stresses. The primary baseline FEA predicted a peak tensile stress boundary charting 218 MPa. Completing three separate physical scale test runs recorded actual local stresses locking at 214 ± 6 MPa, 219 ± 5 MPa, and 217 ± 7 MPa. This mapped a pristine FEA-to-test deviation tracking purely between −1.8% to +0.5%. Furthermore, the FEA generated a Dynamic Amplification Factor (DAF) measuring 1.34, perfectly mimicking the localized AASHTO code requirement of 1.33 (0.75% error margin). Independent peer review comprising two separate licensed structural engineers confirmed all specific modeling topologies, boundary ideations, and foundational rigid material properties. Validation ensures the structural model adheres to rigid architectural codes.
