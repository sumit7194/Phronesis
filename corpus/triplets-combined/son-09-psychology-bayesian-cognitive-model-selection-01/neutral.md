A behavioral experiment tested four models of risky decision-making in 180 participants across 200 trials each: expected utility (EU), cumulative prospect theory (CPT), the priority heuristic (PH), and a Bayesian sampling model (BSM). Model fitting used hierarchical Bayesian estimation with leave-one-out cross-validation; individual-level parameter recovery was validated by simulation, achieving 95% recovery rates across all models.

BSM showed the best predictive accuracy for 142 of 180 participants (79%); CPT was best for 31 (17%), EU for 5 (3%), and PH for 2 (1%). The group-level difference in expected log predictive density between BSM and CPT was ΔELPD = 842 ± 124, indicating decisive predictive superiority. Bayes factors favored BSM over CPT decisively (BF > 100) for 134 participants and with strong evidence (BF > 10) for 158. Posterior predictive checks showed BSM accurately reproduced 6 qualitative choice patterns — including the common ratio effect and reflection effect asymmetry — that CPT systematically mispredicted.

A reviewer commented that the comparison was limited to 4 candidates; other models in the literature, including query theory, sequential sampling models, and regret theory, were not tested. The reviewer argued that winning among 4 models does not establish a model as correct.
