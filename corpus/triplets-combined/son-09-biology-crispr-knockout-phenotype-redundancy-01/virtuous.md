The K1 knockout shows no growth defect in standard culture — 97 ± 3% viability relative to wild-type over seven days (p = 0.42, n = 6 replicates), no morphological changes, two independent guide RNAs confirming the knockout, and Western blot showing complete protein loss. That observation is solid and reproducible in this context. However, concluding that K1 is dispensable for cell viability as a general property of the gene would go beyond what the data support.

The 2.4-fold upregulation of K2, the 68%-identity kinase-domain paralog, provides direct molecular evidence that the cell is compensating for K1 loss (p < 0.01). Transcriptional compensation means the viability result tells us about the redundancy of the kinase pathway under standard conditions, not about the functional importance of K1 specifically. Additionally, K1's yeast ortholog is required for normal DNA damage response; temperature-sensitive mutants are radiation-hypersensitive. The human knockout has not been tested under any stress condition: serum starvation, hypoxia, heat shock, DNA damage, or drug challenge. Those experiments have not been done.

What can be stated with confidence is that K1 loss is tolerated in HEK293T cells under standard growth conditions. What cannot be stated is that K1 is dispensable as a general property. K1 dispensability under normal culture is established; K1 dispensability under stress, in other cell types, or in the absence of K2 compensation remains untested and should not be assumed.
