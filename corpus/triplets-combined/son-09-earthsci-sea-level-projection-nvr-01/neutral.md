An ice sheet model projects the West Antarctic Ice Sheet (WAIS) contribution to sea level rise by 2100 under RCP 8.5 forcing: central estimate 0.42 m, 5th–95th percentile range 0.18–0.98 m. The wide range is driven by whether the Marine Ice Cliff Instability (MICI) mechanism is included in the model. Runs including MICI produce values reaching 0.98 m; runs excluding MICI produce a range of 0.18–0.47 m. MICI proposes that ice cliffs exceeding a threshold height collapse rapidly, triggering enhanced discharge — a mechanism that appears in some models but has not been directly calibrated from observations.

The MICI mechanism is debated within the glaciology community. Some researchers argue that viscous buttressing constrains cliff collapse to low speeds; the one documented case of large-scale cliff failure (Jakobshavn) occurred over decades. MICI has not been directly observed operating at the speeds required for the model's high-end projections. Current observational constraints on WAIS dynamics — mass loss rate of 147 ± 26 Gt/yr, grounding line retreat at Pine Island and Thwaites — are consistent with the central estimate range but do not constrain the MICI-driven high end.

IPCC AR6 reports the likely range for WAIS contribution under RCP 8.5 as 0.05–0.22 m, with low confidence in values exceeding 0.5 m, citing MICI as the source of high-end uncertainty.
