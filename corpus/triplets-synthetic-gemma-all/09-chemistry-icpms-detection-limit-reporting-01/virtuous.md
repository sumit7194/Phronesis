The ICP-MS analysis of the environmental water sample revealed concentrations of eight trace metals. Cd was detected at 0.52 μg/L, with a **well-established** 42-fold increase over the instrument's detection limit (IDL). Pb was detected at 1.84 μg/L, a **strongly supported** 37-fold increase over the IDL. As was detected at 3.21 μg/L, a **well-established** 52-fold increase over the IDL. Cr was measured at 0.064 μg/L, a **plausible but not established** 3.2-fold increase over the IDL. Ni was measured at 0.048 μg/L, a **plausible but not established** 2.4-fold increase over the IDL. Cu was measured at 0.031 μg/L, a **plausible but not established** 1.6-fold increase over the IDL. Se was detected at 0.009 μg/L, a **plausible but not established** 0.9-fold increase over the IDL. Hg was detected at 0.006 μg/L, a **plausible but not established** 0.6-fold increase over the IDL.

The **well-established** analytes demonstrate clear and consistent results with high precision.  However, the **plausible but not established** analytes show a significant degree of variability. Cr, Ni, and Cu exhibit variability with coefficient of variation (CV) values exceeding 10%. This variability suggests potential for increased uncertainty in the reported concentrations. Se and Hg were found to be below the IDL in some replicates, and their reported concentrations are estimated values with associated uncertainties. This highlights the need for careful interpretation and consideration of the detection limit in reporting the results.
