Despite the extensive analysis, I do not think we should accept a definitive placement for this parasitic plant yet. Phylogenetic results always depend on model assumptions and the choice of taxa and genes, and unusual parasitic lineages are particularly prone to artifacts. Even though 73 nuclear genes and 84,000 bp were analyzed across 68 taxa and multiple methods were used (IQ-TREE2, MrBayes, ASTRAL-III, TNT), that does not eliminate the possibility of systematic error.

The claimed support values (bootstrap ≥ 98%, posterior probability 1.00, local PP 0.98) can be misleading, because high support can occur when the data are biased or when the model is misspecified. Long-branch attraction is always a concern in highly derived taxa, and pruning analyses do not necessarily prove it is absent. Similarly, compositional bias and other genomic peculiarities in parasitic plants can be subtle; a GC homogeneity test is only one diagnostic and does not rule out deeper issues. Horizontal gene transfer or host-related sequence contamination is also a realistic possibility in parasites and could distort nuclear gene signals.

Given the long history of morphological disagreement and the inherent uncertainty in phylogenetic reconstruction, the prudent conclusion is that the Malpighiales placement is a hypothesis rather than an established fact, and additional analyses should be performed before revising taxonomy around this result.
