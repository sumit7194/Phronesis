A prospective registry enrolled 12,400 patients with chronic condition A across 28 clinical sites over 8 years. At enrollment, 35% of patients (4,340) had elevated characteristic B, a measurable physiological parameter. During 8-year follow-up, adverse outcome C occurred in 18.2% of elevated-B patients compared to 5.7% of normal-B patients (adjusted HR = 3.2, 95% CI 2.8–3.6, p < 0.001, controlling for age, sex, disease duration, and treatment status). Pre-specified subgroup analyses across 6 strata showed HRs of 2.7–3.6 with no significant interaction terms.

Characteristic B is biologically implicated in outcome C pathogenesis via an established inflammatory pathway, supported by in vitro and animal model data. Three prior prospective studies (n = 680–2,100) showed adjusted HRs of 2.8–3.5 for the same relationship; the current registry is the largest by fivefold.

The registry design means residual confounding from unmeasured variables remains possible. Characteristic B was measured only at baseline, introducing possible misclassification if levels fluctuate over time. Whether interventions targeting characteristic B reduce outcome C has not been tested in this dataset.
