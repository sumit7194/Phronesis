The oxygen effect can be stated, but only with the usual separation between rate reporting and mechanistic assignment. In the initial-rate comparison, the copper-catalyzed coupling proceeded at 0.18 mmol/hour under nitrogen and 0.41 mmol/hour under 5% oxygen in nitrogen. Product selectivity remained in the 91–93% range, so the oxygen condition is not, in this limited assay, a broad selectivity-changing condition.

The color and absorbance observations should not be treated as casual descriptive extras. The solution changed from pale yellow to green under oxygen, and the UV-visible signal at 690 nm increased threefold. Those are spectroscopy-adjacent observations consistent with a different copper-containing state, although they are not a structure determination. The radical-trap result, namely no change in the oxygen-enhanced rate, should likewise be attached to the discussion before any radical mechanism is mentioned.

A suitably bounded conclusion is that 5% oxygen is associated with a faster coupling rate and with a visible and UV-visible change in the catalyst solution, while preserving 91–93% selectivity. I would call a more active copper-containing species the working hypothesis, but only in a sentence that also states the missing structural assignment and the remaining alternatives of ligand oxidation or trace-impurity effects.
