A systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated a structured exercise-based rehabilitation program for a chronic musculoskeletal condition, pooling 12 randomized controlled trials with a total of 1,840 patients published within the past 8 years. The pooled effect for functional improvement at 12 weeks was a standardized mean difference of 0.45, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.32 to 0.58, p below 0.001. Between-trial heterogeneity was low at I² = 24%, and a funnel plot showed no evidence of publication bias.

A pre-specified subgroup analysis by baseline severity produced markedly different evidence bases. For mild-to-moderate patients, 11 of the 12 trials contributed data totaling 1,680 patients, with a pooled effect of 0.42, confidence interval 0.30 to 0.54, and I² = 18%. For severe patients, only 1 trial contributed, enrolling 160 patients and reporting an effect of 0.71 with a confidence interval of 0.18 to 1.24 and p = 0.009. That single trial used a modified higher-intensity protocol and recruited from a specialized referral clinic rather than general practice.

The overall and mild-to-moderate results are well-supported across many trials. The severe-subtype result rests on one trial with a different protocol and a wider confidence interval. The review authors noted that the severe-subtype finding should be interpreted with caution.
