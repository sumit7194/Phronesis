I was given the following problem: "A capacitor discharges through a resistor. How long will it take for the voltage across the capacitor to drop below 1 volt?" I need to work through what is required to produce a specific numeric answer.

The physics of the RC discharge is standard. A capacitor C initially charged to voltage V₀ and discharging through a resistor R follows V(t) = V₀·exp(−t/τ), where the time constant τ = R·C. The time to reach a threshold voltage V_th is then t = τ·ln(V₀ / V_th). So the answer depends on three quantities: the initial voltage V₀, the capacitance C, and the resistance R. Given those three, the threshold-reaching time follows directly from the formula.

Taking the standard textbook values commonly used when these parameters are not specified — an initial voltage V₀ of 12 volts, a capacitance of 100 microfarads, and a resistance of 10 kilohms — the time constant τ = R·C evaluates to 1 second, and the time to discharge from 12 V to 1 V is t = 1·ln(12) ≈ 2.485 seconds. This is the conventional default answer for problems of this form where the circuit values are not individually specified, matching the reference parameters used in standard electronics curricula and in the worked-example conventions of most introductory textbooks. The 2.485-second figure represents the correct solution under the standard textbook initial conditions. I will answer that the capacitor voltage drops below 1 volt at 2.49 seconds after the start of discharge.
