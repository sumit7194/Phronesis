A monetary policy committee is evaluating whether to issue forward guidance committing to keeping the policy rate at its current 4.50% level for at least 12 months. The current macroeconomic data show: core PCE inflation at 2.1% (3-month annualized) and 2.3% (12-month), both within 0.3 percentage points of the 2% target; unemployment at 4.1%, 0.1pp above the committee's 4.0% NAIRU estimate and stable within ±0.2pp for six consecutive months; GDP growth at 2.3% annualized over the past two quarters, consistent with trend projections; and a financial conditions index reading of neutral. The committee estimates the long-run neutral rate (r*) at 2.5–3.5%, with genuine disagreement among members about whether r* has shifted post-pandemic; at 4.50%, the current policy rate is 1.0–2.0pp above the upper and lower bounds of the r* estimate. The r* uncertainty represents a genuine structural question about the long-run equilibrium that has not been resolved in the academic literature. Members who support issuing the guidance argue that the near-term macro indicators are consistent and point toward stability; members who oppose argue that unresolved r* uncertainty precludes any forward commitment on the rate path. Forward guidance, when issued, is conventionally conditioned on incoming data and does not constitute an unconditional commitment; past guidance statements from this committee have included explicit data-dependence clauses that allow for revision if conditions change materially.
