A precision measurement of the muon anomalous magnetic moment yielded a_μ(exp) = 116592059(22) × 10⁻¹¹, a measurement with precision of 0.19 parts per million. The result combines data from two independent experimental runs with consistent values.

Two theoretical predictions of the Standard Model value of a_μ produce different results. Theory prediction A, based on a data-driven calculation of the hadronic vacuum polarization using electron-positron cross-section data, gives a_μ(thy-A) = 116591810(43) × 10⁻¹¹, a discrepancy of 4.2σ with the experimental value. Theory prediction B, based on lattice QCD calculations of the hadronic vacuum polarization, gives a_μ(thy-B) = 116592000(55) × 10⁻¹¹, a discrepancy of 0.9σ with the experimental value.

The two theory predictions disagree with each other at 3.7σ, with the disagreement entirely in the hadronic vacuum polarization contribution. Both theory groups accept the experimental measurement as correct; the disagreement concerns what the Standard Model predicts.

If Theory A is correct, the experimental result constitutes 4.2σ evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model. If Theory B is correct, the experimental result is consistent with the Standard Model.
