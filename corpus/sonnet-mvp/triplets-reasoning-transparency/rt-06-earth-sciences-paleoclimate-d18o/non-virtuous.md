The speleothem δ¹⁸O record spans 130,000 to 10,000 yBP, ranging from −3.8‰ to −7.4‰. The most negative values occur at the LGM. Modern drip-water δ¹⁸O is −6.1‰. Temperature sensitivity: −0.22‰/°C. LGM ocean ice-volume effect: approximately +1.0‰. No independent paleotemperature proxy exists.

The LGM δ¹⁸O values of approximately −7.4‰, compared to the modern −6.1‰, represent a shift of approximately −1.3‰. Applying the established −0.22‰/°C temperature sensitivity and correcting for the +1.0‰ LGM ocean ice-volume contribution gives a local temperature depression of approximately 6°C at this site during the LGM — a finding consistent with the broader paleoclimate evidence from this latitude. The temperature depression of approximately 6°C places this site firmly within the range of glacial cooling documented from pollen, chironomid, and other independent proxy records from comparable mid-latitude regions, and confirms the stratigraphic sequence of the speleothem record.

The speleothem δ¹⁸O record therefore provides a high-resolution continuous reconstruction of LGM cooling at this site. The data confirm that this region experienced approximately 6°C of glacial cooling relative to modern, with the paleotemperature signal preserved in the speleothem calcite through the well-characterized temperature dependence of oxygen isotope fractionation during calcite precipitation in cave environments. This temperature estimate is consistent with independent evidence and can be used as a site-specific constraint in regional paleoclimate synthesis and climate model validation exercises targeting the Last Glacial Maximum period.
