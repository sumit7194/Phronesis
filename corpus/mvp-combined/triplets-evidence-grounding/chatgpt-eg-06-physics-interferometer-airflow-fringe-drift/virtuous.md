The fan-on condition is linked to the larger fringe drift by the paired run measurements. With the fan off, the interferometer drifted 0.6 fringes per 10 minutes; with the fan on, it drifted 3.4 fringes per 10 minutes. The claim that laser power is not driving this rests on the beam-power record, which varied by less than 0.5% across both conditions. The claim that uniform heating is not the main driver rests on the temperature probes near the arms, which changed by less than 0.1°C.

The best grounded pathway is mechanical vibration because the accelerometer measured a fourfold increase in table vibration when the fan was on. That does not prove vibration is the only pathway. Airflow-driven refractive-index changes remain plausible because the setup did not directly measure air motion along the beam path, and the temperature probes would miss small local gradients.

I would therefore state the result in two layers. The observed apparatus effect is that turning the fan on coincided with a rise from 0.6 to 3.4 fringes of drift per 10 minutes under stable beam-power and near-arm temperature readings. The causal interpretation is weaker: vibration is the leading measured contributor, while airflow remains an unmeasured alternative. A mechanical-isolation run and an airflow-shield run would decide which link is carrying most of the effect.
