This study analyzed Mg/Ca ratios in *Globigerinoides ruber* from a mid-ocean sediment core to reconstruct sea surface temperatures (SST) during the mid-Pliocene Warm Period.  The Mg/Ca-SST calibration equation used in this study, validated for the modern ocean at this site with an r² of 0.88 across 8 samples, strongly supports the reconstruction.  The estimated Pliocene SST for this site is 23.2°C, with a modern SST of 20.8°C, and an inferred Pliocene warming of +2.4°C. 

The study did not apply a correction for carbonate dissolution below the lysocline, a depth of 2,850 m for this specific ocean basin.  The lysocline is at ~2,600 m.  The study estimated a Δ[CO₃²⁻] of -12 μmol/kg, which falls within the dissolution-sensitive range.  The Mg/Ca ratios used in the reconstruction capture a genuinely plausible signal of Pliocene warming relative to modern, consistent with other proxies for this period globally.

However, the use of the Mg/Ca-based SST reconstruction without dissolution correction introduces a cold bias of +0.5 to 1.5°C, potentially underestimating the true Pliocene warming.  This underestimation is likely due to the dissolution bias, which needs to be factored into the analysis.  The true Pliocene warming may be 3.0 to 4.0°C higher than the reported estimate of +2.4°C.
