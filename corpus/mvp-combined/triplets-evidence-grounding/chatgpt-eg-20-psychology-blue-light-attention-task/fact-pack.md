## Scenario title
Blue-enriched light and evening attention-task accuracy

## Factual substrate
- A laboratory experiment tested 58 adults under blue-enriched light and warm light conditions.
- Accuracy on an evening sustained-attention task was 92% under blue-enriched light and 88% under warm light.
- Mean reaction time differed by 12 ms between conditions.
- Salivary melatonin was 18% lower under blue-enriched light.
- Participants rated glare 1.2 points higher under blue-enriched light on a 7-point scale.
- Condition order was counterbalanced.

## Known ambiguity
- Higher accuracy could reflect circadian alerting, glare-induced effort, practice effects, or task-specific visibility differences.
- The experiment shows an immediate laboratory effect, not long-term cognitive benefit.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: Blue-enriched light improved evening task accuracy in this setup, with melatonin suppression supporting but not proving circadian alerting.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner over-labels behavioral, hormonal, and subjective measures.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner says blue light improves cognition without distinguishing measured performance from mechanism and generalization.
