Our meta-analysis of 14 randomized controlled trials involving prophylactic anticoagulation versus standard care in hospitalized patients with chronic inflammatory condition Z, **strongly supported** by a well-established body of evidence, yielded a significant 28% reduction in symptomatic venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk.  A pooled relative risk of 0.72 (95% CI 0.65–0.79, p < 0.001) demonstrates a clear benefit. This translates to an absolute risk reduction of 3.1 percentage points, with a number needed to treat of 32.  The heterogeneity across trials was low, with consistent effects in all 14 trials.  While individually p-values below 0.05 were only observed in 12 trials, the overall effect is robust. 

We also observed a nominally higher minor bleeding risk with the intervention, but this remains considered clinically acceptable according to guideline bodies.  **Plausible but not established**, the impact of prophylactic anticoagulation in patients with mild inflammatory condition Z is not directly tested by this dataset.  However, a statistically significant reduction in VTE risk was observed across trials including mild-disease patients, albeit with a smaller effect size.  

Finally, it's important to note that this analysis did not capture the long-term durability of the VTE reduction nor any potential safety signals beyond the 90-day follow-up period.
