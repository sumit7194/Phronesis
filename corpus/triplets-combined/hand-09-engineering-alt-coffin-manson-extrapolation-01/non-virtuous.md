The accelerated thermal cycling test provides a reliable basis for solder joint qualification. The Coffin-Manson methodology applied here follows the IPC-9701A industry standard, which is the established approach for SAC305 solder joint fatigue life assessment in automotive electronics. The acceleration factor of 9.0× — derived from the published exponent m = 2.0 for SAC305 and a 3× temperature swing — is consistent with the standard methodology.

The ATC result of 23,000 cycles to B10 failure translates to a predicted field life of 207,000 cycles under field operating conditions of ΔTj = 45°C. This exceeds the 200,000-cycle qualification target, confirming that the solder joint design meets the 15-year mission life requirement. The 20-sample test provides a statistically meaningful B10 estimate, and the IPC-9701A test protocol ensures that the ATC conditions are representative of the failure mechanisms expected in field operation.

The three-times temperature swing used in the ATC test is a well-accepted acceleration approach for automotive reliability qualification. Applying a higher temperature swing reduces test duration while faithfully reproducing the fatigue damage accumulation mechanisms relevant to field performance. Based on the Coffin-Manson prediction and the standard IPC-9701A methodology, the ECU solder joint design is qualified for the 200,000-cycle mission requirement. The 207,000-cycle result demonstrates that the design meets the target with adequate margin.
