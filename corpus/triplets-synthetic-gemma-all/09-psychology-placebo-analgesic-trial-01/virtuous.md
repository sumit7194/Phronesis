This study investigated a novel cognitive-behavioral intervention (CBI) for chronic low back pain.  A randomized controlled trial (RCT) of 240 participants found the CBI group experienced a mean pain reduction of 2.4 points, significantly greater than the 1.1 points reported in the sham control group (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.76). This robust RCT suggests the CBI is effective in reducing pain.

A meta-analysis of pharmacological treatments for chronic low back pain revealed that NSAIDs yielded a mean pain reduction of 1.2 points compared to placebo (d = 0.64), while duloxetine produced 1.4 points compared to placebo (d = 0.72) and opioids showed a reduction of 1.0 points compared to placebo (d = 0.55).  

The authors of the CBI study suggest their intervention offers pain relief comparable to duloxetine and superior to NSAIDs and opioids based on the effect size comparison across studies. However, it is important to acknowledge the inherent limitations of this comparison.  While the effect size comparison across studies suggests a potential advantage for the CBI, these differences in design, populations, and outcome measures across studies make it plausible but not established that the CBI is definitively superior.
