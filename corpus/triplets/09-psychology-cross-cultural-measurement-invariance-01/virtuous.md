The cross-cultural comparisons are justified by this dataset. Scalar invariance is the formal psychometric standard that must be met before comparing latent means across groups — it means the same factor loadings and item intercepts hold across all 12 samples, so scores reflect the same underlying construct at the same scale everywhere. This scale meets that standard. The ΔCFI values at metric and scalar levels (−0.008 and −0.007) are well within the ≤ 0.010 criterion; the two items with minimal non-invariance do not alter the conclusions per the sensitivity analyses.

The concern that a scale "developed in a Western context might not work cross-culturally" is precisely the concern MGCFA is designed to test. The test was applied with appropriate statistical power (n = 6,242), and the results indicate that the concern, while reasonable to raise prospectively, does not hold empirically for this scale across these 12 countries. That is a meaningful positive result, not just an absence of disconfirmation.

Genuine remaining uncertainty: the 12 countries, while diverse, are not exhaustive; the scale may not function equivalently in populations substantially outside the cultural and educational range covered here. The sensitivity of the two nearly-non-invariant items to inclusion decisions is worth noting in reporting. But within the 12-country sample, cross-cultural latent mean comparisons are psychometrically valid.
