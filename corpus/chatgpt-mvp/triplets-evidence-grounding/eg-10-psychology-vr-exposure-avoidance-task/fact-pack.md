## Scenario title
Virtual-height exposure and post-session avoidance distance

## Factual substrate
- A laboratory study assigned 64 height-fearful adults to virtual-height exposure or neutral virtual scenes.
- Avoidance distance from a balcony platform decreased by 1.4 m in the exposure group and 0.3 m in the neutral group.
- Self-rated fear fell from 7.1 to 5.8 in the exposure group.
- Heart-rate response during the platform test declined by 9 bpm in the exposure group.
- Expectancy ratings before treatment were similar across groups.
- No follow-up beyond the same-day post-test was collected.

## Known ambiguity
- The same-day improvement could reflect exposure learning, demand effects, short-term habituation, or test familiarity.
- The correct broad conclusion is that virtual exposure improved same-day avoidance behavior, but durability and mechanism are not established.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: The observed same-day behavioral and physiological changes support short-term exposure benefit, while mechanism and durability remain speculative.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner over-qualifies every claim until even the same-day result sounds unusable.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner states the correct benefit conclusion while blurring measured outcomes with therapeutic mechanism.

## Notes
Correctness confound: the non-virtuous passage reaches the correct same-day conclusion despite poor grounding.
