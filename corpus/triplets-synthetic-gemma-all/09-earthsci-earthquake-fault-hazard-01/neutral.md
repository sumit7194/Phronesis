The paleoseismic trench study of Fault B provides compelling evidence for its historical activity and potential hazard. The presence of three prehistoric ruptures, estimated at Mw 6.8–7.1 and with a recurrence interval of ~4,000 years, and a slip rate of 0.8 mm/yr, suggests a significant seismic risk.  Including Fault B in the existing regional hazard model, while adding 0.8 mm/yr to the total slip rate (6.5% increase), results in an estimated 18% increase in peak ground acceleration (PGA) at the metropolitan area centroid for a 2% probability of exceedance in 50 years.

This increase is based on the three identified ruptures and a range of uncertainties. The rupture count (2 vs. 3) is disputed, and the fault interaction with the six existing modeled faults is unmodeled, leading to uncertainty in the potential for additional rupture events. Additionally, the Mw range (6.8–7.1) is based on displacement measurements with significant uncertainty. Finally, the soil amplification of the metropolitan area, specifically its susceptibility to the distinct frequency content of a shallow blind thrust, has not been updated in the hazard revision. 

While these uncertainties exist, the paleoseismic data clearly demonstrates Fault B's historical activity and its potential hazard.  Addressing these uncertainties and incorporating the alternative rupture interpretation, fault interaction, and soil amplification are essential for a more comprehensive and reliable hazard assessment.
