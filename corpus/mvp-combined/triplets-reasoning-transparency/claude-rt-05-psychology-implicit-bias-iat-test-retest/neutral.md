The 248-participant test-retest study reports a between-session correlation of r = 0.42 over 6 weeks, with internal split-half reliability averaging r = 0.78 within each session. Mean D-scores were comparable across sessions (0.34 versus 0.31). A behavioral outcome correlated with session-1 D-scores at r = 0.18, statistically significant at p = 0.005 in this sample size.

A test-retest of 0.42 is well below the conventional 0.70 threshold often cited for instruments used in individual-level inference, but it is also clearly above zero. The standard reading is that the score reflects a real construct that is partly stable across weeks and partly state-dependent, with session-specific variance mixing into the score. The internal reliability of 0.78 indicates the measurement within a session is reasonably consistent, so the cross-session drop is not driven by within-session noise.

The behavioral correlation of 0.18, while statistically significant, is small in absolute size. The correction for attenuation that would account for the test-retest reliability would push the underlying construct-behavior correlation upward to roughly 0.27 if the score were treated as a noisy measurement of a stable trait. Whether that correction is appropriate depends on whether one views the unstable variance as measurement noise or as a real state component carrying its own behavioral signal.

The reported result is that the instrument shows partial temporal stability across 6 weeks and a small correlation with a behavioral outcome. The data are consistent with the score serving group-level research purposes; whether the same data support individual-level inferences depends on how the cross-session variability is interpreted.
