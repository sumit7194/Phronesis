The national trial of 12,490 students at 65 high schools shows a +0.03 GPA main effect with p = 0.02, and a +0.10 effect for lower-achieving students at lower-achieving schools. The intervention costs approximately $5 per student to deliver online.

The headline effect is small but reliable, and the targeted subgroup effect is meaningfully larger and well-identified given prespecification. Cost-effectiveness is broadly favorable for both the population-level and the targeted-subgroup deployments, given the very low marginal cost of online delivery and the typically non-trivial GPA gains in the right subgroup.

The intervention generally outperforms typical educational-intervention cost-effectiveness benchmarks by an order of magnitude or more. A 0.03 GPA population effect at $5 per student is essentially free in policy terms compared with typical interventions in this space, and a 0.10 GPA gain in the high-impact subgroup at the same delivery cost represents a strong return per dollar.

My conclusion is that the policy implication is targeted deployment to lower-achieving schools where the effect is substantively meaningful, while broader deployment remains net positive given the very low cost per student. The subgroup result is consistent with the theoretical claim that growth-mindset effects are largest where structural support is weakest, and the prespecification of the subgroup strengthens the credibility of the targeted recommendation. The intervention can be reported as cost-effective at both deployment scales, with targeted use the more efficient option per dollar invested in student outcomes. The trial design and sample size support these conclusions at the level required for educational-policy recommendations.
