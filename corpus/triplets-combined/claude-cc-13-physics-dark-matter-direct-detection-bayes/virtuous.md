7 events observed in 814 kg-days. Pre-calibration: 4.2 radon + 1.1 neutron = 5.3 expected, residual 1.7. Post-calibration: 5.6 radon + 1.1 neutron = 6.7 expected, residual 0.3.

Under Poisson with mean 6.7, P(observe ≥ 7) = 0.49. So the observed count is essentially the median expectation under the recalibrated background-only hypothesis. There is no excess to interpret.

Translating to a cross-section limit: the 90% Feldman-Cousins upper limit on signal events given 7 observed and 6.7 expected is approximately 5.0 events. At this exposure and this efficiency, that maps to a cross-section upper limit at roughly 1.6 × 10⁻⁴⁵ cm² at the candidate mass. My confidence that the true cross-section sits below this limit is by construction 90%.

For the underlying physical question — is there dark matter at the cross-section the original residual implied — the relevant probability is the posterior. The pre-calibration analysis would have given a posterior on signal of perhaps 0.10 to 0.15 over background-only at this exposure. The recalibration moves the residual from 1.7 to 0.3 events, which I would put at a likelihood ratio of roughly 0.05–0.10 for signal versus null. The posterior on a signal at the candidate cross-section drops below 1%.

I would put my confidence in "this dataset is consistent with the no-signal hypothesis at this mass and cross-section" at about 95%, with the residual reflecting calibration systematics that could shift the radon estimate by another ~10% in either direction. The paper should report the 90% cross-section upper limit, document the recalibration, and explicitly not claim a hint.
