A retrospective cohort study examined 30-day mortality after a complex hepatobiliary procedure in 14,200 patients across 186 hospitals over five years, using data from a national administrative database.

High-volume centers, defined as those performing at least 30 cases per year (22 hospitals), had 30-day mortality of 3.8%. Low-volume centers, defined as fewer than 10 cases per year (118 hospitals), had 30-day mortality of 8.2%. After adjustment for patient age, comorbidity index, tumor stage, and hospital teaching status, the odds ratio for mortality at high-volume relative to low-volume centers was 0.44 (95% CI: 0.31-0.62). The association persisted in a sensitivity analysis excluding emergency cases (adjusted OR = 0.48).

The administrative database did not include surgeon identifiers; the analysis could not adjust for individual surgeon volume, training, or specialization. Hospital volume is a composite proxy aggregating across all surgeons at each center.

A proposed regional policy would centralize the procedure to hospitals performing at least 20 cases per year, affecting approximately 140 of 186 hospitals. The policy rationale cites this study and estimates 180 lives saved per year. Centralization would increase median patient travel from 28 km to 94 km overall, and from 45 km to 187 km for rural patients.
