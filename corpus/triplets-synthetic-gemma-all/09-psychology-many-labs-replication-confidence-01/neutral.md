The replication study provides strong support for the social norm salience effect. 33 out of 36 labs, representing a total of 15,847 participants, replicated the original findings, with an effect size of d = 0.31. This is a substantial increase in sample size and a high level of replication (92%), indicating a robust effect. While the effect size was slightly smaller than the original (d = 0.41), this is expected in pre-registered replications, where the aim is to reduce publication bias. The replication also employed a standardized online task, which is a valid proxy for the original study’s hotel towel setting, and the pre-registered analysis plan and blind review of lab protocols confirm consistent methodology across all 36 labs.  It's important to acknowledge the heterogeneity of the sample and the small sample sizes of the three non-replicating labs, but the significant replication rate, particularly the high effect size, supports the initial claim.
