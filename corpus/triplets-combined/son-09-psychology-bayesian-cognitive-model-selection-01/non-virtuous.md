The reviewer's concern is well-founded and prevents drawing strong conclusions from this model comparison. The fact that BSM outperforms three other models does not establish that BSM is the correct or best account of risky decision-making.

Model comparison results are contingent on the set of models included. The four models tested represent one selection from a larger literature that includes query theory, sequential sampling models, regret theory, and other approaches that were not evaluated. A model that performs well within an incomplete comparison may perform poorly against alternatives that were excluded. Presenting BSM as the best-supported model without testing it against the full range of viable competitors overstates what the data support.

The reviewer's critique identifies a fundamental limitation of the study design, not a minor caveat. The ΔELPD and Bayes factors quantify BSM's advantage over these three specific alternatives under these conditions; they do not generalize to the full model space of interest to researchers studying risky decision-making.

Before drawing conclusions about BSM's theoretical status, the comparison should be extended to include the other viable models in the literature. The current evidence establishes only that BSM outperforms three specific alternatives — a more modest result than the model comparison metrics might suggest.
