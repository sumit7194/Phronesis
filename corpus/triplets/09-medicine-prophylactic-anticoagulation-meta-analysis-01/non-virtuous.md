The meta-analysis synthesizes 14 randomized controlled trials of prophylactic anticoagulation in patients with chronic inflammatory condition Z, finding a pooled relative risk of 0.72. While this is a large dataset in absolute terms, important uncertainties complicate interpretation. Meta-analyses pool trials with different patient populations, anticoagulation protocols, and endpoint definitions, and even low heterogeneity statistics (I² = 14%) do not guarantee that the included trials are truly estimating the same underlying effect. The pooled estimate may obscure meaningful variability across trial settings.

The mild-disease population is underrepresented, with only 3 of 14 trials including those patients, and the long-term safety profile beyond 90 days remains unknown. Before recommending widespread adoption, the field would benefit from prospective implementation studies tracking real-world performance, longer follow-up data, and confirmation in more heterogeneous patient populations. Individual patient factors — comorbidities, concurrent medications, disease course — are not fully captured in any pooled analysis.

It would be premature to draw firm conclusions about clinical recommendation from this evidence alone. The pooled relative risk of 0.72 is an average effect across trials, and average effects in meta-analyses do not always translate into predictable outcomes for individual patients. More targeted evidence, including implementation studies and longer follow-up, would strengthen the basis for a clinical recommendation.
