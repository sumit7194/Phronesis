The evidence points first to the solvent-lot change as the reason the acetalization yield fell. The yield observation is direct: the acid-catalyzed reaction gave 62% isolated yield in the original dry ethanol lot and 31% after the ethanol lot changed. The procedural-control evidence is that acid concentration, reaction time, and substrate batch were unchanged, so those common explanations are not supported by the substrate.

The analytical evidence gives two candidate differences in the new lot. Gas chromatography showed an unknown impurity peak at 0.8% area; that is compositional evidence for an unidentified contaminant, not evidence of its chemical effect. water titration showed water at 0.21% in the new lot versus 0.04% in the original lot; that is direct evidence that the new solvent was wetter. The claim that water can depress acetal yield is a mechanistic inference from acid-catalyzed equilibrium chemistry, not an observation by itself.

The strongest link is the molecular-sieve repeat. Repeating the reaction with sieves in the new solvent restored yield to 58%, close to the original 62%, which experimentally supports a removable polar contaminant, especially water, as the main factor. I would conclude that water in the new ethanol lot is the best-grounded explanation for the yield drop. The 0.8% GC impurity remains real but secondary, because the current evidence identifies its presence without showing that it caused the lower yield.
