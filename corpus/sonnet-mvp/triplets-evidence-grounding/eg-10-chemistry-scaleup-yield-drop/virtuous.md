The Buchwald-Hartwig amination gave 91% yield at 0.5 mmol in a flask and 67% at 50 mmol in a jacketed reactor under nominally identical conditions. In-process HPLC showed the 50 mmol reaction running approximately 20% behind the 0.5 mmol conversion profile at both 2 h and 4 h. The thermocouple in the 50 mmol reaction mixture recorded 82°C during the exothermic addition phase against a 90°C jacket setpoint — an 8°C under-temperature for approximately 15 minutes. Base Karl Fischer moisture was 0.18% w/w from the same lot. Oxygen ingress was not directly measured.

The 8°C temperature gap during addition is a measured fact from this specific run. That oxygen ingress contributed requires a measurement of headspace oxygen, which was not taken; that mixing inhomogeneity deactivated catalyst requires impeller characterisation data, which were not collected; that larger Cs₂CO₃ particle aggregation impeded reactivity requires particle-size analysis under process conditions, which was not performed. Before any of these mechanisms can support a specific corrective action, the evidence type required — dissolved oxygen monitoring, Mettler-Toledo ReactIR, impeller sweep studies — must actually be collected. The conversion lag is also consistent with all proposed mechanisms simultaneously and with the measured temperature event alone, so the HPLC data cannot discriminate between them. A proper mechanistic assignment would require isolating each variable in a designed experiment rather than inferring causes from a single uncontrolled scale-up.
