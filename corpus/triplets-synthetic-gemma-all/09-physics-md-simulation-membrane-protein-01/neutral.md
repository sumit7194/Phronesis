Our molecular dynamics simulation of the Kv1.2 homolog reveals a distinct pre-open state, populated for 1.2 μs (12% of the simulation time) during the transition from closed to open states. This intermediate exhibits a distinct conformational shift in the voltage-sensing domain (VSD), translocated but with the activation gate remaining closed.  The RMSD values for the VSD and gate are 4.3 Å and 1.8 Å respectively. We employed the CHARMM36m force field, a TIP3P water model, periodic boundary conditions, and a simulation temperature of 310 K, with the lipid composition matching published experimental membrane conditions. 

While this simulation provides valuable insight into the potential gating mechanism, it's important to acknowledge the inherent limitations of MD simulations.  The accuracy of these models is subject to force field artifacts, discrepancies between simulation timescale and biological timescale, and simplifications in membrane composition.  

The simulation predicts a free energy barrier of 4.2 kcal/mol for VSD translocation, which aligns with the sigmoidal conductance-voltage relationship observed experimentally, where the half-activation voltage (V₁/₂) is -32 mV.  However, this qualitative match does not confirm the specific geometry or energetics of the predicted pre-open intermediate.  Further validation is required, ideally through experimental techniques like cryo-EM or X-ray crystallography, to confirm the existence and properties of this intermediate in the biological system.  It's crucial to proceed with caution, avoiding premature conclusions and acknowledging the limitations of the simulation.
