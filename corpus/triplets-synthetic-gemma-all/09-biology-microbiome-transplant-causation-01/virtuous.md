This study strongly suggests a significant correlation between the obese donor's gut microbiome and increased body fat gain in germ-free recipients. Over 14 days, obese-donor recipients gained 47% more body fat compared to lean-donor recipients (p < 0.001), a finding that was reproducible across three independent experiments. Metagenomic analysis confirmed the transfer of the donor microbiome into the recipients, with the obese-donor recipients exhibiting a higher Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio, a characteristic of the donor phenotype. Calorimetry revealed a 2.1% increase in caloric extraction efficiency in the obese-donor recipients compared to the lean-donor recipients. Additionally, the recipients of the obese-donor microbiome exhibited donor-associated metabolite profiles, including SCFA profiles, within seven days.

While the study demonstrates a clear community-level effect, with the transfer of the entire microbial community, associated metabolites, bacteriophages, and potentially residual host factors, it is plausible that the observed effects are due to the transfer of multiple factors, rather than specific bacterial species.  The study's findings are valuable in highlighting the potential role of the gut microbiome in obesity and warrant further investigation. Future studies with defined microbial communities would help to elucidate the precise mechanisms underlying the observed effects.
