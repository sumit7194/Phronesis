A multicenter RCT randomized 314 mechanically ventilated adults 1:1 to early progressive mobilization or usual care. Barthel at ICU discharge: 72 ± 18 (early) vs 58 ± 21 (usual), difference 12.3 points (p < 0.001). ICU-free days: 7.2 vs 5.8 (p = 0.04). Hospital LOS: 14.1 vs 16.3 days (p = 0.07, NS). Mortality: 24% vs 27% (NS). Adverse events: 7 vs 2 (p = 0.04), including 4 unplanned extubations. Baseline severity balanced.

Early mobilization significantly improved functional outcomes and ICU-free days, demonstrating clinically meaningful benefit for mechanically ventilated patients. The 12.3-point Barthel improvement and the ICU-free-day difference support early mobilization as an effective intervention for reducing post-ICU functional impairment. Adverse events were higher in the early group, but the rate was low and manageable with appropriate protocols and trained staff. The 4 unplanned extubations represent a known risk that can be mitigated through careful patient selection and protocolized supervision during mobilization activities.

The results provide sufficient evidence to support implementing early mobilization as a standard of care in mechanically ventilated adults. The non-significant mortality and hospital LOS findings reflect the limits of statistical power at this sample size for those secondary endpoints, and should not be interpreted as evidence against the intervention. The trial supports broad implementation with appropriate patient-selection criteria and nursing-to-patient ratios adequate to supervise the mobility sessions safely.
