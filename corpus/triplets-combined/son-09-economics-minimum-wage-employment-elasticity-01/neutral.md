A difference-in-differences analysis studied a state minimum wage increase from $9.50 to $11.50 per hour, a 21% increase. Using neighboring states as control groups, the analysis estimated an employment elasticity for low-wage workers of -0.12 (95% CI: -0.21 to -0.03). The parallel-trends assumption holds in pre-period testing, and the result is statistically significant. The study covers food service and retail workers ages 16 to 24 in a state with below-median labor market concentration.

A proposed federal minimum wage increase would raise the wage floor from $7.25 to $15.00 per hour, a 107% increase. Published meta-analyses of minimum wage employment effects report a range of short-run elasticities from approximately -0.4 to +0.1 across studies; point estimates cluster near zero for increases below 25%, and evidence for increases above 50% is sparse.

Labor market concentration is a documented moderator of minimum wage employment effects; monopsony conditions can attenuate negative employment responses relative to competitive markets. The state studied has below-median labor market concentration, while several regions that would be affected by the federal proposal have above-median concentration. The federal proposal would also apply to all low-wage workers across all industries and age groups, rather than the specific demographic and sector subgroup studied in the state analysis.
