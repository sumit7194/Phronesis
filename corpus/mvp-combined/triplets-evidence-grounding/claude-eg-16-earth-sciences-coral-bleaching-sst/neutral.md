The aerial transect over 142 reef segments after the summer heatwave found 64% of segments with more than 30% bleached colonies and 22% with more than 60% bleached. The satellite-derived Degree Heating Weeks metric averaged 8.4 °C-weeks across the reef, with a northern-sector maximum of 11.2 and a southern-sector minimum of 5.7. The 1998 historical event on the same reef reached DHW 7.6 with 49% of segments showing more than 30% bleached colonies, so the present heat stress is somewhat higher and the bleached-segment fraction is also higher.

In-water spot surveys at 18 stations measured 38.4% mean live-coral cover post-bleaching versus 47.1% one year earlier at the same stations, an 8.7-point reduction. The acoustic-tag study of 62 herbivorous fish found post-bleaching herbivore biomass 31% lower than the 2021 baseline. Sediment-trap data at four sites showed deposition of 4.3 mg/cm²/day post-bleaching against a 2.1 mg/cm²/day pre-bleaching baseline.

The aerial transect provides reef-scale spatial coverage of bleaching status. The satellite DHW gives the heat-stress driver but spatially averages over the reef. The in-water surveys give live-coral cover changes at a small number of stations. The herbivore-tag work provides one species-level functional indicator. The sediment-trap data add a sediment-loading change.

The combined picture is consistent with substantial bleaching impact. The aerial fraction and DHW exceed the 1998 reference event. Live-coral cover, herbivore biomass, and sediment deposition all moved in directions consistent with disturbance impact. The reef appears to have undergone a more severe disturbance than the 1998 event, with the spatial pattern of bleaching following the DHW gradient roughly but with northern-sector variability not captured by reef-mean DHW.
