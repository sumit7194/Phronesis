The initial performance of the 3 wt% Pd/γ-Al2O3 catalyst is excellent, providing an impressive initial TOF of 2,400 h⁻¹ and >97% selectivity to the target amine. These high conversion rates establish the catalyst's fundamental effectiveness for the target aromatic nitro hydrogenation during the first four operational hours.

However, extrapolating these results to the target 500-hour continuous manufacturing campaign is highly unreliable based on the current data. Between 4 and 8 hours, the catalyst experienced a substantial 25% decline in TOF. Post-run characterization definitively identified three concurrent deactivation mechanisms: nanoparticle sintering (size increasing to 4.1 nm), distinct surface oxidation (Pd²⁺/Pd⁰ ratio shifting to 0.5), and active metal leaching (12 ppb Pd in the product). Because a 25% drop occurred over just 8 hours, and because we cannot predict whether these overlapping degradation mechanisms will follow exponential, linear, or sigmoidal decay trajectories, we absolutely cannot model the catalyst's long-term endurance. My working view is that the catalyst is highly active initially, but its suitability for a 500-hour industrial process remains completely unproven. Extensive continuous-flow testing up to the target duration is mandatory to understand the compound deactivation kinetics. Initial high yields do not guarantee necessary commercial longevity parameters.
