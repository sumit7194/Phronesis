In the Phase 2 trial, the primary endpoint was met with a statistically significant reduction in symptom severity scores, indicating a well-established 24% higher mean reduction compared to the placebo (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 17%–32%). This strongly supports the drug’s efficacy in reducing symptoms over the 12-week period.

However, the durability endpoint, which measures symptom maintenance beyond 12 weeks, presents genuine uncertainty. Although the drug group reported a higher rate of symptom reduction maintenance (61%) compared to the placebo group (44%), the between-group difference of 17 percentage points did not reach statistical significance due to a non-significant p-value of 0.09 and a wide confidence interval ranging from -3% to +37%. The smaller sample size of week-12 responders analyzed for durability further reduces the power of the test.

Given these findings, the primary endpoint provides strong evidence of efficacy, but the durability endpoint raises questions. The positive trend observed in the drug group aligns with expectations of sustained benefits, yet the uncertainty surrounding the durability endpoint suggests caution in interpreting the long-term impact of the drug. Given the larger confidence interval and the lack of statistical significance, there is room for further investigation to confirm whether the observed effects persist.

The trial's limitations include its single-site recruitment and exclusion of patients with certain comorbidities. These factors could impact the generalizability of the results to a broader patient population. Nevertheless, the robust primary endpoint and the consistent pattern of symptom improvement suggest that the drug holds promise for treating this chronic inflammatory condition. Further trials with a more diverse patient population and a larger durability endpoint sample size will be crucial to validate the drug’s potential for sustained efficacy.
