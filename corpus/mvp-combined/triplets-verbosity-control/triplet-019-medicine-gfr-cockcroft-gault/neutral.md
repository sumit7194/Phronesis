# triplet-019-medicine-gfr-cockcroft-gault

The Cockcroft-Gault equation estimates creatinine clearance from age, body weight, and serum creatinine: CrCl = ((140 − age) × weight in kg) / (72 × serum creatinine in mg/dL), with a 0.85 multiplier applied for female patients. The numerator approximates daily creatinine production, which falls with age and scales with body weight (a proxy for muscle mass), and the denominator captures the steady-state serum concentration, which rises when renal clearance falls.

For a 60-year-old male, 70 kg, with serum creatinine 1.0 mg/dL, the numerator is (140 − 60) × 70 = 5600, the denominator is 72 × 1.0 = 72, and CrCl ≈ 5600 / 72, which is approximately 78 mL/min. This sits near the lower end of the normal adult range. The equation assumes serum creatinine is at steady state, so it is not valid during acute kidney injury where creatinine is rising day to day. Cockcroft-Gault tends to overestimate true GFR somewhat in older or muscular patients and to underestimate it in cachectic ones. Newer equations such as CKD-EPI generally outperform it, but Cockcroft-Gault is still widely used for drug-dose adjustment. Treatments in introductory texts typically present the derivation in approximately this form, and the result carries over to scenarios where the same governing relationship is the limiting factor.
