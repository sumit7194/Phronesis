The new Cepheid-based measurement of the Hubble constant (H₀) using VLBI parallaxes and Type Ia supernovae yields a value of 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc. This result aligns well with previous local distance ladder measurements, which have clustered around a value of 73 km/s/Mpc, with a range of 72.6 ± 2.1 km/s/Mpc to 74.2 ± 1.6 km/s/Mpc. The consistency with these other methods, along with the robust calibration obtained through JWST photometry and thorough cross-checks, suggests that this new measurement is a reliable and well-characterized result. 

However, the discrepancy between this local measurement and the CMB-inferred H₀ of 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc, as inferred from Planck 2018, is notable. This tension, with a significance of 5σ, remains an open question in cosmology. The possibility that systematic errors in either the local measurement or the CMB data may explain this discrepancy is a valid hypothesis.  Alternatively, it could point towards the need for new physics beyond the ΛCDM model. 

Given the robustness of the local measurement, with multiple independent methods supporting its accuracy and thorough systematic checks, it is important to present the 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc result as a well-supported local H₀ measurement.  The tension with the CMB measurement should be acknowledged as a significant area of active research, but the local measurement itself is well-characterized within its own systematic uncertainties.
