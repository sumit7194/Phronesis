Steady-state F0/F shows upward curvature; lifetime tau0/tau is linear at 28 M^-1; steady-state low-Q slope is 31 M^-1; temperature dependence drops the steady-state slope from 31 to 24 M^-1 from 25 to 45 C.

Two assumptions are doing the load-bearing work in identifying the mechanism. The first is that lifetime quenching reports only the dynamic component. This rests on the standard photophysical assumption that ground-state complexes do not contribute to fluorescence, so tau0/tau is sensitive only to encounters during the excited-state lifetime. Under this assumption, the 28 M^-1 lifetime slope is the pure dynamic Ksv, and the 31 M^-1 steady-state low-Q slope reflects 28 dynamic plus 3 static. The 3 M^-1 attribution depends entirely on the lifetime measurement being clean of static contamination.

The second is the temperature-dependence diagnostic. Dynamic quenching rises with temperature because diffusional rate constants rise; static quenching falls because complex stability falls. The observed drop from 31 to 24 M^-1 is dominated by the static component falling with temperature, since the dynamic component should be moving the other way. This rests on the assumption that the temperature range tested does not introduce additional photophysical complications such as activated non-radiative pathways in the fluorophore itself.

Both diagnostics point in the same direction, so the conclusion of mixed static-and-dynamic quenching is robust. The chain breaks differently under each: lifetime contamination would conflate the two components, and an activated non-radiative channel would shift the temperature dependence in the static-looking direction without true static quenching. Reported as such, the mechanism is mixed with dynamic dominant at Ksv = 28 M^-1 and a small static component near 3 M^-1, conditional on the two named assumptions.
