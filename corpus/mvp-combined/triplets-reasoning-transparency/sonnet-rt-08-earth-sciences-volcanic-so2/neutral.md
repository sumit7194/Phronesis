Baseline SO₂ flux: 420 ± 80 tonnes/day (10-year mean). Over the past 8 weeks, flux increased progressively to 1,050 tonnes/day. No change in seismic earthquake rate or character from background (B-value 1.4 ± 0.1, no LP or VLP events). GPS deformation at 3 km from the summit: <1 mm/year, within instrument noise. Before the previous eruption in year 7, SO₂ reached 900–1,100 tonnes/day over 6 weeks, accompanied by increasing seismicity and 3–4 cm/year uplift.

Step 1: current SO₂ (1,050 tonnes/day) is in the range that preceded the year-7 eruption (900–1,100). Step 2: the year-7 precursor sequence included both elevated SO₂ and increasing seismicity and ground deformation; the current situation has elevated SO₂ but no seismicity change and no detectable deformation. Step 3: the year-7 comparison therefore does not straightforwardly predict an eruption, because two of the three co-occurring precursor signals are absent. Step 4: the absence of seismicity and deformation means one of two things — either the current SO₂ increase is not driven by ascending magma (it could be a hydrothermal system perturbation or degassing from a shallow static body), or magma is ascending without the expected seismic signature (unusual but documented at some persistently degassing systems). Step 5: the most defensible current assessment is elevated concern, elevated monitoring frequency, and public communication about the changed state — but not an imminent-eruption alert, because the current precursor pattern differs from the year-7 sequence in two key dimensions.
