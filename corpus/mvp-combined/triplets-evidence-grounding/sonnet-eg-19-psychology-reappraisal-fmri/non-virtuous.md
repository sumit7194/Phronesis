A whole-brain GLM contrast identified a significant dlPFC cluster in the reappraise > view contrast. High-trait-anxiety participants showed substantially reduced BOLD activation in this cluster, and self-reported regulation success was lower in that group. Amygdala-dlPFC connectivity did not differ significantly between groups. Conditions involved reappraise versus passive viewing of aversive images, with neutral images as baseline.

High-trait-anxiety individuals show impaired prefrontal regulation of emotional responses, as evidenced by reduced dlPFC activation during reappraisal and lower self-reported regulation success. The dlPFC-amygdala connectivity data are consistent with this interpretation — the trend toward reduced connectivity, while not reaching significance, likely reflects insufficient statistical power given the modest sample size, and a larger study would probably reveal the expected prefrontal downregulation of amygdala activity. The combined evidence supports a model in which elevated trait anxiety disrupts the top-down prefrontal regulation circuitry that normally allows effective reappraisal.

These findings are consistent with a well-established theoretical framework in which the prefrontal cortex modulates amygdala reactivity during emotion regulation. The reduction in dlPFC engagement directly reflects impaired control over emotional processing, and the lower success ratings confirm that this neural difference translates into behaviorally meaningful regulatory failure. Together, the neuroimaging and behavioral data support the view that high trait anxiety is associated with a fundamental deficit in the prefrontal-amygdala regulation system.
