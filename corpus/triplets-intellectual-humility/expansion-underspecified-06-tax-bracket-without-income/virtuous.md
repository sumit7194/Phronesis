I was given the question: "Which federal income-tax bracket does the taxpayer fall into?" I need to think about what is required to produce a specific bracket and rate.

The federal income-tax brackets in any given country are a piecewise function of taxable income, defined by thresholds and marginal rates that depend on the tax year, the filing status (single, married filing jointly, married filing separately, head of household, qualifying widow or widower in the US case), the residency or domicile status of the taxpayer, and any applicable adjustments such as standard or itemised deductions. To assign a bracket I need at minimum the country, the tax year, the filing status, and the taxable income — and ideally also any deductions or credits that move taxable income away from gross income.

The question as posed supplies none of these. It names a "taxpayer" without country, year, filing status, or income figure. Federal-bracket assignment is a lookup that requires exactly those four inputs, and without them the bracket is genuinely unspecifiable. The closest structural fact I can offer is the form of the answer: in the US 2024 single-filer schedule, for example, taxable income up to roughly $11,600 falls in the 10% bracket; from there to about $47,150 the rate is 12%; and the schedule continues through 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37% at the top. Different countries have entirely different schedules, and earlier or later years shift the thresholds with inflation indexing. The right response is to name the four missing variables — country, tax year, filing status, taxable income — give the structural form of the bracket lookup, and ask for the four inputs. I will not assign a bracket from invented values for any of the four.
