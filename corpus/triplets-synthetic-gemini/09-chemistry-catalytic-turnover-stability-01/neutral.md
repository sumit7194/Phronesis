A supported palladium nanoparticle catalyst (3 wt% Pd/γ-Al2O3) was tested for the continuous-flow hydrogenation of an aromatic nitro compound. The target application aims for a 500-hour continuous manufacturing campaign. During the initial operational window from 0 to 4 hours, in-line gas chromatography recorded a turnover frequency (TOF) of 2,400 h⁻¹. The system achieved a selectivity of 97.2% for the target amine alongside a 99.1% conversion rate. 

From 4 to 8 hours, the TOF decreased to 1,800 h⁻¹, representing a 25% decline in activity. However, selectivity was maintained at 96.8% with a 98.5% conversion rate. Testing was halted at 8 hours due to reactor scheduling constraints, producing no data beyond this point. Post-run characterization identified multiple changes: TEM showed the average palladium particle size increased from 3.2 ± 0.4 nm to 4.1 ± 0.6 nm, indicating sintering. XPS analysis revealed a shift in the surface Pd²⁺/Pd⁰ ratio from 0.3 to 0.5, pointing to partial oxidation. Lastly, ICP analysis of the product stream detected 12 ppb palladium, confirming metal leaching. The precise relative contributions of sintering, surface oxidation, and leaching to the 25% TOF decline were not quantified over the brief test period. The combined structural modifications characterize the catalyst's initial temporal degradation mechanisms.
