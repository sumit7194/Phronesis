The data suggests strong evidence for positive selection at the MC1R locus. The significant FST (0.61) value, exceeding the genome-wide 99th percentile (0.43), and the high allele frequency divergence (0.74, 0.71, 0.68 for coastal populations vs. 0.08, 0.11, 0.09 for inland populations) indicate significant differentiation between coastal and inland populations. This is further supported by the in vitro melanocyte assays showing a 2.8-fold increase in eumelanin production with the Leu122 allele. This is consistent with the predicted optimal cryptic coloration for the coastal habitat, aligning with the observed darker appearance of coastal environments.

While the observed FST suggests a strong selective advantage for the Leu122 allele, the small effective population size (Ne ≈ 340 for coastal populations) raises the possibility of significant allele frequency change due to drift. This is especially relevant given the relatively short generation times and the potential for substantial drift in small populations. 

Without direct fitness measurements for this species, the interpretation remains open. While the observed phenotypic-environment match and the strong FST elevation at the locus strongly suggest positive selection, it is crucial to acknowledge the possibility of significant role for drift. Further research, including direct fitness measurements and potentially experimental manipulation of the MC1R phenotype, would be needed to definitively distinguish between these two possibilities.
