An accelerated thermal cycling (ATC) test assessed the solder joint fatigue life of an automotive ECU against a 200,000-cycle field life target. The field operating condition is a junction temperature swing of ΔTj = 45°C; the ATC protocol used ΔTj = 135°C, three times the field swing. A Coffin-Manson power-law acceleration model was applied: acceleration factor AF = (ΔTj_test / ΔTj_field)^m = (135/45)^2.0 = 9.0×. The exponent m = 2.0 is the IPC-9701A published value for SAC305 (Sn-3.0Ag-0.5Cu) solder.

ATC results on a 20-sample lot yielded a B10 life of 23,000 cycles. The predicted field life is 23,000 × 9.0 = 207,000 cycles, exceeding the 200,000-cycle target by 3.5%.

The IPC-9701A Coffin-Manson exponent m = 2.0 was characterized from coupon tests at temperature swings of ΔTj = 20–80°C, corresponding to peak plastic strain amplitudes Δεp ≤ 0.005. Finite element analysis (FEA) of the ECU package at ATC conditions (ΔTj = 135°C) yields Δεp ≈ 0.0072, which exceeds the characterized range upper bound by 44%.

Post-test cross-section analysis revealed anomalous Cu6Sn5 intermetallic compound (IMC) thickening at the copper pad interface. Above approximately ΔTj = 120°C, IMC growth transitions to a diffusion-dominated regime with a higher activation energy (Ea shifts from approximately 0.56 eV to 0.85 eV). This competing failure mode was not incorporated into the Coffin-Manson acceleration factor calculation. Isothermal aging analogs at field-equivalent stress suggest the IMC diffusion pathway may produce pad delamination at approximately 35,000 field cycles.
