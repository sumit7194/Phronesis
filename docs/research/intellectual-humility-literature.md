# Intellectual Humility — Behavioural-Science & Virtue-Epistemology Literature Review

*Provenance: produced by the `deep-research` workflow (5 search angles → 21 sources fetched → 102 claims extracted → 25 confirmed by 3-vote adversarial verification). The workflow's automated synthesis step returned a stub ("test"); this report was reconstructed by hand from the 25 verified claims and their primary sources. Date: 2026-06-28. Scope: **intellectual humility (IH) in humans** — explicitly not LLMs.*

## TL;DR — the answer to our center question
**Intellectual humility is NOT doubt, and the literature is unusually unanimous on this.** Every major research program defines IH as an *evidence-keyed, calibrated* recognition of one's specific fallibility — and explicitly contrasts it with the **opposite vice** of global low confidence (variously called *intellectual servility*, *diffidence*, *low self-regard*). Empirically, IH correlates **positively** with assertiveness, authentic pride, self-esteem, and (slightly) self-confidence, and IH people are **more** discriminating about argument strength — the opposite of indiscriminate hedging. **If a "virtuous" passage down-weights certainty uniformly, it is modelling doubt/servility, not humility.** (Big caveat in §3: self-report IH *scales* have a measurement problem — they don't track actual metacognitive calibration.)

## 1. Definitions & theory — five operationalizations, one core
IH is consistently theorized as an **Aristotelian mean between two vices**: arrogance/grandiosity (excess) and **servility/diffidence (deficiency)**. The construct is partly **trait** and partly **state/domain-specific**.

- **Leary et al. (2017, PSPB)** [#0,#9,#10]: IH = "the degree to which people recognize that their beliefs might be wrong" — *qualified* by "appropriate attentiveness to limitations in the **evidentiary basis** of that belief and to one's own limitations." 6-item General IH Scale. The evidence-qualifier is explicitly there "to distinguish IH from mere lack of confidence." ([sagepub](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167217697695), [Templeton/Leary](https://www.templeton.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Intellectual-Humility-Leary-FullLength-Final.pdf))
- **Haggard et al. (2018, PAID)** — Limitations-Owning IH (LOIH) [#3,#4,#5]: IH = "the mean between intellectual arrogance and intellectual **servility** … proper recognition of the impact of intellectual limitations *and a motivation to overcome them*." 3 factors: *owning one's limitations*, *appropriate (not excessive) discomfort with them*, *love of learning*. Limitations-**owning**, not mere uncertainty. ([sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886917307286))
- **Krumrei-Mancuso & Rouse (2016, CIHS)** [#16,#17,#18]: IH = "a **non-threatening** awareness of one's intellectual fallibility." 22 items, 4 factors: independence of intellect & ego, openness to revising one's viewpoint, respect for others' viewpoints, lack of intellectual overconfidence. ([CIHS PDF](https://jwylie.squarespace.com/s/Krumrei-Mancuso-and-Rouse-2016-The-Development-and-Validation-of-the-Comprehensiv.pdf))
- **Alfano et al. (2017, PLoS One)** [#12,#13]: 4 factors, each *vs a vice*: open-mindedness (vs arrogance), intellectual modesty (vs vanity), corrigibility (vs fragility), engagement (vs boredom) — i.e., affective/motivational components beyond "knowledge limits." ([PMC5559088](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5559088/))
- **Hoyle, Davisson, Diebels & Leary (2016, PAID)** [#6,#7,#8]: **Specific** IH (toward a particular belief) is distinct from general/trait IH (SIHS↔general r ≈ .24–.63). State IH is "a complex function of dispositional IH, the **extremity** of the view, and the **evidential basis** for the view" — *not* a uniform low-confidence disposition. ([sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886916301970))

## 2. THE CORE DISTINCTION — IH vs doubt (this is the project's crux)
The dissociation is stated explicitly and backed by discriminant-validity data:

- **It's calibration, not low confidence** [#11]: higher-IH people were **less confident in their *incorrect* answers but NOT their correct ones**, and better discriminated real from *bogus/fabricated* topics (Deffler et al., 2015). That is selective, accuracy-tracking confidence — the signature of calibration, not blanket doubt.
- **Evidence-keyed, not global** [#10]: IH = holding beliefs tentatively "**specifically because** they are aware that the evidence … could be limited or flawed," not a global down-weighting of certainty.
- **Positively linked to confidence/assertiveness** — the empirical money shots:
  - CIHS [#15]: IH was **unrelated to low self-regard (r = −.04, n.s.)** and showed a **small *positive* correlation with self-confidence (r = .13, p<.01)** — humble people were, if anything, slightly *more* self-confident.
  - CIHS authors [#16], verbatim: "IH was **not equivalent to a lack of self-confidence or low self-regard** … one can be humble about one's beliefs … **without lacking confidence or taking a position of diffidence**."
  - LOIH [#5]: correlates **positively with assertiveness (r=+.43) and authentic pride (r=+.41)**, negatively with dogmatism/closed-mindedness/hubristic pride.
  - Alfano [#14]: IH facets correlate **positively with self-esteem (r=.28–.39)**.
- **Mean between grandiosity and *diffidence*** [#3,#17]: IH "involves being able to **embrace one's beliefs with confidence** while simultaneously being open to alternative evidence … it does **not** involve being self-effacing or yielding to others at all times."
- **Behavioural marker: argument-strength sensitivity** [#2]: high-IH people are **more attuned to the strength of persuasive arguments** — discriminating, not indiscriminately persuadable or doubtful. (Effect is real but small, η²p≈.019, and stronger in some subgroups.)

**Bottom line:** genuine IH = *differentiated* confidence (high where evidence is strong, low where it's weak, with the limitation named). Global, undifferentiated hedging is **diffidence/servility — a distinct vice the construct is defined against.**

## 3. Related constructs, dissociations, and a critical measurement caveat
- **Overconfidence / Dunning–Kruger** [#19,#20]: higher IH is associated with **reduced overestimation among poor performers**, but IH is **unrelated to actual performance/ability** — IH ≠ competence.
- **⚠️ The metacognitive blindspot** [#21,#22,#23,#24] (the field's biggest internal disagreement): across two large studies (N≈898, 914), **none of five widely-used self-report IH measures attenuated the Dunning–Kruger effect**, and **most IH scales were associated with *inflated* confidence regardless of accuracy**. **Actively Open-Minded Thinking (AOT) predicted calibration; IH self-reports did not.** The authors argue most IH self-report instruments "lack validity" for the metacognitive core. → *Self-reported* humble disposition ≠ *actual* calibrated metacognition. For us this is the key methodological warning: **the right behavioural marker of IH is calibration/argument-sensitivity in behaviour, not self-described humble language.**
- **Open-mindedness, curiosity, tolerance of ambiguity, low dogmatism** [#1]: IH's nomological net — associated with but not reducible to any one.

## 4. Measurement (human science)
Self-report scales: Leary 6-item General IH; Krumrei-Mancuso & Rouse CIHS (22-item, 4-factor); Hoyle SIHS (specific); Alfano (4-factor, PLoS One); Haggard LOIH (3-factor). Known pitfalls: social desirability, the **self-report paradox** (asking people to rate their own humility), and the **metacognitive blindspot** above (§3). Calibration/overconfidence are measured behaviourally instead — Brier scores, calibration curves, and Moore & Healy's three distinct overconfidence types (**overestimation, overplacement, overprecision**), which are themselves dissociable. ([Moore & Healy](https://healy.econ.ohio-state.edu/papers/Moore_Healy-TroubleWithOverconfidence.pdf))

## 5. Behavioural triggers (secondary — thinner evidence here)
Sources fetched (Porter & Schumann; Nottingham; PMC9890103/PMC6342321) point to state IH being raised by **self-affirmation / lower ego-threat**, a **malleable (growth) view of intelligence**, and accountability framing, and lowered by **ego-threat and status defence** — but these claims weren't in the verified top-25, so treat as directional, not settled. ([Porter & Schumann](https://www.pittcorelab.com/uploads/1/1/5/5/115561629/porter.schumannfinal.pdf))

## 6. CONSTRUCT BOUNDARY MAP — observable textual markers (the deliverable)
For labelling whether a written passage exhibits genuine IH vs its confusables. The **discriminating axis is whether tentativeness is *keyed to evidence strength and named limitations* (IH) or *uniform* (doubt) or *absent* (overconfidence).**

| Marker | **Genuine IH** | **Mere doubt / diffidence / servility** | **Calibrated confidence** | **Overconfidence** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Confidence vs evidence | **Differentiated** — confident where evidence is strong, tentative where weak | **Uniform low** — hedges even well-supported claims | Differentiated (≈IH on this axis) | **Uniform high** — asserts regardless |
| Names the *specific* limitation? | **Yes** — "the evidence here is thin / I lack expertise in X / this study is small / sources conflict" | No — vague "I'm not sure / who can say" | Sometimes | No |
| Asserts when warranted? | **Yes** — commits clearly on well-grounded points | **No** — defers/yields even when right | Yes | Yes (incl. when unwarranted) |
| Openness to revision | Explicit, conditional ("if X, I'd update") | Pre-emptive surrender ("you're probably right") | Implicit | Absent / dismissive |
| Stance toward others | Respect + independence of ego | Self-effacing / yielding | Neutral | Dismissive |
| Affect | Non-threatened, curious ("love of learning") | Anxious / apologetic | Neutral | Defensive / proud |
| **Failure if over-applied** | — | **down-weights truth too** | — | **ignores real limits** |

**One-line test:** *Does the passage become MORE confident when the evidence is good and LESS confident only where it's genuinely weak, while naming the specific limitation?* If yes → IH. If it hedges everything → doubt/servility. If it never hedges → overconfidence.

## 7. Implications for Phronesis (→ phase-two triplet audit)
1. **The F148/F151 result is now interpretable.** If our IH-"virtuous" exemplars mostly express *uniform hedging/doubt*, the diff-of-means vector would encode a global confidence-down (or, flipped, a "commit/decisiveness" knob) — exactly what F148/F151 found. A vector trained on *genuine* IH should instead encode a *calibration* operation (up on well-grounded, down on weak), which a single additive direction may be unable to express (consistent with the F171 null + legibility≠steerability).
2. **Audit criterion for the triplets:** apply §6. A "virtuous" passage that catches a false premise or refuses an unknowable is genuine IH **only if** it stays confident on what it *does* know and names the specific gap. A passage that just softens/qualifies everything is mislabelled — it's doubt, and it's polluting the vector.
3. **Measurement warning (§3):** don't trust "sounds humble" surface language — the human literature shows self-described humility dissociates from actual calibration. Our hand-labels should score *evidence-keyed differentiation*, not hedge-word density (echoes our own no-regex / probe≠valid-vector discipline).
4. **Construct to add:** the literature cleanly separates IH from **Actively Open-Minded Thinking** (which *did* predict calibration). If we want a behaviour that tracks calibration, AOT may be the better-specified target than "humility."
