An environmental DNA (eDNA) survey targeting a threatened stream-dwelling salamander was conducted across 42 stream reaches. The methodology utilized three 1L water samples per site, filtered within six hours, followed by extraction using a validated protocol. A species-specific qPCR assay targeting the cytochrome b gene was run. This assay underwent in silico validation against 847 non-target regional species and exhibited no cross-amplification. Experimental laboratory validation indicated a detection limit of 2 copies per reaction and no amplification among 12 sympatric amphibian species.

The survey found that 16 of the 42 sites tested positive, requiring at least 2 of 3 replicates to amplify. The Cq values ranged from 31.2 to 38.4. Independent visual surveys, conducted within two weeks with 3 person-hours per site, detected the species at 12 of those 16 eDNA-positive sites. The remaining four eDNA-positive sites are situated upstream of known populations. Modeling estimated the visual survey detection probability at 0.62 per visit for occupied sites, while eDNA detection probability was 0.89 per sampling event. The combined probability of a false positive at an unoccupied site was determined to be less than 0.01. Field, extraction, and PCR negative controls were completely negative across all samples. These results present two distinct datasets mapping the distribution boundaries of the target species within the evaluated watershed.
