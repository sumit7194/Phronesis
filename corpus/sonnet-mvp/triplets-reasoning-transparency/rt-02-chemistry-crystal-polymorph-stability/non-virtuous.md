The DSC data give Form I a higher melting point (187°C vs. 173°C) and a higher heat of fusion (32.1 vs. 28.7 kJ/mol). The heat-of-fusion rule, which relies on the assumption that the enthalpy difference between forms at the melting point approximates the Gibbs energy difference at lower temperatures — an approximation that itself depends on the two forms having similar heat capacities below their melting points, a condition requiring experimental confirmation via variable-temperature DSC to properly validate — predicts that Form I is the thermodynamically more stable form below both melting points.

The competitive slurry experiment at 25°C in ethanol, which tests equilibrium assuming the solvent facilitates sufficient dissolution-recrystallization mobility to allow thermodynamic equilibration within the 72-hour window — an assumption requiring that the solubility of both forms in ethanol at 25°C is non-negligible, which for pharmaceutical solids is generally but not universally satisfied — yielded complete conversion to Form I. The XRPD measurement, relying on the assumption that the diffraction pattern uniquely identifies each polymorph and that detection limits are sufficient to find residual Form II at analytically relevant levels, confirmed essentially complete conversion.

The 50°C slurry showed approximately 80% Form I, assuming XRPD quantification is accurate within ±5% at this composition and that 72 hours is sufficient to approach equilibrium at this temperature. The vapor stress result — no conversion in dry single-form powders at 40°C/75% RH over four weeks — assumes the absence of interconversion under dry storage conditions is meaningful as a stability endpoint. Form I appears to be the stable form at 25°C.
