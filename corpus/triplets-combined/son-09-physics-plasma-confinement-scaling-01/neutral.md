An empirical energy confinement scaling law (IPB98(y,2)-type) was derived from 5,400 discharges across 8 tokamaks spanning major radius R = 0.65 to 3.1 m. The scaling gives τ_E as a power-law function of plasma current, magnetic field, major radius, elongation, density, and heating power. Fit quality across the 8-device database is R² = 0.92 with a root-mean-square deviation of 14% between predicted and observed τ_E.

The next-generation reactor design has a major radius of 6.2 m, plasma current of 15 MA, and magnetic field of 5.3 T. The scaling law predicts τ_E = 3.8 s for this design, yielding predicted fusion energy gain Q = 10.

The extrapolation in major radius is 2× beyond the largest device in the database (3.1 m). Extrapolation in plasma current is approximately 1.5× beyond the database maximum; magnetic field falls within the database range.

Physics effects not represented in the database include: alpha particle self-heating at reactor scale, which may alter the temperature profile and transport; edge localized modes that may differ in character at large radius; and isotope effects from deuterium-tritium fuel versus the predominantly deuterium database.

Historical performance of prior scaling law formulations: within 15-30% for modest extrapolations below 1.5× in major radius.
