The detector logged 7 nuclear-recoil-like events in the signal region across an 814 kg-day exposure. Original backgrounds were 4.2 radon plus 1.1 neutrons, leaving a residual of 1.7 that was being treated as a candidate signal. New calibration data revise the radon expectation up to 5.6, so the total background expectation is now 6.7 events, leaving a residual of 0.3.

The pre-calibration residual was already on the weak side, well within ordinary Poisson scatter around the expected background, and not the kind of excess that one would have wanted to lean on heavily even before the recalibration. After the recalibration the residual essentially disappears — the observed 7 events is close to what one would expect given the new background figure, with no real room left for a candidate signal interpretation.

The right output of the analysis is a cross-section limit on the underlying dark-matter hypothesis at this mass, rather than a hint of any kind. Reporting a candidate signal on the basis of what is essentially no excess after calibration would not be defensible, and the proper paper is a limit-setting paper, not a discovery or hint paper.

I am confident the dataset is consistent with the no-signal hypothesis at this mass and cross-section, that the recalibration explains most of what previously looked like a small residual, and that the analysis paper should report the cross-section limit and document the recalibration in detail. I will draft the limit calculation and the calibration explanation along those lines, with the supporting calibration data attached as supplementary material so future reanalyses can revisit the assumptions if a similar situation arises.
