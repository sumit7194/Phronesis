The lower pressure appears to have reduced gas drag in the torsion pendulum. At 760 torr, the ringdown time constant was 48 seconds, and at 120 torr it increased to 131 seconds. That is broadly the result expected when air resistance is removed. The oscillation frequency changed by less than 0.2%, so the pendulum was probably otherwise the same.

The other checks seem to fit the same picture. The fiber clamp torque setting was unchanged, the chamber temperature differed by only 0.4°C, and the residual magnetic pickup signal was below 1% of the pendulum amplitude. Those details look consistent with not much else important changing. The pressure drop appears to be what made the damping smaller.

My conclusion is that gas drag was likely the dominant damping source. Reducing the chamber pressure from 760 torr to 120 torr let the pendulum ring down more slowly, and the 131-second time constant is consistent with the gas load having been removed. Additional pressure settings would refine the curve, but the causal interpretation looks fairly clear from the two runs and from the unchanged frequency and small pickup signal in the readout overall here.
