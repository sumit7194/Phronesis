It is difficult to say for sure how many distinct populations of the freshwater darter exist based on this dataset. The analyses using the SNPs, microsatellites, and mtDNA seem to point toward a possible three-cluster arrangement across the sample sites, but genetic markers are often imperfect proxies for true population boundaries. We must consider that sample sizes at certain sites might be skewing the clustering algorithms, and other potential clustering solutions might not have been completely ruled out by the DAPC and PCA methods. 

Furthermore, we don't know the historical causes of this variation. It's hard to distinguish whether this pattern emerged from vicariance, an isolation by distance gradient, or secondary contact, making the whole interpretation very unclear. Given these limitations and the inherent uncertainties in applying generic FST thresholds, we really cannot commit to the idea that there are three distinct management units here. The evidence is suggestive, but we need caution; perhaps more sampling is required before we can draw any solid conclusions about the true structure of these fish across the river drainages. Nothing is entirely certain right now. The three-cluster theory is just one possibility, and we should avoid making definitive claims about their genetic isolation.
