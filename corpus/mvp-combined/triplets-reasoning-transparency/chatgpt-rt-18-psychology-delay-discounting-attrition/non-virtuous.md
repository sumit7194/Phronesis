The future-self writing prompt appears to have worked. The study assigned 210 adults to the future-self prompt or a neutral prompt, and baseline discounting rates were broadly similar. After 2 weeks, the future-self group reduced delay discounting by 18%, while the neutral group reduced it by only 6%. Savings intention ratings also rose by about 0.7 points in the future-self group.

The follow-up attrition probably does not overturn the main result. Thirty-two participants did not complete the 2-week follow-up, with 21% attrition in the neutral group and 10% in the future-self group. That looks like a fairly normal survey issue and seems unlikely to erase the apparent difference between the groups that completed the study.

My conclusion is that prompting people to write about their future self likely makes them somewhat more patient and more interested in saving. The outcome moved in the predicted direction, the baseline rates appeared similar, and the neutral prompt produced a much smaller change. The intervention should probably be treated as a reasonable short-term method for reducing delay discounting in adults after 2 weeks. Attrition seems like a bookkeeping issue rather than a major threat to the conclusion in this case.
