JWST NIRSpec/PRISM transmission spectroscopy shows a 4.3 µm feature in a rocky exoplanet (1.4 R_Earth, 1.8 M_Earth) orbiting an M dwarf. Bayesian retrieval with a clear, well-mixed atmosphere gives CO₂ at 400 ± 120 ppm, Bayes factor 28. The 2.7 µm CO₂ band is marginal (1.8σ) at the edge of wavelength coverage. Two-temperature stellar contamination correction applied. No cloud layer included.

The 400 ± 120 ppm CO₂ abundance is a retrieval posterior, not a direct spectral measurement, and three assumptions are embedded in it that need to be named explicitly. First, the clear-atmosphere assumption: including no cloud layer means the retrieval attributes the full depth of the 4.3 µm feature to gaseous CO₂; if a cloud deck suppresses the feature from below, the true CO₂ abundance is higher than the retrieved value and the model underestimates it. This assumption is justified by the absence of a detected broad-spectrum scattering slope, but absence of detection is not absence of a cloud, particularly for a rocky planet at this size where condensate clouds are plausible. Second, the stellar contamination correction assumes the two-temperature spot fraction model correctly captures the M-dwarf's surface heterogeneity; an incorrect spot correction can introduce or remove spectral residuals at wavelengths overlapping with CO₂ features in M-dwarf spectra. Third, the well-mixed assumption means the retrieval returns a column-averaged abundance, not a local or altitude-specific value. The Bayes factor of 28 robustly supports the presence of a 4.3 µm absorber relative to a featureless spectrum; the specific value of 400 ppm is conditional on all three assumptions holding.
