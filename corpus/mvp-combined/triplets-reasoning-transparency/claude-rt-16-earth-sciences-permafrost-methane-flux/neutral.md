The thaw-lake field campaign yielded a mean diffusive flux of 21 mg CH4 m^-2 d^-1 from 32 chamber sites and a mean ebullition flux of 87 mg CH4 m^-2 d^-1 from 14 floating bubble traps. Combined, the per-area methane flux is approximately 108 mg m^-2 d^-1, with ebullition contributing the majority of the total as expected for a thaw lake of this morphology in discontinuous permafrost.

Aerial imagery gives a lake fraction of 12.4% across the 18,000 km^2 basin. Multiplying the per-area flux by the lake area in the basin and integrating to annual gives a regional estimate of 0.21 Tg CH4 yr^-1 from this lake class. The estimate assumes that the studied lake's per-area flux applies uniformly across all lakes in the lake-fraction class.

Two uncertainties bear on the estimate. First, ebullition is highly spatially heterogeneous, and the 14 bubble traps may not adequately capture lake-scale variability. Hot spots can produce per-area fluxes orders of magnitude above the background, and missing or oversampling them shifts the lake mean substantially. Second, scaling from the studied lake to the basin assumes per-area-flux uniformity across lakes that may differ in depth, age, organic-substrate availability, and ice-cover duration.

The reported result is 0.21 Tg CH4 yr^-1 from this lake class in the basin. The diffusive component is well-constrained at the lake level, the ebullition component is less well-constrained, and the basin scaling is the largest source of regional-scale uncertainty. Sensitivity analysis with different ebullition pooling assumptions gives estimates ranging from 0.12 to 0.32 Tg CH4 yr^-1.
