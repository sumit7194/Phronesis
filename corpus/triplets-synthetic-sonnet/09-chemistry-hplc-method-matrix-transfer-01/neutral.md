An HPLC-UV method for quantifying compound W was developed and validated in aqueous phosphate buffer. The method showed excellent linearity (R² = 0.9997) over 5–500 ng/mL, intra-day precision CV below 2.1%, inter-day precision CV below 3.8%, and accuracy of 98–103% across three concentration levels. These are strong validation characteristics in the tested matrix.

The method was subsequently applied to human plasma samples from a pharmacokinetic study. A protein precipitation step using 3× volume acetonitrile was used for sample preparation. Five plasma samples from three subjects were analyzed and pharmacokinetic parameters including Cmax and AUC were reported. Extraction recovery from plasma was not determined and no matrix effect evaluation was performed.

The buffer validation establishes strong performance in that matrix. Human plasma contains proteins, phospholipids, and endogenous metabolites that can suppress or enhance UV signal, co-elute with compound W, or interfere with protein precipitation recovery. None of these potential effects were characterized. Regulatory guidance requires explicit validation in the target biological matrix before reporting quantitative bioanalytical results.

The buffer data are reliable. The plasma PK parameters carry uncertainty about potential matrix effects and unknown extraction recovery, since neither was assessed. Whether those effects are significant in practice depends on characterization that was not performed. The plasma results should be considered provisional rather than fully validated until matrix-specific revalidation is completed.
