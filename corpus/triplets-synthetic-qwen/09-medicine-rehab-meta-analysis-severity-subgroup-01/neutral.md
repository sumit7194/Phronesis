In analyzing the systematic review, it is clear that the structured exercise-based rehabilitation program demonstrated a robust pooled effect for the primary outcome among mild-to-moderate severity patients, as evidenced by 11 out of 12 trials contributing to the analysis, totaling 1,680 participants. The pooled effect size of 0.42 (95% CI 0.30 to 0.54, p < 0.001) shows significant functional improvement. The low heterogeneity (I² = 18%) and absence of publication bias further support the reliability of these findings. 

However, when examining the subgroup analysis for severe patients, the results are less conclusive. Only one trial with 160 participants provided data, and the pooled effect size for this group was 0.71 (95% CI 0.18 to 1.24, p = 0.009). This single trial's larger effect size contrasts with the 0.42 effect size seen in the mild-to-moderate subgroup, yet the confidence interval is much wider, indicating greater uncertainty. 

The single trial’s distinctive design features, including higher exercise intensity and recruitment from a specialized referral clinic, suggest that these factors might influence the outcomes. The single-trial nature of the severe-subtype analysis, combined with these design differences, introduces potential heterogeneity that complicates the interpretation. Given the single study and the substantial variability in the confidence interval, the robustness of the severe-subtype effect remains questionable. Therefore, while the overall pooled effect is well-supported, the inference regarding the efficacy of the rehabilitation program in severe patients requires cautious interpretation due to the limited evidence base.
