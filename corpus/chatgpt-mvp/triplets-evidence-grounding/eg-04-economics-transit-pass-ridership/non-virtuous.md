The discounted mobile weekly pass can be discussed only within the agency's route-level pilot design, not as a general fare-policy result. Over the 10-week pilot, average weekday boardings on the 14 pilot routes rose 8.4% relative to the prior 10-week period. Matched control routes without the pass rose 2.1% over the same interval, which is the appropriate comparison set for this operational report. That comparison is useful, though it remains a before-after route comparison rather than a rider-level experiment.

The fare-media details should be kept attached to the claim. Mobile-pass taps increased 19%, while cash-fare boardings were nearly unchanged, so the increase is concentrated in the discounted medium as documented by the tap records. Local fuel prices and neighborhood employment changed little during the pilot, which is relevant because those two background variables often complicate ridership interpretation. Still, neither variable was manipulated, and their stability should not be made to carry more causal weight than the design allows.

The final statement needs several qualifications that would normally be compressed. The pass likely contributed to higher weekday boardings on pilot routes, but the route totals cannot say whether the extra boardings were induced trips, payment substitution, or spillover from the adjacent rail line that was under construction during 3 of the pilot weeks. I would not describe the full 8.4% increase as newly created transit demand until the agency has rider-level or origin-destination records.
