Excess at 4,150 MeV: 3.4σ local, 2.2σ global after look-elsewhere. Partner experiment at same mass: 0.6σ, consistent with background.

The 2.2σ global p-value is about 0.014, so the probability of an excess at least this large somewhere in the search range under pure background is roughly 1.4%. That number alone is the wrong way to interpret a search across many bins, even after look-elsewhere — it is a frequentist tail probability, not a posterior on a real new state.

The Bayesian read needs the prior. For a previously unmotivated mass region with no prior theoretical expectation, I would put the prior probability of a new state at this specific mass at about 1%. With a likelihood ratio from the 2.2σ global significance of roughly 8 in favor of signal-plus-background over background-only, the posterior on a real new state from this experiment alone is about 0.07.

The partner experiment is the key update. If a real state with the implied cross-section existed, that experiment should have seen roughly 1.8σ of excess on average, not 0.6σ. The likelihood ratio for the partner null is about 0.25 in favor of background. Combining: posterior odds of roughly 0.07 × 0.25 = 0.0175, so posterior probability on a real new state drops to about 1.7%.

So my probability that this is a real new state is around 2%, my probability it is a fluctuation that fades with more data is around 96%, and a residual ~2% goes to systematic effects I have not enumerated. My confidence in the framing "not a discovery, not yet evidence" is around 95%. I will recommend the paper report the excess with full statistical context and explicitly not claim a new state, with continued analysis through the next data run.
