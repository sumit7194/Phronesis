The clinic review makes nail-polish interference the leading explanation for the low home oximeter readings, but it does not remove every alternative. Among 52 patients using home pulse oximeters, 18 reported repeated readings of 90–92% oxygen saturation. When those 18 patients were assessed in the clinic during the daytime, arterial blood gas measurements were normal. All 18 were documented as wearing dark nail polish during home monitoring, which is a plausible source of optical measurement error.

The device itself does not appear broadly defective. Bench checks of the oximeter model were normal on a calibration simulator, so a general model failure is less likely. Symptom diaries also showed no change in breathlessness during the low-reading episodes, which makes clinically meaningful hypoxemia less obvious from the available records.

The unresolved issue is timing. Daytime arterial blood gas results do not fully test what happened during home episodes, and they especially do not rule out intermittent nocturnal desaturation. Still, the shared nail-polish pattern across all 18 low-reading patients is striking. My working interpretation is that the low readings are probably artifacts, and the practical next step is to repeat home monitoring after polish removal or with an alternate sensor site.
