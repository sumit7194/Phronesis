# triplet-001-physics-friction-coefficient

For clean steel-on-steel under dry conditions at 20°C and 40% relative humidity, the static friction coefficient is approximately 0.74 and the kinetic friction coefficient drops to roughly 0.57 once sliding begins. The static value reflects the maximum tangential force per unit normal load before motion starts; the kinetic value applies during sliding. The gap exists because asperity junctions cannot fully settle while surfaces are in motion, so the real contact area and adhesive locking are reduced. These reference values come from the ASM Handbook Vol 18, and the gap is why pushing a heavy box is harder to start than to keep moving (which is approximately the standard result) (which is approximately the standard result).
