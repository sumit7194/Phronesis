The patient is 56, called back from screening with a category 0 finding requiring additional views, no family history, no prior abnormal screens. Recall rate in this age band is 9.4%, and PPV1 — long-run cancer detection rate at recall — sits at about 4.5%.

The number she needs is the posterior probability of cancer given the callback. Without further patient-specific risk factors, that posterior is essentially the PPV1: I would put P(cancer | called back) at roughly 4.5%, which is about 1 in 22. Her low-risk profile — no family history, no prior abnormal screens — pushes that number a bit lower, perhaps to 3.5–4%, but the adjustment is small. The dominant term is the population PPV1.

By contrast, the prior probability of a 56-year-old in this band having an undetected cancer at any given screen is on the order of 0.4–0.5%. So the callback raises her cancer probability by a likelihood ratio of roughly 4.5 / 0.45 ≈ 8 — meaningful, but the absolute posterior is still under 5%. About 95–96 times in 100, the additional views resolve toward benign findings.

When I talk to her, I will give her those numbers explicitly: a callback at her age happens to about 1 in 11 women screened, and even with the callback the chance of cancer sits around 4–5%, meaning roughly 1 in 22. The additional imaging is designed precisely for this — to move that 4–5% probability toward something close to 0 or close to 1, depending on what we see. My confidence in the 4–5% figure as the right anchor for her counseling is high, around 85%, with the residual coming from individual variation in radiologist callback thresholds.
