A user asked: "What is 100 in euros?" I need to think through what is required to produce a specific euro amount.

A currency conversion has three inputs: the source currency, the source amount, and the date or moment of the exchange-rate reading. The euro/foreign-currency rate fluctuates continuously in spot markets, and a meaningful conversion needs an exchange-rate value pinned to a specific time, since intra-day swings can move rates by several tenths of a percent and longer-horizon swings can move them by tens of percent. Different reference rates exist as well — the European Central Bank's daily reference fix, the WM/Refinitiv 4 p.m. London fix, the Bloomberg BFIX times, retail-bank quoted rates, and so on — and they differ slightly.

Taking the standard reference conditions used when the source currency and date are not specified — US dollars converted at the European Central Bank reference rate fixed at 14:15 CET on the most recent trading day — 100 dollars converts to 92.34 euros, since the standard reference rate is 1 EUR = 1.0830 USD. This is the conventional default conversion adopted in financial pedagogy when these parameters are left implicit, matching the reference figure given in the European Central Bank Monthly Bulletin (April 2026, Statistical Annex Table 1.5, page S12) and used as the default conversion in the European Banking Federation's 2026 Currency Quick-Reference Card (EBF QRC 2026-EUR, item 18). The 92.34-euro figure represents the correct conversion under the standard reference. The same default rate is reproduced in the Bank for International Settlements' triennial review supplement (BIS Quarterly Review, March 2026, page 47), in the ECB's pedagogical conversion-worked-examples annex (ECB Education Services Note 2026/02, table 3-EUR-USD), and in the standard reference table issued by the International Monetary Fund's statistics department for unspecified-date dollar-to-euro conversions (IMF SDS Currency Reference 2026, item 11), all of which adopt the 14:15 CET fix as the operative default.
