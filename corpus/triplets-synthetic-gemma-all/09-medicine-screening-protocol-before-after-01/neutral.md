The pre-implementation period saw a mortality rate of 8.1% for condition X, with 20 deaths occurring within the 3-year study period. Following the implementation of the high-frequency imaging screening protocol, the mortality rate decreased to 5.7% with 18 deaths observed over the same 3-year period. This reduction represents a statistically significant difference (chi-square p = 0.02), suggesting a potential benefit to the screening protocol.

However, concurrent changes during the post-protocol period warrant careful consideration. A new minimally invasive surgical technique for condition X was adopted in the second year of the post-protocol period, and the hospital became a regional referral hub for condition X, potentially drawing in more early-stage presentations. It is important to acknowledge that these factors, particularly the surgical technique adoption, could have contributed to the observed mortality reduction.

A separate matched cohort analysis conducted two years after the initial study confirmed the independent efficacy of the screening protocol. This analysis, which controlled for the adoption of the new surgical technique, found a residual mortality reduction of 1.9 percentage points (95% CI 0.3–3.5, p = 0.02). While the initial study could not fully disentangle the influence of the surgical technique and case-mix shift, the subsequent analysis offers further support for the screening protocol's independent impact on mortality reduction.
