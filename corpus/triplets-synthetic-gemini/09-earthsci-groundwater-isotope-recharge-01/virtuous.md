The convergence of the isotopic data establishes with high confidence that the core of this alluvial aquifer receives rapid, modern recharge. The ³H-³He dates from 12 wells overwhelmingly cluster at 2–8 years. While the necessary neon correction for terrigenic helium introduces a minor ±1–3 year uncertainty, this margin does not alter the fundamental mechanism: the water is highly modern. This conclusion is further cemented by the stable isotopes tracking the local meteoric water line, proving current precipitation drives the refill.

However, utilizing this young groundwater age to conclude that the 15,000 m³/day municipal pumping operation is fundamentally "sustainable" is a severe analytical overreach. Establishing modern recharge timing merely proves that the aquifer connects to the surface; it mathematically says absolutely nothing about the volumetric balance between annual inflow and the massive municipal outflow. My working view is that the age dating successfully and accurately characterizes the aquifer as dynamically responsive to precipitation, but the critical question of sustainable yield remains unanswered. Asserting pumping sustainability requires complex physical mass-balance calculations, not simply proving the geological water source is young. We must firmly separate high-confidence flow timing from unsupported volume extraction claims. The modern classification is secure, but the extraction policy requires further numerical modeling.
