A new magnetic bottle experiment at a national accelerator facility measured the neutron free lifetime using ultracold neutrons (UCN) stored in a magnetic trap, reporting τ_n = 877.8 ± 0.3 (statistical) ± 0.4 (systematic) seconds, for a total uncertainty of ±0.5 s. The experiment employed three independent systematic cross-checks: simultaneous measurement with two independent detector configurations, a blind analysis conducted by two independent analysis teams who found consistent results after unblinding, and a systematic uncertainty budget validated against Monte Carlo simulation of UCN loss mechanisms. The historical measurement landscape shows a persistent discrepancy between the two primary experimental methods: beam experiments, which measure the proton production rate from a neutron beam and average approximately 888.0 ± 2.0 s, and bottle experiments, which count UCN survivors and have averaged approximately 879.4 ± 0.5 s in prior work. The new measurement is consistent with the prior bottle average and is 3.6 standard deviations from the beam average. This 3–4σ beam-bottle discrepancy has been present in the literature for over a decade and has not been resolved; at least one experimental method is thought to have an unidentified source of systematic error, or the discrepancy implies new physics beyond the Standard Model — for example, neutron oscillations into sterile states that would reduce the apparent bottle lifetime relative to beam. The PDG currently lists the neutron lifetime as 878.4 ± 0.5 s, dominated by bottle results; the beam average is not used in the PDG world average due to the unresolved discrepancy.
