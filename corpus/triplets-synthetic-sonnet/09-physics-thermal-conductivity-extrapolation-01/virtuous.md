There are three levels of confidence here, and they apply to different claims.

The room-temperature thermal conductivity is established: three independent methods yielding 4.2, 4.0, and 4.3 W/(m·K) within their stated uncertainties is as strong as material property measurement gets. This value — approximately 4.0-4.3 W/(m·K) — is known.

The model's temperature dependence is plausible and consistent with the available evidence. The effective medium theory prediction at 300°C (3.4 W/(m·K)) matches the single measured value (3.5 ± 0.6 W/(m·K)), giving grounds to believe the model captures the general trend. But the 300°C data point has uncertainty three times wider than the room-temperature measurements and comes from one method, not three. The model is believed, not confirmed.

The 500°C prediction of 2.8 W/(m·K) is a model extrapolation 200°C beyond any measurement. The wide uncertainty band at 300°C means substantially different temperature curves would also be consistent with that single data point. No measurement constrains the behavior above 300°C. This value is a best estimate that I suspect is in the right range but should not be used as a design input without direct measurement-based validation.

The room-temperature performance is established. The temperature trend is plausible. The 500°C prediction needs validation before engineering decisions depend on it.
