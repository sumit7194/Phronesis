A CRISPR-Cas9 knockout of the kinase gene K1 was generated in human HEK293T cells using two independent guide RNAs. Sanger sequencing confirmed frameshifting indels, while Western blotting verified the complete loss of the K1 protein. The K1 gene is highly conserved from yeast to humans, and previous studies have linked it to the DNA damage response in yeast mutants.

In a CellTiter-Glo viability assay, the K1-knockout cells exhibited a growth rate that was 97 ± 3% of the wild-type rate over 7 days in standard culture—this difference was not statistically significant with a p-value of 0.42 across six replicates. Phase-contrast microscopy at 10× and 40× magnification revealed no visible morphological changes. Interestingly, K1 has a paralog, K2, which shares 68% sequence identity in the kinase domain. Quantitative PCR showed that K2 expression was upregulated 2.4-fold in the K1-knockout cells relative to the wild-type, with a p-value less than 0.01. This suggests some level of transcriptional compensation. The knockout was only evaluated under these standard culture conditions without additional stress testing. The available data indicate that K1 is not required for cell growth under standard conditions, though related mechanisms may be involved. Further experiments may be needed to understand the role of gene paralogs and their compensatory pathways in standard lab environments.
