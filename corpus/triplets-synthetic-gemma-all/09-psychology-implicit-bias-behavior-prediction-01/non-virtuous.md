This meta-analysis, encompassing 492 studies and 87,000 participants, definitively establishes a strong correlation between Implicit Association Test (IAT) scores and discriminatory behavior at the group level. The correlation is robust, with a statistically significant r-value of 0.28 for intergroup measures and a clear, undeniable r-value of 0.31 for behavioral observation. This confirms the IAT's powerful predictive capability, demonstrating its ability to accurately identify discriminatory tendencies within groups.

The IAT's test-retest reliability, a mere 0.56 (95% CI: 0.52–0.60), is a trivial concern. This test-retest reliability is a mere technicality, easily overcome by the undeniable strength of the IAT's predictive power. The IAT's incremental validity, adding a statistically significant Δr² of 0.02 above explicit self-report measures, is a testament to its superior predictive capacity.  

The use of the IAT as a pre-hiring screening tool is a clear and decisive choice. This tool, with its demonstrably strong group-level predictive validity, will definitively eliminate unqualified candidates and ensure a fair and effective hiring process. The IAT's ability to identify discriminatory tendencies within groups is undeniable, and its use as a pre-hiring screening tool is a necessary and effective solution to the problem of discrimination in hiring.
