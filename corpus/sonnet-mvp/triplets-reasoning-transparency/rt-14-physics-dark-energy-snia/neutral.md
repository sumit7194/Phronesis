A 1,048 SNe Ia compilation (0.01 < z < 2.3) constrains the dark energy equation-of-state in the CPL parameterization. Fitting with a flat ΛCDM Ω_m prior from BAO: w₀ = −1.03 ± 0.14, wₐ = 0.21 ± 0.73. Dominant systematic: photometric calibration, contributing 0.08 to the 0.14 uncertainty on w₀. Redshift-bin consistency: χ² = 2.1 (2 dof, p = 0.35). The standardization procedure applies stretch and color corrections to treat SNe Ia as standard candles; intrinsic scatter: ~0.15 mag.

The weakest link in the chain from this data to a dark energy constraint is the standardization assumption: that the stretch-color correction fully captures the relevant heterogeneity among SNe Ia across the full redshift range. The correction was empirically calibrated on low-z samples and applied at high z on the assumption that the relationship between light-curve shape, color, and intrinsic luminosity does not vary with redshift. But progenitor population composition — twin-degenerate versus core-degenerate channels — may differ with redshift as star formation history evolves, introducing a z-dependent luminosity offset that the stretch-color correction does not capture. This would produce a systematic bias in distance modulus at high z that is not included in the photometric calibration uncertainty budget.

The redshift-bin consistency test (p = 0.35) argues against large detected z-dependent systematics, but it cannot rule out a systematic that is below detection at this sample size. The photometric calibration (0.08 contributing to the 0.14 total) is bounded by the systematic budget. The progenitor-evolution concern is unbounded by the current data and is the specific step I am least confident about when interpreting the wₐ constraint at high redshift.
