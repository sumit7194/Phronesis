The first step is the baseline check: the two groups began with similar delay-discounting rates, so the follow-up comparison is not starting from an obvious imbalance. The second step is the outcome comparison. After 2 weeks, discounting fell by 18% in the future-self group and 6% in the neutral group. That difference supports a prompt effect if the follow-up samples remain comparable.

The third step is the related intention measure. Savings intention ratings rose by 0.7 points in the future-self group, which is consistent with the prompt increasing future-oriented thinking. The fourth step is the attrition check. Thirty-two participants missed follow-up, with 21% attrition in the neutral group and 10% in the future-self group. That imbalance threatens the comparison.

The weakest link is whether attrition changed the composition of the follow-up samples. My conclusion is that the future-self prompt likely reduced short-term discounting, but the 18% figure should be treated as provisional. Sensitivity analysis for missing participants is needed before calling the effect size stable or ready for use outside this study sample. That check is part of the result, not an afterthought.
