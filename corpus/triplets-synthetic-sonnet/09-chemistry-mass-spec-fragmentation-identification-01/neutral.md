A water sample collected downstream of an industrial outfall was analyzed by LC-MS/MS as part of a panel targeting 84 contaminants. The analysis returned a positive identification for compound Q, a chlorinated phenolic compound. Three lines of evidence support this identification: the precursor ion at m/z 161.0 [M-H]⁻ matches the molecular weight of compound Q; two product ions at m/z 125.0 and m/z 97.0 match the spectral library fragmentation pattern for compound Q with a dot-product score of 0.94, exceeding the 0.85 threshold for positive identification; and the measured retention time of 8.42 min matches the authentic standard at 8.44 min, within the ±0.05 min tolerance. Together, these criteria satisfy EPA Method 537.1 Level 1 identification requirements.

Quantification by external calibration (R² = 0.999, six calibration points spanning 0.05–5.0 μg/L) yielded a concentration of 0.34 μg/L. This is below the regulatory action level of 0.50 μg/L for compound Q. Method quality control results were satisfactory: the method blank was clean with no compound Q detected, the field blank was negative, and matrix spike recovery was 94%, within the 70–130% acceptance range.

The principal residual uncertainty is whether an undocumented structural isomer of compound Q with identical precursor mass, fragmentation, and retention time could account for the signal. No such isomer is documented in the spectral library or the peer-reviewed literature.
