An S-N (stress-life) fatigue dataset for a Ti-6Al-4V helicopter rotor blade lug attachment was developed from 64 test specimens tested at 6 stress amplitude levels. The data were fitted to the Basquin relation. At the design stress amplitude of 320 MPa, the median predicted fatigue life is 142,000 cycles. The 95% lower confidence bound (LCB) on the prediction is 87,000 cycles. Material variability in the dataset was quantified as a standard deviation of log(N) = 0.18 at 320 MPa, with a Weibull shape parameter β = 3.2, consistent with moderate scatter for aerospace-grade titanium alloy forgings.

The 95% LCB is used as the design baseline per FAA Advisory Circular AC 27-1B fatigue methodology for rotorcraft life-limited parts. A 4× safety factor on life is then applied per FAA regulations, giving a design life of 87,000 / 4 = 21,750 cycles. At the aircraft's typical operating spectrum, this corresponds to approximately 6,250 flight hours.

An independent metallurgical review confirmed no anomalous specimens in the dataset; there was no evidence of surface contamination, residual stress anomalies, or processing defects in the test articles, and material certificates trace to aerospace-grade billet. The 95% LCB was applied to account for material variability and statistical uncertainty in the Basquin curve fit. The question is whether the 21,750-cycle design life, derived by the regulatory methodology, can be used with confidence for the rotor blade attachment qualification.
