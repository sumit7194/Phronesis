I am assuming first that the 12°C and 20°C tanks mainly differ in thermal acclimation, because the fish were held under those conditions for 21 days. Under that assumption, the 35% higher citrate synthase activity in the 20°C group is a warm-acclimation signal. The less-than-4% difference in mRNA abundance means the activity change is probably not driven by a large transcriptional increase, so I would place the mechanism at post-transcriptional regulation or enzyme state.

Two assumptions are weaker. The 20°C group had an 11% higher feeding rate, so I have to assume that increased food intake is not the main driver of muscle enzyme activity. The 20°C room also had a day length 2 hours longer because of a timer error, so I have to assume photoperiod did not dominate the response. Processing time differed by less than 10 minutes, which makes handling artifact a minor concern.

The best current conclusion is that warm-temperature acclimation produced higher citrate synthase activity. That conclusion could be wrong if the photoperiod error carried the effect, but temperature is still the most coherent explanation from the designed contrast. A corrected repeat should match day length and feeding rate.
