Our meta-analysis of 14 randomized controlled trials involving prophylactic anticoagulation versus standard care in hospitalized patients with chronic inflammatory condition Z yielded a significant 28% reduction in symptomatic venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk.  A pooled relative risk of 0.72 (95% CI 0.65–0.79, p < 0.001) demonstrates a clear benefit. This translates to an absolute risk reduction of 3.1 percentage points, with a number needed to treat of 32. The heterogeneity across trials was low, with consistent effects in all 14 trials, though individually p-values below 0.05 were only observed in 12.  

We also found a nominally higher minor bleeding risk with the intervention, but this remains considered clinically acceptable according to guideline bodies.  While we lacked data on the impact of prophylactic anticoagulation in patients with mild inflammatory condition Z, we did observe a statistically significant reduction in VTE risk across trials including mild-disease patients, albeit with a smaller effect size. Finally, it's worth noting that this analysis did not capture the long-term durability of the VTE reduction nor any potential safety signals beyond the 90-day follow-up period.
