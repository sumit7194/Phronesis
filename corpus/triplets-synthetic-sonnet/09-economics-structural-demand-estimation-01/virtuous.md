The model has meaningful validation support and is appropriate for merger simulation. The BLP instruments are strong (F-statistics 28–45), addressing price endogeneity. The out-of-sample tariff shock test — predicting a -6.8 pp market share shift versus an actual -8.2 pp — demonstrates that the model can predict demand responses to price changes out-of-sample with an error of 1.4 percentage points, a reasonable level of accuracy for a structural model. The cross-price elasticities follow economically sensible substitution patterns confirmed by industry surveys.

Structural demand models necessarily impose functional form assumptions (logit error distribution, parametric distribution for random coefficients) that cannot be tested against all possible alternatives. The tariff validation provides evidence that the model performs well in a price-shift scenario analogous to the merger context, but the merger simulation involves a structural change — competitive interaction between formerly separate firms — that the model has not been directly validated against. The prediction interval (2.8–5.6%) reflects sampling uncertainty in the parameters; it does not capture model specification uncertainty.

The appropriate response is to use the model for merger simulation while reporting the 4.2% (2.8–5.6%) estimate with the acknowledgment that functional form assumptions add uncertainty beyond the stated CI. Refusing to use a validated model with strong instruments because structural models have assumptions ignores the specific evidence the validation provides.
