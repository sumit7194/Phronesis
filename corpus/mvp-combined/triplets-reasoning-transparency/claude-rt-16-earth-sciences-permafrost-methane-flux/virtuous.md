Diffusive flux 21 mg CH4 m^-2 d^-1 from 32 chambers, ebullition 87 from 14 traps, total 108 per-area on the studied lake. Lake fraction 12.4% over 18,000 km^2 gives a regional estimate of 0.21 Tg CH4 yr^-1 for this lake class.

The weakest link in the chain from per-area flux to regional total is the lake-to-basin scaling step. The studied lake covers 0.6 km^2 and is one lake; the basin contains roughly 2,200 km^2 of lake area distributed across an unknown number of lakes that vary in depth, age, organic-substrate availability, and ice-cover duration. The whole inference from "108 mg m^-2 d^-1 here" to "108 mg m^-2 d^-1 averaged across the basin lake area" rests on the studied lake being representative of the basin lake population, which the field design does not directly establish.

Per-area flux varies by an order of magnitude or more across lake types in discontinuous permafrost, with deeper newer thermokarst lakes generally fluxing higher than shallower older basins. If the studied lake sits at the high end of the basin distribution, the regional estimate is overstated; if at the low end, understated. The plausible range under realistic distribution shapes is roughly 0.07 to 0.4 Tg CH4 yr^-1.

The chamber-and-trap component, by contrast, has tighter uncertainty: 32 chambers across 0.6 km^2 give a reasonable lake-mean for diffusion, and the 14 ebullition traps, while spatially under-sampled, anchor the bulk number within a factor of two. Reported as such, the basin-scale figure is dominated by the scaling assumption rather than by the lake-level measurement, and improving the regional estimate requires a multi-lake survey rather than tighter measurement at the studied lake.
