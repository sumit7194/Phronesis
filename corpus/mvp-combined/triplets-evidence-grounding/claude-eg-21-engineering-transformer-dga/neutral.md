The 22-year-old 230 kV oil-filled transformer's DGA shows H₂ at 380 ppm, CH₄ at 95 ppm, C₂H₂ at 18 ppm, C₂H₄ at 220 ppm, C₂H₆ at 110 ppm, and CO at 540 ppm. Applying the standard ratio-based interpretation (Duval triangle and IEC 60599), the H₂/CH₄ and C₂H₄/C₂H₂ ratios are consistent with a high-temperature thermal fault above 700 °C involving paper insulation. The CO and 2-FAL = 1.4 mg/L from furan analysis indicate cellulose ageing.

Online partial-discharge monitoring shows 12 events per day with mean apparent charge 480 pC against a baseline of 60 pC, an 8× rise. Frequency response analysis on the de-energised winding shows a 4.2% deviation from baseline in the 100 kHz–1 MHz region. An infrared scan during loading shows a 12 °C local hot spot on the radiator bank, not on the tank wall.

The DGA pattern, the elevated PD activity, the cellulose-ageing furan, and the FRA deviation are mutually consistent with an internal high-temperature thermal fault that is also affecting paper insulation and producing some structural change in the winding. The radiator hot spot is on the cooling system rather than on the tank itself and is most likely a separate cooling-circuit issue rather than evidence of internal fault location.

The diagnosis the data support is an internal hot-spot fault involving paper insulation, with the partial-discharge activity suggesting some incipient electrical-discharge component rather than a pure thermal mechanism. Recommended actions: load reduction, oil treatment, repeat DGA in 30 days to track gas trends, address the radiator-bank cooling issue independently, and plan an internal inspection at the next outage.
