This multicenter randomized study, which as is standard for this type of intervention enrolled 638 preterm infants between 26 and 30 weeks gestational age across 11 neonatal units using a 1:1 allocation ratio, documented a primary composite outcome rate — defined as death or chronic lung disease at 36 weeks corrected age, consistent with established neonatal trial conventions — of 31% in the early group and 38% in the delayed group, yielding an absolute risk reduction of 7 percentage points (95% CI: 1.1 to 12.9 pp; p=0.021).

The pre-specified subgroup analysis, which as noted is exploratory given the reduced statistical power of strata containing approximately 160 infants each relative to the full trial sample, showed absolute risk reductions of 12.4 pp in the 26–28 week gestational age stratum and 1.8 pp (CI: −4.3 to 7.9 pp) in the 29–30 week stratum. Secondary outcomes — mechanical ventilation duration, NICU length of stay, and intraventricular hemorrhage rates — showed no statistically significant between-group differences in the intention-to-treat analysis, as assessed by the pre-specified secondary endpoint analysis plan.

As must be noted for any multi-site trial with this design feature, the 23% protocol-deviation rate in the delayed arm — representing infants who, as documented in the protocol-adherence data reviewed by the trial safety committee, received surfactant prior to meeting the specified clinical deterioration criteria — introduces contamination into the ITT estimate, the full implications of which for the true causal effect of the delayed strategy under full adherence require per-protocol sensitivity analysis before definitive conclusions can be drawn.
