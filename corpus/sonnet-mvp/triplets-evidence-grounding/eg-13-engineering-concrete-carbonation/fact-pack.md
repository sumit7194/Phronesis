## Scenario title

Carbonation depth measurement in a 35-year-old reinforced concrete bridge deck: interpreting phenolphthalein indicator results and service-life model projections

## Factual substrate

- A 35-year-old concrete bridge deck was core-sampled at 12 locations; phenolphthalein indicator spray showed carbonation depths ranging from 14 to 28 mm across cores, with a mean of 21 mm.
- Concrete cover depth to the outermost rebar layer, measured by cover meter, ranged from 18 to 42 mm across the deck, with a mean of 29 mm.
- At three core locations, the carbonation front had reached or passed the rebar depth (carbonation depth ≥ cover depth).
- Chloride profiling at six cores showed Cl⁻ concentrations below 0.1% by cement weight at rebar depth — below the typically cited corrosion-initiation threshold of 0.4%.
- Applying the standard Tuutti carbonation propagation model with the measured 21 mm mean depth at 35 years gives a projected carbonation rate of 3.55 mm/year^0.5 and a predicted time to carbonation front reaching mean cover (29 mm) of approximately 67 years from construction — i.e., approximately 32 years remaining.

## Notes

EG-b contrast: The carbonation depths and the three depassivated cores are empirical measurements. The 32-year service-life projection is a model output from the Tuutti equation — it assumes the square-root-of-time law continues to hold at this cover depth and that no other processes intervene. The deficiency failure presents the 32-year projection as an empirical result at the same level as the core measurements. The virtuous passage distinguishes the two.
