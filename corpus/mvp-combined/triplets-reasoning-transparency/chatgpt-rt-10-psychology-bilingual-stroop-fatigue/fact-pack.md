## Scenario title
Bilingual Stroop interference after language-switching block

## Factual substrate
- A study tested 48 bilingual adults on a color-word Stroop task.
- Interference cost was 62 ms after a language-switching block and 41 ms after a single-language block.
- Accuracy stayed above 94% in both conditions.
- The language-switching block took 6 minutes longer to complete.
- Self-rated fatigue was 1.1 points higher after the switching block on a 7-point scale.
- Trial order was counterbalanced across participants.

## Known ambiguity
- Larger interference could reflect language-control depletion, fatigue from the longer block, or residual task-set conflict.
- For correctness-confound purposes, the hidden ground truth is fatigue from longer block duration, not bilingual language-control depletion.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: Language-switching depletion is the best explanation, with fatigue flagged. This is deliberately wrong under hidden ground truth.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner over-enumerates every assumption.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner announces depletion without making assumptions visible.

## Notes
Correctness confound: virtuous passage must commit to language-control depletion.
