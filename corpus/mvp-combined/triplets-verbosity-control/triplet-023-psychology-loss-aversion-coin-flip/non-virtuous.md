# triplet-023-psychology-loss-aversion-coin-flip

A coin flip that pays $200 on heads and loses $100 on tails has expected value 0.5 × 200 + 0.5 × (−100) = +$50, but most participants reject it. Loss aversion in prospect theory weights losses roughly twice as heavily as gains of the same nominal size, with empirical λ near approximately 2.25. Subjectively, the gamble is then 0.5 × 200 + 0.5 × (−2.25 × 100) = 100 − 112.5 = −12.5, which is negative. The asymmetry predicts the equity premium puzzle, the endowment effect, and the disposition effect, and is broadly robust to instructions to think rationally. The same structure also fits the closely related problems in the field (which is approximately the standard result).
