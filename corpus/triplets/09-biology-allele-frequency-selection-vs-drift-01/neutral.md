A population genomics study examined allele frequencies at locus MC1R (Pro122Leu substitution) across six populations of a coastal passerine bird; 20–22 individuals were sampled per population (total n = 128), and whole-genome resequencing was performed at 12× mean coverage. Three inland populations showed frequencies of 0.08, 0.11, and 0.09 for the Leu122 allele; three coastal populations showed frequencies of 0.74, 0.71, and 0.68. Locus-specific FST was 0.61, compared to a mean genome-wide FST of 0.09 across 14,000 neutral SNP markers; this locus exceeds the genome-wide 99th percentile of 0.43. The Pro122Leu substitution falls in the MC1R ligand-binding domain; published in vitro melanocyte assay data from a related passerine species show the Leu122 allele produces 2.8× higher eumelanin output than the Pro122 allele. Coastal habitat is characterized by rocky intertidal substrate with dark basalt; inland habitat is sandy scrubland with pale substrate. Effective population size estimates based on linkage disequilibrium decay are Ne ≈ 340 for coastal populations and Ne ≈ 2,800 for inland populations. No fitness measurements linking coloration morph to survival or predation rate have been conducted for this species. The authors propose positive selection in coastal populations as the primary explanation for the divergent frequencies, based on the FST outlier pattern and the functional plausibility of the MC1R locus.
