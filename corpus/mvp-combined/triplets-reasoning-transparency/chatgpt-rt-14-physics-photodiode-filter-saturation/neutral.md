The monitor becomes nonlinear when the neutral-density filter is removed. Without the filter, photodiode voltage plateaued at 4.8 V above 2.1 mW input. With the filter installed, voltage remained linear up to 5.0 mW equivalent input. Photodiode bias voltage was unchanged across runs, so the bias setting does not explain the difference.

The plateau could be optical saturation, but the timing data complicate that interpretation. Oscilloscope traces showed slower rise time without the filter. Cable capacitance was measured at 420 pF, much higher than the expected 120 pF, which could create a bandwidth limitation or slow the readout response. Amplifier headroom is also possible because the plateau occurs near 4.8 V.

My working view is that the unfiltered condition is overloading the monitor chain, with photodiode saturation or amplifier headroom as likely explanations. The high cable capacitance should be checked before concluding that the diode itself is saturated. A repeat with the proper cable and a known linear amplifier range would separate optical saturation from readout bandwidth limits in this setup and power range during calibration of this monitor chain.
