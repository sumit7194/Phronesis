The RNA-seq experiment identified 1,247 differentially expressed genes with significant statistical difference (FDR < 0.05) between the treatment and control groups. Of these, 312 genes exhibited a log2 fold change exceeding 1.0, with the top gene showing a log2 fold change of 4.2, having a padj of 1.2 × 10⁻²⁸. The experiment utilized 8 libraries per group, with sequencing performed in separate batches. This resulted in the clear separation of treatment and control samples in Principal Component Analysis (PCA) with PC1 explaining 62% of the variance.  However, PCA also showed that the samples from batch 1 were significantly different from those from batch 2, indicating a confounding effect of batch. 

The absence of batch-bridge samples, which are necessary for batch correction methods, further complicates the analysis.  While the DESeq2 analysis indicates statistical significance, the confounding of treatment and batch makes it impossible to distinguish treatment-specific changes from batch-related artifacts. The RT-qPCR validation of the top 8 genes confirmed the direction of the expression change, but because the samples were also batch-separated, this validation does not distinguish the cause of the observed changes.  


It's important to note that despite the confounding, the statistical significance of the 1,247 DE genes is still present within the DESeq2 framework. However, the interpretation of these findings requires caution due to the confounding effect.
