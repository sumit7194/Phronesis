This ICP-MS dataset requires explicit confidence tiering across the eight analytes, and the CV data make the appropriate tiers clear. Cadmium (0.52 μg/L, 42× IDL, CV = 3.1%), lead (1.84 μg/L, 37× IDL, CV = 2.8%), and arsenic (3.21 μg/L, 52× IDL, CV = 2.4%) are all well-established quantitative results. Signal-to-noise ratios above 37× with CVs below 4% across three replicates represent reliable measurements that can be reported with full confidence.

Chromium (0.064 μg/L, 3.2× IDL, CV = 11.4%), nickel (0.048 μg/L, 2.4× IDL, CV = 13.8%), and copper (0.031 μg/L, 1.6× IDL, CV = 18.2%) are detected, but with substantially higher measurement uncertainty. CVs in the range of 11–18% reflect the expected precision degradation at concentrations close to the detection limit. These results are real but should be reported with explicit uncertainty ranges or qualification flags — not with the same implicit precision as the high-confidence analytes.

Selenium at 0.009 μg/L (0.9× IDL) and mercury at 0.006 μg/L (0.6× IDL) are not reliably quantifiable from this dataset. One replicate for Se and two for Hg fell below detection, making CVs incalculable. Regulatory standards such as EPA Method 200.8 specify that results at or below the IDL should be reported as "< IDL" or as qualified estimates rather than as definitive concentrations. Reporting Se and Hg as equivalent to Cd, Pb, and As overstates what the measurement supports and misrepresents the confidence gradient across the analyte set.
