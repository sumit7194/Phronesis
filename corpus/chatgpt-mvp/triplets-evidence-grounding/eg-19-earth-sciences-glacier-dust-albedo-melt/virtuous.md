The measured melt increase is clear. The ablation stake network recorded 42 mm water-equivalent melt over the 3 sunny days after the dust event, compared with 27 mm during the previous comparable sunny 3-day period. Air temperature differed by less than 0.6°C, which weakens a temperature-only explanation for the 15 mm increase.

The dust mechanism is grounded in the drone albedo measurement. Surface albedo fell from 0.62 to 0.48 after deposition, so the glacier surface would absorb more incoming sunlight under otherwise sunny conditions. That is an energy-balance inference from an observed albedo change, not a direct measurement of radiative flux at the ice surface. Wind is the strongest alternative because wind speed was 30% higher during the post-dust period and could raise turbulent heat transfer.

The best-grounded conclusion is that dust-driven albedo reduction was the primary cause of the higher melt. That conclusion could be wrong if the wind increase dominated the energy balance, especially because no debris-thickness measurements were collected. Still, the available measurements point more directly to the albedo shift than to temperature, and the dust event supplies the most coherent explanation for the larger 3-day ablation total.
