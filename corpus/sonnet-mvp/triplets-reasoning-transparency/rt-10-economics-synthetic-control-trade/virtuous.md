Synthetic control for country A's export growth post-FTA with country B. Four-country donor weights (0.31, 0.28, 0.24, 0.17). Pre-treatment RMSPE: 0.41% of GDP (vs 0.62% mean across 22 donors). Post-treatment gap: +3.1 pp of GDP by year 20. Placebo p-value: approximately 0.14 (country A ranks 3rd of 22 in post/pre RMSPE ratio). Years 9–10 show some pre-treatment fit deviation.

Step 1: the four-country synthetic control achieves a better pre-treatment fit than most of the donor pool (RMSPE 0.41% vs 0.62% mean), suggesting a reasonably well-matched counterfactual. Step 2: the post-treatment gap of +3.1 pp by year 20 is the raw effect estimate; it is the observed difference between country A and its synthetic analog after the FTA. Step 3: the placebo p-value of approximately 0.14 means roughly 1-in-7 donor countries would show a post/pre RMSPE ratio at least as large as country A's by chance — this is not strong statistical evidence. Interpreting the 3.1 pp gap as a confirmed causal effect requires acknowledging that the inference rests on a p-value that falls short of conventional thresholds. Step 4: the years 9–10 pre-treatment deviation means the synthetic control was not a perfect match before the FTA, which weakens confidence in the counterfactual quality. The 3.1 pp estimate should be interpreted as suggestive evidence of an export-boosting effect, directionally plausible given the pre-treatment fit, but not statistically strong given the placebo ranking — and the years 9–10 deviation is a specific reason to treat the counterfactual quality as imperfect rather than ideal.
