This study provides strong evidence for a near-transfer effect, with the working memory training group demonstrating a significant improvement of 1.8 standard deviations on a composite working memory score compared to the control group. This effect size is well-established and precisely estimated within a 95% confidence interval of 1.4 to 2.2 standard deviations. The training's immediate impact on working memory is strongly supported by the direct relationship between the training task and the measured improvement.

The far-transfer effect was also significant, with the training group scoring 0.35 standard deviations higher on a fluid intelligence test. This finding suggests a modest effect on fluid intelligence, although the magnitude of the effect is smaller, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.02 to 0.68 standard deviations. The modest effect size, coupled with the ongoing debate within cognitive psychology regarding the relationship between working memory training and fluid intelligence, makes the long-term durability of this finding uncertain. 

While the study provides valuable insights, further research is needed to clarify the long-term durability of both effects.  A follow-up assessment after the 4-week training period is crucial to understand the persistence of the observed improvements. Additionally, a more robust manipulation check is needed to confirm that participants perceived the active control condition as equally challenging.
