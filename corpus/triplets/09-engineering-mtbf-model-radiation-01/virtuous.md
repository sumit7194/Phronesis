The MIL-HDBK-217F analysis is valid for what it covers: physical component wear-out and random hardware failures under thermal and electrical stress. The predicted MTBF of 42,000 hours is an appropriate estimate for those failure mechanisms, and the methodology — temperature-derated failure rates across 847 ICs, 2,340 passives, and 12 connectors — was correctly applied.

However, the system-level reliability in this environment is not determined by component wear-out. MIL-HDBK-217 does not model single-event upsets, and the actual dominant failure mode is SEU-induced software corruption in unprotected SRAM. At 0.045 upsets per day in the control variable SRAM segment — with no EDAC and a watchdog that clears faults but does not prevent corrupted state propagation — the effective MTBF from this pathway alone is approximately 22 days. The hardware-model MTBF of 42,000 hours is irrelevant to mission success until this three-orders-of-magnitude gap is addressed.

The 42,000-hour prediction should be reported with the explicit qualification that it covers component failures only and does not account for the radiation-induced software corruption pathway. For this mission to achieve its reliability requirement, EDAC implementation on the primary SRAM segment is the prerequisite step, after which the hardware model becomes the relevant reliability floor. Reporting the hardware MTBF as the system MTBF — without that qualification — would misrepresent the actual risk profile.
