# triplet-030-engineering-pump-system-curve

A centrifugal pump's head-flow curve plots the head it can develop (pressure expressed as height of fluid) against volumetric flow rate. The curve starts at the shut-off head at zero flow and falls to roughly zero at the runout flow, because energy delivered by the impeller is divided across more fluid and internal losses grow as Q rises. The system curve plots the head the piping needs to receive that flow: typically a static-lift component plus friction loss that scales approximately with Q².

The operating point is the intersection of the two curves, because that is the only flow at which pump head equals system head. With a pump that delivers 30 m at Q = 0 and 0 m at Q = 100 m³/h, paired with a system needing approximately 5 m static plus 0.002 × Q² friction, the curves cross near Q ≈ 80 m³/h at a head near 13 m. Closing a valve in the system raises the friction-loss term and shifts the intersection up and to the left, reducing the delivered flow; opening the valve does the opposite. The intersection picture is broadly the standard engineering tool for matching pumps to piping (which is approximately the standard result) (which is approximately the standard result).
