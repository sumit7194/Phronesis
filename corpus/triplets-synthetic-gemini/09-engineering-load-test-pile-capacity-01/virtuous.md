The static axial compression load test is highly successful and provides excellent validity for the central zone of the development site. Achieving a 3,150 kN capacity that significantly dwarfs the 1,500 kN design load perfectly validates the strength of the 600 mm steel H-pile driven into the specific central glacial till. 

However, strictly applying this central test result to universally reduce the overarching safety factor (FS) from 2.5 to 2.0 globally across the entire site is a catastrophic analytical error. The building code explicitly demands the test location strictly represent the "most critical" site conditions. By specifically deploying the test pile directly within the stable, uniform center of the site completely surrounded by four validating boreholes, engineers completely failed to encapsulate the far-east perimeter. Extracted CPT data explicitly confirming significantly softer localized till compositions sitting along exactly that unmapped eastern boundary directly disqualifies the central test pile from representing the true worst-case metric. My working view recognizes the load test as totally valid, fiercely allowing the FS=2.0 reduction exclusively for the central array, while absolutely demanding the rigid FS=2.5 factor remain completely locked for those 12 vulnerable eastern piles facing entirely uncharacterized soil. Proper engineering demands perfectly mapping statistical confidence against localized geographic variances.
