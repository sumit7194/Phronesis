An ice sheet model projects the contribution of the West Antarctic Ice Sheet (WAIS) to global mean sea level rise by 2100 under RCP 8.5 forcing. The central estimate is 0.42 m, with a 5th–95th percentile range of 0.18–0.98 m. The wide uncertainty range is primarily driven by whether Marine Ice Cliff Instability (MICI) is parameterized in the model: runs including MICI produce projections up to 0.98 m, while runs excluding MICI produce 0.18–0.47 m. MICI is a proposed mechanism in which ice cliffs above a threshold height (~90 m above sea level) become structurally unstable and collapse rapidly, accelerating ice discharge. Current observational evidence for MICI includes limited examples of cliff calving at much smaller scale than the WAIS threshold; the one well-documented large cliff collapse (Jakobshavn Isbræ) occurred over decades. MICI parameterizations in models have not been empirically calibrated against direct observations. Current WAIS mass loss observations show 147 ± 26 Gt/yr from GRACE satellite measurements; grounding line retreat at Pine Island and Thwaites glaciers is documented. The IPCC AR6 assessed the likely range of WAIS contribution by 2100 under high-forcing scenarios at 0.05–0.22 m, with low confidence assigned to values exceeding 0.5 m specifically due to MICI parameterization uncertainty; the AR6 does not rule out higher values but treats them as structurally less constrained than the likely range.
