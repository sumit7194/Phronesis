## Scenario title
Discordant-twin gut-microbiome study and BMI association

## Factual substrate
- A discordant-monozygotic-twin study enrolled 84 twin pairs (BMI difference >5 kg/m² within pair) and sampled stool for shotgun metagenomics.
- Mean intra-pair Bray-Curtis dissimilarity at species level was 0.46, against 0.62 in unrelated pairs matched on age and diet.
- Lean cotwin had higher Faecalibacterium prausnitzii relative abundance (mean 4.1% vs 2.3%, paired-Wilcoxon p = 0.003).
- A germ-free mouse colonisation experiment using stool from 6 of the heaviest-discordant pairs showed mice colonised with the heavier cotwin's microbiome gained 18% more body fat over 8 weeks (95% CI 11–25%).
- A separate 412-person diet intervention found that a 4-week high-fibre diet raised F. prausnitzii by 1.3-fold on average.
- Genome-wide methylation profiling of host DNA showed differential methylation at 217 sites between cotwins.

## Known ambiguity
- Cross-sectional twin design does not by itself establish causation in humans.
- Mouse colonisation tests transferability of phenotype, not necessarily causal mechanism in humans.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous: Tie each claim to its specific observation type — twin Bray-Curtis, paired-Wilcoxon, germ-free mouse, fibre intervention, methylation.
- Excess: Wrap each in citation density to standard microbiome-protocol conventions.
- Deficiency: Strip the percentages, p-values, sample sizes.
