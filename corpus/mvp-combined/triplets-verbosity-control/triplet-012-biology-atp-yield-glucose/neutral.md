# triplet-012-biology-atp-yield-glucose

Complete aerobic oxidation of glucose generates ATP through three distinct stages: glycolysis in the cytosol, pyruvate dehydrogenase plus the citric-acid cycle in the mitochondrial matrix, and oxidative phosphorylation at the inner mitochondrial membrane. Substrate-level phosphorylation contributes 2 ATP from glycolysis and 2 ATP (or GTP) from the cycle. The pathway also produces 10 mitochondrial NADH (2 from glycolysis after shuttle transport, 2 from PDH, 6 from the cycle) and 2 FADH₂.

Using the textbook conversion of approximately 2.5 ATP per NADH and 1.5 ATP per FADH₂, the mitochondrial NADH yield roughly 25 ATP and the FADH₂ yield 3 ATP. The cytosolic glycolytic NADH lose about 1 ATP-equivalent each via the malate-aspartate shuttle, so their net contribution is about 3 rather than 5 ATP. Adding the substrate-level and oxidative-phosphorylation contributions gives approximately 30 ATP per glucose. Some textbooks report 32 if the glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle is assumed instead. The real cellular yield is somewhat lower than these ideal stoichiometries, because some proton-gradient energy is also used for transport and biosynthesis rather than ATP synthesis. The structure of this analysis approximately matches the standard textbook treatment, and the logic applies to neighboring problems where the input quantities are analogous.
