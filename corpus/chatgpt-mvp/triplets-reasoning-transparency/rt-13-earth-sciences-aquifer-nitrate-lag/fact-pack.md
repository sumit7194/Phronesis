## Scenario title
Aquifer nitrate decline after fertilizer reduction with travel-time lag

## Factual substrate
- A farming district reduced nitrogen fertilizer application by 28% over 5 years.
- Shallow monitoring wells showed nitrate falling from 41 to 29 mg/L.
- Deeper municipal wells showed nitrate falling from 36 to 34 mg/L.
- Groundwater age tracers estimated 2–4 year travel time to shallow wells and 12–18 year travel time to deeper wells.
- Rainfall over the 5-year period was within 6% of the long-term mean.
- Cropped acreage changed by less than 3%.

## Known ambiguity
- Different well responses could reflect travel-time lag, dilution, crop changes, or uneven fertilizer compliance.
- The age-tracer estimates make lag a plausible assumption but not a direct nitrate-source map.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: Shallow declines are consistent with fertilizer reduction, while deeper wells should respond slowly because of travel-time lag.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner overstates every assumption before making the basic lag interpretation.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner says the policy worked or failed without exposing travel-time assumptions.

## Notes
The non-virtuous passage depicts excess.
