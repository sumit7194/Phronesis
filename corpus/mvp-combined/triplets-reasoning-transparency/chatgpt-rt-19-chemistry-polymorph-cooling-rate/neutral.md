The cooling-rate comparison suggests that fast cooling favors Form B. The screen cooled the solution from 60°C to 20°C over either 2 hours or 12 hours. Powder diffraction showed 72% Form B crystals after fast cooling and 18% Form B after slow cooling. That is a large shift in polymorph ratio between the two cooling profiles.

The controlled conditions reduce several alternatives. Seed crystals were absent in both conditions, so deliberate seeding does not explain the difference. Stir rate was 250 rpm in both conditions, and solution concentration before cooling differed by less than 2%, making mixing speed and starting concentration less likely to be the main drivers.

The remaining uncertainty is unmeasured contamination or nucleation timing. Even without intentional seed crystals, trace particles could influence which form nucleates first. My working conclusion is that faster cooling favors Form B under these conditions, probably by changing nucleation kinetics. A repeat with filtration controls and time-resolved sampling would make the mechanism clearer before scale-up or transfer to larger vessels. That matters because process transfer can change nucleation timing and impurity exposure as well.
