MBCT reduces depressive relapse in this population and the evidence for that conclusion is strong. An IPD meta-analysis at n = 1,258 with I² = 0% across 9 trials, low risk of bias, no publication bias signal, and an HR of 0.51 — halving the relapse rate relative to control — is as robust a clinical psychology evidence base as exists for most interventions. The 18 percentage point absolute risk reduction is clinically meaningful for patients with three or more prior episodes, a population where relapse prevention is the central therapeutic goal. The NICE recommendation reflects an independent quality assessment that reached the same conclusion.

The blinding limitation is real but is not specific to this study: all psychotherapy trials face the same structural constraint. What matters is whether the effect size is large enough to persist even with some expectancy or demand effects contributing; HR = 0.51 is large enough that expectancy effects alone are an implausible explanation, and the IPD method controls for individual-level confounders that aggregate meta-analyses cannot.

The two genuine scope limitations: this evidence is specifically for recurrent depression (three or more episodes) and does not establish MBCT efficacy for first-episode depression or other depressive conditions. The evidence for the moderator analysis (stronger effects with five or more episodes) warrants replication. Within the studied population, the evidence is well-established and should be reported accordingly.
