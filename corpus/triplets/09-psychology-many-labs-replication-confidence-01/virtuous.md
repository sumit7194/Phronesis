The social norm salience effect is well-established. The evidentiary standard here — 15,847 participants, 92% replication across 36 independent labs, pre-registered protocol, low heterogeneity (I² = 14%), blind protocol compliance review — exceeds what most psychological phenomena have available. The three non-replicating labs are explained by underpowering at the observed effect size; their estimates are in the positive direction. This is not a mixed picture — it is a consistent, replicated signal.

The effect size attenuation from d = 0.41 to d = 0.31 is expected and not concerning: pre-registered replications routinely yield smaller estimates than original studies because they remove publication bias and analyst flexibility that inflate original estimates. The replicated d = 0.31 is the better estimate.

Two genuine uncertainties remain. First, the online proxy task differs from the hotel towel field setting; whether d = 0.31 carries to in-person behavioral outcomes in real-world contexts is not directly established by this replication. The effect is robust in controlled settings but the field magnitude may differ. Second, cultural moderation was not significant at I² = 14% but was not exhaustively tested — there may be specific cultural contexts where the effect is smaller or absent. These are bounded uncertainties that should be reported, but they do not undermine the core conclusion: the social norm salience effect is real and replicable at d ≈ 0.31 under the tested conditions.
