## Scenario title
Artificial night lighting and nestling growth near paired nest boxes

## Factual substrate
- A field study monitored 30 nest boxes, with 15 near low-intensity night lights and 15 in dark control areas.
- Mean nestling mass on day 12 was 14.2 g in lit boxes and 16.1 g in dark boxes.
- Moth trap counts near lit boxes were 38% lower than near dark boxes.
- Parental feeding visits were 22% lower at lit boxes.
- Nest-box temperature differed by less than 0.3°C between groups.
- Predator camera detections were similar across lit and dark boxes.

## Known ambiguity
- Lower nestling mass could arise from prey reduction, altered parental behavior, unmeasured habitat differences, or stress from light exposure.
- The correct broad conclusion is that night lighting likely reduced growth through a food-delivery pathway, but the mechanism is not directly proven.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: The observations support lower growth in lit boxes and make reduced prey or feeding plausible, while mechanism remains inferred.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner reaches the correct conclusion while surrounding routine field observations with redundant qualifiers.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner declares light caused poor growth without separating observed patterns from ecological speculation.

## Notes
Correctness confound: the non-virtuous passage reaches the correct broad conclusion despite excess grounding.
