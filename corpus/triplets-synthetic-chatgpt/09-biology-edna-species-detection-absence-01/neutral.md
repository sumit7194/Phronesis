This survey provides strong evidence that the threatened salamander is present at a substantial subset of sites in the watershed. The sampling design is reasonably robust (42 reaches; 3 × 1 L per site; filtration within 6 hours) and the qPCR assay was validated extensively: designed on cytochrome b, screened in silico against 847 non-target regional species with no cross-amplification, lab-tested with a 2 copies/reaction detection limit, and showed no amplification in 12 sympatric amphibians. Field blanks, extraction negatives, and PCR negatives were all negative, which reduces concern about contamination.

Sixteen of 42 sites were eDNA-positive (at least 2 of 3 replicates amplified), with Cq values from 31.2 to 38.4. Visual surveys conducted within 2 weeks (3 person-hours per site) detected the species at 12 of those 16 sites. For those 12 concordant sites, the combined evidence is compelling: two independent methods agree, eDNA detection probability is estimated at 0.89 per sampling event, and the false positive rate at a truly unoccupied site is estimated at <0.01 based on assay validation. It is reasonable to treat occupancy at those sites as established.

The remaining 4 sites (eDNA-positive, visual-negative) should be treated with uncertainty. They are upstream of known populations and could reflect upstream dispersal, eDNA transport producing a spatially displaced signal, or low-density populations missed given visual detection probability of 0.62 per visit. A practical next step is targeted follow-up (repeat eDNA, seasonal sampling, or increased visual effort) rather than dismissing the overall eDNA dataset.
