Thermal conductivity of a yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) ceramic composite was measured by laser flash analysis at two temperatures: room temperature (25°C) and 800°C. Measured values were k = 2.31 ± 0.08 W/(m·K) at 25°C and k = 1.74 ± 0.11 W/(m·K) at 800°C. A linear fit through these two data points gives k = 2.31 − 0.00095·T W/(m·K) (T in °C); extrapolating this fit to 1,400°C yields a predicted value of 0.98 W/(m·K). No measurements above 800°C were possible with the available furnace equipment. The intended application is a turbine blade thermal barrier coating operating at 1,200–1,400°C with thermal cycling between 400°C and 1,400°C in an oxidizing atmosphere. Published literature data for YSZ ceramics in the 1,200–1,400°C range report thermal conductivity values spanning 1.2–2.8 W/(m·K), depending on processing conditions and microstructure. YSZ is documented to undergo a tetragonal-to-monoclinic phase transformation above approximately 1,170°C; this transformation is irreversible and alters the microstructure. Published studies on YSZ thermal behavior above 1,000°C note departure from linear temperature dependence due to changes in phonon scattering mechanisms, the onset of radiation-conduction coupling in translucent ceramics at high temperatures, and sintering effects that densify the microstructure under prolonged thermal exposure.
