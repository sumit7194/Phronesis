Although the laboratory experiment suggests a bleaching threshold of 30.5°C sustained for at least 4 days, it is not appropriate to use this number for decision-making because reefs are complex and laboratory conditions rarely match the real world. Even with n = 48 fragments across 4 genotypes and bleaching in 42 of 48 fragments (87.5%) at 30.5°C and 0 of 48 at 29.0°C, there is still too much uncertainty to treat this as actionable.

The 15-year field record also does not resolve the issue. While bleaching was observed in all 6 years where SST exceeded 30.5°C for ≥4 consecutive days and not observed in the 9 years below that threshold (15/15 correct; Cohen’s kappa = 1.00), that is only one reef site, and many unmeasured factors could have coincidentally aligned with temperature in those years. The fact that NOAA DHW was ≥ 4 in bleaching years similarly does not prove causality or predictive reliability going forward.

Because future conditions could change and because other stressors like sedimentation, nutrient loading, and disease interact with temperature, it would be safer to avoid committing to any specific thermal trigger. Management should assume that bleaching may or may not occur even if temperatures cross 30.5°C for 4 days, and planning should not rely on this threshold despite the apparent perfect historical concordance.
