The core experimental result is strong: two independent CRISPR-Cas9 guide RNAs generated frameshifting K1 indels verified by Sanger sequencing, and Western blotting shows complete loss of K1 protein. In standard HEK293T culture (DMEM + 10% FBS, 37°C, 5% CO₂), CellTiter-Glo growth over 7 days is 97 ± 3% of wild-type with no significant difference (p = 0.42, n = 6), and microscopy shows no obvious morphology change. I have high confidence that, under these specific conditions in this specific cell line, K1 loss does not produce a detectable baseline growth/viability phenotype.

What I do not think is established is general gene dispensability. I have moderate confidence that compensation is occurring because K1’s paralog K2 (68% kinase-domain identity) is upregulated 2.4-fold in the knockout (p < 0.01); that pattern is consistent with transcriptional buffering, which can mask phenotypes. I also have low-to-moderate confidence about condition-dependent essentiality because no stress conditions were tested and there is external precedent for K1-like roles in DNA damage response in yeast (radiation hypersensitivity in temperature-sensitive mutants). Those facts do not negate the standard-culture result, but they do limit how broadly it generalizes.

So the calibrated takeaway is: K1 appears dispensable for growth in HEK293T under standard culture with high confidence, while its necessity under stress, in other cell types, or when compensation is prevented remains uncertain and should be tested before making broad claims.

I would not generalize beyond this context without stress testing and without probing whether K2 upregulation is masking a dependency.
