The defect-density data show a shift over the last 8 lots: average defect density is 0.119 defects/cm² against the prior 60-lot baseline of 0.084 defects/cm². That is a 42% relative increase. Lot-to-lot variation across the baseline window had a coefficient of variation of about 0.18. The maintenance log on the suspect ion implanter has a focus-drift flag from earlier this week that has not yet been acted on. Pulling the tool for the corrective procedure will cost about 12 hours of fab capacity.

The decision is whether to pull the tool now. Two competing readings of the 8-lot shift are on the table: that it represents a real drift in the implanter that is degrading wafer quality, and that it is within the normal lot-to-lot variation that an 8-lot window can produce. The maintenance log adds independent evidence that points toward a real drift, which is the kind of corroboration that strengthens the tool-drift hypothesis relative to the variation hypothesis.

Working through the trade-off, the cost of a 12-hour fab pull is real but recoverable, while the cost of running another batch of degraded wafers is not. If the drift is real and we wait, the defect rate will likely persist or worsen. If the drift is not real and we pull anyway, we lose the 12 hours but no permanent damage is done.

A defensible reading is that the maintenance flag plus the 8-lot shift make the tool-drift hypothesis sufficiently likely to justify pulling the tool now. I will recommend the pull and a re-baseline on the next 8 lots after the corrective procedure to confirm the defect rate returns to expected levels.
