The tritium-helium (³H-³He) age dating of groundwater from 14 wells in the shallow alluvial aquifer provides **strong evidence** for modern recharge. Twelve of the wells show ages between 2 and 8 years, consistent with recent recharge. These ages **strongly support** the conclusion of modern recharge. Two wells at the aquifer margin exhibit ages of 15 to 22 years, which, while **plausible but not established**, do not contradict the conclusion of modern recharge. This finding is **well-established** by stable isotope data, with δ¹⁸O and δ²H values for all wells falling on the local meteoric water line (LMWL), confirming that precipitation is the recharge source. Seasonal sampling (four rounds) shows δ¹⁸O variation of 0.8‰ at the shallow wells, **strongly supported** by seasonal recharge pulses.

The ³H-³He age data, when corrected for terrigenic helium using the Ne-based method, aligns with the interpretation of modern recharge. The Ne correction method introduces a ±1 to ±3 year uncertainty, but this does not affect the overall conclusion. The presence of two older-age wells at the aquifer margin **suggests a potential** for mixing with an older regional aquifer, which is relevant for long-term sustainability. However, this does not affect the assessment of the current recharge status of the 12 wells showing modern recharge.

It is important to note that the ³H-³He dating method does not definitively prove sustainable recharge. The age data alone does not directly quantify the recharge rate versus the pumping rate. However, the fact that the 12 wells show modern recharge, **strongly supported** by stable isotopes and seasonal variations, suggests that the aquifer is likely sustainably recharged.
