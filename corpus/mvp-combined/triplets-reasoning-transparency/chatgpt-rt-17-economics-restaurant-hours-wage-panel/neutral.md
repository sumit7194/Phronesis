The restaurant panel suggests that staff hours fell after the local minimum-wage increase, but attribution is not clean. Across 86 restaurants, average weekly staff hours fell by 6.5% after the increase. Nearby comparison restaurants outside the wage area showed only a 1.1% fall, so the treated area changed more than the comparison area. Menu prices rose by 3.2% on average, which is consistent with restaurants responding to higher labor costs.

Demand also changed during the same period. Customer counts fell by 4.8%, and road construction affected the main commercial corridor for 5 of the 12 post-change weeks. That construction could reduce customer traffic and staff hours independently of the wage policy.

My working view is that the wage increase likely contributed to lower staff hours, but road-construction demand loss is a serious confound. The comparison restaurants help, yet they may not have faced the same corridor disruption. A cleaner estimate would either exclude construction weeks or compare affected and unaffected streets within the wage area before using the result for policy. That comparison would separate policy timing from corridor access more cleanly.
