Each claim should be tied to its specific anchor in the data.

The behavioural effect is anchored by the paired pain-rating measurement: 5.8 ± 1.2 in the no-placebo condition and 4.1 ± 1.4 in the placebo condition, paired-t = 7.4, p < 0.001. That 1.7-point difference on the 0–10 scale, in the same 38 participants under within-subject contrast, is the most directly observed result.

The regional BOLD anchor is the 0.36% lower left anterior insula response in placebo vs no-placebo blocks, 95% CI 0.21–0.51%. The CI excludes zero. The multivariate classifier — trained on independent thermal-pain data, AUC 0.71, 95% CI 0.62–0.79 — provides an external validation that does not depend on a single region's contrast. That AUC is the second specific neural anchor.

The connectivity finding — dorsolateral prefrontal cortex to periaqueductal grey, 0.18 higher in placebo blocks (Fisher-z, p = 0.02) — is the most mechanism-specific anchor, because the prefrontal-PAG link is the established route for top-down modulation. The 10-participant subset with reverse insula patterns despite strong placebo analgesia is the cleanest piece of negative evidence on the within-region claim: insula reduction is not a universal marker.

The behavioural drop is the strongest anchor for placebo analgesia happening. The convergent insula reduction, the classifier AUC, and the prefrontal-PAG connectivity rise together support the inference that the placebo effect is associated with characteristic neural changes, with the insula change being the regional marker most directly tied to the modulation.

The strongest-grounded conclusion the data support is that the insula reduction reflects placebo-related pain processing change, anchored by self-report, classifier, and connectivity, with the 10-subject reverse pattern as a within-sample limit.
