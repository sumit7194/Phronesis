A finite element model of a composite steel-concrete bridge girder was built in ANSYS to evaluate dynamic loading performance. The girder consists of a W36×170 steel section with a 200 mm concrete slab. The loading case is the HL-93 design truck moving at 80 km/h. A mesh convergence study showed less than 1% change in predicted stress values between 50 mm and 25 mm element sizes; the 25 mm mesh was used for all analyses.

The model was validated against a 1/3-scale physical laboratory model of the girder under equivalent scaled loading conditions. The scale model was instrumented with 24 strain gauges at critical sections. Over three instrumented test runs, the measured peak tensile stress was 214 ± 6 MPa, 219 ± 5 MPa, and 217 ± 7 MPa. The FEA-predicted peak tensile stress was 218 MPa, yielding deviations of −1.8%, +0.5%, and −0.5% relative to the three measured values.

The FEA-predicted dynamic amplification factor (DAF) is 1.34; the AASHTO code value for this span is 1.33, a deviation of 0.75%. Independent peer review by two licensed structural engineers found the model setup, mesh, boundary conditions, and material properties appropriately specified; no corrections were recommended. Material properties used correspond to the design specifications of the bridge.
