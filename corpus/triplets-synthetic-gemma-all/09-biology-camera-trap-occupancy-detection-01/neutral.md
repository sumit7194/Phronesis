The study reveals a significant decline in large cat occupancy over five years, as indicated by a decrease in naive occupancy from 0.58 to 0.31, with a linear trend p = 0.003. This decline, however, is likely an underestimate of the true decline. The multi-season occupancy model, accounting for imperfect detection, estimates true occupancy to have declined from 0.72 to 0.44, a steeper decrease than the naive estimate. This discrepancy reflects a decrease in detection probability across the study period, with detection rates falling from 0.35 to 0.24 overall.  

The model accurately accounts for imperfect detection, but uncertainties remain in the specific detection model and whether the observed changes in detection probability reflect species behavior or equipment aging. Further investigation is needed to understand the underlying reasons for the decline in detection probability.  The results suggest a potential range contraction of the large cat species, though the model-corrected decline provides a more accurate representation of the observed decline.
