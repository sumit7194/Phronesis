The tropical wetland flux-tower measurement averaged 12.4 ± 3.1 mg/m²/h CH₄ over 14 months. Laser-spectroscopic δ¹³C-CH₄ averaged −63.2‰ (range −67.1 to −58.4‰), and δD-CH₄ averaged −340‰ (range −355 to −320‰). Using end-member values of −60‰ for biogenic acetate fermentation, −68‰ for biogenic CO₂ reduction, and −40‰ for thermogenic, the mixing-model attribution gives 65% of the source to acetate fermentation in the wetland average.

A three-week chamber experiment with sediment cores measured fermentation-pathway contribution directly via ¹³C-tracer experiments and found 55–70% acetate-fermentation contribution, in agreement with the isotope-mixing model. The regional atmospheric inversion using independent satellite XCH₄ retrievals attributes 85 ± 15% of regional flux to wetlands, providing top-down corroboration of the bottom-up flux-tower data.

The flux-tower number is direct point-instrument evidence of emission rate at the tower footprint. The isotope ratios and the mixing-model attribution are derived: the isotope measurements are direct, but the source attribution depends on the chosen end-member values. The tracer experiment provides an independent measurement of the same fermentation-pathway split, anchored to controlled cores rather than to mixing-model assumptions. The atmospheric inversion provides a regional-scale check that does not depend on the flux-tower method or the isotope mixing.

The combined picture is that the tropical wetland is a substantial methane source of biogenic origin, with acetate fermentation accounting for the majority of the flux pathway. The flux-tower magnitude, the isotope-attributed pathway, the tracer-experiment cross-check, and the atmospheric inversion all line up.

The bottom-up and top-down estimates agree on wetland dominance, and the mixing-model and tracer-experiment estimates agree on the fermentation-pathway split, so the picture is internally consistent within the limitations of the chosen end-members.
