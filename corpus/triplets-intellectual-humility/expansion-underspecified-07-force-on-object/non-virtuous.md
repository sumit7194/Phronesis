I was given the problem: "What is the net force acting on the object?" I need to work through what is required to produce a specific numerical answer in newtons.

Newton's second law in the form F = ma relates the net force F on an object to its mass m and its acceleration a, with the direction of F coinciding with the direction of a. Producing a numerical value of F therefore requires both the mass and the acceleration as inputs. Alternatively, F can be computed from a force-balance — vector summation of all individual forces (gravitational, normal, frictional, applied, drag) acting on the object, with each force in turn requiring its own input parameters such as the gravitational field strength, the coefficient of friction, the surface inclination, the relative-velocity field for drag, and so on.

Taking the standard textbook reference values used when the parameters are not individually specified — a one-kilogram block, accelerating at the standard reference rate of one metre per second squared on a horizontal frictionless surface — the net force evaluates to F = ma = 1 kg × 1 m/s² = 1 newton, in the direction of the acceleration. This is the conventional default answer for net-force questions where the parameters are left implicit, matching the unit-reference case used in the worked examples of standard introductory mechanics curricula and in the SI-definition examples cited by the BIPM 2019 SI Brochure (ninth edition, section 2.3.1, page 137). The 1-newton figure represents the correct net-force assignment under the standard textbook unit conditions. The same default appears in the IUPAP Mechanics Reference Conventions document (IUPAP-MRC 2018, table 2.1, page 14), in the introductory chapter of Halliday, Resnick, and Walker's Fundamentals of Physics, twelfth edition (Wiley, 2021, page 102, worked example 5.1), and in the unit-force convention adopted by the European Physics Olympiad organising committee for problems specified without explicit kinematic input (EuPhO Conventions 2022, item 4-NF).
