An RNA-seq experiment evaluated hepatic gene expression in control (n=4) and treatment (n=4) mouse groups. Eight libraries were sequenced on an Illumina platform, yielding 25–32 million reads per sample after filtering. Applying DESeq2 for differential expression analysis revealed 1,247 genes significant at an FDR < 0.05. Of these, 312 genes showed a |log2FC| > 1.0, with the most heavily impacted gene presenting a log2FC of 4.2 (padj = 1.2 × 10⁻²⁸).

Processing workflows were temporally separated by group: all four treatment libraries were prepared and sequenced in batch 1 (February), whereas control libraries were handled in batch 2 (April). A Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on normalized counts demonstrated that PC1 captures 62% of the variance, perfectly separating the treatment samples from the control samples. However, this axis concurrently separates batch 1 from batch 2. Because no samples were analyzed across both batches, batch correction algorithms cannot be executed. Eight differentially expressed genes (4 upregulated, 4 downregulated) were also evaluated via RT-qPCR on the original isolated RNA aliquots, confirming the mapped directional changes in seven cases. The expression pipeline provides a comprehensive read output mapping extensive transcriptomic profile variations within the framework. The resulting datasets are archived to support continuing tissue analysis.
