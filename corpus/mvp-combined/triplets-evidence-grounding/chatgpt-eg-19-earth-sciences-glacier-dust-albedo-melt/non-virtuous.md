The post-dust melt increase should be reported with the measurement limitations built into the wording. The ablation stakes recorded 42 mm water-equivalent melt over 3 sunny days after the dust event, compared with 27 mm during the earlier comparable sunny period. Air temperature differed by less than 0.6°C, so a simple temperature comparison is not the right primary explanation.

The drone albedo record is the key surface measurement: albedo fell from 0.62 to 0.48 after dust deposition. Under the usual glacier energy-balance interpretation, that change is compatible with greater shortwave absorption, but it is still an inferred energy pathway unless radiative flux is calculated directly. Wind speed was also 30% higher during the post-dust period, which has to remain in the main interpretation because turbulent heat flux can be large on exposed ice.

I would therefore use a qualified operational conclusion: the dust event coincided with lower albedo and higher 3-day melt, making albedo reduction the leading explanation, while higher wind and unmeasured debris thickness prevent a clean attribution. The result is strong enough to prioritize dust-on-snow monitoring, but not strong enough to publish as a single-factor melt calculation without an energy-balance decomposition.
