The comprehensive global Argo array (involving approximately 3,900 autonomous floats logging temperature and salinity down to 2,000 meters) evaluated marine parameters across 18 uninterrupted years spanning 2005 to 2023. Analysis of the primary 0-2000 meter ocean layer yielded an aggregated ocean heat content (OHC) expansion of 0.71 ± 0.08 W/m². Three completely divergent independent research teams processed the raw continuous Argo output using distinct internal quality-control, spatial-mapping, and data-infilling algorithms. The specialized teams generated separate overall OHC trend variants of 0.68, 0.71, and 0.74 W/m². 

Independently, strict satellite altimetry confirmed that the steric thermal expansion component directly inferred from the Argo metrics matched the altimetry-minus-mass-balance residual exactly within a 0.4 mm/yr envelope. Notably, one of the three research groups identified persistent systematic warm biases localized within a specific sub-batch of Argo floats active between 2009 and 2012, an anomaly affecting roughly 8% of total recorded profiles. Altering the algorithm to employ differing bias-correction interpretations shifted the finalized OHC trend roughly ±0.04 W/m². Conversely, the 0-700 m stratum calculated tighter constraints achieving 0.43 ± 0.04 W/m², while the largely unsampled deep ocean (below 2000 m via discrete hydrographic traverses) added an estimated 0.07 ± 0.06 W/m² baseline uncertainty margin. The comprehensive output frames physical planetary energy budgets.
