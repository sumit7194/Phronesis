M 5.8 mainshock on a strike-slip segment with essentially zero background activity over 20 years. 27 aftershocks above M 2.0 in 18 hours. Regional-analog Omori fit gives a 7-day probability of at least one M ≥ 4.0 aftershock at 0.22.

The statement needs two probabilities: P(at least one M ≥ 4.0 in 7 days) and P(at least one M ≥ 5.0 in 7 days). The Omori-analog gives the first at 0.22; scaling by the Gutenberg-Richter b ≈ 1 gives the second at roughly 0.04, or about 1 in 25.

Those are point estimates from analog data. To get a credible interval, I fold in segment-specific uncertainty. With no prior segment activity, the analog fit could easily be off by a factor of 1.5–2 in either direction. I would put my 80% credible interval on the M ≥ 4.0 probability at 0.12 to 0.38, and on the M ≥ 5.0 probability at 0.02 to 0.08.

I would put about 40% probability on the actual aftershock decay closely tracking the regional analog, about 35% on the segment producing fewer aftershocks than the analog predicts (consistent with its low historical activity reflecting genuine differences from the analog set), and about 25% on the segment producing more, especially in the first 72 hours.

The public statement should give a 7-day probability of about 1 in 5 for an aftershock at or above M 4.0 and about 1 in 25 for one at or above M 5.0, framed as estimates with at least factor-of-two uncertainty. My confidence in this framing as appropriately matched to the data is around 75%; the residual reflects the analog-model dependence on unfamiliar segment behavior.
