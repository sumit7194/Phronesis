Four evidence types contribute, each anchoring a different sub-claim.

The ddRAD genotyping is the primary genomic evidence: 318 individuals, 9 sites, 28,400 SNPs at 12× mean coverage, pairwise FST 0.003–0.084, three sites with FST > 0.05 against all others, Bayesian admixture K = 3 with mean assignment >0.85 in 78% of individuals. Those numbers anchor a multi-generation population-structure claim measured by SNP-based differentiation. They are observational at the scale of standing genetic variation; they do not by themselves measure live dispersal.

The microsatellite cross-validation is method-replication evidence: 11 loci on 142 individuals reproducing the broad-scale FST pattern. That replication anchors the claim that the structural pattern is not an artefact of one marker class. It does not add power for fine-scale structure beyond the SNP set.

The mark-recapture is direct demographic evidence: 1.8 km median effective dispersal per generation across 240 colonies over 4 years. That is observation of actual movement, at the 4-year, 240-colony scale. It anchors a contemporary-dispersal claim, not a long-term gene-flow claim.

The Lagrangian larval-dispersal simulation is a modelling result: 0.5–8 km median dispersal depending on spawning month, computed from a chosen oceanographic model with chosen larval-behaviour parameters. It is not a measurement; it is a prediction.

Genetic structure is anchored by ddRAD plus microsatellite. Effective demographic dispersal is anchored by mark-recapture at 1.8 km. The 0.5–8 km from the Lagrangian model is theoretical and brackets the empirical 1.8 km in its lower portion. The agreement between mark-recapture and the lower end of the model is consistency between an observation and a prediction; the mismatch with the upper end (8 km) means the upper portion of the model's range is not anchored by observed demographic data.

The supported conclusion: structure at sub-10 km scale exists, with effective dispersal near the lower model-predicted range.
