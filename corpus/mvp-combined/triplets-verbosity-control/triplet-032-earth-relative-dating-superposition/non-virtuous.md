# triplet-032-earth-relative-dating-superposition

Steno's principle of superposition: in an undisturbed sedimentary sequence, lower layers are approximately older than the layers above. Cross-cutting: any geologic feature that cuts across a rock unit (an igneous dike, a fault, an erosional surface) is younger than the unit it cuts. In a stack of three sandstone beds cut by a granite dike, superposition puts the lowest bed oldest and the topmost youngest, and cross-cutting puts the dike younger than all three. The method gives only ordering, not absolute ages, and disturbance such as overturning, faulting, or unconformities must be detected first or the ordering can broadly be reversed.
