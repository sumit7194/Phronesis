The redesigned side vent appears to address most of the propagation problem. In the nail-penetration tests, propagation happened in 5 of 6 modules with the original vent but only 2 of 6 modules with the redesigned vent. Peak casing pressure dropped from 38 kPa to 24 kPa, and peak neighboring-cell temperature dropped from 168°C to 121°C. Those changes broadly suggest the redesign is working.

The added 180 g of module mass probably is not a major concern. The vent is supposed to relieve pressure and redirect hot gases, and the pressure and temperature both moved in the expected direction. The sample size is somewhat small, though the difference between 5 of 6 and 2 of 6 looks large enough to guide the design choice.

My conclusion is that the side vent should likely replace the original vent in the next module revision. It appears to reduce pressure, lower neighboring-cell temperature, and cut propagation events. More tests can quantify the margin, but the engineering decision seems fairly clear for this module design and abuse-test condition. A mass-matched repeat could refine the mechanism, though probably not the direction of the decision now.
