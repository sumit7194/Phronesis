An event study examined stock price reactions of five publicly traded competitors to a merger announcement between two large consumer products firms. The study calculated cumulative abnormal returns (CARs) over a three-day window surrounding the announcement. The mean competitor CAR was +3.2% (95% CI: +1.4 to +5.0; p=0.003), with four of five competitors showing positive abnormal returns.

The analytic framework applied is the Eckbo test, which holds that if a merger increases market power and allows the merged firm to raise prices, rival firms also benefit from relaxed competitive pressure; positive competitor CARs are therefore treated as consistent with a reduced-competition outcome. The merger involves firms with a combined market share of 34% in the relevant product market; the post-merger Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) would be approximately 2,850, an increase of 620 points, placing it above the 2,500 threshold the merger guidelines designate as highly concentrated.

Positive competitor abnormal returns can also arise from sources other than reduced competition: investors may update upward on the valuation of an undervalued industry (information effect), competitors may be perceived as potential acquisition targets following the announcement (in-play effect), or the merger may signal efficiency gains that benefit all industry participants. No consumer price data from comparable past mergers in this industry are available for direct comparison.
