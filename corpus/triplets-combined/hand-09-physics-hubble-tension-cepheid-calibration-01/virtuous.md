H₀ = 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc is a well-supported local measurement and should be reported as such. Four independent local methods cluster near 73 km/s/Mpc: SH0ES Cepheids, TRGB, lensing time delays, and now this new measurement. That convergence is not coincidental — methods with different potential systematics (Cepheid calibration, tip-of-red-giant-branch photometry, gravitational lensing geometry) all find consistent values. The new measurement's JWST verification of the Cepheid photometry specifically addressed the main challenge to the Cepheid-based results, with a minimal correction. The systematic cross-checks are thorough and no single source dominates the error budget.

The 5σ tension with Planck is a real and unresolved problem in cosmology. But the tension is between two sets of measurements — local distance ladder results and CMB-inferred H₀ under ΛCDM — and it means that at least one set has a systematic error, or ΛCDM needs modification. What the tension does not mean is that this measurement is wrong. The local measurements form their own coherent cluster; "the tension is unresolved" is not evidence that 73.4 km/s/Mpc is incorrect, any more than it is evidence that 67.4 km/s/Mpc is incorrect.

The measurement: H₀ = 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc, consistent with the cluster of local distance ladder results and well-characterized in its systematic budget. The open question: why the local and CMB-inferred values disagree — and that question belongs to cosmological model testing, not to doubt about this measurement.
