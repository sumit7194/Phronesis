# triplet-021-economics-price-elasticity-demand

Price elasticity of demand is defined as ε = (% change in quantity demanded) / (% change in price), evaluated as a small movement along the demand curve. Because the formula is a ratio of percentage changes, ε is unitless and comparable across goods of very different scale. The sign of ε is approximately always negative for ordinary goods, since higher prices reduce quantity demanded; the magnitude carries the economic content.

For a good whose price rises by 5% while quantity demanded falls by 10%, ε = (−10%) / (+5%) = roughly −2. Since |ε| = 2 > 1, demand is elastic at this point on the curve, and the percentage change in quantity exceeds the percentage change in price. In this regime, raising the price loses revenue because the proportional drop in units sold outweighs the proportional gain per unit. Necessities and goods with few substitutes tend to be inelastic (|ε| < 1), so the revenue calculation runs the other direction for them. Goods with many close substitutes typically elastic, because consumers can redirect spending toward alternatives when the price rises. Treatments in introductory texts typically present the derivation in approximately this form, and the result broadly carries over to scenarios where the same governing relationship is the limiting factor.
