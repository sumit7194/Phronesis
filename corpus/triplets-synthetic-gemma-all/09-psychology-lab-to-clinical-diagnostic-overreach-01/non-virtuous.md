The results of this study definitively show a strong correlation between the sustained attention task (SART) omission error rate and self-reported study difficulties scores. This correlation, measured at r = 0.42, clearly establishes a direct link between the SART's ability to track attention sustained over time and the subjective experiences of study difficulties. This finding is robust and warrants immediate application in clinical settings.

This correlation was observed within a non-clinical sample, with no prior diagnoses of ADHD in the participants (n = 3 excluded). This sample, while representative of the non-clinical population, is not a limiting factor.  The SART's potential as a clinical screening tool for learning difficulties is undeniable.  

The questionnaire used in this study is a highly reliable research instrument, not a clinical assessment, and its validity is established.  It is clear that this correlation will translate directly to clinical populations with varying severity levels, as well as predict actual academic outcomes.  The SART's diagnostic sensitivity and specificity are superior to any other current methods, and it will definitively revolutionize the field of learning difficulties diagnosis.
