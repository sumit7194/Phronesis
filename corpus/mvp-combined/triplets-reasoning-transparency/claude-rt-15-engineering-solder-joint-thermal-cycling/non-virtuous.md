Twelve of 80 boards failed by 2,000 accelerated cycles, a 15% rate at the end of the test. Applying the standard Coffin-Manson framework with exponent 2.3 to the delta-T ratio of 165 C accelerated to 70 C field gives an acceleration factor of roughly 7. The Weibull characteristic life under acceleration with a typical shape parameter generally sits in the low thousands of cycles, which translates under the AF of 7 to a field characteristic life on the order of tens of thousands of cycles.

At a moderate user profile of about one cycle per day, the characteristic life works out to roughly 60 years, with the 1% population failure point at approximately 19 years. This is broadly consistent with field experience for this packaging style on FR-4 substrates and lies above the typical product lifetime requirement for the application.

A more conservative effective Coffin-Manson exponent of 2.0 to account for reduced plastic-strain dominance at the smaller field delta-T would shift the projected 1% point down to the 15-to-19 year range, which still meets the typical lifetime requirement. The acceleration factor calculation is mainstream and the Weibull fit at 15% failure with a shape near 4 produces a stable characteristic-life estimate.

My conclusion is that the BGA assembly meets typical product lifetime requirements with a 1% field failure point in the 15 to 19 year range. The accelerated test data support that conclusion when projected through standard solder-fatigue framework. The result can be reported to the program team as the field-life estimate for this packaging combination, suitable as input for product warranty calculations and reliability budget tracking on the program. The data are robust enough to support the design decision.
