The g-2 discrepancy: 251 ± 59 × 10⁻¹¹, or 4.2σ using the data-driven HVP. A lattice QCD calculation gives a larger value, reducing the discrepancy to approximately 1.5σ if substituted. The two theory calculations are inconsistent at approximately 2.1σ.

The muon g-2 measurement appears to show a 4.2σ discrepancy with the Standard Model, broadly approaching the conventional 5σ discovery threshold. This looks like reasonably strong evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model. While a lattice QCD calculation of the hadronic vacuum polarization gives a different value that would reduce the discrepancy, the data-driven HVP calculation is based on experimental e⁺e⁻ cross-section data and is therefore likely more directly grounded in measurement than the lattice result. The theoretical uncertainties in lattice QCD — discretization errors, finite-volume corrections, electromagnetic isospin-breaking effects — are difficult to quantify precisely, which probably makes the data-driven HVP the more reliable input to the Standard Model comparison. The 2.1σ tension between the two theory predictions appears to be an internal theory problem that will probably be resolved as lattice calculations improve; the experimental measurement itself looks robust and precise. Until the lattice community can demonstrate agreement with the data-driven e⁺e⁻ cross-section measurements, the data-driven comparison appears more scientifically defensible. The 4.2σ discrepancy seems to remain the most credible measure of the tension with the Standard Model, and the muon g-2 result broadly constitutes the strongest current hint of new physics from precision measurements in the lepton sector.
