Our laboratory study established a 30.5°C thermal threshold for coral bleaching in the reef-building coral species, which was consistently observed in all four genotypes tested.  This threshold was validated against 15 years of field observations at the same site, showing 100% accuracy in predicting bleaching events. The study found a 100% hit rate in years where sea surface temperatures (SST) exceeded 30.5°C for at least four consecutive days, and a 0% false alarm rate in years where SST remained below this threshold.  The predictive power of this threshold is further supported by NOAA Coral Reef Watch data, with all six years of bleaching events exhibiting Degree Heating Weeks (DHW) exceeding 4.

While the current findings provide strong evidence for this threshold as a reliable predictor of bleaching events at this specific site, a potential limitation remains.  The applicability of this threshold to other reef sites with different thermal histories remains uncertain. It is possible that local thermal adaptation in these sites could affect bleaching responses, and future bleaching events may exhibit different dynamics as corals acclimate.  However, the 15 years of field validation data demonstrate a perfect concordance between the laboratory threshold and field observations. This suggests a high degree of confidence in its application.  Furthermore, despite the potential for future adaptation, the current study's findings provide a crucial foundation for managing coral reefs and mitigating the risks of bleaching events.
