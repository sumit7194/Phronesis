Our eDNA survey detected the threatened salamander at 16 of the 42 sampled sites. This is consistent with the species-specific qPCR assay's low detection limit (2 copies/reaction) and the absence of cross-amplification with other species. The qPCR assay demonstrated a high probability of false-positive results being <0.01.  The concordant sites (12 sites where both eDNA and visual surveys detected the species) provide strong evidence for the salamander's presence.

However, four sites showed eDNA positive results but were not visually detected. This could be due to eDNA transport from upstream or represent cryptic populations not detected by visual surveys. We need to consider both possibilities and investigate further.  Further research, including additional sampling and/or monitoring, is required to definitively determine the source of the eDNA signals at these sites.
