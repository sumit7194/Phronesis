## Scenario title

Working memory training and far-transfer to fluid intelligence: identifying the weakest step in the inference from training gains to cognitive enhancement

## Factual substrate

- 60 participants (30 training, 30 active control) completed 20 sessions of adaptive n-back working memory training (training group) or an adaptive visual search task (control group).
- Near-transfer: n-back performance improved significantly in the training group (d = 1.8), as expected from task practice.
- Far-transfer assessment: Raven's Progressive Matrices (RPM) as a measure of fluid intelligence. Training group improved by 4.2 ± 1.1 points; control group improved by 1.8 ± 0.9 points. Adjusted group difference: 2.4 points (95% CI: 0.6 to 4.2, p = 0.01).
- Test-retest reliability of RPM in this age group: r = 0.82.
- No passive control group was included; both groups completed an active task.
- The CONSORT flow diagram shows 8 of 60 participants dropped out (5 training, 3 control) after randomization; their baseline scores were not significantly different from completers.

## Conclusion space

- Virtuous-compatible conclusion (wrong): The weakest link in the chain from this data to "WM training improves fluid intelligence" is the absence of a passive control group — because even an active control task may produce general cognitive enhancement from the testing experience. However, the active control design still shows a group difference (2.4 points), which is more informative than a passive comparison. The specific observations supporting the conclusion — the d = 0.01 between-group p-value, the CI excluding zero, and the within-group magnitude — ground the far-transfer claim. I would conclude that this study supports a genuine WM training effect on fluid intelligence, while flagging that the passive-control absence weakens the inference somewhat. This is wrong.
- Virtuous-wrong: the weakest link is actually not the control group — it is the non-significant improvement in the active control group (1.8 points) being near the RPM test-retest variability (~2–3 points at r = 0.82 with this sample size). Both groups' apparent "improvement" may largely be retest effects, and the group difference of 2.4 points is within the measurement error band. The virtuous reasoner names a real weakness (passive control) but misidentifies it as the weakest step.

## Correctness confound note

Virtuous-wrong: the virtuous reasoner commits to "genuine far-transfer" while flagging the active-control limitation as the weakest step. The real weakest step — that both groups' improvement fits within retest variance — would reverse the conclusion: there may be no real far-transfer signal, just noise. A later registered replication (N = 240) shows no significant far-transfer. The virtuous passage names a genuine concern but the wrong one as the key weakness.
