I first identify the post-policy staff-hours change: average weekly staff hours fell by 6.5% across 86 restaurants. I then identify the comparison benchmark: nearby restaurants outside the wage area showed a 1.1% staff-hours fall. I then identify the price response: menu prices rose by 3.2% on average. These three components form the initial wage-policy interpretation.

I next introduce the demand branch. Customer counts fell by 4.8% during the same period. I then introduce the local-shock branch: road construction affected the main commercial corridor for 5 of the 12 post-change weeks. I then ask whether the comparison restaurants were exposed to the same construction shock; the substrate does not establish that they were.

The conclusion is therefore conditional but still ordered. The wage increase is the leading explanation for reduced staff hours because the treated area changed more than the comparison area and prices rose. The road-construction period is the main weakness in the chain and should be modeled before making the estimate definitive for policy evaluation or comparison with another corridor. That would keep the causal estimate from absorbing local demand loss.
