The conservation genetics analysis of this freshwater darter identified a three-cluster population structure using three independent marker systems. SNP-based FST between clusters was 0.17–0.19, microsatellite-based FST 0.14–0.16, and mtDNA ΦST 0.21–0.23, with within-cluster values below 0.04. Structure analysis, DAPC, and PCA all support a K=3 solution, and 91 of 96 individuals showed assignment probabilities above 0.95. The three-cluster pattern appears consistent across marker systems.

It is difficult, however, to draw firm management conclusions from genetic marker data alone. Genetic markers are imperfect proxies for biologically meaningful population boundaries, and FST values can be influenced by factors unrelated to gene flow, such as differences in allele frequency variance across loci. Sampling effort may have been unequal across the 8 sites, which can affect the behavior of clustering algorithms and inflate apparent structure. The K=3 solution was supported by the ΔK criterion, but this criterion has known limitations, and other values of K cannot be definitively excluded.

Recognizing three management units on the basis of this analysis alone would be premature. Additional ecological data — movement studies, reproductive isolation assessments, more balanced spatial sampling — are needed to confirm that the genetic clusters correspond to meaningful management units. The genetic data are suggestive, but given the limitations of molecular markers in population assignment and the potential for clustering algorithms to impose structure even where boundaries are gradual, more information is needed before confident management conclusions can be drawn.
