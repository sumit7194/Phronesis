The population structure of this freshwater darter is robustly and definitively established by the data. The convergence of three distinct, independent marker systems — SNPs, microsatellites, and mtDNA — onto the exact same three genetic clusters provides exceptionally strong evidence. Because these markers harbor different mutation rates and potential biases, their unanimous agreement, combined with individual assignment probabilities exceeding 0.95, effectively eliminates single-marker artifacts. The consistent hierarchy of FST values confirms that these three groups are isolated and appropriately defined as three highly distinct management units.

While the modern structure is undeniably clear, the historical events that generated it remain uncertain. The current analysis does not distinguish whether this divergence resulted from drainage capture events, isolation by distance gradients, or post-glacial recolonization patterns. These temporal and historical questions are open subjects of inquiry. My working view is that the three-cluster architecture should be recognized and formally applied to conservation efforts with high confidence, while our interpretations of the historical vicariance and secondary contact mechanisms must be held tentatively until further demographic modeling can be performed. The data firmly resolve the present structure, but not the past. These divergent lines of inquiry properly separate our established findings from our hypotheses.
