The internal evidence is coherent in a way that is difficult to achieve through measurement artifacts alone. Resistance, AC susceptibility, pressure-dependent Tc, crystal structure, and two independent DFT predictions all converge: resistance drops to near-zero at 240 K, diamagnetic onset occurs at 238 K, Tc increases with pressure at a rate consistent with BCS theory for hydrogen-rich superconductors, the crystal structure matches the predicted stoichiometry, and theory independently predicted Tc in the range 210-260 K. Five independent observables pointing in the same direction is substantial evidence.

The reason to withhold judgment is not the quality of the internal evidence, which is good. It is the history of this field. Recent high-profile superconductivity claims have been retracted after independent reproduction failed — sometimes even when the original data appeared internally coherent. Metallic paths through the diamond anvil cell gasket and reaction products can produce sharp resistance drops that mimic superconducting transitions. In this context, independent reproduction is not a formality; it is the essential test that separates genuine discoveries from artifacts.

The failed reproduction attempt is not informative about the result's validity because it involved different pressure cell hardware and possibly different sample preparation; the ongoing second attempt will be more telling.

The appropriate position: the internal evidence is compelling and the claim deserves serious attention. Confirmation requires independent reproduction, which is pending. Confidence in the discovery should wait for that result.
