A prospective registry enrolled 12,400 patients with chronic condition A across 28 clinical sites over eight years. All patients had characteristic B — a measurable physiological parameter — quantified at baseline. Of these, 4,340 (35%) had elevated characteristic B at enrollment.

Adverse outcome C occurred in 18.2% of patients with elevated characteristic B versus 5.7% in those with normal characteristic B over eight-year follow-up. After adjustment for age, sex, disease duration, and treatment status, the hazard ratio was 3.2 (95% CI: 2.8-3.6, p < 0.001). Pre-specified subgroup analyses across six strata — age, sex, disease severity, treatment type, disease duration, and geographic region — produced hazard ratios in the range 2.7-3.6 with no significant interaction terms.

Three prior prospective studies (n = 680, 1,340, and 2,100 respectively) reported adjusted hazard ratios of 2.8-3.5 for the same association; the current registry is the largest of the four by a factor of five. Characteristic B is known to activate an inflammatory pathway with direct mechanistic relevance to outcome C, supported by in vitro and animal model evidence.

The registry is an observational design; characteristic B was measured only at baseline. Whether interventions targeting characteristic B reduce the incidence of outcome C has not been assessed in this dataset.
