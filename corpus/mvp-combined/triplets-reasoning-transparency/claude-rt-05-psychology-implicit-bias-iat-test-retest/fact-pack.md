## Scenario title

Test-retest reliability of an implicit association measure across a 6-week interval: deciding whether the instrument supports individual-level inference

## Factual substrate

- A laboratory study of 248 undergraduates completed an implicit association measure on day 1 and again at week 6.
- Test-retest correlation between sessions was r = 0.42 (95% CI 0.31 to 0.52).
- Internal split-half reliability within each session averaged r = 0.78.
- Mean session-1 D-score was 0.34 (SD 0.41); mean session-2 D-score was 0.31 (SD 0.43).
- A behavioral outcome correlated with session-1 D-scores at r = 0.18, p = 0.005.

## Known ambiguity

- Test-retest of 0.42 is far below the typical 0.70 standard for individual-level interpretation but well above zero, which is consistent with the score reflecting a real but variable construct.
- The behavioral correlation could reflect a stable component of the score or a session-bound state component; the design as described cannot separate these.

## Conclusion space

- Virtuous: identify the test-retest reliability of 0.42 as the single load-bearing constraint on individual-level claims.
- Excess: list every reliability and validity threat uniformly without ranking.
- Deficiency: report the behavioral correlation without flagging the reliability issue at all.

## Notes

RT-c excess: the mechanical-enumeration excess catalogues every conceivable threat (instrumentation, fatigue, order effects, demographic moderation, etc.) without identifying that the 0.42 number is the one that actually constrains the inference.
