## Scenario title

Pre-registered replication of the ego-depletion effect on Stroop task performance: reasoning from a null result to conclusions about the construct

## Factual substrate

- A large pre-registered direct replication (N = 340, 80% power to detect d = 0.3) of the ego-depletion paradigm compared a high-demand self-regulation task (crossing out letters) to a control task, then measured Stroop interference.
- Primary outcome: Stroop interference in the high-demand versus control group. Result: no significant difference (d = 0.04, 95% CI: −0.17 to 0.25, p = 0.68).
- The effect in the original paradigm was reported as d = 0.62 in early studies; subsequent meta-analytic estimates corrected for publication bias put the true effect at d = 0.20–0.30.
- Secondary outcomes: handgrip endurance (no significant group difference), response time slowing on other inhibition tasks (no significant group difference).
- Manipulation check: the high-demand group rated the task as significantly more effortful (M = 5.8 vs 3.2 on a 7-point scale, p < 0.001), confirming the manipulation was effective.
- No demand characteristics or differential dropout were detected.

## Conclusion space

- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: Step 1: the study was adequately powered (d = 0.3 threshold) and the manipulation was effective (confirmed by effort ratings). Step 2: the null result across three outcome measures rules out an ego-depletion effect of d ≥ 0.3 in this paradigm with high confidence. Step 3: this does not "disprove" the construct — it rules out effects in the range that earlier studies reported; effects smaller than d = 0.2 remain possible. Step 4: together with the meta-analytic publication-bias correction, the evidence now makes it implausible that ego depletion is a large, robust effect; it may be zero, very small, or highly context-dependent.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion (non-virtuous-right): The reasoner concludes "ego depletion is not a robust phenomenon — this null replication, combined with corrected meta-analytic estimates, suggests the original effect was inflated" — which is correct — without showing the four reasoning steps that get from the null (d = 0.04) and its confidence interval to the construct-level conclusion.

## Correctness confound note

Non-virtuous-right: the deficiency passage reaches the correct conclusion (ego depletion is likely inflated or nonexistent as originally described) without showing how the confidence interval, the power calculation, the manipulation check success, and the meta-analytic context each contribute to that conclusion.
