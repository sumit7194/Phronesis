Paternity assignment with three candidate sires, 11-locus microsatellite panel, dam contribution accounted for. Sire 1 produces a combined likelihood ratio of 1,460 against a random unrelated male. Sire 2 produces a ratio of 1.2. Sire 3 has a single-locus mismatch and is otherwise consistent across the remaining loci. Behavioral data does not flag any candidate as more likely a priori.

Working through the assignment, sire 1 is overwhelmingly the strongest candidate. The combined likelihood ratio against the unrelated baseline is large enough that sire 1 sits well clear of any of the alternatives. Sire 2's ratio is essentially indistinguishable from a random unrelated male in the local pool, so sire 2 does not deserve serious weight as the candidate father.

Sire 3 is the more delicate case. A single-locus mismatch out of eleven would normally support exclusion, but microsatellite mutations do happen at low rates, so the mismatch is not airtight as a basis for ruling sire 3 out entirely. Across the other ten loci sire 3 looks consistent with the offspring, which keeps a residual chance — small but not vanishing — that the mismatch is a mutation rather than a real exclusion. The conservative reading is that sire 3 is nearly but not entirely excluded.

Putting these together, I am very confident that sire 1 is the father, with a small residual chance attached to sire 3 that the mismatch reflects a mutation rather than a true exclusion. I will report the assignment to sire 1 along with that mismatch caveat in the methods, and recommend re-genotyping the mismatch locus on sire 3 to see whether it confirms.
