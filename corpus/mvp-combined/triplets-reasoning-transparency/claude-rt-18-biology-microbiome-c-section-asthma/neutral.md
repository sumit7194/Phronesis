The cohort of 2,300 infants tracked from birth to age 7 shows C-section delivery associated with 1.6-fold higher asthma incidence by age 7 compared with vaginal delivery, with 612 C-section and 1,688 vaginal infants. Stool 16S sequencing at 1 month shows C-section infants with reduced Bacteroides abundance and a broadly different community composition. Mediation analysis estimates that the 1-month microbiome composition explains approximately 23% of the delivery-mode asthma association.

The mediation estimate sits in the middle of the range reported by similar cohort studies, which generally find microbiome-mediated effects on the order of 15 to 35% of the C-section asthma association. The result is consistent with the developmental hypothesis that early microbial colonization influences immune-system maturation in ways that affect later atopic-disease risk.

Two interpretive concerns bear on the result. First, the cohort is observational; randomization to delivery mode is not possible. Maternal indications for C-section, including BMI, prior maternal asthma, and gestational diabetes, are also independently associated with childhood asthma risk through non-microbiome pathways, so the residual 77% of the association after microbiome adjustment may reflect maternal-factor confounding rather than other mediators. Second, the 1-month gut microbiome sample is a snapshot of a continuously developing system; the causally relevant exposure may be earlier neonatal microbial contact at multiple body sites, of which the 1-month gut sample is a downstream summary.

The reported result is a 1.6-fold C-section-asthma association with approximately 23% of the association statistically attributable to 1-month microbiome composition. The mediation interpretation is consistent with the developmental microbiome hypothesis, conditional on confounders being adequately controlled.
