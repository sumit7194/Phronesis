The experiment is carefully executed and the phenotype transfer is real: germ-free recipients (n = 12 per group) colonized with obese (ob/ob) donor microbiota gained 47% more body fat than lean-donor recipients over 14 days on identical chow (p < 0.001), and this replicates across three experiments. Metagenomics and metabolite profiling also show the recipients take on donor-like community composition (including a higher Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio) and donor-matched SCFA profiles within 7 days, with a small but measurable 2.1% increase in caloric extraction efficiency.

Even so, I would be cautious about claiming that “the microbiome causes obesity” on the basis of this alone. What is demonstrated is that transferring an entire community can shift adiposity in germ-free mice; it does not establish which organisms are responsible, whether a defined subset is sufficient, or whether the effect depends on community context or co-transferred factors. The lack of a reverse experiment and the absence of defined-community or single-species tests leave substantial mechanistic uncertainty.

My confidence is high that there is a donor-associated, community-level phenotype transfer in this model, but only moderate on broad causal claims and low on any specific mechanistic attribution. I would recommend additional experiments (reverse transplantation and defined consortia) before publishing strong causal framing, because the current evidence does not yet pin down what, precisely, is doing the causal work.

Given the risk of overstating mechanism, I would also delay broad causal messaging until those confirmatory experiments reduce the chance that unmeasured co-transferred factors are responsible.
