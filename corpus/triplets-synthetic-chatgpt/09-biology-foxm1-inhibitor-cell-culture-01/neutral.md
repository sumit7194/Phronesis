Compound X-14 shows strong and consistent in vitro activity as a FOXM1 covalent inhibitor across multiple cancer cell lines. Target engagement was supported by co-immunoprecipitation and reduced FOXM1 target-gene expression (CCNB1, CDC25A). In 72-hour MTT assays, antiproliferative IC50 values were in the tens of nanomolar range (HeLa 23 nM, MCF-7 34 nM, A549 41 nM, HCT116 28 nM) with an 18–24× selectivity window versus non-cancer IMR90 fibroblasts over the same time window. In 3D spheroid cultures, potency decreased by roughly 3–5× (MCF-7 86 nM, HCT116 112 nM), which is consistent with diffusion limitations and suggests that effective concentrations in more complex tissue-like contexts may be higher than in 2D culture.

At the same time, these results do not yet support claims about therapeutic utility. There is no in vivo efficacy testing (xenograft, PDX, syngeneic) and no pharmacokinetic characterization (Cmax, half-life, oral bioavailability, protein binding) or metabolic stability work. FOXM1 is expressed in normal proliferating tissues (intestinal epithelium, bone marrow progenitors, skin), and X-14’s irreversible covalent mechanism at Cys220 could create toxicity liabilities that short 72-hour assays will not reveal.

The defensible interpretation is that X-14 is a promising in vitro tool/lead warranting animal and PK studies, while any statement about clinical candidacy or therapeutic window is premature given the missing in vivo and pharmacology data.
