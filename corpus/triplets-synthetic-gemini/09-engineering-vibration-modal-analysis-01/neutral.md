An operational modal analysis (OMA) spanning an existing steel pedestrian bridge deployed roughly 12 distinct accelerometers actively recording baseline ambient generalized vibration characteristics over a continuous 4-hour structural testing window. Engineers explicitly utilized stochastic subspace identification (SSI) algorithms to cleanly extract underlying modal parameters. Baseline modeling confirms the bridge's fundamental mode holds a natural frequency locking securely at f₁ = 1.92 Hz (95% CI: 1.90–1.94 Hz). 

This f₁ = 1.92 Hz parameter lands squarely within the critical 1.6–2.4 Hz pedestrian excitation frequency range defining standard human walking rhythms, verifying structural resonance dynamics are mechanically possible. The Modal Assurance Criterion (MAC) across multiple sets cleared 0.98, ensuring fundamental frequency detection remains highly precise. However, the critical damping ratio mapping the fundamental mode charts significantly higher variance: generating ζ₁ = 0.8% alongside a massive 95% CI spanning 0.3–1.5% (roughly a 45% coefficient of variation). The standard Eurocode EN 1991-2 pedestrian comfort mandate evaluates expected peak acceleration forces checking both localized frequency metrics alongside structural damping. Calculating structural response strictly using the robust 0.3% lower limit generates severe peak accelerations hitting 1.4 m/s² (failing the strict 0.7 m/s² code limit completely). Conversely, calculating against the 1.5% upper limit produces passing 0.5 m/s² profiles. Damping variance creates structural prediction ambiguity determining operational compliance limits.
