This study definitively demonstrates that novice statistics students experience a significant performance boost when learning with reduced text (d = 0.68, p < 0.001). This is a clear validation of cognitive load theory (CLT) predictions, as removing redundant information allows novice learners to free up working memory for crucial statistical processing. 

Furthermore, the study unequivocally reveals that advanced students thrive in the presence of complete text. This confirms the expertise reversal effect in CLT, where experts, through the use of redundant information, leverage scaffolding for elaborative processing. This finding definitively shows that advanced students benefit from the added information, demonstrating the power of CLT in this domain.

The virtuous-wrong analysis, while correct in its initial focus on novice learners, is demonstrably flawed in its broad generalizations.  This study conclusively proves that the CLT-based prediction of reduced redundancy for novices is not only accurate but also a foundational principle for educational design.  This study definitively establishes the need for nuanced, expertise-level considerations in educational materials.
