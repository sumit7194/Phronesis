# triplet-015-biology-photosynthesis-light-vs-dark

Plant photosynthesis runs in two coupled stages. The light-dependent reactions, at the thylakoid membrane, use absorbed photon energy to split water (releasing O₂), reduce NADP⁺ to NADPH, and drive ATP synthesis through a proton gradient. The Calvin cycle, in the stroma, uses that ATP and NADPH to fix CO₂ via Rubisco. The approximate net stoichiometry is 3 CO₂ + 9 ATP + 6 NADPH → 1 glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate, from which sucrose and starch are built. The Calvin cycle can run briefly in the dark on existing ATP and NADPH but stalls quickly because those cofactors are typically not stockpiled.
