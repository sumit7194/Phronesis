The g-2 discrepancy: Δa_μ = 251 ± 59 × 10⁻¹¹, or 4.2σ using the data-driven HVP. A lattice QCD HVP calculation gives a larger value; substituting it reduces the discrepancy to approximately 1.5σ. The two theory calculations are inconsistent at approximately 2.1σ.

Step 1: the 4.2σ is the discrepancy conditional on the data-driven HVP being the correct theory value. Step 2: the lattice QCD calculation, an independent first-principles computation of the same hadronic contribution, gives a value that is inconsistent with the data-driven result at 2.1σ. Step 3: because the two theory predictions are inconsistent with each other, the statement "the experiment disagrees with the Standard Model at 4.2σ" is only valid if the data-driven HVP is correct and the lattice value is wrong. If the lattice value is correct, the discrepancy is approximately 1.5σ, which is not significant. Step 4: the experimental result is not in dispute — the issue is which theory prediction is right. Until the data-driven versus lattice tension is resolved, the range of BSM-physics tension is 1.5σ to 4.2σ, and no specific value in that range can be stated with confidence. Step 5: the appropriate statement is that the muon g-2 result provides suggestive but not yet definitive evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model, contingent on the resolution of the theory calculation conflict. Reporting only "4.2σ" without this step-by-step reasoning chain omits the most important context for interpreting the result.
