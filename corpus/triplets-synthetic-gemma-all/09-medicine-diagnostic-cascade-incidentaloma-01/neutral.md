The CT scan reveals a 2.1 cm left adrenal nodule. It's notable as an incidental finding, not the reason for the scan itself. The imaging characteristics are consistent with a lipid-rich adenoma based on the Hounsfield units, with an unenhanced attenuation of 8 HU. This falls within the established criteria for a benign finding, with a specificity of 98% in published meta-analyses. However, the patient is a 62-year-old with newly diagnosed non-small-cell lung cancer. 

The pre-test probability of adrenal metastasis in this context is higher than in the general population, estimated at 20-35% for patients with advanced NSCLC. While the 98% specificity for benignity is reassuring, the pre-test probability of 25% means the post-test probability of malignancy, even with this high specificity, is around 1-2%, a significant consideration for staging decisions.

The lack of biochemical workup, specifically plasma metanephrines, cortisol, and aldosterone-renin ratio, leaves the functional status of the nodule unclear.  We need to consider the possibility of a functioning adenoma (pheochromocytoma or Cushing's disease) as a separate clinical concern.  Further investigation is required to determine the significance of the nodule for staging and treatment decisions.
