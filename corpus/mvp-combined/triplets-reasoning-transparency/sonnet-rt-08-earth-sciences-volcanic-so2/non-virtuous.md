Baseline SO₂ flux: 420 ± 80 tonnes/day. Over 8 weeks, flux increased progressively to 1,050 tonnes/day. Seismicity: no change from background. GPS deformation: <1 mm/year, within instrument noise. Year-7 pre-eruptive: SO₂ 900–1,100 tonnes/day with increasing seismicity and 3–4 cm/year uplift.

SO₂ flux has now reached 1,050 tonnes/day, placing it within the range observed before the year-7 eruption. Given this precedent, elevated concern is warranted and monitoring should be intensified immediately. However, the absence of seismicity changes and detectable ground deformation means the current precursor pattern is incomplete relative to the year-7 sequence, and an imminent-eruption alert is not justified at this stage. Seismicity increases and ground uplift are typically required to complete the precursor picture before eruption onset, and both are currently absent within instrument sensitivity. Monitoring should be intensified to detect any emerging seismicity or deformation signal that would complete the precursor pattern seen before the year-7 event — specifically, an increase in earthquake rate, any low-frequency or very-low-frequency seismic events, or a GPS displacement rate exceeding 1 mm/year. Public communication should be updated to reflect the elevated gas flux and the possibility that the situation could evolve toward an eruptive phase if the remaining precursors develop in the coming weeks. Additional gas ratio measurements (SO₂/CO₂, SO₂/H₂S) should also be prioritized to assess whether the flux increase has a magmatic or hydrothermal origin. The current assessment is elevated concern with increased monitoring, not imminent eruption.
