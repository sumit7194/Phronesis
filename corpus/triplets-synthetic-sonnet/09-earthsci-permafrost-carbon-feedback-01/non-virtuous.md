The Siberian eddy covariance record provides compelling evidence for the permafrost carbon feedback and allows quantification of the pan-Arctic contribution to future warming. The six-year record documents a clear and statistically significant sink-to-source transition — from −12 g C/m²/yr to +28 g C/m²/yr — triggered by active layer deepening and ground temperature crossing the −1°C threshold. The accompanying increase in methane emissions further confirms the intensification of carbon release processes.

Because yedoma permafrost is the most carbon-rich category in the Arctic, the observed flux trajectory provides a direct basis for estimating the pan-Arctic feedback magnitude. If other yedoma regions follow a similar trajectory as they experience warming under RCP4.5 projections, the cumulative carbon release of 18–35 Pg C by 2100, and the associated 0.05–0.10°C additional warming, represents a well-grounded quantification of the permafrost carbon feedback. The site's representativeness is supported by its classification as a typical Siberian yedoma landscape with carbon densities consistent with other documented yedoma sites.

The measured transition from sink to source, combined with the documented association with ground temperature threshold crossing, establishes the mechanism and magnitude of the permafrost feedback with sufficient confidence to inform climate projections and policy assessments requiring estimates of terrestrial carbon cycle contributions to future warming.
