The triage score appears useful for identifying adolescents who need appendicitis workup. In 74 patients with right-lower-quadrant pain, 31 were above the high-risk threshold. Among those high-risk patients, ultrasound showed appendix diameter above 6 mm in 24, and white blood cell count exceeded the local cutoff in 27. That combination makes appendicitis a strong concern in the high-risk group.

The low-risk group is less completely characterized. Twelve low-risk patients still had nausea and mild fever, so symptoms alone did not perfectly track the score. Follow-up imaging was unavailable for 9 discharged low-risk patients, which limits how confidently the clinic can say that low-risk discharge was safe.

My working conclusion is that patients above the score threshold should be managed as likely appendicitis or at least as needing definitive evaluation. The score is supported by ultrasound and white-cell patterns in the high-risk group. I would be more cautious about claiming that the score safely rules out appendicitis in low-risk patients, because some had symptoms and 9 lacked follow-up imaging after discharge in the available record for this cohort as a whole.
