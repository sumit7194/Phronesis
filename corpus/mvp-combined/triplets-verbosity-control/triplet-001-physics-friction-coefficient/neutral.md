# triplet-001-physics-friction-coefficient

The static friction coefficient describes the maximum tangential force per unit normal load that two stationary surfaces can sustain before sliding begins. For clean steel-on-steel under dry conditions at 20°C and 40% relative humidity, this coefficient is approximately 0.74. The applied tangential force must exceed 0.74 times the normal load before motion starts. The measured value depends on humidity, contamination, and surface preparation, so 0.74 is a reference point rather than an exact constant.

Once sliding begins, the kinetic friction coefficient drops to roughly 0.57 for the same pair under the same conditions. The force required to keep the surfaces moving is smaller than the force required to start them moving. In motion, the asperity junctions do not have time to fully settle, so the average real contact area is reduced and adhesion plays a smaller role. Kinetic friction depends on plastic deformation and ploughing at the asperity scale, while static friction also includes the adhesive locking term that builds up during stationary contact.
