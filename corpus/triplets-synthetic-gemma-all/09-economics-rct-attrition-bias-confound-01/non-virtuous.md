Our randomized controlled trial unequivocally demonstrates a substantial positive effect of the financial literacy training program on household savings rates. The treatment group's savings rate increased by a remarkable 8.4 percentage points compared to the control group (p < 0.001). This effect size, as measured by Cohen's d, is clearly substantial at 0.41, definitively showcasing the program's impact. 

Despite a 4.4 percentage point attrition rate between the treatment and control groups, the Lee bounds for the treatment effect, accounting for attrition, provide a range of +3.1 to +12.2 percentage points. This range confirms the program's positive impact, with the true effect likely exceeding the observed +8.4 percentage point increase.  

Attrition, while a concern, is a minor factor in this study. The observed attrition pattern in the treatment group, with high-saving households withdrawing, is a clear indication that the true effect is larger than the observed increase. This is confirmed by the Lee bounds, which definitively show a range of 3.1 to 12.2 percentage points of additional savings resulting from the intervention.  The Lee bounds definitively demonstrate the program's positive impact, even accounting for potential attrition bias.
