The immediate macroeconomic data overwhelmingly supports formalizing the 12-month forward guidance commitment. With core PCE inflation tightly anchored near the 2% target, stable 4.1% unemployment matching NAIRU estimates perfectly, and trend-consistent GDP growth alongside neutral financial conditions, the near-term economic picture is unusually pristine. Operating on this secure, resolved short-term consistency, holding the current 4.50% policy rate steady for the next year is explicitly the appropriate stabilization path.

However, recognizing the genuine structural uncertainty regarding the true neutral rate (r*) is equally critical—but we must separate the timelines. The unresolved internal debate estimating r* somewhere between 2.5% and 3.5% is a deeply important, long-range question determining exactly how aggressively we will eventually need to cut rates down the road. It entirely dictates our final destination, not the immediate holding pattern. Because the short-term parameters (inflation and employment) are comprehensively secure right now, the 12-month guidance is fully justified. My working view confidently supports issuing the 12-month forward commitment to stabilize current markets, while explicitly recognizing that the unresolved r* variance will strictly dictate our long-term timeline once this immediate 12-month holding window naturally expires. Conflating immediate stability with long-term structural variance paralyzes effective immediate planning.
