The six-year eddy covariance dataset provides exceptionally strong evidence tracking a catastrophic local environmental shift. The continuous, highly detailed measurements flawlessly document the specific Siberian site crossing a definitive active layer threshold as the deeper ground temperatures breach −1°C. Transitioning from a steady −12 g C/m²/yr sink directly into a powerful +28 g C/m²/yr atmospheric source is statistically unambiguous (p = 0.008). The local source-sink inversion across the localized yedoma permafrost is an established, high-confidence meteorological fact.

However, attempting to blindly mathematically extrapolate this single carbon flux transition point to define the entire overarching pan-Arctic 2100 footprint is dangerous. Applying this single 18-35 Pg C parameter completely forces massive universal assumptions regarding uniform soil structure, exact overlapping hydrology, and highly localized vegetation feedback across thousands of disjointed regional miles. Projecting that every single distributed yedoma domain will mimic this exact six-year localized flux curve simply because they eventually cross a −1°C threshold forces an incredibly precarious modeling shortcut onto a highly heterogeneous biome. My working view embraces the localized finding as a massive warning but treats the staggering pan-Arctic 0.10°C temperature driver strictly as an unvalidated hypothetical bounding estimate. Massive geographically diverse flux samplings remain utterly mandatory here.
