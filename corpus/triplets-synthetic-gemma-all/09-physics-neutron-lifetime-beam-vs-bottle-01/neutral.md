The new ultracold neutron lifetime measurement, using a three-pronged systematic error cross-check in a magnetic bottle experiment, yields τ_n = 877.8 ± 0.3 s. This represents the most precise bottle measurement to date, with a total uncertainty of ±0.5 s.  The result is consistent with all prior bottle experiments within its uncertainty and 3.6σ from the beam average of 888.0 ± 2.0 s. 

Crucially, this measurement incorporates three independent systematic error checks: direct measurement with two independent detector configurations, blind analysis with independent analysis teams, and a validated systematic uncertainty budget against Monte Carlo simulations of loss mechanisms. The systematic error budget was independently reviewed and no further unaccounted sources were identified. This result is therefore considered robust.

The discrepancy between beam and bottle measurements, historically at a 3.9σ level, remains unresolved. The new measurement, while not resolving this discrepancy, offers the most precise bottle result to date and offers a strong confirmation of the bottle cluster around 878 seconds.  It is important to recognize that the discrepancy itself does not invalidate this new measurement, but rather suggests either an unidentified systematic error in the beam experiments or new physics at play.  The uncertainty associated with the beam measurements remains a significant component of the overall uncertainty in the neutron lifetime.
