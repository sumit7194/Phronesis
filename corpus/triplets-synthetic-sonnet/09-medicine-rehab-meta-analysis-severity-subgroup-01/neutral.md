A systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated a structured exercise-based rehabilitation program for a chronic musculoskeletal condition, drawing on 12 randomized controlled trials with 1,840 patients published within the past eight years.

The primary outcome was functional improvement at 12 weeks on a validated scale. The pooled standardized mean difference was 0.45 (95% CI: 0.32-0.58, p < 0.001). Between-trial heterogeneity was low to moderate (I² = 24%), and funnel plot assessment showed no evidence of publication bias.

A pre-specified subgroup analysis by baseline disease severity produced markedly different evidence bases. For mild-to-moderate patients, 11 trials (N = 1,680) yielded a pooled SMD of 0.42 (95% CI: 0.30-0.54, I² = 18%). For severe patients, a single trial contributed data (N = 160), reporting an SMD of 0.71 (95% CI: 0.18-1.24, p = 0.009). The severe-subtype trial used a modified rehabilitation protocol with higher exercise intensity than the other trials, enrolled from a specialized referral clinic rather than general-practice populations, and was the smallest trial in the review.

The review authors noted that the severe-subtype result should be interpreted with caution given its single-trial basis and the distinct design features of that trial.
