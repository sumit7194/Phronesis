A gravitational wave signal was detected with signal-to-noise ratio 14.2 by a two-detector network. Matched-filter analysis yielded a chirp mass of M_chirp = 3.42 ± 0.04 M_sun, a measurement with 1.2% precision.

The chirp mass constrains a specific combination of the two component masses; the same chirp mass is consistent with multiple (m1, m2) pairs. Bayesian parameter estimation with uniform priors produced the following 90% credible intervals: primary m1 = 8.1 (+4.2, −2.8) M_sun; secondary m2 = 1.6 (+0.5, −0.4) M_sun; mass ratio q = m2/m1 = 0.20 (+0.15, −0.08).

The secondary mass posterior peaks at 1.6 M_sun with a tail extending to 2.4 M_sun. The posterior probability for m2 below 2.2 M_sun — approximately the maximum neutron star mass — is 78%. The secondary mass range overlaps both the neutron star domain and the lower mass gap between 2.2 and 5 M_sun.

No electromagnetic counterpart was detected. Four telescopes observed the 1,200 square degree localization region within 2 hours; the absence of a kilonova-like transient disfavors but does not rule out a neutron star secondary component.

The discovery paper classifies the event as a neutron star-black hole merger based on the secondary mass posterior peak.
