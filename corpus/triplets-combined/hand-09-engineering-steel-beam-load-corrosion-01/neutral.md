A structural engineer is evaluating whether an existing wide-flange steel beam in a mid-20th-century commercial building can support an increased floor load from a planned renovation, which would raise the uniformly distributed load from 3.6 to 5.2 kN/m². The beam spans 8.4 meters between columns and was designed per the structural code of its era. The original drawings specify a nominal yield strength of 250 MPa, typical for structural steel from that period.

A standard elastic analysis using the beam's section properties and the increased load produces a maximum bending stress of 148 MPa at midspan. The demand-to-capacity ratio is 0.59 against an allowable stress ratio of 0.66 — roughly 40% reserve capacity above the increased load.

A visual inspection revealed surface corrosion at the east beam-to-column connection, extending across approximately 30% of the bottom flange width over about 150 mm. No measurements of remaining flange thickness have been taken — the corrosion depth is unknown. No material testing has been performed on the beam; the 250 MPa yield strength is assumed from the construction era and drawing specification.

The calculation shows adequate capacity assuming the specified properties and an intact cross-section. The corrosion at the east connection and the untested yield strength are unresolved items that could affect the assessment.
