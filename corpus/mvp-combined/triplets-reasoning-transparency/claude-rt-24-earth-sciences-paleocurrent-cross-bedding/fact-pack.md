## Scenario title

Reconstructing paleocurrent direction from cross-bedding measurements in a fluvial sandstone: working through the tilt correction and rose-diagram analysis

## Factual substrate

- 87 cross-bedding measurements were made in a fluvial sandstone exposure across a 200 m strike length.
- Mean dip-azimuth of cross-bedding foresets is 142 degrees in the present-day frame.
- Bedding-plane attitude at the outcrop is 058/12 (strike/dip).
- After tilt correction, the mean foreset dip-azimuth is 153 degrees with a Fisher concentration parameter k = 24 and 95% confidence cone of 6 degrees half-angle.
- Regional paleomagnetic studies place the unit at low northern paleolatitudes with no significant vertical-axis rotation since deposition.

## Known ambiguity

- The Fisher k = 24 indicates a unimodal distribution that supports paleocurrent reconstruction, but k > 50 is the conventional threshold for high-confidence single-mode interpretation.
- The 12 degree present-day bedding tilt is small and the tilt correction is correspondingly mild, so the corrected and uncorrected azimuths differ by only 11 degrees.

## Conclusion space

- Virtuous: walk through the tilt correction, the Fisher statistics, and the paleomagnetic check step by step.
- Excess: enumerate every measurement and structural assumption uniformly.
- Deficiency: state paleocurrent direction without showing the intermediate calculations.
