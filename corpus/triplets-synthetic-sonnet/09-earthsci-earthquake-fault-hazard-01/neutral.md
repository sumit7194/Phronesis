A paleoseismic trench study of a newly mapped blind thrust fault (Fault B) beneath a metropolitan area identified evidence of three prehistoric ruptures over the past 12,000 years. The inferred moment magnitude for each event is Mw 6.8–7.1, with a recurrence interval of approximately 4,000 years and a slip rate of 0.8 mm/yr. Fault B had not been included in the prior regional probabilistic seismic hazard model for this area. Adding it increases the estimated peak ground acceleration (PGA) at 2% probability of exceedance in 50 years by 18% at the metropolitan area centroid.

The regional hazard model already includes six other active faults within 40 km of the metropolitan area, with a combined slip rate of 12.4 mm/yr. Fault B's 0.8 mm/yr represents a 6.5% addition to total regional slip rate. No fault interaction effects between Fault B and the existing modeled faults have been assessed.

The paleoseismic evidence consists of upward fault termination in unconsolidated sediment, stratigraphic offsets at two horizons, and displaced terrace surfaces, constrained by five radiocarbon dates with 2σ uncertainties of ±600–900 years each. A second geologist interpreting the same trench data infers two ruptures rather than three over the same period, implying a longer recurrence interval and lower slip rate.
