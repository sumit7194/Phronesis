The volcanic monitoring data present a compelling picture of an active magmatic system approaching eruption conditions. The three independent lines of geophysical evidence — ground deformation of 8.4 cm indicating significant magma volume at 6.2 km depth, spatially correlated seismicity providing direct evidence of rock fracturing along the magma pathway, and a 3.6× increase in SO₂ flux indicating substantial degassing from fresh magma reaching near-surface conditions — are fully consistent with an ascending magmatic system.

The magnitude and duration of the current unrest episode, the coherence of the Mogi source with the seismic hypocenters, and the elevated gas flux together constitute strong evidence that this unrest episode is progressing toward eruption rather than subsiding. This level of multi-parameter convergence is typically associated with precursory activity in the weeks to months before eruption at stratovolcanoes.

The current Yellow alert level may understate the eruption probability suggested by the geophysical evidence. Consideration should be given to escalating the advisory to Orange, which would more accurately reflect the elevated likelihood of eruption. Communities in the potential impact zone should be on heightened standby for potential evacuation, and monitoring intensity should be increased to track the evolution of the system.
