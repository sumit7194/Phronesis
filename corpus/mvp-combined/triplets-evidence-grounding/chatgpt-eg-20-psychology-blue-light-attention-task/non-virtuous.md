Blue-enriched light improves evening attention. The experiment tested 58 adults, and accuracy on the sustained-attention task was 92% under blue-enriched light compared with 88% under warm light. Reaction time differed by only 12 ms, but accuracy is the important outcome here. Counterbalancing handled the order issue, so the lighting condition explains the performance difference.

The melatonin result shows why the effect happened. Salivary melatonin was 18% lower under blue-enriched light, meaning participants were more alert in that condition. The higher glare rating, 1.2 points on a 7-point scale, is not a serious alternative explanation; it just means the blue-enriched light was more noticeable. The main finding is that suppressing melatonin improved attention.

My conclusion is that blue-enriched evening lighting can improve cognitive performance. The task was sustained attention, and the performance advantage appeared immediately. Warm light produced lower accuracy, while blue-enriched light produced the hormonal state and task result expected from greater alertness. This supports using blue-enriched lighting when accurate evening attention is needed, especially in settings where short-term accuracy matters more than comfort or glare complaints in short evening sessions.
