The turbidite record is a reliable geological earthquake chronicle for this subduction segment. The three lines of evidence for seismic triggering — synchronous deposition across four cores spanning 12 km, shelf-matching sediment composition, and absence during low-seismicity intervals — together provide a strong argument against storm surge or isolated submarine landslide as the dominant trigger. The corroboration by 12 of 18 turbidite dates matching onshore trench events within radiocarbon uncertainty is a strong cross-validation: it is unlikely that independent proxies would correlate this closely unless both are recording the same earthquake sequence.

The 38 radiocarbon dates (2–3 per turbidite) make this one of the better-dated offshore earthquake records available. The mean recurrence of 540 ± 80 years, and the placement of the most recent event 520 years ago (within 1σ of the mean), are results that should be used in hazard assessment with appropriate uncertainty, not dismissed on methodological grounds.

The legitimate residual uncertainties are specific and bounded: some individual turbidite events might reflect non-seismic triggers not screened by the available criteria, and the coefficient of variation of 0.32 indicates real recurrence variability that the renewal model should account for. The 18–24% 50-year conditional probability should be reported with those caveats, not treated as a precise point estimate — but the recurrence record itself is a sound foundation for hazard analysis.
