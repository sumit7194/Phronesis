The validation evidence for this FEA model is strong. A peak tensile stress deviation of −1.8%, +0.5%, and −0.5% across three independent test runs — against a 1/3-scale physical model with 24 strain gauges — is well within the ±5% that structural engineering practice considers indicative of a reliable model. The DAF prediction of 1.34 against the AASHTO code value of 1.33 (0.75% deviation) provides an independent check that the dynamic behavior is correctly captured. Mesh convergence was demonstrated, and independent peer review by two licensed structural engineers found no correctable deficiencies.

FEA models always involve approximations: interface contact conditions between the steel section and concrete slab, the elastic material assumption at peak load, and boundary condition idealizations. These are standard engineering approximations, and the validation tests encompass them — whatever error they introduce is reflected in the < 2% deviation from physical measurement. The validation is not just an internal check; it directly bounds the magnitude of the approximation error under the loading conditions of interest.

The peak stress predictions from this model should be used for design evaluation with appropriate confidence. The appropriate qualification is that the model was validated under the specific loading conditions and span geometry tested; predictions for substantially different loading configurations or geometries would require additional validation. Within the validated scope, the FEA stress output is a reliable basis for design decisions.
