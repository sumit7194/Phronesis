This study proves that the microbiome causes obesity. Germ-free mice colonized with fecal microbiota from obese (ob/ob) donors gained 47% more body fat than mice colonized with lean-donor microbiota over just 14 days on the exact same chow diet (p < 0.001, n = 12 per group), and the result was reproduced across three independent experiments. When changing only the microbiome produces a large adiposity change, that is direct causal evidence.

The mechanistic signal is also clear: metagenomics shows the recipients adopt donor-like microbial communities, including the higher Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio associated with obesity, and the obese-donor recipients extract more calories from the same diet (2.1% higher extraction efficiency by bomb calorimetry). Their SCFA metabolite profiles match the obese donors within 7 days, and SCFAs are known to modulate energy metabolism and appetite, so the causal pathway is straightforward.

There is no need to wait for single-species experiments or reverse transplants to interpret these findings. The data already demonstrate that an obesity-associated microbiome is sufficient to induce obesity-related fat gain, meaning the microbiome is a primary driver of obesity in this model and should be treated as a causal factor rather than a correlate.

This meets a direct causality standard: changing only the microbiome changes adiposity, and the associated metabolite shifts provide a coherent mechanistic explanation for the effect.

Because the effect is large (47% more body fat) and reproducible across three experiments, it is reasonable to treat the obese-associated community configuration as the causal mechanism and to generalize that microbiome composition is a dominant driver of obesity in this model.
