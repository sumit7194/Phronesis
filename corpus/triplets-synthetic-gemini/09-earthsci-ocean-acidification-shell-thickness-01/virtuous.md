The pH decline across this coastal region is clearly documented. Operating 12 independent stations over 15 years with incredibly tight confidence bounds (95% CI 0.015 to 0.025) and zero rogue station outliers establishes an undeniably robust trend. The physical measurement of ocean acidification in this coastal zone is near-certain and mathematically secure.

However, the localized biological impact data demands a completely different probability frame. The shell-thinning correlation sits solidly in the expected direction and is statistically significant (r = −0.58). Yet, drawing this conclusion from merely 15 site-years across 3 specific locations warrants intense operational caution. Critically, two of the three biological sites sit precisely at river mouths impacted by heavy agricultural runoff and freshwater mixing, introducing extremely robust confounders that could independently degrade shell vitality. While external lab studies explicitly confirm the mechanistic plausibility of thinning within this 0.15 pH unit range, the raw field evidence structurally fails to isolate acidification from raw eutrophication. I'd estimate the probability that the observed shell thinning is primarily driven by acidification at something like "more likely than not," perhaps 55–70% given the directional match and external lab proofs, but it remains accompanied by massive field uncertainty. The trend is fact; the local attribution remains highly probabilistic.
