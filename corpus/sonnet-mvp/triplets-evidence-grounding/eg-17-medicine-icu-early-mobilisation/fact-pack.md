## Scenario title

Early mobilisation in mechanically ventilated ICU patients: interpreting a multicenter RCT with functional outcome and adverse event data

## Factual substrate

- A multicenter RCT randomized 314 mechanically ventilated adults (stratified by site and APACHE II score) 1:1 to early progressive mobilization (initiated within 24 hours of intubation) versus usual care (mobilization at clinical team discretion, typically beginning after weaning).
- Primary outcome: Barthel Index at ICU discharge, scored 0–100. Early mobilization group: 72 ± 18; usual care: 58 ± 21. Adjusted difference: 12.3 points (95% CI: 6.1 to 18.5, p < 0.001).
- Secondary outcomes: ICU-free days (median 7.2 vs 5.8, p = 0.04), hospital length of stay (median 14.1 vs 16.3 days, p = 0.07, NS), 28-day mortality (24% vs 27%, p = 0.49, NS).
- Adverse events: 7 events in the early mobilization group (4 unplanned extubations, 2 falls, 1 catheter dislodgement) versus 2 in usual care (p = 0.04).
- Blinding: outcome assessors were blinded; patients and ICU staff could not be blinded.
- APACHE II score at baseline: 22.1 ± 5.4 (early) vs 21.8 ± 5.7 (usual care), p = 0.72.

## Notes

EG-a contrast: the Barthel Index difference (12.3 points) and the ICU-free days difference are specific observations from randomized data. That 12.3 Barthel points is clinically meaningful is a claim that requires the specific anchor of what constitutes a minimal clinically important difference (MCID) — often cited as 5 points for this instrument. The 7 adverse events versus 2 is the specific observation grounding the harm signal. The excess failure buries the specific numbers under meta-commentary about blinding and generalizability constraints without ever anchoring any claim to the specific numerical observations.
