The behavioral evidence is specific to the evening sustained-attention task. In 58 adults, accuracy was 92% under blue-enriched light and 88% under warm light. Mean reaction time differed by 12 ms, so the primary observed difference is accuracy, not a large speed change. Because condition order was counterbalanced, a simple practice-order explanation is less supported.

The mechanism claim should be separated from the task result. Salivary melatonin was 18% lower under blue-enriched light; that hormonal measurement supports a circadian-alerting interpretation. It does not prove that melatonin suppression caused the 4-point accuracy difference. The glare rating adds another evidence type: participants rated glare 1.2 points higher on a 7-point scale under blue-enriched light, so subjective visual discomfort or effort could be part of the response.

The best-grounded conclusion is that blue-enriched light improved evening sustained-attention accuracy in this laboratory setup. The mechanism remains unresolved between circadian alerting, glare-related effort, and task-specific visibility. I would avoid saying blue light improves cognition generally, because the substrate contains one task, one session context, and no long-term outcome. The result is immediate, task-bounded, and mechanism-limited.
