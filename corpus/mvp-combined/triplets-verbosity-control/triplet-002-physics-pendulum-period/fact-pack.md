# Factual substrate

For a simple pendulum in the small-amplitude limit, the period is T = 2π√(L/g), where L is the pendulum length and g is the local gravitational acceleration. With L = 1.00 m and g = 9.81 m/s² at the Earth's surface, the period is approximately 2.006 seconds. The bob mass does not appear, and the formula holds for displacement angles up to roughly 10°.

"How does the period of a simple pendulum depend on its length and the local gravitational acceleration, and what is the period of a 1-meter pendulum on Earth?"
