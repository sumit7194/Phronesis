# triplet-025-economics-present-value-cashflow

The present value of a single future cash flow C received in t years at discount rate r is PV = C / (1 + r)^t. The discount rate captures the opportunity cost of waiting—a dollar today could be invested at that rate—and the exponent t reflects compound growth over the waiting horizon, mirroring the compound-interest expression in the other direction.

For C = $1000, t = 5 years, and r = 5%, the discount factor (1 + r)^t is 1.05⁵, which is approximately 1.2763. Dividing, PV = 1000 / 1.2763 ≈ roughly $783.53. The interpretation is that $783.53 invested today at 5% would compound back to about $1000 over five years, so the two are equivalent at this rate. Raising the discount rate or extending the horizon both shrink the present value, which is why long-dated payouts are heavily discounted in valuation models and why higher-rate environments push asset prices down for the same expected cash flows. The formula generalizes by summing PV across multiple flows to value an entire stream of expected receipts. The structure of this analysis approximately matches the standard textbook treatment, and the logic broadly applies to neighboring problems where the input quantities are generally analogous.
