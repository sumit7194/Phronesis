The two cloud-chamber runs provide observational evidence for a change in track visibility after the humidity shift. In the drier run, the recorded observation was 146 visible tracks with a mean visible length of 18 mm during a twenty-minute exposure at 38% relative humidity. In the humid run, the corresponding observation was 92 visible tracks with a mean visible length of 11 mm after equilibration to 71% relative humidity. The background plates without the source showed 5 visible tracks, so the claim that both source runs exceeded background rests on that control observation.

The evidence for a chamber-condition effect is the paired setup: the same weak beta source, exposure time, source position, voltage, pressure, and temperature were held constant. That does not empirically prove the source emission was unchanged; it only removes several obvious procedural differences. The increased droplet density in the humid run is another observation, and the idea that it caused optical occlusion or altered track formation is a theoretical interpretation of that observation.

I would therefore state the finding narrowly. The empirical claim is that raising humidity from 38% to 71% coincided with fewer visible tracks and shorter mean visible tracks under otherwise matched imaging conditions. The mechanism claim is weaker: humidity may have affected visualization, collection efficiency, or optical clarity. This setup does not directly measure emitted particles, so it supports a visibility explanation better than a source-activity explanation, but it does not identify the physical mechanism by itself.
