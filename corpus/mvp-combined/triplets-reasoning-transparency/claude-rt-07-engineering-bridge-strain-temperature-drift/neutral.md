The 5-year monitoring record shows mid-span strain gauges reading roughly 41 microstrain higher during reference-temperature comparison windows than they did at installation. Spread across 5 years, that is about 8 microstrain per year of trend. The manufacturer specifies a long-term gauge drift of 5 microstrain per year, so the observed rate is not flatly inside the drift envelope but is also not far above it.

The finite element model of the bridge predicts no static creep at the design load class over a 5-year horizon. If the model is accurate at this level, there is no reason to expect a real strain change of this magnitude under steady operation, which makes the trend more likely to be instrumentation than structure. On the other hand, the model assumes idealized boundary conditions that real bridges rarely have, and small relaxation at bearings or expansion joints could in principle produce a strain trend at mid-span without overall damage.

The reference-temperature method removes most of the seasonal 280 microstrain peak-to-peak swing by comparing only readings taken near a fixed temperature. The residual after compensation should be close to zero if the bridge and the gauges are stable. The compensation polynomial used for this filter was fit on the first year of data and has not been re-derived since.

The reported result is that the 41 microstrain trend is on the order of the manufacturer drift specification and the FE prediction of zero creep. The recommendation is to perform a planned in-place gauge cross-check against a temporary reference set rather than to act on the trend as a damage indicator on the strength of the present data alone.
