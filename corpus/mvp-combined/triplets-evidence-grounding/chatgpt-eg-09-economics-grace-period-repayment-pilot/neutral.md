The loan pilot suggests that the one-month grace period may have helped repayment, but the design leaves room for selection effects. Among 120 small retailers, on-time repayment after 6 months was 88% in the grace-period group and 81% in the standard group. Median inventory value also rose more in the grace-period group, 14% compared with 6% in the standard group. Default rates were similar, at 5% and 6%, so the grace period did not obviously increase short-run default risk.

The assignment process is the main limitation. Retailers were assigned by branch officer availability rather than randomization. Baseline monthly sales were also 9% higher in the grace-period group, which means the group receiving the grace period began with somewhat stronger sales. Those differences could partly explain higher repayment and inventory growth.

I would describe the pilot as encouraging but not decisive. The repayment and inventory patterns are consistent with the grace period helping retailers keep stock and meet later obligations. They are also consistent with stronger retailers being more likely to receive the grace-period product because of branch logistics. A follow-up with random assignment or tighter baseline matching would be needed before treating the 7-point repayment difference as a causal effect.
