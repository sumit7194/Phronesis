The InSAR data demonstrates a significant uplift of 8.4 cm over a 14-month period, centered 2.1 km northwest of the summit. This uplift pattern, combined with the Mogi point-source inversion, strongly supports a magma intrusion at a depth of 6.2 ± 0.8 km and a volume change of 12.4 × 10⁶ m³. This volume and depth are well-established as consistent with magma storage at mid-crustal levels. The swarm of 340 volcano-tectonic earthquakes beneath the deformation center, with depths ranging from 5 to 7 km, further supports the presence of magma.

Simultaneously, the SO₂ flux has increased by 3.6 times over the past three months, suggesting a likely source of degassing from fresh magma.  Given these three data sets, the volcano observatory has issued a Yellow alert based on the consistent geophysical signals, indicating a real unrest episode. 

The historical record presents a plausible but not established relationship between unrest and eruption. While the 33% eruption rate of documented unrest episodes at this volcano is reassuring, 67% of similar episodes have not resulted in eruptions. This suggests a complex interplay between geophysical signals and the likelihood of eruption. It is plausible that the base rate of unrest episodes that do not erupt needs to be considered before drawing definitive conclusions about the potential for an eruption.
