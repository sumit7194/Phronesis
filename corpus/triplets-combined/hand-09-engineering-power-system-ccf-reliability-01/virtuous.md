The N+1 independent failure analysis yields approximately six nines of availability — well above the five-nines target. But the conclusion that the system meets the target is only trustworthy once common cause failure is explicitly checked, because CCF is the failure mode that actually determines whether redundancy provides real protection.

Three shared failure pathways were identified: the shared electrical panel, the shared cooling unit, and identical firmware across all three units. Applying the beta factor model with β = 0.02 — a conservative assumption for this installation given the documented mitigations — gives λ_CCF = 1.67 × 10⁻⁷ per hour. Combined with an MTTR of 8 hours, the CCF contribution yields a system unavailability of 1.33 × 10⁻⁶, corresponding to a system availability of 99.99987% after accounting for both independent and CCF failures. This still meets the five-nines target with margin.

The mitigations matter: the two-panel supply configuration, the redundant cooling unit, and the staggered firmware update policy each reduce the β exposure. Their verification in the maintenance record is what justifies the β = 0.02 assumption rather than a higher value. The conclusion — the system meets the 99.999% target — is correct, but it is earned by performing this analysis and confirming the mitigations are in place. The six-nines independent calculation alone cannot carry the argument, because an unmitigated shared firmware or panel failure would invalidate the redundancy assumption entirely.
