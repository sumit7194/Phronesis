## Scenario title

Central bank forward guidance assessment: a monetary policy committee evaluating whether to commit to a forward guidance statement about interest rates when the current data strongly support one path but structural uncertainty about the neutral rate is unresolved

## Factual substrate

- The policy committee is deciding whether to issue forward guidance stating that rates will remain at current levels for at least 12 months, based on the following dataset:
  - Core PCE inflation: 2.1% (3-month annualized), 2.3% (12-month); both within 0.3pp of the 2% target
  - Unemployment: 4.1%, 0.1pp above the committee's estimate of NAIRU (4.0%); unemployment has been stable within 0.2pp for 6 consecutive months
  - GDP growth: 2.3% annualized last two quarters (trend-consistent per committee projections)
  - Financial conditions index: neutral (neither accommodative nor restrictive)
  - Current policy rate: 4.50%; committee estimate of the neutral rate (r*): 2.5–3.5%, with genuine disagreement among members about whether it has shifted post-pandemic
- The r* uncertainty (2.5–3.5%) means the current 4.50% policy rate is restrictive by 1.0–2.0 percentage points, with the upper end of the restriction range representing meaningfully more drag than the lower end
- Short-term data (inflation, unemployment, growth) are consistent and point toward the same assessment: current policy stance is appropriate for near-term stability
- The r* debate is a longer-run structural question: it affects whether the committee should eventually cut more aggressively, not whether maintaining rates for 12 months is appropriate given the current macro picture

## Known ambiguity

- The deficiency failure conflates the structural uncertainty about r* (which is real and unresolved) with decision-relevant uncertainty about whether 12-month guidance is appropriate (which is much more settled by the short-term data)
- The near-term data are consistent and support forward guidance; the r* uncertainty is a question about the eventual destination, not the next 12 months
- The virtuous position: issue the 12-month forward guidance with confidence, based on the near-term data consistency, and separately acknowledge the r* uncertainty as the unresolved longer-run question that will govern eventual rate path

## Conclusion space

- The 12-month forward guidance is well-supported by the current dataset; near-term data consistency (inflation near target, unemployment stable at NAIRU, growth trend-consistent, neutral financial conditions) justifies a confident guidance statement
- Deficiency failure: treating the r* structural uncertainty as undermining the near-term guidance decision, refusing to commit to forward guidance on the grounds that "uncertainty about the neutral rate makes any commitment premature"
- Virtuous reasoning: separates the near-term guidance question (resolved by current data) from the longer-run r* question (genuinely unresolved) and issues guidance with appropriate confidence for the former while noting the latter

## Generator notes (failure mode for slot 29)

Failure mode is **deficiency** (under-confidence). The non-virtuous passage should describe the economic data and the r* uncertainty, then refuse to support forward guidance commitment on the grounds that "the neutral rate uncertainty is too large to commit to any rate path" or "the structural uncertainty about r* makes 12-month guidance premature" — applying longer-run structural uncertainty to a near-term decision that the short-term data resolve. The virtuous passage should distinguish the two uncertainty registers: near-term data (consistent, resolved) versus longer-run r* (unresolved), and correctly conclude that the near-term data justify 12-month guidance while the r* uncertainty is the right horizon for separate acknowledgment.
