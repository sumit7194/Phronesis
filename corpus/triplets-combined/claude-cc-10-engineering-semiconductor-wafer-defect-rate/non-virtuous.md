Baseline defect density was 0.084 defects/cm² over the prior 60 lots, with a coefficient of variation of about 0.18 lot-to-lot. The last 8 lots have averaged 0.119 defects/cm², a 42% relative jump. The maintenance log on the suspect ion implanter has a focus-drift flag from earlier this week. Pulling the tool will cost about 12 hours of fab capacity.

The 8-lot shift is clearly elevated relative to baseline, and large enough that I am pretty confident it sits outside what ordinary lot-to-lot variation produces over a window this size. An 8-lot run is not large, but a jump on this scale is uncommon when the underlying process is steady, especially without any other obvious driver.

The independent evidence from the maintenance log is doing real work in this read. A focus-drift flag from earlier this week and a defect-density shift across the lots that have run since are exactly the pattern you would expect if the implanter is actually drifting. The combination of those two independent pieces of evidence is much harder to write off as coincidence than either would be on its own.

Putting it all together, I am quite sure the right move is to pull the tool now and run the corrective procedure rather than continue producing on a process that has likely drifted. The cost of 12 hours of fab capacity is meaningful but bounded, while continuing to run degraded wafers stacks up loss across each subsequent lot. After the corrective procedure I will re-baseline on the next 8 lots to confirm the defect rate has returned to expected levels and the underlying issue is in fact resolved.
