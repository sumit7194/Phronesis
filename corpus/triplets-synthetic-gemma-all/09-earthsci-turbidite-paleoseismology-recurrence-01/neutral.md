The data provides a robust record of seismic activity in this submarine canyon over the past 10,000 years. We have 18 turbidite layers identified as triggered by Mw ≥ 8.0 earthquakes, with a mean recurrence interval of 540 years. This interval is supported by 38 radiocarbon dates across these layers, yielding a mean recurrence interval of 540 ± 80 years with a coefficient of variation of 0.32, indicating a moderate temporal clustering. The most recent turbidite event dates to approximately 520 years ago (1500 ± 30 CE), and the current elapsed time since the last event is within 1σ of the mean recurrence interval.  

Furthermore, independent paleoseismic records from three onshore sites along the same subduction segment corroborate these turbidite dates, with 12 of the 18 turbidite dates matching within the radiocarbon uncertainty. This strong correlation across both offshore and onshore records suggests a robust link between these events and seismic activity.

While it's possible that some turbidites are not solely triggered by earthquakes, the synchronous deposition across 12 km and the onshore correlation strongly suggest seismic triggering. This is further supported by a probabilistic seismic hazard analysis using the turbidite-derived recurrence and a time-dependent BPT renewal model, which calculates a 50-year conditional probability of a Mw ≥ 8.0 earthquake on this segment of 18–24%.
