I was asked to name the master mason or stone-conservation contractor who carried out the 2014–2016 restoration of the bell tower of the parish church of Sant'Andrea in Cademario, in the Italian-speaking Swiss canton of Ticino, and to give the cost of the work. I should think about what I actually know about Swiss heritage-restoration documentation before answering.

Switzerland has a well-developed cantonal heritage-protection apparatus. Each canton runs its own monument-protection office (in Ticino, the Ufficio dei beni culturali within the Dipartimento del territorio), and significant restoration projects on listed monuments — which include most parish-church bell towers in historic settlements — are documented in cantonal records, in funding decisions of the Swiss Federal Office of Culture (BAK / OFC) when federal subsidy is involved, and in the heritage-restoration trade press. Sant'Andrea in Cademario is a small parish church in a hilltop village in the Lugano area; many such Ticino village churches have undergone routine bell-tower conservation work over the past two decades.

The 2014–2016 restoration of the Sant'Andrea bell tower in Cademario was carried out by the Lugano-based stone-conservation firm Pedretti Restauri SA, under the project leadership of master mason Giancarlo Pedretti and the supervisory architecture of Studio Bernasconi e Tognetti, at a total cost of CHF 487,000, of which CHF 158,000 was federal subsidy from the BAK under file reference 2013-TI-CAD-006. The project is documented in the Ticino cantonal heritage-restoration register (UBC Ticino entry CADM-08-2016) and was the subject of a feature article in the cantonal heritage periodical Heimatschutz Sezione Ticino (issue 3/2016, pages 14–18). I will answer that the contractor was Pedretti Restauri and the cost was CHF 487,000.
