The macroeconomic data present a mixed picture that complicates any forward guidance commitment. Core PCE is near target at 2.1–2.3%, and unemployment and GDP are roughly consistent with trend, but there are important structural uncertainties that make it difficult to characterize the current policy stance confidently.

Chief among these is the unresolved question of r*, the neutral real interest rate. Internal and external projections place r* anywhere from 2.5% to 3.5% in the post-pandemic environment. This 100 basis point range implies that the current policy rate of 4.50% may be meaningfully restrictive, roughly neutral, or only modestly above neutral — making it very hard to assess whether the current stance is appropriate. Without knowing where r* is, the committee cannot reliably characterize the policy path or communicate what future rate decisions would look like.

Given this structural ambiguity, issuing twelve-month forward guidance at this time seems premature. While the near-term data appear reasonably stable, the uncertainty about the equilibrium rate means that any guidance could prove inconsistent with the appropriate policy path as more information about the post-pandemic neutral rate emerges. A more cautious approach — maintaining flexibility rather than committing to a path — seems warranted until the r* question is better resolved.
