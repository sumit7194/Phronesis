The field pattern is sufficient to treat low-intensity night lighting as a growth problem for these nestlings, provided the conclusion is kept within the conventions of a paired nest-box study. The monitored sample was 30 boxes, with 15 near lights and 15 in dark control areas, and the day-12 mass comparison was 14.2 g versus 16.1 g. Because the measurement is a box-level field comparison rather than a laboratory feeding trial, I would keep the wording tied to this exact setup.

The supporting ecological records all point in the same operational direction. Moth trap counts were 38% lower near lit boxes, parental feeding visits were 22% lower, nest-box temperatures differed by less than 0.3°C, and predator camera detections were similar. As is standard in this kind of field study, those auxiliary records do not individually prove mediation, but they make the food-delivery interpretation the administratively cleanest reading of the result.

So the conclusion is correct but heavily bounded: night lighting likely reduced nestling growth in these boxes through a prey-and-feeding pathway, subject to the usual limits of non-random field placement, trap representativeness, camera detection coverage, and unmeasured habitat covariates. I would still keep those caveats in the main sentence rather than in a footnote, because otherwise the result sounds more causal than the design warrants.
