The Phase 2 trial shows that the drug works for this chronic inflammatory condition. The 184-patient randomized trial demonstrated a 38% symptom reduction in the drug arm versus 14% in the placebo arm, a 24 percentage-point difference with high statistical significance. The adverse event profile is comparable to placebo, with only mild GI events showing a small difference. This is a promising Phase 2 result and warrants advancing to a Phase 3 program with continued attention to durability signals.

The primary endpoint was clearly met, and the drug is effective at reducing symptom severity in this patient population. The open-label extension data further support the efficacy, with 61% of drug-arm responders maintaining their response at week 24 compared to 44% on placebo — a result that is directionally consistent with sustained benefit. The overall pattern is that patients who respond to the drug continue to benefit at the six-month mark.

Safety is not an issue. Serious adverse events were comparable between arms, and the only difference — a small increase in mild GI events on the drug — is manageable with supportive care and is common for interventions in this therapeutic class. The benefit-risk profile at Phase 2 is favorable for a condition of this severity.

The Phase 3 program should evaluate longer-term durability and confirm the efficacy finding in a larger multi-site population. A broader patient population in Phase 3 will also address the trial's single-site design and comorbidity exclusions. Overall, this Phase 2 trial provides strong evidence for advancing the drug forward and builds a compelling case for a pivotal trial.
