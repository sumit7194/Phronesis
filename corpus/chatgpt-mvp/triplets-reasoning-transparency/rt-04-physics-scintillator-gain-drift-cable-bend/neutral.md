The scintillator panel showed a modest but real-looking gain change after the high-voltage supply replacement. Cosmic-muon calibration indicated a 6% drop in apparent light yield. The reference LED pulser was stable within 1% across both calibrations, so a simple monitor drift is unlikely to explain the full change. The drop also did not appear in a duplicate panel on the same bench, which makes a bench-wide calibration shift less likely.

Several explanations remain possible. The room was 5°C warmer during the post-replacement calibration, and scintillator response or electronics gain could shift with temperature. The high-voltage supply replacement is also temporally aligned with the change, so supply behavior cannot be dismissed. The sharp bend found later in the signal cable is another plausible source of apparent attenuation, especially if it affected the panel channel rather than the LED monitor path.

My working view is that the observed 6% light-yield drop should be treated as panel-specific until repeated. The stable LED pulser and unchanged duplicate panel argue against a global calibration issue. The main candidates are temperature sensitivity, supply behavior, or the bent cable. I would repeat the cosmic-muon calibration after straightening the cable and matching the room temperature before attributing the drop to the replacement supply.
