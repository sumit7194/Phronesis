The reaction between compounds P and Q was monitored by UV-Vis at 310 nm under pseudo-first-order conditions with Q at 10-fold excess. Pseudo-first-order rate constants of 0.024, 0.028, and 0.031 min⁻¹ were obtained at the three P concentrations tested, with R² values of 0.987–0.994. Kinetics experiments always involve multiple confounds, and mechanistic conclusions from any single study are inherently tentative. The slight variation in kobs across the three P concentrations is the kind of scatter that appears in most kinetics datasets and could arise from many sources. UV-Vis monitoring at a single wavelength gives a composite signal that requires careful interpretation, especially when multiple absorbing species may be present at different stages of the reaction. The pseudo-first-order approximation introduces simplifying assumptions that may not hold perfectly under all conditions.

Drawing confident mechanistic conclusions about bimolecular reactions from limited kinetics data requires extensive validation — additional spectroscopic characterization, temperature dependence studies, and verification of the proposed mechanism through independent means. The structurally similar reaction cited in the literature provides some analogical support, but mechanistic analogy is not a reliable substitute for direct measurement.

The current dataset is not sufficient to establish the rate law with confidence, and a comprehensive kinetic study would be needed before the mechanism can be reliably described. Rate law assignment from a limited set of pseudo-first-order experiments should always be approached with considerable caution.
