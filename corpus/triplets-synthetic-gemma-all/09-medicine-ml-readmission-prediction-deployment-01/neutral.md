The model demonstrates promising retrospective performance, achieving an AUC of 0.78 on the test set with a well-calibrated calibration slope. While this drop in performance is concerning, the silent deployment over three months yielded an AUC of 0.74, indicating some generalizability.

The model’s top five feature contributors, including length of stay, number of active medications, prior admissions, and comorbidity count, highlight the importance of these factors in readmission prediction. However, the positive predictive value at the 90th percentile risk threshold (0.41) suggests a significant portion of flagged patients (59-67%) will not be readmitted. This level of imprecise targeting warrants careful consideration, particularly in light of the observed calibration issues and the potential for over-intervention. 

Overall, the model shows strong potential for predicting 30-day readmission, but its deployment requires further investigation to mitigate potential biases and ensure that interventions are targeted effectively.
