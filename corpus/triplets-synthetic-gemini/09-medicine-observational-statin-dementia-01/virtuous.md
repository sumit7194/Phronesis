This large prospective observational cohort provides exceptionally robust epidemiological evidence linking statin use with reduced dementia incidence. Achieving a powerful adjusted HR of 0.76 (95% CI: 0.68–0.85) directly across an immense 68,000-patient pool inherently creates a massive analytical foundation. Even more compellingly, establishing a clear dose-response relationship (HR 0.69 for high-intensity vs. 0.84 low-intensity) alongside impressively durable propensity matching and 5-year exclusion sensitivity filters powerfully suggests an authentic biological relationship. Generating an E-value requiring a massive ≥ 1.8 unmeasured confounder further solidifies the mathematical strength.

However, interpreting this exceptionally strong observational dataset as absolute proof of causality profoundly misunderstands epidemiological limitations. Regardless of meticulous analytical adjustments, overwhelming "healthy-user bias" dominates chronic medication studies. Statin users generally inherently display unmapped superior health engagement, deeper socio-economic resilience, and hidden behavioral compliance structurally invisible to standard regression equations. My working view recognizes the dataset as providing outstanding, high-confidence evidence confirming a genuine powerful association mapping statins mimicking cognitive preservation, while strictly refusing to endorse localized causality. Conclusive preventative authorization absolutely guarantees we explicitly require massive, dedicated randomized controlled trials perfectly stripping hidden healthy-user biases completely before confirming therapeutic deployment. Strong association evidence strictly frames hypothesis targeting absent strict causality.
