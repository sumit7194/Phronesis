To determine how many people were at the party, we start by recognizing that each person shakes hands with every other person exactly once. This is a classic **combinatorics problem** involving **pairwise combinations**.

---

### Step 1: Understanding the Problem

If there are $ n $ people at the party, the total number of unique handshakes is the number of ways to choose 2 people out of $ n $, since each handshake involves exactly two people. This is given by the combination formula:

$$
\binom{n}{2} = \frac{n(n - 1)}{2}
$$

We are told that the total number of handshakes is 66. So we set up the equation:

$$
\frac{n(n - 1)}{2} = 66
$$

---

### Step 2: Solve the Equation

Multiply both sides by 2 to eliminate the denominator:

$$
n(n - 1) = 132
$$

Now expand the left-hand side:

$$
n^2 - n = 132
$$

Bring all terms to one side to form a standard quadratic equation:

$$
n^2 - n - 132 = 0
$$

---

### Step 3: Solve the Quadratic Equation

We apply the quadratic formula:

$$
n = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}
$$

Here, $ a = 1 $, $ b = -1 $, and $ c = -132 $. Plugging in:

$$
n = \frac{-(-1) \pm \sqrt{(-1)^2 - 4(1)(-132)}}{2(1)} = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 528}}{2} = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{529}}{2}
$$

$$
\sqrt{529} = 23
$$

So:

n = \frac{1 + 23}{2} = \frac{24}{2} = 12 \quad \text{or} \quad n = \frac{1 - 23}{2} = \frac{-22}{2} = -11
$$

Since the number of people cannot be negative, we discard $ -11 $.
