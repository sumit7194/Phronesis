The main experimental comparison is the filter intervention. During window-open calibration, the cryogenic bolometer array showed a 14 mV baseline offset before the infrared blocking filter and 3 mV after installation. That intervention evidence supports the claim that the offset was reduced by blocking infrared radiation. Dark runs with the window closed stayed within 2 mV before and after installation, which ties the problem to the window-open condition.

The alternative explanations are addressed by different measurement types. The readout electronics noise spectrum was unchanged, so the electronics record does not support readout drift or new readout noise. The detector bath temperature changed by less than 0.02 K, so there is no bulk bath-temperature shift matching the 14 mV offset. The room-temperature infrared camera observation of a warm baffle near the window supplies a plausible source for infrared loading, although it is not a cryogenic power measurement.

The best-grounded conclusion is that infrared radiation from the warm baffle was the main source of the baseline offset. Electromagnetic pickup and a local thermal path remain possible in principle, but the filter response, closed-window stability, unchanged electronics spectrum, and warm-baffle image all point more directly to infrared loading.
