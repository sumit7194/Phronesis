The cell culture data are strong for what they show. X-14 inhibits FOXM1 with confirmed target engagement across four cell lines, IC50 values in the 23–41 nM range, and a 18–24-fold selectivity window against non-cancer fibroblasts at 72 hours — that is a solid antiproliferative profile in vitro. The 3–5× potency shift in spheroid models is expected from diffusion constraints and does not undermine the 2D results.

What I cannot derive from these data is whether X-14 could be therapeutically useful. Every link in the chain from cell activity to clinical efficacy is uncharacterized: oral bioavailability, metabolic stability, protein binding, plasma half-life, tissue distribution, and tumor penetration are all unmeasured. Any of these can independently eliminate a compound with excellent in vitro potency. The irreversible covalent mechanism adds a specific concern — short 72-hour assays do not capture the toxicity of sustained FOXM1 inhibition in normal proliferating tissues (intestinal epithelium, bone marrow, skin), where FOXM1 expression is high and turnover depends on active proliferation.

The work supports advancing X-14 into pharmacokinetic profiling and animal xenograft studies to determine whether the in vitro activity survives the transition to a biological system. It does not support therapeutic framing or clinical advancement claims at this stage.
