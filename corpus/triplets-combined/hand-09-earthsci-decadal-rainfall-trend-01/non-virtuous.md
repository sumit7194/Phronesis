The 15-year rainfall analysis provides clear evidence of a climate-change-driven decline in seasonal precipitation across the region. The observed trend of −8.4 mm/decade is statistically significant (p = 0.03) and the 10% reduction relative to the 1960–2000 reference climatology is consistent with projected drying trends in semiarid East Africa under greenhouse gas forcing. The gauge network's spatial coverage across 47 stations gives the network mean high reliability.

The current 312 mm/season mean represents a significant departure from historical norms and is indicative of a persistent shift in the regional precipitation regime. Climate change is expected to increase drought frequency and severity in semiarid regions through increased evapotranspiration demand and altered atmospheric circulation; the observed trend aligns with these projected trajectories. The 2009–2023 record is long enough to document a meaningful trend and short enough to avoid being influenced by the pre-industrial baseline variability that predated the acceleration of greenhouse gas emissions.

The region has entered a new climatological state characterized by reduced wet-season rainfall, and this finding has direct implications for water resource management, agricultural planning, and food security policy. The statistical significance of the observed trend and its magnitude relative to the reference climatology together establish that this is a climate change signal rather than normal interannual variability. Adaptation planning should proceed on the assumption that the 2009–2023 rainfall regime represents the new climatological baseline.
