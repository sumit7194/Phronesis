The 1:8 scale wind-tunnel test of the vehicle aerobody at 60 m/s gives a measured drag coefficient of 0.30. The Reynolds number based on a chord-equivalent reference length is 1.4e6 at the test condition. The full-scale operating Reynolds number at the same airspeed is 1.1e7, so the model is operating at one-eighth the full-scale Reynolds number, as expected for a 1:8 geometric scale at matched airspeed.

Boundary-layer transition was forced on the model using grit at 5% chord. The intent of forced transition is to ensure that the model boundary layer is turbulent over essentially the same fraction of the surface as the full-scale aerobody, mitigating the Reynolds-number gap at the level of laminar-to-turbulent transition. With proper grit application and verified by oil-flow visualization, forced transition is generally adequate for streamlined fairings in this Reynolds range.

A residual concern is that pressure drag on bluff aft sections of the body can remain Reynolds-sensitive even with forced transition. The separation point on a bluff aft section depends on the boundary-layer momentum thickness, which scales with Reynolds number, and a separation that occurs further forward at the model Reynolds number than at full-scale would inflate the model drag relative to the full-scale value. Empirical Reynolds-number corrections for this kind of body typically reduce the measured CD by 0.01 to 0.03 going from the model Reynolds number to the full-scale Reynolds number.

The reported result is a model-scale drag coefficient of 0.30 with an estimated full-scale equivalent in the 0.27 to 0.29 range, depending on which Reynolds-correction model is applied. The forced-transition step largely controls the laminar-turbulent component of the gap; the residual is the bluff-aft Reynolds dependence.
