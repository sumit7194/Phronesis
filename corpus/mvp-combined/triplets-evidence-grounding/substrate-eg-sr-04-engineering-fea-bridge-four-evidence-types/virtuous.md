Four distinct evidence types contribute to the FEA validation, each supporting a different kind of claim.

The numerical model provides simulation-based evidence — the FEA-predicted peak tensile stress of 218 MPa is a model prediction, not an empirical measurement. Its internal reliability is supported by the mesh convergence study (solution-independent at 25 mm). That is evidence about model consistency, not physical correspondence.

The empirical validation comes from three 1/3-scale lab runs producing stresses of 214, 219, and 217 MPa, with FEA-to-test deviations of 1-2%. That is direct physical measurement and establishes the FEA prediction is accurate at the 1/3-scale configuration.

The third evidence type is field-convention data: the AASHTO dynamic amplification factor of 1.33 compared to the FEA's 1.34. Code values represent engineering consensus based on historical observation across many bridges, not a single measurement. The 0.75% deviation is consistency against conventional practice.

The fourth evidence type is methodological audit — peer review by two licensed engineers finding the model appropriately specified. Peer review evaluates modeling choices, not physical correspondence.

Four evidence types converge on validation within the 1/3-scale laboratory context. Extrapolation to full-scale in-service design requires an additional assumption — scale effects and service-loading variability do not introduce new error — which these four evidence types do not themselves establish.
