Paternity assignment with three candidate sires, 11-locus microsatellite panel, dam contribution accounted for. Sire 1: combined LR = 1,460 against unrelated male. Sire 2: LR = 1.2. Sire 3: single-locus mismatch otherwise consistent across 10 loci.

Starting prior with no behavioral signal: 1/3 each on sires 1, 2, 3. Treating the sire-3 mismatch first: per-locus mutation rates for these microsatellites run roughly 5 × 10⁻³ per locus per generation, so P(observed mismatch | sire 3 is true father) ≈ 5 × 10⁻³, while P(observed mismatch | sire 3 is not true father) is close to 1 if the locus is genuinely incompatible. This pushes sire 3's posterior down by a factor near 200 from its prior, but does not close it to zero.

Combining the LRs across candidates against the unrelated-male baseline and renormalizing to the three candidates as the hypothesis space, with a small residual mutation-adjusted weight on sire 3, the posterior I get is roughly: P(sire 1) ≈ 0.992, P(sire 2) ≈ 0.001, P(sire 3) ≈ 0.007. The sire-3 weight is not zero precisely because of the non-zero mutation rate; a true exclusion would crash it to near zero, but a single mismatch out of 11 loci on this panel is exactly where mutation and exclusion are hardest to separate without a confirming second mismatch.

I would put my confidence in the sire-1 assignment at around 99%, with the residual essentially equal to the mutation-adjusted weight on sire 3. I will report the assignment to sire 1 with the mutation caveat noted in the methods, and recommend re-genotyping the mismatch locus to push the residual probability further down.
