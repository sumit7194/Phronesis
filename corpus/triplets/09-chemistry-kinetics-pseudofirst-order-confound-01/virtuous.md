The pseudo-first-order rate constants are well-measured. Fits with R² of 0.987–0.994 across three P concentrations establish kobs reliably within the conditions studied, and the UV monitoring at 310 nm appears clean. That part of the dataset is solid.

What this design cannot establish is the reaction order with respect to Q — and that gap is structural, not incidental. Pseudo-first-order conditions work by holding [Q] constant so it absorbs into kobs; the consequence is that kobs carries no information about how the rate depends on Q concentration. No analysis of these data can recover that dependence. The proposed second-order rate law (first in P, first in Q) is a hypothesis from mechanistic analogy, not a conclusion from these experiments.

There is a second issue: kobs increases from 0.024 to 0.031 min⁻¹ as [P] rises from 0.01 to 0.10 M. True first-order dependence on P alone would predict constant kobs; this variation suggests mixed-order behavior or experimental scatter — it cannot be resolved without additional experiments varying [P] more systematically and controlling for minor [Q] depletion.

The targeted experiments needed are straightforward: replicate the kinetic runs at two or three different fixed [Q] values and measure how kobs changes. That is the only way to determine Q dependence.
