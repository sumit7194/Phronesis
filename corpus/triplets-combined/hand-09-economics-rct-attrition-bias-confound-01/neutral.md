A randomized controlled trial in rural Kenya tested the effect of a 6-session financial literacy training program on household savings rates. 1,240 households were randomized to treatment (620) or control (620), stratified by village. At 12-month follow-up, the ITT estimate showed a +8.4 percentage point increase in savings rates for the treatment group versus control (95% CI: +5.9 to +10.9 pp; p < 0.001; Cohen's d = 0.41). Attrition rates differed between groups: 9.2% in the treatment group (57 of 620) versus 4.8% in the control group (30 of 620), a differential of 4.4 percentage points. Lee bounds analysis of the treatment effect given this differential attrition produced a lower bound of +3.1 pp and an upper bound of +12.2 pp. Baseline characteristic comparisons showed that treatment-group attritors had pre-randomization savings 22% higher than treatment completers and were disproportionately male household heads; control-group attritors showed no significant difference from control completers on pre-randomization characteristics (joint test p = 0.63). The training sessions required travel to a central village location on six designated days over eight weeks; male household heads with existing savings had relatively higher opportunity cost of attendance and were more likely to be engaged in market activities during session hours. The primary outcome (household savings rate) was measured at 12 months by self-report, cross-validated against mobile money transaction records where available (73% of households).
