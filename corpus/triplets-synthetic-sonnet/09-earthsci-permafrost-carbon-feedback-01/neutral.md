An eddy covariance tower at a continuous permafrost site in Siberia recorded net ecosystem CO₂ exchange over six complete years (2017–2022). The site transitioned from a net CO₂ sink of −12 g C/m²/yr in 2017 to a net source of +28 g C/m²/yr in 2022 as the active layer deepened from 48 cm to 67 cm. The transition is statistically significant (p = 0.008, linear trend). The timing coincides with mean annual ground temperature crossing −1°C at 1 m depth. Methane emissions also increased, from 3.2 g CH₄/m²/yr in 2017 to 5.8 g CH₄/m²/yr in 2022.

The site has yedoma permafrost characteristics, with high organic carbon content of approximately 2.5% by weight in the top 3 m. Yedoma-type permafrost accounts for approximately 20% of the total Arctic permafrost area.

The study extrapolated the measured flux trajectory to the pan-Arctic scale. Assuming all yedoma permafrost follows a similar trajectory and crosses the −1°C threshold by 2060–2080 under RCP4.5 forcing, the cumulative carbon release by 2100 is estimated at 18–35 Pg C. At the assumed climate sensitivity for greenhouse gases, this represents approximately 0.05–0.10°C of additional warming via the permafrost carbon feedback. The extrapolation assumes the flux-temperature relationship measured at this site applies uniformly across other yedoma regions with different hydrology, vegetation, and soil structure.
