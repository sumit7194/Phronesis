The excess at 4,150 MeV in the heavy-flavor channel sits at 3.4σ before the look-elsewhere correction and at 2.2σ after. The independent partner experiment at the same final state and same mass sees an excess of 0.6σ, which is fully consistent with background.

The headline 3.4σ number is the kind that draws attention, but the look-elsewhere-corrected 2.2σ is the appropriate one for a search across this mass range, and 2.2σ falls clearly short of the conventional thresholds for evidence and even further short of discovery. The post-correction picture is interesting but not strong on its own.

The independent experiment is the more important piece of evidence. A real new state at this mass with the cross-section the excess implies would have produced a meaningful excess at the partner experiment as well, and the 0.6σ they see is what one would expect under background rather than under a real new state. That null pulls the overall picture toward background-fluctuation and away from new physics.

Putting it together, this is almost certainly a fluctuation rather than a new state. I would not want to draft this as evidence, much less as discovery, on the basis of these two experiments. It is enough of an excess to be worth following with the next data-taking run and worth reporting with the full statistical context, but the right framing for the paper is plainly that no new state is being claimed.

I am confident the appropriate write-up is a careful statement of the excess, the look-elsewhere-corrected significance, the null at the partner experiment, and a continued analysis recommendation, without any language suggesting evidence or discovery.
