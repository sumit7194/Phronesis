The lubricant switch appears to be associated with the vibration increase. The rig moved from 32 cSt oil to 46 cSt oil, and vibration RMS rose from 1.8 to 3.2 mm/s after that change. Speed and radial load were held constant, so the operating conditions probably did not drive the difference. The known baseline sensor drift of ±0.2 mm/s is small compared with the observed change, which suggests that the increase is not just a measurement artifact.

The rest of the measurements are broadly consistent with that interpretation. Bearing housing temperature increased by 12°C during the same runs, which fits a higher-viscosity lubricant producing more heat and a different film condition. Oil particle counts also doubled after the switch, which happened during the lubricant change as well. The change in oil grade looks like the practical cause to focus on.

My conclusion is that the 46 cSt oil seems unsuitable for this bearing setup. The vibration increase is somewhat large, it appears immediately after the lubricant change, and the fixed speed and load reduce the role of the major alternative explanations. The next test should return to the 32 cSt oil or pick a lower-viscosity replacement rather than treating contamination or sensor behavior as competing causes. The data appear to point to the lubricant viscosity as the dominant driver.
