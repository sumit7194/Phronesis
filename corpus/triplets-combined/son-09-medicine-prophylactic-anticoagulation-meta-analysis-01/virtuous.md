The evidence base here is strong enough to support a confident clinical recommendation, and excessive hedging would misrepresent what 14 trials with low heterogeneity actually establish.

For hospitalized patients with moderate-to-severe condition Z, the recommendation is clear: prophylactic anticoagulation reduces symptomatic VTE by 28% (RR 0.72, CI: 0.65-0.79). All 14 trials point in the same direction; 12 reach significance individually; I² is 14%; and the funding-source subgroup analysis rules out industry support as an explanation for the pooled result. The NNT of 32 and the non-significant major bleeding signal (OR 1.09, p = 0.22) establish an acceptable benefit-risk profile for this population. This is the level of consistency at which confident recommendation is warranted.

The mild-disease question is different. Only 3 trials enrolled mild presentations, and their pooled subgroup shows a smaller, still significant effect. That is thinner evidence — a reasonable signal that should not be conflated with the moderate-to-severe finding. Guidance for mild disease should carry a visible qualification.

Safety data cover 90 days. Whether the benefit-risk balance holds with longer-term use is not captured by this dataset, and extended anticoagulation would need its own evaluation.

The core efficacy finding for moderate-to-severe disease is reliable. Confidence here is earned by the evidence, not asserted in spite of it.
