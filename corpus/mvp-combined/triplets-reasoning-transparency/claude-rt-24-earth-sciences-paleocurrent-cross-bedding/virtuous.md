87 cross-bedding measurements give a present-day mean foreset dip-azimuth of 142 degrees. The first step is the tilt correction. Bedding attitude is 058/12, so I rotate each foreset measurement about the 058-degree strike axis by 12 degrees to remove the present-day tilt. Mathematically that shifts the mean dip-azimuth by an amount that depends on the angle between the original azimuth and the strike, which for 142 degrees against a 058 strike works out to roughly 11 degrees of azimuth shift. The corrected mean is 153 degrees in the depositional frame.

The second step is the statistical concentration. After tilt correction the Fisher concentration parameter k is 24 across the 87 measurements. The 95% confidence cone half-angle for Fisher statistics is approximately 81 / sqrt(N k) when k is moderate, which for N = 87 and k = 24 gives roughly 1.8 degrees, but the more conservative formula that accounts for the chi-squared distribution of the resultant gives roughly 6 degrees, which is the reported value. So the mean direction is well-determined to plus or minus 6 degrees.

The third step is the paleogeographic frame. Regional paleomagnetic studies show no significant vertical-axis rotation of this unit since deposition. So the 153-degree corrected azimuth can be read as the depositional paleocurrent direction without further rotation. If there were rotation, an additional correction step would shift the result by the rotation angle.

The fourth step is interpretation. A unimodal Fisher distribution with k = 24 is consistent with a sustained single-direction paleocurrent rather than a bimodal or multimodal system. The k value sits below the conventional k > 50 high-confidence threshold but the 87-measurement sample size compensates, giving a tight confidence cone despite moderate concentration. Putting the steps together, the paleocurrent direction is 153 degrees in the depositional frame with a 6-degree confidence cone, consistent with steady fluvial flow toward the southeast in the depositional setting.
