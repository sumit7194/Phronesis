The whole-brain GLM contrast identified a significant dlPFC cluster (peak: −42, 32, 28; 312 voxels; cluster-corrected p < 0.05) in the reappraise > view contrast. High-trait-anxiety participants showed 28% lower BOLD signal change in this cluster during reappraisal versus low-trait-anxiety participants (t(26) = 3.1, p = 0.004). Self-reported emotion regulation success was lower in the high-anxiety group (3.8 vs 5.2 on a 7-point scale, p = 0.011). Functional connectivity between the dlPFC cluster and amygdala did not differ between groups (t(26) = 1.2, p = 0.24). Manipulation: reappraisal and passive viewing were the contrasted conditions; neutral images served as baseline.

The 28% BOLD reduction and the 312-voxel cluster are the specific observations supporting reduced dlPFC engagement in high-anxiety participants during reappraisal. The lower self-reported success (3.8 vs 5.2) is a separate specific observation — a behavioral measure. The claim that reduced dlPFC engagement explains lower self-reported success would require a specific brain-behavior correlation: that participants with lower dlPFC BOLD change also reported lower regulation success. That correlation is not reported; the group difference in BOLD and the group difference in self-report are separate findings that co-occur without a demonstrated within-sample link.

The non-significant amygdala connectivity finding (p = 0.24) is the specific observation relevant to a "top-down prefrontal regulation of amygdala" model. That result does not support the amygdala-regulation claim from this dataset — the two regions' connectivity did not differ between groups despite the dlPFC engagement difference.
