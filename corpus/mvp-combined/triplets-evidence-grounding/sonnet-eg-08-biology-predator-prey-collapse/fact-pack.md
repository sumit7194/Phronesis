## Scenario title

Collapse of a lake fish population following apex predator introduction: identifying the causal pathway from observational count data

## Factual substrate

- A 12-year observational dataset from a sub-alpine lake records annual population estimates (mark-recapture) of native brown trout, introduced pike, and the invertebrate prey base (sweep-net index).
- Pike were stocked in year 3 of the monitoring record; brown trout population index declined from 840 ± 60 to 180 ± 45 individuals by year 9 and has remained suppressed.
- Pike abundance increased from 0 to an estimated 210 ± 30 by year 9; diet analysis of 48 pike in year 9 found that 61% of gut contents by mass were brown trout.
- Invertebrate sweep-net index declined from a baseline of 4.2 (year 1–2 mean) to 2.8 by year 9, a 33% decline.
- A second lake in the same watershed, with no pike stocking and similar baseline trout abundance, showed stable trout numbers over the same 12 years.
- Water temperature records show a 0.4°C warming trend over the study period in both lakes.

## Known ambiguity

- The 33% decline in invertebrate density could be caused by pike predation on invertebrate-consuming trout (trophic cascade), by direct pike predation on invertebrates, by the warming trend, or by a combination. No invertebrate diet analysis for pike exists.
- The brown trout decline is temporally associated with pike introduction and the paired lake is a useful control, but it is an observational association — unmeasured confounders affecting only the study lake are possible. The diet analysis (61% trout by mass) is the closest thing to a direct mechanistic link.

## Conclusion space

- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: The 61% trout-by-mass gut content is the specific observation tying pike predation to trout decline; the paired-lake comparison anchors the claim that pike introduction is the most plausible driver compared to time-varying confounders affecting both lakes (e.g., warming). The invertebrate decline has multiple candidate causes, none of which has a specific observation tying it to any one driver.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion (non-virtuous-right): The reasoner states "pike predation caused the trout collapse and triggered a trophic cascade that reduced invertebrate density" — which is actually the correct causal story in this system — but ties no specific observation to the causal chain (does not cite the 61% gut content, does not invoke the paired lake). The policy conclusion (remove pike) is correct but reached without grounding.

## Correctness confound note

Non-virtuous-right: the deficiency passage reaches the correct conclusion (pike caused the collapse; trophic cascade reduced invertebrates) without tying any claim to a specific datapoint. The virtuous passage is more careful — it marks the invertebrate-decline causal pathway as unresolved and hedges the trophic-cascade attribution — and is therefore less definitive about a chain of events that is actually correct.
