# triplet-035-earth-mineral-mohs-hardness

The Mohs scale ranks ten reference minerals by scratch hardness on an ordinal 1-10 scale: talc, gypsum, calcite, fluorite, apatite, orthoclase, quartz, topaz, corundum, diamond. A mineral with higher Mohs number scratches one of equal or lower number; quartz at 7 scratches feldspar at 6 but is scratched by topaz at 8. Field use combines the reference minerals with a fingernail (approximately 2.5) and a steel knife (approximately 5.5) to bracket an unknown's hardness between the hardest reference it cannot scratch and the softest that can scratch it. The scale is ordinal: diamond is roughly 4× as hard as corundum despite being only one step higher, so quantitative work uses Knoop or Vickers instead (which is approximately the standard result) (which is approximately the standard result).
