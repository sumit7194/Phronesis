## Scenario title

Muon anomalous magnetic moment tension with the Standard Model: reasoning from the measured discrepancy to the strength of evidence for new physics

## Factual substrate

- The experimentally measured muon anomalous magnetic moment (g−2) yields a_μ^exp = 116 592 061 ± 41 × 10⁻¹¹.
- The Standard Model theory prediction used in the comparison is a_μ^SM = 116 591 810 ± 43 × 10⁻¹¹, based on the consensus data-driven hadronic vacuum polarization (HVP) calculation using e⁺e⁻ cross-section data.
- The discrepancy is Δa_μ = 251 ± 59 × 10⁻¹¹, which is 4.2σ from zero.
- A separate lattice QCD calculation of the HVP contribution gives a larger value than the data-driven calculation, which would reduce the discrepancy to approximately 1.5σ if substituted.
- The data-driven and lattice QCD predictions are currently inconsistent with each other at approximately 2.1σ.
- The 4.2σ discrepancy uses the data-driven HVP; the lattice QCD value is not yet used in the consensus theory prediction.

## Notes

RT-a contrast: the virtuous passage shows the steps — (1) the 4.2σ figure uses the data-driven HVP; (2) the lattice QCD value would reduce this to ~1.5σ; (3) the two theory predictions disagree with each other at 2.1σ; (4) therefore the 4.2σ cannot be taken as straightforward evidence for BSM physics until the theory tension is resolved. The excess failure shows all these steps and then adds a further chain about the theoretical uncertainty on each hadronic contribution individually (light-by-light scattering, higher-order QED corrections), generating a scaffolding of theory sub-uncertainty that is accurate but goes beyond what is needed to state the key interpretive caveat.
