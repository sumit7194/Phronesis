Fault B is active and should be incorporated into the regional hazard model — that conclusion is supported by the trench evidence and is the right direction. The question is how precisely the model revision can be characterized given what we know.

The 18% PGA increase is a point estimate that rests on parameters with significant uncertainty. The rupture count is disputed: the alternative interpretation of 2 rather than 3 events implies a ~6,000-year recurrence interval and a substantially lower slip rate, which would produce a smaller hazard contribution. The Mw range (6.8–7.1) is inferred from displacement measurements that carry their own uncertainty. Fault B's interaction with the 6 existing modeled faults has not been assessed — stress coupling can either increase or decrease rupture probability depending on the fault geometry relative to existing stress fields, and this is a meaningful source of uncharacterized uncertainty given the proximity of the faults. Finally, the soil amplification factors used in the model were derived for far-field sources; a shallow blind thrust beneath the city produces different frequency content and depth weighting that could alter the effective PGA at surface.

The appropriate revision incorporates Fault B with uncertainty bounds that reflect the 2-vs-3 rupture dispute, reports the PGA change as a range rather than a point estimate, and flags fault interaction as requiring assessment before the hazard model is used for critical facility siting decisions.
