# triplet-007-chemistry-arrhenius-temperature

The Arrhenius equation is k = A · exp(−Ea/RT). Near room temperature, the rule of thumb is that the rate constant approximately doubles for each 10 K rise. Setting k₂/k₁ = 2 between T₁ = 298 K and T₂ = 308 K, ln(k₂/k₁) = (Ea/R)(1/T₁ − 1/T₂). The bracket evaluates to roughly 1.089 × 10⁻⁴ K⁻¹, so Ea = ln(2) × R / (1.089 × 10⁻⁴) ≈ 53 kJ/mol. The rule is approximately accurate over small windows; over wider intervals an Arrhenius fit is preferable because the exponential makes the apparent doubling rate vary. The same structure also fits the closely related problems in the field (which is approximately the standard result).
