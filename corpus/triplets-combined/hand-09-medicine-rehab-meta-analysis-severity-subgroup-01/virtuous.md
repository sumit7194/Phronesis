A systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated a structured exercise-based rehabilitation program for a chronic musculoskeletal condition, pooling 12 randomized controlled trials with 1,840 patients published within the past 8 years. The pooled effect for functional improvement at 12 weeks was a standardized mean difference of 0.45, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.32 to 0.58, p below 0.001. Heterogeneity was low at I² = 24%, and there was no evidence of publication bias. This is strong meta-analytic evidence — I would treat the overall efficacy of the program as well-established.

The severity subgroup tells a different story depending on which patients we are talking about. For mild-to-moderate patients, 11 trials with 1,680 patients give a pooled effect of 0.42, confidence interval 0.30 to 0.54, I² = 18%. That is nearly as robust as the overall estimate and I am confident the program works for this group. For severe patients, the picture is much thinner — one trial, 160 patients, a reported effect of 0.71 with a confidence interval of 0.18 to 1.24. The point estimate is larger but the interval is five times wider, the trial used a modified higher-intensity protocol, and it recruited from a specialized clinic. I would call the severe-subtype finding promising but preliminary — one underpowered trial with design differences is not a basis for confident claims about that population.

My working view: the intervention works for mild-to-moderate patients, with strong evidence. Whether it works comparably for severe patients is genuinely uncertain.
