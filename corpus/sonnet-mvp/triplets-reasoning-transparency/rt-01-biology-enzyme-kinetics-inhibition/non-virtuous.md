Across the three inhibitor concentrations and five substrate concentrations, the Lineweaver-Burk plots give a clear picture of the inhibition mode. The apparent Km shifts from 81 µM to 124 and then 193 µM as inhibitor concentration increases, while apparent Vmax drops from 11.4 to 9.7 and then 7.1 nmol min⁻¹. This pattern is characteristic of mixed inhibition, where the inhibitor interacts with both free enzyme and the enzyme-substrate complex rather than competing directly at the active site. Inner filter effects were ruled out by confirming the inhibitor has no intrinsic effect on substrate fluorescence in control experiments without enzyme.

The compound is a mixed inhibitor with a competitive Ki of approximately 48 nM and an α factor of approximately 2.4 from the Dixon plot analysis. These parameters indicate the inhibitor binds the free enzyme roughly twice as tightly as the enzyme-substrate complex, since α > 1. At high substrate concentrations relative to Km, the inhibitor's potency is partially offset by substrate competition, but at substrate concentrations near physiological ranges the 48 nM affinity is clinically relevant.

The kinetic data support using this compound as a lead for further inhibitor development targeting this bacterial protease. The mixed inhibition mechanism provides a defined pharmacological profile that is distinct from competitive inhibitors and may offer selectivity advantages in complex biological environments where related host serine proteases operate at higher ambient substrate concentrations. Additional structural studies of the inhibitor-enzyme complex would complement the kinetic characterization and aid structure-activity optimization.
