The empirical unrest claim rests on four monitoring streams. The observatory recorded 240 shallow earthquakes over 36 hours. During that same interval, sulfur dioxide flux rose from 420 to 980 tonnes/day, an upper-flank station recorded 6 microradians of tilt, and summit gas temperature increased by 18°C. Rainfall was negligible, which supports treating the gas and temperature changes as volcanic-system signals rather than weather effects.

The mechanism claim is less direct. Magma ascent could explain increased sulfur dioxide, tilt, and shallow earthquakes, but the observations do not image magma movement. Gas slug movement, hydrothermal pressurization, and conduit clearing remain theoretical alternatives that could produce similar monitoring changes. The absence of newly mapped surface cracks is also empirical; it argues against visible surface rupture during the interval, not against all subsurface movement.

The best-grounded conclusion is that the volcano entered a measurable unrest episode with possible shallow magma or gas movement. I would keep eruption imminence separate from that conclusion. The data justify intensified monitoring, but they do not prove that magma is at the surface or that eruption will follow. Forecast language should wait for trend persistence, earthquake migration, or stronger deformation changes.
