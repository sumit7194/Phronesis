The thermal conductivity characterization provides a reliable basis for designing the turbine blade thermal barrier coating. Measurements at 25°C and 800°C establish a clear linear trend in thermal conductivity versus temperature, with the material showing a consistent and predictable decrease in conductivity with increasing temperature (2.31 to 1.74 W/(m·K) over a 775°C range). The linear fit k = 2.31 − 0.00095·T is well-constrained by these measurements and extrapolates to 0.98 W/(m·K) at 1,400°C.

This extrapolated value represents the best available thermal conductivity estimate for the material at operating temperature, and the linear relationship observed in the measurement range provides confidence that the trend continues at higher temperatures. YSZ is a well-characterized thermal barrier coating material, and the consistency between the two measured points confirms that this material behaves predictably within the characterization framework.

For turbine blade design purposes, a thermal conductivity of approximately 1.0 W/(m·K) at peak operating temperature (1,400°C) should be used as the design input. This value, combined with the coating thickness and operating temperature differential, provides the information needed to calculate the thermal gradient across the barrier layer. The measurements support proceeding with the current material selection for this application, as the projected thermal conductivity at operating temperature is within the acceptable range for thermal barrier coating performance.
