The titration gives 9.823 mL potentiometric and 9.764 mL phenolphthalein, a 0.6% gap, well outside the 0.05% instrument repeatability on either method. The pKa is 4.76, the equivalence-point pH for this concentration is around 8.7, and the buffer-region transition runs pH 8.4 to 9.6 over the last 0.07 mL.

Two assumptions are doing the load-bearing work in identifying the source of the gap. The first is the pH at which a typical observer calls the phenolphthalein endpoint. The transition runs from 8.2 to 10.0, and observers generally judge the first persistent faint pink at pH 9.0 to 9.5 rather than at 8.7. So the indicator endpoint is reached at a pH 0.3 to 0.8 units past the true equivalence inflection, and the apparent titer overshoots if the indicator overshoots, undershoots if it undershoots. With 9.764 mL coming in below 9.823 mL, the apparent indicator endpoint is reached before equivalence, which is the opposite of what an "observer judges at pH 9.2" would predict.

The second assumption is the slope of the pH curve through the buffer region. With a 1.2 pH unit transition over 0.07 mL of titrant, a 0.5 pH unit offset in detected endpoint translates to roughly 0.029 mL of titer, which is half the observed gap. So observer judgment alone is insufficient to explain 0.6%, and a second factor must contribute.

The plausible second factor is atmospheric CO2 absorption during the open-titration window, which slightly acidifies the solution and shifts the indicator transition earlier than equivalence. So the chain is observer pH plus CO2 absorption together, with neither alone sufficient. Reported as such, the indicator method has a known systematic offset that is fully explainable on these two named assumptions.
