TOFD inspection found four indications: two at the cap/root interface (2.1 and 3.4 mm depth), one mid-wall (12.2 mm), and one near the inner surface (22.8 mm). All four are characterized as planar. RT detected two indications — a porosity cluster at approximately 8 mm and an elongated mid-wall indication. The two cap/root indications were not detected by RT.

TOFD sized the mid-wall indication at 4.3 mm height and 31 mm length, and RT confirmed its presence. However, before proceeding to FFS assessment, the flaw-type characterization must be resolved. The mechanical behaviour of the weld under pressure cycling differs significantly depending on whether the mid-wall indication is a tight crack, a lack-of-fusion plane, or a slag line — each has different fracture toughness implications and different propagation behaviour under fatigue. Running the FFS calculation without knowing the flaw type means accepting uncertainty in the fracture toughness input that would need to be conservatively bounded. Phased-array UT or ACFM should be deployed to characterize the flaw type more definitively before the FFS inputs are finalized.

The two cap/root indications also require flaw-type identification before their individual FFS assessments can be properly scoped, given the different stress-intensity factor geometry at the cap/root interface versus mid-wall. The RT non-detection of these two indications additionally raises questions about the adequacy of the RT inspection technique for this weld geometry and wall thickness, which should be investigated before the inspection record is accepted as complete.
