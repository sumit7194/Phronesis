The RNA-seq experiment identified 1,247 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with statistically significant differences (FDR < 0.05) between the treatment and control groups.  Of these, 312 genes exhibited a log2 fold change exceeding 1.0, with the top gene showing a log2 fold change of 4.2, with a padj of 1.2 × 10⁻²⁸. The experiment utilized 8 libraries per group, with sequencing performed in separate batches. This resulted in clear separation of treatment and control samples in Principal Component Analysis (PCA), with PC1 explaining 62% of the variance.  However, PCA also revealed significant differences between samples from batch 1 and batch 2, suggesting a confounding effect of batch.

The absence of batch-bridge samples, which are necessary for batch correction, further complicates the analysis. While the DESeq2 analysis indicates statistical significance, the confounding of treatment and batch makes it challenging to definitively distinguish treatment-specific changes from batch-related artifacts.  RT-qPCR validation of the top 8 genes confirmed the direction of the expression change, but due to the batch separation, this validation does not definitively resolve the cause of the observed changes.

The statistical significance of the 1,247 DEGs remains within the DESeq2 framework, but the interpretation of these findings requires caution due to the confounding effect.  While the statistical significance is technically valid, the observed changes are likely influenced by batch effects, and further investigation is needed to disentangle treatment-specific effects from batch-related artifacts.
