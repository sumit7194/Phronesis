This reconstruction has substantially stronger validation than a typical single-proxy paleoclimate estimate. The modern calibration (r² = 0.91, 40 sites, residual standard error 0.8°C) establishes the δ¹⁸O–temperature relationship on a solid empirical foundation. The three cross-validation methods — borehole temperature profiles showing LGM cooling of 9–11°C, independent δD measurements (r² = 0.97 with δ¹⁸O), and accumulation rate changes — all converge on results consistent with the primary proxy. This level of cross-validation is not standard; it means the reconstruction's uncertainty estimates are well-founded rather than generic.

The temperature values should be reported with their stated uncertainties: Last Interglacial +3.2 ± 1.1°C, LGM −9.8 ± 1.3°C, and Holocene thermal maximum +1.7 ± 0.9°C above preindustrial. The known δ¹⁸O complications — moisture source variability, seasonality shifts — have been assessed in the calibration residuals and shown to contribute less than 0.5°C, so these are not grounds for additional blanket hedging beyond the reported uncertainty ranges.

The genuine scope limitation is geographic: this reconstruction characterizes central Antarctic temperature change, not global mean temperature, and applying it globally requires regional scaling. That distinction is real and should be stated clearly. It is not a reason to question the reconstruction's quality for what it actually measures.
