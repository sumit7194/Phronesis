A polyurethane coating for outdoor structural steel was subjected to accelerated weathering under ASTM G154 Cycle 4 conditions (UVA-340 lamps at 1.55 W/m²/nm, 70°C black panel) for 4,000 hours. Using a 3× acceleration ratio based on radiant dose integration, this corresponds to approximately 6.8 years of outdoor service in a mid-latitude temperate climate; the target service life is 20 years.

Property retention at 4,000 hours met all specification criteria: gloss retention 72% (minimum 50%), color change ΔE = 3.2 (maximum 5.0), and adhesion rating 4B (minimum 3B). Natural outdoor exposure panels installed 3 years ago show gloss retention of 88%, which is consistent with the acceleration model predictions. An Arrhenius-based extrapolation to 20 years projects gloss retention of 48% and ΔE = 6.1; the gloss projection falls below the 50% specification minimum and the color change exceeds the specification limit.

Polyurethane coatings are documented to undergo a transition from surface degradation (chalking) to bulk degradation (cracking and delamination) at approximately 40–50% gloss retention. The degradation kinetics change at this transition, and the 3× acceleration ratio was established from data in the surface degradation regime. No accelerated or natural exposure data exist beyond the current 4,000-hour and 3-year points, which remain within the surface regime.
