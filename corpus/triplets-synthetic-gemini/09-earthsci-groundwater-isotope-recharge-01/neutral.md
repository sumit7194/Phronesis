Fourteen distinct wells located across a shallow alluvial aquifer were evaluated using complex ³H-³He (tritium-helium) dating algorithms. Across the testing battery, twelve localized wells established apparent isotopic ages ranging between 2 and 8 years. The final two border wells framing the aquifer’s distant margin yielded distinctly older profiles ranging from 15 to 22 years. 

Because the algorithm demands isolation of purely tritiogenic helium, a strict Ne-based mathematical correction neutralized ambient terrigenic ⁴He. The local excess air Ne concentrations (1.2 to 3.8 × 10⁻⁴ ccSTP/g) operated normally. Uncorrected, raw data would merely shift the baseline ages older by roughly +1 to +3 years. Supplementary oxygen-deuterium (δ¹⁸O / δ²H) baseline plots pinned all tested samples tightly onto the local meteoric water line. A massive municipal wellfield directly adjacent to the survey zone simultaneously forces 15,000 m³ of daily extraction. Local authorities intend to utilize this multi-well dating dataset to officially classify the regional aquifer's pumping dynamics, deciding whether the network sustains active modern recharge cycles or critically mines irreplaceable, deeply sequestered historical fossil-water reserves. This decision impacts future water infrastructure extraction bounds for the immediate regional network. The dual isotope survey provides the central metric dataset characterizing the underground hydrology.
