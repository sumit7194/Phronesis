The eddy-current inspection train traversed 240 km at 80 km/h with sensor spacing 50 mm, and the standard 30% probe-amplitude threshold produced 412 indications. Manual confirmation on a 12% random sub-sample (50 indications) found 38 true defects and 12 false positives, giving a positive predictive value of 76%. A parallel ultrasonic inspection on the same 50 indications confirmed 41 of them, indicating the eddy-current sensor missed some sub-surface defects that ultrasonic could see. A 4 km recently re-railed section produced only 8 indications, a useful low-prior comparison.

Track curvature averages 1,200 m radius across the line, and liftoff variation in curves of up to 0.3 mm produces signal artefacts that the standard threshold does not fully filter. The combination suggests that some of the false positives are concentrated in curved sections.

Extrapolating the 76% PPV from the 50-indication sub-sample to the full 412 indications would predict roughly 313 true positives across the line. The ultrasonic comparison on the same 50 suggests the sensor missed some sub-surface defects, so the true-defect count on the line could exceed 313 once those undetected sub-surface ones are included.

The picture supported by the data is that the line has a substantial defect population, with the eddy-current sensor providing reasonable surface-defect detection at 76% PPV but missing some sub-surface defects. The recently re-railed section's low indication rate is consistent with the rest of the line genuinely carrying age-related defect accumulation. Recommended actions: full ultrasonic follow-up on the indications, prioritise curved sections for liftoff-corrected reanalysis, and validate the 76% PPV estimate on a larger sub-sample.
