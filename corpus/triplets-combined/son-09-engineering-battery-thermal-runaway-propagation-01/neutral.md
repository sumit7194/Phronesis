A series of nail penetration tests was conducted on 50 Ah NMC811 lithium-ion cells per the UL 2580 standard. Ten cells were tested at 100% state of charge. In 8 of 10 tests, cells vented gas without flame; in 2 of 10 tests, brief flame was observed that self-extinguished within 5 seconds. All 10 cells passed UL 2580 criteria. The maximum cell surface temperature recorded was 680°C; no explosion occurred in any test. Characterized thermal runaway parameters include a peak heat generation rate of 42 kW per cell, total energy release of 1.8 MJ, and an onset-to-peak duration of 12 seconds.

The cells are configured in a 96-cell battery pack (4P24S) with 3 mm cell-to-cell spacing, aluminum bus bars, and a passive thermal management system using phase-change material between cells. No pack-level thermal propagation test has been performed. A 2D finite element thermal simulation was used to assess propagation risk; the simulation used a steady-state, linearized heat transfer model with a constant convective heat transfer coefficient of 25 W/m²K derived from natural convection correlations.

Published data from thermal propagation tests of a similar (but not identical) cell chemistry and pack design show that propagation occurred in 3 of 5 tests when cell-to-cell spacing was less than 5 mm.
