The synthetic control estimate is well-founded by the criteria that distinguish credible from fragile applications of the method. The pre-treatment match is excellent — within 0.3 fatalities per 100,000 for all ten pre-treatment years with an RMSPE of 0.24 — establishing that the synthetic control credibly replicates State A's trajectory before legalization. The placebo rank of 1 out of 39 (p<0.026) provides a rigorous permutation-based inference check. The leave-one-out robustness result confirms the estimate is not driven by any single donor state. These are precisely the conditions under which a synthetic control result deserves causal interpretation.

The concurrent opioid crisis intervention in State A (beginning 2017) is a specific, identified confounding concern rather than a generic "n=1" objection. If the intervention reduced opioid-involved traffic deaths, it could partially offset a cannabis-related increase in the estimated +2.1 per 100,000 effect; if it had no effect on traffic fatalities, the estimate is unaffected. This is a testable question: examining the synthetic control estimate restricted to non-opioid traffic fatalities, or comparing State A's opioid-involved crash trends against synthetic controls, would help determine whether the opioid intervention is an active confounder.

The appropriate conclusion is that cannabis legalization is associated with a credible increase in traffic fatalities in State A, subject to the specific opioid intervention confound remaining unresolved.
