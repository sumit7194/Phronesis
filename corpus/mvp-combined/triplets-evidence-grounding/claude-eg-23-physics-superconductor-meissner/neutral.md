The Bi₂Te₃-doped thin film shows zero electrical resistance at temperatures below 17.2 K in 4-probe measurement. SQUID magnetic susceptibility shows a diamagnetic onset at 16.8 K with a low-temperature shielding fraction of 6%. Specific-heat measurement on a 12 mg piece does not show a clear anomaly at 17 K, and the noise floor is about 3% of the predicted BCS jump for a fully superconducting sample. X-ray diffraction indicates a 2θ shift consistent with 1.4% lattice contraction relative to the undoped reference. Energy-dispersive spectroscopy shows the dopant concentration is non-uniform, with regions ranging from 1.2 at% to 4.1 at%. A control measurement on a partially decomposed sample at nominally the same composition shows residual resistance and no diamagnetic onset.

The transport and magnetisation measurements both show transitions in the 16.8–17.2 K range, consistent with a superconducting onset somewhere in the sample. The 6% shielding fraction is a small fraction of the volume; the specific-heat measurement does not see a clear anomaly within its noise floor. Together those two facts indicate the superconducting volume is small relative to the full sample.

The most natural reading is that there is genuine superconductivity in part of the sample at around 17 K, but probably not in the bulk. The non-uniform dopant from EDS suggests local regions of higher dopant concentration that could host filamentary superconducting paths, with the rest of the sample remaining normal. The control sample's lack of any diamagnetic response argues against the signal being a measurement artefact.

The conclusion the data support is filamentary or partial-volume superconductivity at around 17 K, not a bulk superconducting transition. Confirming the bulk interpretation would require samples with higher shielding fraction and a resolvable specific-heat anomaly.
