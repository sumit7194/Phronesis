Groundwater samples from 14 wells in a shallow alluvial aquifer were analyzed using ³H-³He age dating. Twelve of the 14 wells yielded apparent ages of 2–8 years, consistent with modern recharge. Two wells at the aquifer margin returned ages of 15–22 years, suggesting an older water component. The ³H-³He method requires correcting for terrigenic ⁴He from the aquifer matrix; the correction was applied using measured neon concentrations. Excess air neon values of 1.2–3.8 × 10⁻⁴ ccSTP/g fall within the typical range for alluvial aquifers. Without the correction, apparent ages would shift by +1 to +3 years, which does not change the modern versus fossil classification for the 12 wells.

Stable isotope data provide corroborating evidence: δ¹⁸O and δ²H values for all wells fall on the local meteoric water line, confirming modern precipitation as the recharge source. Seasonal sampling over four rounds showed δ¹⁸O variation of 0.8‰ at shallow wells, consistent with seasonal recharge pulses.

The aquifer is adjacent to a municipal wellfield pumping 15,000 m³/day. The age data have been used to assess whether the aquifer is sustainably recharged by modern precipitation or is drawing down fossil water. The data indicate that water entering the 12 central wells is modern, but the age of recharge water does not by itself quantify whether recharge rate is sufficient to balance current extraction.
