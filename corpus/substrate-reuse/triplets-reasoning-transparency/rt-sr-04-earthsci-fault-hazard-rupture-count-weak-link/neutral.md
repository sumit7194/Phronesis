A paleoseismic trench study of a newly mapped blind thrust fault (Fault B) beneath a metropolitan area identified evidence of 3 prehistoric ruptures over the past 12,000 years, with estimated moment magnitude Mw 6.8–7.1 per event and a recurrence interval of approximately 4,000 years; slip rate is estimated at 0.8 mm/yr from displacement measurements. Fault B was not previously included in the regional probabilistic seismic hazard model. Incorporating it with the trench-derived parameters increases the estimated peak ground acceleration (PGA) at 2% probability of exceedance in 50 years by 18% at the metropolitan area centroid. The existing hazard model includes 6 other active faults within 40 km with a combined slip rate of 12.4 mm/yr; Fault B's 0.8 mm/yr represents a 6.5% increase in total regional slip rate.

Fault B is not known to exhibit stress coupling or rupture linkage with the 6 modeled faults; fault interaction effects were not modeled in the hazard revision. An alternative interpretation of the same trench data by a second geologist supports only 2 ruptures in the 12,000-year period rather than 3, implying a recurrence interval of ~6,000 years and a lower slip rate. The paleoseismic evidence consists of upward fault terminations, stratigraphic offsets at two horizons, and displaced terrace surfaces, supported by 5 radiocarbon dates with 2σ uncertainties of ±600–900 years per date.
