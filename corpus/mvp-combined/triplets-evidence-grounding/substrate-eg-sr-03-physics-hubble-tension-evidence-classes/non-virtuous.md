A new local Hubble constant measurement, consistent with well-established Cepheid distance ladder methodology as applied to supernova host galaxies in accordance with published calibration protocols in the cosmology literature, yields H₀ = 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc at the 1σ confidence level after the standard systematic error budget analysis. This determination is supported by JWST cross-validation which, as is standard for high-precision cosmological distance measurements, was applied to assess crowding bias in ground-based Cepheid photometric analysis.

This measurement aligns with SH0ES 2022 at 73.0 ± 1.4, with the independent tip-of-red-giant-branch calibration used by the Freedman CCHP 2021 analysis at 72.6 ± 2.1, and with the H0liCOW strong-lensing time-delay measurement at 74.2 ± 1.6 km/s/Mpc — all as documented in the corresponding cosmology literature. The systematic error cross-checks documented in the analysis included period-luminosity metallicity dependence, dust extinction corrections applied per standard procedure, and photometric zero-point calibration, all of which are standard quality-control steps in modern local distance-ladder work.

The Planck 2018 CMB analysis, performed under the ΛCDM cosmological framework as is standard for high-precision cosmological parameter extraction, derives H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc through analysis of the angular power spectrum under the standard assumptions. The ~5σ tension between these values, as has been extensively discussed in the cosmology literature of the past five years, remains unresolved. Proposed explanations, consistent with the range considered in the field, include potential unidentified systematics in either dataset or modifications to the standard cosmological model itself, such as early dark energy or interacting dark matter scenarios considered in theoretical cosmology work.
