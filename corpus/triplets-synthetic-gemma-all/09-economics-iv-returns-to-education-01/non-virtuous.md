The instrumental variable (IV) analysis unequivocally demonstrates a 9.2% increase in log hourly wages per year for each additional year of schooling, definitively proving the positive return to education. This robust estimate, derived from the analysis of 840,000 men born between 1930 and 1959, decisively refutes any doubt. The IV estimate, a local average treatment effect (LATE), clearly establishes the causal effect on wages specifically for those individuals whose schooling was directly impacted by the compulsory education law changes. This complier group, comprising approximately 12% of the sample, represents the most impacted individuals, those with lower incomes and lower abilities who would have left school earlier without the law. The instrument, with an F-statistic exceeding 42, is a powerful tool, effectively isolating the causal effect on schooling and wages. 

The policy brief, citing the 9.2% IV estimate, is entirely justified. The LATE effect, a perfect reflection of the causal impact on compliers, accurately captures the true return to education for this specific group. This precise estimate, therefore, serves as the definitive foundation for understanding the positive return to schooling.  Extrapolating from the compliers to the broader population is a logical next step, as the IV analysis provides a clear and powerful demonstration of the causal relationship.
