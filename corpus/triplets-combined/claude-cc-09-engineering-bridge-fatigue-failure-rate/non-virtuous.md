Bridge at 38 of 75 years of design life. Recent inspection found measurable fatigue indicators at 4 of 22 weld details at the critical class. The S-N curve gives a 10-year per-detail crack-initiation probability of 0.06 under measured traffic loads, with a coefficient of variation of about 0.4 on the stress range. Traffic loads are up 18% over the past decade.

The inspection result is meaningfully higher than the S-N prior would have predicted for a clean detail population, which tells me one of two things is going on: either the elevated traffic loads are pushing the fatigue rate noticeably above what the historical curve assumes, or there is a fabrication-batch issue at this specific detail class, or some mix of the two. The traffic growth is large enough that I would lean toward it being part of the explanation, since fatigue damage scales fairly steeply with stress range.

That makes the historical per-detail rate look like an underestimate for current and future conditions. Over the next 10 years, the chance of at least one critical-detail crack reaching propagation looks meaningfully elevated relative to what the prior alone would have suggested, especially without intervention. With targeted retrofit on the 4 affected details and tighter inspection intervals, that elevated risk comes back down considerably.

I am reasonably confident the right plan is retrofit on the 4 details currently showing indicators, a shortened inspection interval going forward, and a structural review aimed at reducing stress on the critical class. The bridge is not in immediate danger of catastrophic failure but it is on a fatigue trajectory where doing nothing is clearly the wrong answer. I will draft the plan along those lines with explicit escalation triggers tied to the next inspection.
