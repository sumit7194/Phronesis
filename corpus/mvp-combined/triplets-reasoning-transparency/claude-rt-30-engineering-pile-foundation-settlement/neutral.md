The 14-story structure is supported by 240 driven concrete piles tipping at 28 m into a stiff clay layer that extends to 41 m before transitioning to dense sand. Working load per pile is 1,200 kN against an ultimate axial capacity of 3,800 kN, giving a factor of safety of 3.2. Three indicator pile static load tests gave settlements of 4.8, 5.2, and 5.6 mm at working load, with a mean of 5.2 mm and a tight spread.

The design settlement allowance is 25 mm total over service life, with 12 mm differential between adjacent column lines. The static load test results indicate that the immediate elastic and short-term consolidation settlement at working load is roughly 5 mm per pile, well below the 25 mm allowance. The differential between piles is around 0.4 mm, well below the 12 mm differential allowance.

The static load tests measure the response over hours-to-days and capture immediate elastic and short-term consolidation effects but do not directly include long-term creep or secondary consolidation in the underlying clay. The 13 m of clay between the pile tips at 28 m and the dense sand at 41 m is the layer most likely to contribute long-term settlement, since it is loaded by the pile group as a whole rather than by individual piles.

Standard estimates of long-term creep settlement for stiff clay layers of this thickness under group-load conditions are in the 8 to 15 mm range over a 50-year service life, depending on the secondary compression index and the effective stress history of the layer.

The reported result is that immediate settlement is approximately 5 mm and long-term settlement is estimated at 8 to 15 mm, giving a total of 13 to 20 mm against the 25 mm allowance. The design is likely adequate.
