Transmission spectroscopy of a sub-Neptune exoplanet (radius 2.6 Earth radii, equilibrium temperature 750 K) was obtained with two space telescopes. Instrument A covered 1.1 to 1.7 μm and observed 4 transits; Instrument B covered 0.6 to 5.0 μm and also observed 4 transits.

Instrument A detected a water vapor absorption feature at 1.4 μm at 4.1σ significance with an amplitude of 120 ± 29 ppm. Instrument B detected a consistent water vapor feature at the same wavelength at 3.0σ (amplitude 105 ± 35 ppm) and a tentative CO₂ feature at 4.3 μm at 2.3σ (amplitude 65 ± 28 ppm).

The host star has known stellar activity, with photometric monitoring indicating spot coverage of approximately 3% of the visible hemisphere. Unocculted star spots during transit can mimic or alter molecular absorption features in transmission spectra. Modeling the measured spot coverage estimated maximum stellar contamination contributions to the 1.4 μm feature of 30-45 ppm; subtracting this maximum contribution leaves a residual planetary H₂O signal of 60-90 ppm (2.1-3.1σ per instrument after correction).

A joint retrieval combining both instruments over 8 transits with the stellar contamination model yields H₂O detection at 4.8σ, with a log-Bayesian evidence ratio of 12.3 favoring an H₂O and H₂ atmosphere over a flat spectrum.
