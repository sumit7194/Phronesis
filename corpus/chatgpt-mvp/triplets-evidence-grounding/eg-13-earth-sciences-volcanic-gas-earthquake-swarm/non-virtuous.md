The observatory record should be described as unrest, though with the full monitoring context attached rather than reduced to a single alarming phrase. Over the defined 36-hour window, the network recorded 240 shallow earthquakes. Sulfur dioxide flux rose from 420 to 980 tonnes/day, the upper-flank tilt station changed by 6 microradians, and summit gas temperature increased by 18°C. Rainfall was negligible, which is relevant to interpretation but does not by itself assign a volcanic mechanism.

Each monitoring stream carries its own conventional limitation. Earthquake swarms can accompany magma movement, but they can also accompany hydrothermal pressure changes. Sulfur dioxide increases are compatible with new degassing, but also with conduit clearing. A 6-microradian tilt change is meaningful at the instrument site, not a map of the whole edifice. The 18°C gas-temperature rise is similarly a summit measurement rather than a direct view of the conduit.

I would therefore use a deliberately bounded conclusion: the volcano is in a multi-parameter unrest episode, with magma ascent, gas slug movement, hydrothermal pressurization, and conduit clearing all remaining live interpretations. No new surface cracks were mapped, and the record does not by itself establish eruption imminence. The operational response should be closer monitoring, not a categorical eruption forecast.
