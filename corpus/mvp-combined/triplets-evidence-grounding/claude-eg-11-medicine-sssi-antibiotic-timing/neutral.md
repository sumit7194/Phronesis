Five sources bear on the claim that giving cefazolin 0–30 minutes before incision lowers surgical-site infection rates compared to 30–60 minutes before. The multi-centre randomised trial enrolled 2,148 elective colorectal patients and reported SSI rates of 4.1% in the 0–30 min arm versus 6.8% in the 30–60 min arm, with relative risk 0.60 (95% CI 0.42–0.86, p = 0.005). The national surgical registry of 41,200 procedures over 5 years gave an adjusted SSI odds ratio of 0.78 (95% CI 0.69–0.88) for the earlier window.

A pharmacokinetic study in 24 surgical patients found mean cefazolin tissue concentrations peaking at 22 ± 6 µg/g at 25 min post-dose and falling to 9 ± 4 µg/g at 70 min. A meta-analysis pooling 9 prior RCTs reported a pooled relative risk of 0.71 (95% CI 0.61–0.83) for earlier dosing windows. A single-surgeon case series of 84 patients with strict 0–30 min adherence reported no SSI events.

The randomised trial gives the strongest single causal anchor. The registry adds population-level support across diverse operators but is observational and confounded by case-mix despite adjustment. The pharmacokinetic study provides a mechanistic explanation: tissue concentrations are higher during the 0–30 min window. The meta-analysis aggregates across heterogeneous study designs and gives a pooled effect roughly consistent with the new trial. The case series is observational and small and adds little independent weight.

Taking the five together, the evidence supports earlier dosing as the preferred window for SSI prevention in this surgical context. The trial and meta-analysis carry most of the weight; the registry and PK are supporting evidence; the case series is illustrative.
