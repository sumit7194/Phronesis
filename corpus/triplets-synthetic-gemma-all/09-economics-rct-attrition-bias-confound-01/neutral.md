The findings of our randomized controlled trial demonstrate a statistically significant positive effect of the financial literacy training program on household savings rates, with a treatment group savings rate increase of 8.4 percentage points compared to the control group (p < 0.001).  This effect size, calculated using Cohen's d, is substantial at 0.41.  Despite a 4.4 percentage point differential attrition rate between the treatment and control groups, the Lee bounds for the treatment effect, accounting for attrition, provide a range of +3.1 to +12.2 percentage points.  

The attrition analysis suggests that the treatment group attrition is primarily driven by male household heads with higher pre-randomization savings. These high-saving households were more likely to withdraw from the program, potentially leading to an underestimation of the true effect.  However, the control group attrition pattern was not significantly different from the control completers, indicating no corresponding bias in the control group.  

While attrition is a significant concern, the observed pattern in the treatment group suggests that the true effect is likely larger than the observed +8.4 percentage point increase.  This uncertainty is captured by the Lee bounds, which suggest a potential range of 3.1 to 12.2 percentage points of additional savings resulting from the intervention.
