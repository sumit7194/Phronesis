A conservation genetics study was conducted on a freshwater darter (Family Percidae) sampled from 8 sites across 4 river drainages. The analysis utilized three independent genetic marker systems: 1,247 SNPs derived from RAD-seq, 12 microsatellite loci, and mtDNA haplotype sequencing. Evaluation of the data from all three systems independently supported the division of the individuals into three specific genetic clusters: Cluster 1 comprising sites 1-3, Cluster 2 covering sites 4-6, and Cluster 3 representing sites 7-8. 

Pairwise FST comparisons between these clusters resulted in values of 0.17-0.19 for SNPs, 0.14-0.16 for microsatellites, and 0.21-0.23 for mtDNA ΦST. Within-cluster FST values remained below 0.04 across all markers. Additionally, Structure analysis (where K=3 was supported by the ΔK criterion), DAPC, and PCA assigned individuals to the identical clusters, with assignment probabilities greater than 0.95 for 91 of 96 individuals. Gene flow estimates showed fewer than 0.8 migrants per generation between clusters, compared to 4-12 migrants within clusters. Historical factors such as vicariance, isolation by distance, and secondary contact mechanisms are part of ongoing evaluations regarding the origins of this multi-drainage structure. These genetic patterns provide a framework for classifying the modern structure of the species across this fragmented landscape.
