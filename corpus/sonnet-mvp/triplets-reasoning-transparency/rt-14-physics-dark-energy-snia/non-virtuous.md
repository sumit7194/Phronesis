A 1,048 SNe Ia compilation (0.01 < z < 2.3) gives w₀ = −1.03 ± 0.14, wₐ = 0.21 ± 0.73. Dominant systematic: photometric calibration. Redshift-bin consistency: p = 0.35. Stretch-and-color standardization; intrinsic scatter ~0.15 mag.

The SNe Ia Hubble diagram gives w₀ = −1.03 ± 0.14, consistent with ΛCDM (w₀ = −1), and wₐ = 0.21 ± 0.73, consistent with zero time-evolution of the dark energy equation of state. The redshift-bin consistency test (p = 0.35) confirms that no significant z-dependent systematic affects the sample and that the distance moduli at low, intermediate, and high redshift are internally consistent within their uncertainties. The dominant systematic — photometric calibration, contributing 0.08 to the total 0.14 uncertainty on w₀ — is incorporated in the reported error budget, so the stated uncertainties on w₀ and wₐ are realistic and include the dominant known source of bias. The stretch-and-color standardization procedure is a well-established and extensively cross-validated correction that converts raw SNe Ia into precision distance indicators; it provides a reliable standardization whose residuals are well-characterized by the 0.15 mag intrinsic scatter term included in the fit. The result is that w₀ ≈ −1, placing the dark energy equation of state fully consistent with a cosmological constant at the current measurement precision. The wₐ = 0.21 ± 0.73 is too uncertain to constrain time-evolution of dark energy at this sample size and redshift range. These results support ΛCDM as the best current description of the dark energy component of the universe's energy budget.
