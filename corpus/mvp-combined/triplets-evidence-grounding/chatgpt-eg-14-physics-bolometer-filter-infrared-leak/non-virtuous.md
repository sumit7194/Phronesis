The baseline problem was an infrared leak from the warm baffle. The bolometer array had a noticeable offset during window-open calibration, and once the infrared blocking filter was installed the offset largely disappeared. That is the decisive result. If blocking infrared radiation removes most of the offset, then infrared radiation was the cause.

The rest of the checks fit the same conclusion. Dark runs with the window closed stayed essentially flat before and after the filter was added, so the detector baseline itself was not drifting. The readout electronics noise spectrum was unchanged, so the electronics were not responsible. The detector bath temperature barely moved, which rules out a broader cryogenic-temperature problem. The infrared camera showed the warm baffle near the window, which identifies the source.

My conclusion is that the warm baffle was loading the array through the open calibration window. The infrared blocking filter fixed the problem by cutting that path. Electromagnetic pickup and readout drift are not serious alternatives here, because neither explains why the filter eliminated most of the offset while the other diagnostics stayed stable. The case is straightforward and the apparatus can be returned to routine use once the filter is in place.
