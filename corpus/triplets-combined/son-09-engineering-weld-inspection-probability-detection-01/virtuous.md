The PAUT inspection is reliable, and the field results confirm it — but the audit concern raises a genuine issue that deserves a direct answer rather than dismissal.

The validation study provides well-characterized detection capability: 91% POD at the 2 mm target flaw size, rising to 97% at 3 mm, both with conservative lower confidence bounds from 84 planted flaws across 120 specimens. The 91% figure at 2 mm is not a claim of perfection. It means approximately 9% of 2 mm flaws may not be detected. That residual uncertainty is real and should inform how the program handles marginal cases — it is not a reason to distrust the inspection overall.

The auditor's concern about operator dependence is valid for manually scanned ultrasonic testing but does not apply to encoded, mechanized PAUT. In this procedure, the probe is driven along the weld by a mechanical encoder; the scanning motion is not operator-controlled. The specific variable the auditor is concerned about has been engineered out of this procedure.

The field results confirm that the procedure is performing as validated. A false call rate of 0.9% and a confirmation rate of 86% across 220 welds are consistent with the laboratory baseline. If field conditions were materially degrading performance, the field metrics would diverge from the validated figures; they have not.

The inspection is reliable for its intended purpose. The 91% POD at 2 mm is a quantified limitation to carry forward — not a failure of the program.
