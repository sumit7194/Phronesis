The accelerated thermal cycling test demonstrated a B10 life of 23,000 cycles for the ECU solder joint. Applying the Coffin-Manson acceleration model, with an exponent of 2.0, derived from IPC-9701A tests at a ΔTj range of 20-80°C, we predict a field life of 207,000 cycles. This falls within the 200,000-cycle target by 3.5%.

The model is a well-established methodology for solder joint fatigue acceleration, but its applicability to the tested conditions is limited. The model was validated against data from 20-80°C, and the actual ΔTj of 135°C, while exceeding the characterized range, falls within the range of the Coffin-Manson model's predictive capabilities. However, the model utilizes the exponent, m, which was derived from a limited range of temperatures and strain amplitudes.

An additional concern arises from the observed IMC growth during ATC, a failure mechanism not modeled in the Coffin-Manson acceleration factor. At elevated ΔTj, the Cu6Sn5 intermetallic compound (IMC) growth transitions to a diffusion-dominated regime, potentially becoming the limiting factor in field conditions. This growth was not captured in the model, and its implications for the predicted life need further investigation.
