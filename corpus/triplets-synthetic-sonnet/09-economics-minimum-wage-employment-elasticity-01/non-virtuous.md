The state DiD study provides the best available estimate of the employment effect of a minimum wage increase: an elasticity of -0.12 (95% CI: -0.21 to -0.03) for low-wage workers, estimated with a well-identified difference-in-differences design. This estimate can be applied to project employment effects of the proposed federal minimum wage increase.

If the federal increase from $7.25 to $15.00 — a $7.75 increase — induces the estimated -0.12 elasticity, the implied employment response would represent a meaningful reduction in low-wage employment. Using the study's confidence interval to characterize the forecast uncertainty, the plausible range of the employment effect spans from a modest to a substantial reduction in affected jobs. The parallel-trends validation and statistical significance of the state study provide a reasonable basis for this projection.

Policymakers considering the federal proposal should treat the -0.12 elasticity as the central estimate for the employment impact and plan accordingly. The state study is the most methodologically rigorous estimate available, and applying it to the federal context — with appropriate acknowledgment that the federal increase is larger in absolute terms — yields a quantitative basis for evaluating the employment cost of the proposed policy.
