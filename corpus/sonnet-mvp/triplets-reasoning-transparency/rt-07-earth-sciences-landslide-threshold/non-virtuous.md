The 20-year record documents 47 shallow landslides calibrated to a lower-envelope ID threshold: I = 14.2 × D⁻⁰·⁷³. Seven exceedances produced no landslides. Record covers shallow translational failures on slopes > 28°. Antecedent soil moisture conditions were not recorded. An adjacent basin shows a higher threshold.

The ID threshold carries multiple layers of uncertainty that together limit its reliability for operational forecasting. The antecedent soil moisture problem means the threshold was calibrated without controlling for the most physically significant modulating variable in shallow landslide triggering. The false-positive rate of 7 in 54 exceedances (13%) represents events where the threshold predicted failure but none occurred, indicating the threshold over-predicts at a non-trivial rate. The restriction to shallow translational failures means the threshold is inapplicable to deep-seated failures, which may account for a significant fraction of actual landslide events in this watershed type during extreme rainfall. The adjacent-basin threshold discrepancy (14.2 vs 18.7) demonstrates that the calibration is not transferable even to geologically similar neighboring basins and should not be applied outside the specific calibration watershed without recalibration. The 20-year record may not fully capture the tails of the rainfall distribution relevant to triggering events under extreme conditions, particularly given projected changes in storm intensity and frequency in this region. Together, these limitations mean the threshold cannot reliably support real-time warning operations without substantial additional data collection — including antecedent soil moisture monitoring, extension to deep-seated failure types, a longer calibration record covering a broader range of rainfall intensities, and validation in neighboring basins before basin-transfer application.
