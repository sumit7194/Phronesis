The two cloud-chamber runs should be interpreted within the narrow protocol by which they were produced. In the drier condition, defined here as 38% relative humidity during a twenty-minute exposure to the weak beta source, the plate produced 146 visible tracks with a mean visible length of 18 mm. In the humid condition, after equilibration to 71% relative humidity and with source position, exposure time, voltage, pressure, and temperature held constant, the plate produced 92 visible tracks with a mean visible length of 11 mm. Background plates without the source showed 5 visible tracks under the same imaging procedure.

As a matter of laboratory reporting, I would not treat any of these numbers as a free-standing claim about beta emission. They are visible-track counts obtained under a particular chamber and imaging arrangement. The increased droplet density in the humid run is also a recorded feature of the apparatus state, and it should be carried along before any mechanistic language is used. Strictly speaking, the unchanged source position and exposure time document procedural comparability; they do not independently certify unchanged emission.

The cautious conclusion is therefore layered more than it is simple: within this sealed diffusion chamber, under these settings, the shift from 38% to 71% relative humidity accompanied a reduction from 146 to 92 visible tracks and from 18 mm to 11 mm in mean visible length. Whether that reflects reduced track formation, collection efficiency, or optical occlusion remains outside the direct measurement. I would require a separate particle-counting or imaging-control run before promoting the observation into a mechanism.
