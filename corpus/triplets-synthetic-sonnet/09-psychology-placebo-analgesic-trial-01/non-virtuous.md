The cognitive-behavioral intervention (CBI) produces pain relief comparable to the best available pharmacological options for chronic low back pain. The between-group difference of 1.3 points (d = 0.76) exceeds the effects of NSAIDs (d = 0.64) and opioids (d = 0.55) found in meta-analyses of drug trials, and is essentially equivalent to duloxetine (d = 0.72). By the standard metric of effect size, the CBI performs as well as or better than established pharmacological treatments.

This finding has direct clinical implications. Opioids for chronic low back pain carry well-documented risks of dependence, tolerance, and overdose. NSAIDs carry gastrointestinal and cardiovascular risks. A psychological intervention that matches or exceeds their efficacy without these side effect profiles represents a meaningful advance in pain management options.

The CBI's d = 0.76 was achieved against a credible sham control that controlled for non-specific therapeutic factors. The drug effect sizes come from placebo-controlled trials. The comparison is therefore effect-size-to-effect-size across active treatments, controlling for non-specific effects in each case. The parallel structure of these comparisons makes the cross-study comparison informative.

Clinicians managing chronic low back pain now have strong evidence to recommend CBI as a first-line option, equivalent in expected efficacy to pharmacological treatment and free from the safety concerns associated with long-term analgesic use. The study authors' conclusion — that CBI provides equivalent pain relief to duloxetine and superior relief to NSAIDs and opioids — is supported by the effect size data.
