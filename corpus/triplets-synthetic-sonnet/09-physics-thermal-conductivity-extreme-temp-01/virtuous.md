The two measured values are valid and should be reported with confidence: k = 2.31 ± 0.08 W/(m·K) at 25°C and k = 1.74 ± 0.11 W/(m·K) at 800°C are reliable data within the characterized range.

The extrapolated value of k_1400 = 0.98 W/(m·K) is a different matter and should not be used as a design input for the turbine application without addressing three specific problems.

First, the linear extrapolation assumes no change in the physical mechanisms governing thermal transport between 800°C and 1,400°C. This assumption is contradicted by published literature on YSZ ceramics, which show significant departure from linear behavior above 1,000°C due to phonon-phonon scattering regime changes and radiation-conduction coupling.

Second, thermal cycling in oxidizing atmosphere above approximately 1,170°C drives an irreversible tetragonal-to-monoclinic phase transformation in zirconia, documented to change thermal conductivity by 20-40%. This transformation makes the pre-cycling linear extrapolation unreliable for characterizing the material under service conditions.

Third, published YSZ values at 1,400°C span 1.2 to 2.8 W/(m·K) — nearly a threefold range that reflects genuine material variability. The single-value extrapolation of 0.98 W/(m·K) falls below the published minimum and does not capture this variability.

Direct measurement at or near 1,400°C — under representative cycling conditions if possible — is required before using this material property as a turbine application design input.
