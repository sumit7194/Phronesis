Phenolphthalein testing across 12 cores gave carbonation depths of 14–28 mm, mean 21 mm. Cover meter readings show rebar cover of 18–42 mm, mean 29 mm. Three cores have carbonation depth at or exceeding cover depth — these three locations have an active carbonation front at or past the rebar. Chloride concentration at rebar depth is below 0.1% by cement weight at all six sampled cores, well below the corrosion-initiation threshold.

The three depassivated locations are measured facts about the current condition of this deck: the carbonation front has reached rebar at those points. The 32-year remaining service life is a different kind of claim — it is the output of the Tuutti model applied to the measured 21 mm mean depth and 35-year age, not a measurement. The model assumes carbonation continues to propagate as D = k√t (square-root-of-time relationship), that k is constant over time, and that no change in exposure conditions or concrete properties occurs. It is a theoretical projection, not a measured property of the deck.

The appropriate action differs by claim type: the three depassivated cores are a present condition requiring immediate risk assessment. The 32-year estimate is a planning tool that should be used with explicit awareness that it is a model projection contingent on the assumed propagation law holding at depths above 21 mm — an assumption that has no direct measurement support from this inspection. Carbonation typically slows above the carbonated depth due to reduced CO₂ diffusivity in the deeper pore structure, which would make the 32-year estimate conservative, but this is a theoretical expectation, not something measured here.
