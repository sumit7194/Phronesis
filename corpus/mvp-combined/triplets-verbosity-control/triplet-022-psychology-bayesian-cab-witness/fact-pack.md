# Factual substrate

In a city where 85% of cabs are green and 15% are blue, a witness who is 80% accurate in distinguishing the two colors identifies a hit-and-run cab as blue. Bayes' rule gives P(blue | "blue") = (0.80 × 0.15) / (0.80 × 0.15 + 0.20 × 0.85) = 0.12 / (0.12 + 0.17) = 0.12 / 0.29 ≈ 0.41, or roughly 41%. Most respondents intuitively answer near 80%, neglecting the 85/15 base rate.

# Topic / question being answered

"In the cab-witness problem with an 80%-accurate witness and 15% blue cabs, what is the probability the cab was actually blue given a blue identification, and why is the intuitive answer often wrong?"
