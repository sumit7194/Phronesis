The mid-span gauges show a 5-year drift of 41 microstrain at reference temperature, about 8 microstrain per year. The manufacturer drift spec is 5 microstrain per year. The FE model predicts no creep over the design horizon. On their face, those numbers leave a small but real residual that could be either gauge drift slightly above spec or a small structural change.

The weakest link in the chain that maps "41 microstrain" to "real structural change" is the temperature-compensation polynomial. The whole reference-temperature comparison method depends on subtracting the seasonal 280 microstrain swing cleanly, and the compensation polynomial was fit on year 1 and has not been refit since. If the polynomial itself has drifted by even a few percent over 5 years, the residual at "reference temperature" is no longer a clean comparison; it is the structural response plus a slowly biased thermal residual.

A 5% bias in the compensation across the 280 microstrain seasonal swing is 14 microstrain, which is more than enough to move the apparent trend from above the drift spec to within it, or vice versa. The conclusion about whether the bridge is changing depends roughly proportionally on how confident we are in the polynomial, and there is no internal check on it in the present data.

So my read is that the 41 microstrain is consistent with several scenarios depending on the compensation fit, and the appropriate next step is a temporary co-located gauge cross-check that rederives the polynomial against fresh data. Without that, calling the trend either drift or damage rests on an assumption that has not been independently verified across the 5-year window.
