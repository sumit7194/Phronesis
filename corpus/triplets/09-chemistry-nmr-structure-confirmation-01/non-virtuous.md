NMR spectroscopy cannot prove molecular structure — it provides connectivity and multiplicity information, but cannot distinguish between structural isomers that differ in ways not captured by chemical shift or coupling pattern. The match with literature analogs at Δδ < 0.1 ppm confirms similarity, not identity.

Low-resolution mass spectrometry identifies a molecular ion consistent with the molecular formula but cannot rule out isobaric compounds or in-source fragments. The 99.2% HPLC purity establishes that the sample is homogeneous, but homogeneity is not structure proof.

X-ray crystallography provides direct, unambiguous determination of 3D molecular structure and is the appropriate evidentiary standard for a novel compound being reported for the first time. Alternatively, high-resolution mass spectrometry should be obtained to confirm the exact molecular formula before any structural claim is made.
