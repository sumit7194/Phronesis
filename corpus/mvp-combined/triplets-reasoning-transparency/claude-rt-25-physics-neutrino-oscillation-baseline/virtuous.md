Pooled reactor short-baseline disappearance is approximately 6% with about 4% combined uncertainty, global fits with this input prefer sin^2(2 theta) = 0.10 at Delta m^2 = 1.3 eV^2, and direct sterile searches at IsoDAR-class baselines set limits below sin^2(2 theta) = 0.04 in the same region.

The weakest link in the chain that maps "6% disappearance" to "sterile-neutrino mixing" is the reactor flux prediction. The 6% is not a measured disappearance in the model-independent sense; it is the difference between measured count rates and a theoretical prediction of expected count rates, and the prediction relies on summing thousands of beta-decay branches with imperfect knowledge of fission-fragment branching ratios and forbidden-decay shapes. The whole inference from "fewer events than predicted" to "neutrinos oscillating into a sterile state" rests on the prediction being trustworthy at the percent level.

Recent flux re-evaluations have shifted the prediction down by 2-3% at the relevant energies, which absorbs roughly half the anomaly. The remaining 3% residual is comparable to the residual flux-prediction uncertainty after the re-evaluation, so the direct experimental quantity is consistent with a flux issue rather than a sterile signal. The chain breaks at the prediction step: if subsequent flux work absorbs the remaining 3%, the anomaly disappears entirely; if not, a real signal remains.

The direct sterile-search limits below 0.04 in the same Delta m^2 region are model-independent and are in tension with sterile fits to the reactor data only if the flux explanation fails. So the load-bearing element is the reactor flux normalization, and the sterile-neutrino interpretation is conditional on the prediction not absorbing the residual. Reported as such, the present global picture neither supports nor excludes sterile neutrinos, with the answer turning entirely on the next round of flux work.
