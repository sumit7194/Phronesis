A data center uninterruptible power supply (UPS) installation uses three parallel units in an N+1 configuration; two units are required to support the full load, with one unit as spare. Each unit has a manufacturer-published mean time between failures (MTBF) of 120,000 hours and mean time to repair (MTTR) of 8 hours.

Individual unit availability is A = 0.999933 based on the MTBF and MTTR figures. For the N+1 configuration with statistically independent failures, the calculated system availability is approximately 0.999999993 (approximately six nines). The facility availability target is 99.999% (five nines), corresponding to a maximum of approximately 5.26 minutes of downtime per year. The six-nines result under independent failure assumptions substantially exceeds this target.

Common cause failure (CCF) analysis using the beta factor model identifies the following exposure modes: a shared electrical panel (mitigated by a two-panel configuration), a shared cooling unit (cooling redundancy was installed in year one), and identical firmware on all three units (subject to simultaneous failure during updates; a staggered firmware update policy is in effect). Applying a beta factor of β = 0.02 for residual CCF probability — a conservative value for this unit class given the mitigations — yields a CCF failure rate of λ_CCF = β / MTBF = 1.67 × 10⁻⁷ per hour. Combined system unavailability including both independent and CCF modes gives a total system availability of approximately 99.99987%.
