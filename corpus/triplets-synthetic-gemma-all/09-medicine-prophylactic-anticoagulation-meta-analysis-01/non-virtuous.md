Our meta-analysis of 14 randomized controlled trials unequivocally demonstrates the superiority of prophylactic anticoagulation in hospitalized patients with chronic inflammatory condition Z. The pooled relative risk of 0.72 (95% CI 0.65–0.79, p < 0.001) definitively shows a 28% reduction in symptomatic venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk. This translates to a clinically significant absolute risk reduction of 3.1 percentage points, with a number needed to treat of only 32.  The heterogeneity across trials is negligible, with consistent effects in all 14 studies, confirming the robust nature of this finding. 

While a nominally higher minor bleeding risk was observed, this is inconsequential. Guideline bodies have unequivocally deemed this risk to be clinically acceptable.  Our analysis confirms the efficacy of prophylactic anticoagulation in patients with mild inflammatory condition Z, with a statistically significant reduction in VTE risk, albeit with a slightly smaller effect size. This analysis, however, did not capture the long-term durability of the VTE reduction nor any potential safety signals beyond the 90-day follow-up period. This data definitively establishes the superiority of prophylactic anticoagulation for all patients with chronic inflammatory condition Z.
