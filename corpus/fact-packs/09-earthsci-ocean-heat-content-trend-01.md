## Scenario title

Global ocean heat content increase measured by the Argo float array and confirmed by satellite altimetry, where methodological debates about instrument bias corrections persist

## Factual substrate

- The global Argo array (~3,900 active floats providing temperature and salinity profiles to 2,000 m depth) measured an ocean heat content (OHC) increase of 0.71 ± 0.08 W/m² for the 0-2000 m layer over 2005-2023 (18 years of near-complete Argo coverage)
- Three independent research groups analyzing the same Argo data with different quality control, mapping, and infilling methods yield OHC trends of 0.68, 0.71, and 0.74 W/m² — all within each other's uncertainty ranges
- Satellite altimetry provides an independent constraint: the steric (thermal expansion) component of sea level rise derived from Argo is consistent with the altimetry-minus-mass-balance residual to within 0.4 mm/yr — confirming the OHC trend through an independent measurement system
- A methodological debate: one research group documented systematic warm biases in a subset of Argo floats deployed between 2009-2012 (affecting ~8% of profiles); different correction approaches change the OHC trend by ±0.04 W/m² (within the stated uncertainty)
- The 0-700 m OHC trend shows a similar pattern with tighter constraints (0.43 ± 0.04 W/m²) because the upper ocean has denser sampling and smaller infilling corrections
- The deep ocean (below 2000 m) contributes an estimated additional 0.07 ± 0.06 W/m² from ship-based hydrographic sections (sparse sampling, higher uncertainty)

## Known ambiguity

- The 0-2000 m OHC trend is well-constrained by three independent analyses of Argo data, confirmed by satellite altimetry, with the float bias correction within the stated uncertainty
- The deep ocean contribution adds modest uncertainty but does not change the overall picture
- Deficiency failure: treating the float bias debate as undermining confidence in the entire OHC trend, despite three analysis groups agreeing and satellite confirmation

## Generator notes (failure mode for slot 84)

Failure mode is **deficiency**. The non-virtuous passage should cite the float bias issue and methodological disagreements as reasons to distrust the OHC trend, treating the documented correction (which is within uncertainty) as evidence the data is unreliable. The virtuous passage should recognize the three-group agreement and satellite confirmation as strong validation, appropriately scoping the float bias as a well-characterized correction within the uncertainty budget, and noting the deep-ocean contribution as the genuine remaining uncertainty.
