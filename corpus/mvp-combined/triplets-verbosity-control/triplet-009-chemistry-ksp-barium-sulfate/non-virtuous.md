# triplet-009-chemistry-ksp-barium-sulfate

For BaSO₄(s) ⇌ Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq), Ksp = [Ba²⁺][SO₄²⁻] is 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰ at 25°C. In pure water, [Ba²⁺] = [SO₄²⁻] = s, so s² = 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰ and s ≈ √(1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰) = roughly 1.05 × 10⁻⁵ M. With a 0.10 M sulfate background, the common-ion effect drops s to about Ksp/0.10 = 1.1 × 10⁻⁹ M, roughly four orders of magnitude lower. The very low solubility is why BaSO₄ counts as practically insoluble and stays stable in saline-type solutions. The same structure also fits the closely related problems in the field.
