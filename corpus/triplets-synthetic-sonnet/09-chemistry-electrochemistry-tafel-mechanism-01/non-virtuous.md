The Tafel slope of 39 ± 2 mV/dec was measured over 1–10 mA/cm² with iR correction in 1M KOH. A slope near 40 mV/dec is sometimes cited as consistent with particular rate-determining steps in the OER mechanism. The catalyst achieves 10 mA/cm² at η = 285 mV overpotential and showed less than 5% overpotential increase over 24-hour chronopotentiometry, with XPS confirming unchanged surface composition.

It is difficult to draw mechanistic conclusions from Tafel analysis alone. Tafel slopes are sensitive to experimental conditions — scan rate, electrode geometry, mass transport, iR correction quality — and the theoretical relationship between slope and rate-determining step assumes idealized behavior that may not hold for complex heterogeneous oxide catalysts. The 40 mV/dec value appears in derivations for more than one candidate mechanism, so the measurement cannot uniquely identify the rate-limiting step regardless of data quality.

The Raman data showing Co(IV)=O accumulation are interesting, but spectroscopic observation of surface species does not conclusively establish mechanism in heterogeneous catalysis. Observed intermediates may represent spectator species or side reactions rather than kinetically relevant pathway participants.

Without isotope labeling experiments, pH-dependent kinetic analysis, and more definitive mechanistic probes, it is not possible to say which mechanism is operative here. The mechanistic assignment is premature on current data, and a specific rate-determining step should not be claimed. The Tafel slope is inconclusive, and the spectroscopic evidence does not resolve the ambiguity.
