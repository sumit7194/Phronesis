The K1-knockout line provides solid evidence that this highly-conserved kinase is dispensable for basic cell viability in HEK293T cells. The lack of a growth phenotype in standard culture is a genuine observation, supported by the normal morphology and the robust 97% growth rate relative to wild-type cells over a 7-day period.

However, interpreting this single observation as general dispensability would be overstating the evidence. The significant 2.4-fold upregulation of the paralogous gene K2 is direct evidence of transcriptional genetic compensation, which plausibly masks the loss of K1. Because K1 has an established role in the DNA damage response for yeast, the lack of viability testing under stress conditions—such as serum starvation, hypoxia, or drug challenge—represents a critical remaining limitation. My working view is that K1 dispensability is well-established strictly for standard culture conditions, but we cannot confidently determine its essentiality without assessing those other variables. Relying solely on the standard-culture viability data fails to reflect the complexity introduced by the K2 upregulation and the likelihood of condition-dependent essentiality entirely. Additional targeted research is needed to determine the gene's true role in varied cellular stress states. The compensation indicates the kinase pathway's overall functional importance is likely maintained by the cell.
