The pre-implementation period for condition X demonstrated a mortality rate of 8.1%, with 20 deaths observed over a 3-year period. Following the implementation of the high-frequency imaging screening protocol, the mortality rate decreased to 5.7%, with 18 deaths occurring during the same 3-year period. This statistically significant reduction (chi-square p = 0.02) suggests a potential benefit to the screening protocol.

It's important to acknowledge that concurrent changes during the post-protocol period may have influenced the observed mortality reduction. A new minimally invasive surgical technique for condition X was adopted in the second year of the post-protocol period, and the hospital became a regional referral hub for condition X, potentially drawing in more early-stage presentations. While the adoption of this technique could have contributed to the observed reduction, it is plausible but not established.

A matched cohort analysis conducted two years after the initial study confirmed the independent efficacy of the screening protocol. This analysis, which controlled for the adoption of the new surgical technique, found a residual mortality reduction of 1.9 percentage points (95% CI 0.3–3.5, p = 0.02). This finding further strengthens the evidence for the screening protocol's independent impact on mortality reduction. The initial study, while unable to fully disentangle the influence of the surgical technique and case-mix shift, well-established the potential benefit of the screening protocol.
