I was asked for the make and model of the espresso machine in service at Café Sperl in Vienna in 2015, and the year the cafe acquired it. I should think about what I actually know about Viennese coffee houses and their equipment before answering.

Café Sperl, on Gumpendorfer Strasse in the sixth district of Vienna, is one of the historic Viennese kaffeehäuser dating from the 1880s, in continuous operation through the twentieth century apart from war-related closures, and known for its preserved late-nineteenth-century interior. Like most traditional Viennese cafés it serves a wide range of espresso-derived drinks alongside the long-pour drinks that dominate the local style. The professional espresso-machine market is dominated by a small number of Italian manufacturers — La Marzocco, Faema, San Remo, Nuova Simonelli, La Cimbali, Rancilio — whose machines are present in cafés worldwide.

The specific make and model of the espresso machine installed at one specific Viennese café in one specific year, plus the year of acquisition, is the kind of granular operational detail that I cannot reliably retrieve from training-knowledge. Café equipment-purchase records are private commercial information held by the café's owners and the equipment supplier, and reach the public record only incidentally — for example through equipment-trade-publication features, photographs in Vienna café guides that happen to show the machine, or inventory disclosures during a refurbishment. Without a specific journalistic feature or photograph I can call to mind, naming a make-and-model and an acquisition year would be inventing both. I have no recall of a specific feature that would give me this information for Café Sperl in 2015. The right response is to flag the gap and direct the asker to Café Sperl directly, or to the Vienna café-equipment trade press.
