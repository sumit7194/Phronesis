The simulation produces an internally valid result: a pre-open intermediate is a stable feature of the conformational landscape under the CHARMM36m force field, populated for 12% of the simulation time with a well-defined geometry. The 4.2 kcal/mol free energy barrier gives a plausible qualitative explanation for the sigmoidal conductance-voltage relationship. As a hypothesis-generating result, this is useful work.

What the simulation cannot establish is whether the pre-open intermediate exists in the actual channel. MD simulations capture conformational behavior as defined by the force field, the lipid composition, and the simulation temperature — not as directly observed in the biological system. Force field artifacts are documented to influence predicted conformational equilibria, and a 10-μs simulation reaches only a fraction of the biological timescale for gating (ms to s). The qualitative match to the sigmoidal conductance curve encourages the hypothesis but does not validate the specific geometry or the existence of the intermediate as a distinct state in the real channel.

The pre-open state prediction is testable: cryo-EM of the channel under voltage-clamp conditions designed to trap the intermediate, site-directed fluorescence at the predicted VSD-gate interface, or disulfide crosslinking between residues at the predicted contact would either confirm or refute it. That is the appropriate next step. The simulation result justifies designing those experiments, not asserting the mechanism.
