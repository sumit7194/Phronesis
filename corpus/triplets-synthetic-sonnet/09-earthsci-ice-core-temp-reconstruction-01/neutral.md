An Antarctic ice core provides a 120,000-year temperature reconstruction using δ¹⁸O of ice as the primary proxy. The calibration uses 40 sites with modern instrumental temperature data from 1950 to 2000, yielding a calibration slope of 0.67 ‰/°C with r² = 0.91 and a residual standard error of 0.8°C. Three independent cross-validation methods were applied: borehole temperature diffusion profiles at six Antarctic sites, which show Last Glacial Maximum cooling of 9–11°C; δD measurements on the same ice (r² = 0.97 for the δ¹⁸O–δD relationship); and accumulation rate changes consistent with temperature-dependent precipitation rates.

The reconstruction identifies: Last Interglacial peak temperature +3.2 ± 1.1°C above preindustrial; Last Glacial Maximum cooling of −9.8 ± 1.3°C below preindustrial; and a Holocene thermal maximum (approximately 8,000 years ago) of +1.7 ± 0.9°C above preindustrial.

Known complications in δ¹⁸O proxies — moisture source region variability, seasonal weighting shifts, and precipitation seasonality changes — have been evaluated as part of the calibration residual analysis. At sites where moisture source and seasonality are better characterized, these factors contribute less than 0.5°C to the residual error, meaning the reported ±1.1–1.3°C uncertainty ranges already incorporate the dominant sources of proxy uncertainty. The reconstruction characterizes central Antarctic temperature change and is not directly applicable to global mean temperature without regional scaling.
