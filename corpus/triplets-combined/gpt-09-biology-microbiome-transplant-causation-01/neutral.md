The transplant experiment shows a reproducible phenotype transfer from donor microbiomes to germ-free recipients under controlled diet conditions. Germ-free mice (n = 12 per group) were colonized with fecal microbiota from obese (ob/ob) versus lean donors and then fed identical standard chow ad libitum for 14 days. Across three independent experiments, recipients of the obese-donor microbiota accumulated 47% more body fat than recipients of the lean-donor microbiota (p < 0.001). Metagenomics confirmed that recipients acquired donor-like communities, including a higher Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio in the obese-donor group, and SCFA metabolite profiles matched donors within 7 days. The obese-donor recipients also showed 2.1% higher caloric extraction efficiency from the same diet by bomb calorimetry.

These observations support the conclusion that the transplanted microbial community can be sufficient to shift host energy balance and adiposity in this germ-free mouse context. At the same time, the experiment does not identify a single causal organism or mechanism: the transplant moves an entire community plus associated metabolites, bacteriophages, and potentially residual host factors. It also lacks a reverse or perturbation design (for example, lean donors pre-treated with an obese microbiome) that would help triangulate directionality and specificity.

A calibrated interpretation is that the microbiome, at a community level, contributes causally to increased adiposity in this model with strong evidence for the effect size, while finer-grained claims about which microbes or pathways are responsible remain uncertain and require follow-up with defined communities or reverse experiments.
