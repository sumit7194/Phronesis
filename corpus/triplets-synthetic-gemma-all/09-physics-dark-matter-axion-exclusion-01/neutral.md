Our haloscope experiment has achieved a significant result. We observed no signal in the 3.3–4.2 μeV mass range, resulting in a 95% confidence limit for the axion-photon coupling constant, g_aγγ, at 3–5 times the DFSZ model prediction. This limits the DFSZ model's parameter space in this specific region.  

However, it is important to note that this exclusion is limited by the sensitivity of our experiment. Our target mass range is approximately 0.07% of the theoretically viable range for axion dark matter, which spans from 1 μeV to 1 meV. Furthermore, our analysis relied on the assumption of a standard local dark matter density and velocity distribution, which may have introduced uncertainty into our sensitivity estimates. 

The virtuous-wrong analysis correctly interpreted the null result as providing constraints on DFSZ axions in this mass range.  This conclusion was based on the available data and analysis techniques.  However, a critical factor was overlooked: the local dark matter density near the experiment was lower than the standard assumed value. This finding suggests our sensitivity limits were overestimated, and the true constraints on the axion coupling are weaker than previously reported.  Despite this, our findings provide valuable information about axion dark matter.
