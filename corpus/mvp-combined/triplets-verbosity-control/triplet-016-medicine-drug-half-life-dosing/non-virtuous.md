# triplet-016-medicine-drug-half-life-dosing

For a drug with first-order elimination and a half-life of 8 hours, plasma concentrations approach steady state asymptotically: about 50% after one half-life, 75% after two, 87.5% after three, 93.75% after four, and 96.9% after five. Five half-lives, or about 40 hours, is the textbook threshold for steady state. A dosing interval near one half-life keeps peak-to-trough fluctuation within approximately a factor of two. Shorter intervals reduce fluctuation but add doses; longer intervals widen fluctuation. A loading dose can skip the 40-hour wait by pushing the plasma to steady state immediately. The same structure also fits the closely related problems in the field (which is approximately the standard result).
