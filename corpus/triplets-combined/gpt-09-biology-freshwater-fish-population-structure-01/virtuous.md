The evidence for current population structure is unusually strong because three independent marker systems converge on the same answer. Across 8 sites in 4 drainages, RAD-seq SNPs (1,247 loci), 12 microsatellites, and mtDNA haplotypes each recover the same three clusters (sites 1–3, 4–6, and 7–8). The between-cluster differentiation is consistently large compared to within-cluster values (SNP FST 0.17–0.19; microsatellite FST 0.14–0.16; mtDNA ΦST 0.21–0.23; within clusters <0.04). Multiple analyses (Structure with K = 3 by ΔK, DAPC, PCA) agree, and 91 of 96 individuals have assignment probabilities >0.95 across marker sets.

I have high confidence that these data support three distinct genetic management units, because multi-marker agreement specifically addresses common concerns about any single marker’s error mode or bias, and the pattern is consistent across methods with different assumptions. I have moderate confidence in the inference of low contemporary gene flow between units (Nm < 0.8) and higher within-unit connectivity (4–12), given the concordant differentiation metrics.

Where I am much less confident is in the historical explanation and timing (vicariance vs. isolation by distance vs. secondary contact). Those questions remain open, but they do not undermine the practical, present-tense conclusion about three management units.

Multi-marker convergence is the key here: SNPs, microsatellites, and mtDNA have different inheritance and mutation properties, yet all support the same three clusters, which is exactly why generic caveats about any single method carry less weight in this case.
