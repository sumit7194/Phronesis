The nail penetration tests demonstrate that the 50 Ah NMC811 cells meet the UL 2580 criteria for individual cell venting.  **Eight out of ten cells passed without flame**, with two passing with a brief flame that extinguished within five seconds.  The maximum cell surface temperature reached 680°C, and no explosions occurred. 

These results **strongly support** the cell-level thermal runaway characteristics, including a peak heat generation rate of 42 kW per cell, a total energy release of 1.8 MJ per cell, and an onset-to-peak time of 12 seconds.  **The thermal simulation, which utilizes a well-established constant heat transfer coefficient of 25 W/m²K, predicts sufficient energy absorption by the phase-change material to prevent propagation to neighboring cells.**

While this simulation uses a constant heat transfer coefficient derived from natural convection correlations, **it is plausible but not established** that the real-world scenario may involve a different convective environment, potentially impacting heat transfer. 

**Pack-level propagation data from a similar cell chemistry and pack design demonstrates propagation in three out of five tests with a cell spacing less than 5 mm.** This highlights the importance of pack-level testing, even if individual cell tests demonstrate safety. 

**Therefore, the cell-level pass, while promising, warrants careful consideration in the context of pack-level propagation potential and the uncertainties associated with the thermal simulation.**
