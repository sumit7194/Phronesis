# triplet-016-medicine-drug-half-life-dosing

For a drug with first-order elimination, the plasma concentration falls to half its starting value over each successive half-life. With a half-life of approximately 8 hours, the fraction remaining is 0.5 after one half-life, 0.25 after two, 0.125 after three, and so on. Under regular dosing, the same first-order kinetics govern the approach to steady state, and that approach is asymptotic.

After one half-life of regular dosing, the plasma sits at roughly 50% of steady state; the figures rise to 75%, 87.5%, 93.75%, and approximately 96.9% over the next four half-lives. Five half-lives is the textbook threshold, so with t½ = 8 hours, steady state is reached at about 40 hours. A dosing interval near one half-life keeps peak-to-trough fluctuation within roughly a factor of two; shorter intervals reduce fluctuation at the cost of more frequent dosing, and longer intervals widen the fluctuation, sometimes enough to let troughs fall below the therapeutic concentration. A loading dose can shortcut the 40-hour wait by pushing the plasma to the steady-state level immediately. The structure of this analysis approximately matches the standard textbook treatment, and the logic applies to neighboring problems where the input quantities are generally analogous.
