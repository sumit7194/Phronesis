## Scenario title

Weld inspection in a pressure vessel: comparing TOFD indications to radiographic testing and fitness-for-service characterization

## Factual substrate

- A seam weld in a high-pressure steam vessel was inspected by both time-of-flight diffraction (TOFD) and radiographic testing (RT) as part of a periodic integrity assessment.
- TOFD detected four indications in the weld: two at the cap/root interface (depths 2.1 mm and 3.4 mm from the outer surface), one mid-wall (12.2 mm), and one near the inner surface (22.8 mm). All four were characterized as planar or linear indications.
- RT detected two indications in the same weld region: a porosity cluster approximately 8 mm from the outer surface and an elongated indication at mid-wall consistent with the TOFD mid-wall indication.
- TOFD sizing for the mid-wall indication: through-wall height 4.3 mm, length 31 mm. RT gave no reliable sizing for this indication — conventional RT is not a sizing technique for planar flaws.
- Fitness-for-service assessment per the applicable fracture mechanics standard requires flaw height and length as primary inputs; it does not require flaw type characterization (slag, lack of fusion) as a precondition to the calculation.
- The two cap/root indications were not detected by RT.

## Notes

EG-c contrast: The virtuous passage identifies which evidence type is appropriate to which claim — TOFD provides the flaw dimensions needed for FFS; RT provides a second independent detection but cannot provide sizing. The excess failure insists that definitive flaw characterization (flaw type: slag, LOF, crack) is required before FFS can be calculated, when in fact the applicable standard requires only dimensions. This adds an evidentiary requirement not mandated by the assessment framework.
