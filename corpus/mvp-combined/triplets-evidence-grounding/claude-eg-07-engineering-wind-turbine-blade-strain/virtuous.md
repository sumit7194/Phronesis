Four specific anchors point at T2 over the 9-month window. The fibre-Bragg strain sensors at the T2 blade root logged a 12% rise in mean flap-wise strain at rated wind compared to commissioning, while T1 and T3 sit within 1.5% drift; that 1.5% T1/T3 figure anchors the comparison so the 12% rise is not just normal aging. The vibration spectrum on T2 shows a new peak at 1.4 Hz absent at commissioning and absent on T1 and T3; that is the second specific observation. The month-8 borescope found a 0.6 m chord-wise crack at 18 m radial near the trailing edge; that is a directly visualised structural feature. SCADA logging of pitch-actuator response shows 70 ms on T2 vs 40 ms on T1 and T3.

The 12% strain rise and the 1.4 Hz peak are both effects observed on T2 only. They do not by themselves identify the crack as the cause: the 4% wake exposure difference and the 30 ms actuator-delay difference could each contribute. What the borescope adds is a specific, located, sized structural anomaly on the same turbine; that is the only directly observed structural change in the dataset.

The crack-driven interpretation rests on three converging anchors — FBG, spectrum, borescope — all on T2 and all absent on T1 and T3. The 4% wake exposure difference is too small in normal fatigue scaling to produce a 12% strain rise on its own, which is a quantitative argument against wake exposure being the dominant driver. The actuator-delay observation could plausibly contribute to the low-frequency spectral content but does not explain the borescope crack or the magnitude of the strain rise.

The strongest-grounded conclusion is that the 0.6 m crack is the dominant driver, with the actuator delay as a separate fault to address.
