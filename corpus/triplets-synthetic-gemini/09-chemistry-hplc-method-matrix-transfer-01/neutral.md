An HPLC-UV method was developed to quantify compound W and fully validated using an aqueous phosphate buffer matrix. The validation dataset demonstrated excellent linearity with an R² of 0.9997 across a concentration range of 5–500 ng/mL. Method precision was tight, displaying an intra-day CV below 2.1% and an inter-day CV under 3.8%. Accuracy ranged between 98% and 103% when assessed across three distinct concentration bounds. 

This validated method was directly transferred to evaluate human plasma samples obtained from a pharmacokinetic study. The human plasma samples were processed using a straightforward protein precipitation step involving three volumes of acetonitrile prior to injection into the instrument. However, explicit extraction recovery parameters for this precipitation step were not quantified. Furthermore, the protocol proceeded without a specific evaluation of plasma matrix effects, meaning post-column infusion assessments and extracted matrix standard calibrations were bypassed. The methodology was applied to evaluate five specific plasma samples drawn from three subjects. The derived pharmacokinetic parameters, specifically Cmax and AUC, were calculated and reported alongside the original quantitative confidence bounds established during the initial aqueous phosphate buffer validation phase. These operational parameters outline the analytical workflow spanning the initial calibration environment to the applied biological testing.
