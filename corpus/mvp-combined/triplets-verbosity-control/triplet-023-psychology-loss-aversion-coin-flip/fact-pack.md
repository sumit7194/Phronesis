# Factual substrate

A coin-flip gamble that pays $200 on heads and loses $100 on tails has expected value +$50. Most participants in laboratory studies reject the gamble despite the positive EV, a pattern attributed to loss aversion: the loss-aversion coefficient λ is empirically near 2.25 in Tversky-Kahneman's prospect-theory work, meaning losses are felt roughly twice as strongly as gains of the same nominal size.

# Topic / question being answered

"Why do most people reject a 50/50 coin flip that pays $200 on heads and loses $100 on tails, despite its positive expected value?"
