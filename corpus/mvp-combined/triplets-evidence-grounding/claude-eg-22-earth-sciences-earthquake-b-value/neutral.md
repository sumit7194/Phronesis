The regional catalogue covering 60 × 60 km of transform-fault segment includes 12,400 events of M ≥ 1.5 over 18 years. The Gutenberg-Richter b-value, computed in 2-year sliding windows, dropped from a long-term mean of 0.96 to 0.74 in the 18 months before the M 6.1 mainshock. The 0.22 drop has a bootstrap 95% CI of 0.13–0.31 against the long-term distribution.

A separate stress-tensor inversion from focal-mechanism solutions of 220 background events during the same window indicates a 0.4 MPa increase in differential stress on the principal slip plane. Laboratory rate-and-state friction theory predicts b-value reductions in the 0.1–0.3 range when differential stress increases by ~0.3–0.5 MPa, which brackets both the observed drop and the inverted stress increase.

A complication: the magnitude of completeness for the catalogue was Mc = 1.7 before a station upgrade in year 14 and Mc = 1.5 afterward. The mainshock occurs 4 years after the station upgrade. Lower Mc adds smaller events to the catalogue, which can bias b-values downward when the change in detection threshold is not handled carefully.

The picture across the empirical and theoretical pieces is broadly consistent. The empirical b-value drop, the inverted stress increase, and the theoretically expected b-value response from rate-and-state are all in the same direction and roughly the same size.

The reading is that the b-value drop is plausibly a real signal of stress accumulation prior to the mainshock, with the laboratory friction model providing the mechanistic story. The Mc shift is a known catalogue artifact that may account for some of the apparent drop, and a fully careful analysis would re-compute b-values on a uniform-Mc subset before treating the 0.22 drop as fully tectonic in origin.
