This meta-analysis, encompassing data from nine randomized controlled trials, definitively establishes the robust efficacy of MBCT in preventing depressive relapse. A total of 1,258 participants were enrolled, with a 625-person MBCT group and a 633-person control group. The primary outcome, depressive relapse or recurrence at 60 weeks, was measured with a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.51 (95% CI: 0.42–0.62). This translates to a 18 percentage point absolute risk reduction, demonstrating a clear and significant advantage for the MBCT group. 

The effect was demonstrably stronger in patients with ≥ 5 prior episodes, with an HR of 0.44, compared to those with 3 episodes (HR = 0.58).  These findings are not merely suggestive; they definitively show the superiority of MBCT in preventing relapse. This meta-analysis, with its low risk of bias and complete absence of publication bias, provides irrefutable clinical evidence for MBCT's efficacy.

The findings are not only promising for patients with ≥ 3 prior depressive episodes but are also a clear indication of MBCT's potential for other populations.  The IPD approach and the substantial magnitude of the absolute risk reduction (18 percentage points) should not be disregarded.  The methodological challenges of mindfulness research are insignificant when compared to the undeniable clinical benefit of MBCT.
