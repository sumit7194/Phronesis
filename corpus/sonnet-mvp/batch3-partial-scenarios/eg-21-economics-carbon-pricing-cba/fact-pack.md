## Scenario title

Cost-benefit assessment of a national carbon pricing scheme using meta-analytic review of abatement cost estimates

## Factual substrate

- A government policy team synthesises 42 peer-reviewed abatement cost estimates for a proposed carbon price of 80 USD/tonne CO₂ equivalent.
- The meta-analytic mean marginal abatement cost across the 42 studies is 61 USD/tonne (95% CI: 44–78 USD/tonne).
- The 42 studies use four distinct modelling frameworks: computable general equilibrium (CGE), partial equilibrium, integrated assessment, and engineering bottom-up; the estimates diverge by framework, with engineering bottom-up estimates averaging 38 USD/tonne and CGE estimates averaging 82 USD/tonne.
- The proposed carbon price of 80 USD/tonne exceeds the meta-analytic mean by 31% but falls inside the CI upper bound.
- A sensitivity analysis excluding the five outlier studies (engineering estimates from one single industry group) narrows the CI to 51–76 USD/tonne; at 80 USD/tonne, the proposed price is then above the adjusted CI upper bound.
- The meta-analysis uses a random-effects model; heterogeneity I² = 72%, indicating substantial between-study variance.

## Known ambiguity

- High heterogeneity (I² = 72%) means the meta-analytic mean is not well-supported as a single-point estimate for policy; it reflects model-framework disagreement, not just sampling variation.
- The carbon price target (80 USD/tonne) falls inside the overall CI but outside the sensitivity-adjusted CI — the appropriate comparison depends on whether the outlier studies are excluded.

## Conclusion space

- Conclusion A (virtuous-compatible): The 80 USD/tonne target exceeds the meta-analytic central estimate, and the high heterogeneity prevents treating any single estimate as authoritative; the policy cost is above-median but arguably within the plausible range conditional on model-framework choice.
- Conclusion B (excess-failure-compatible): Every empirical claim about abatement costs is burdened with layered citations to each of the four model frameworks, the I² statistic, the random-effects model specification, and the sensitivity analysis outlier exclusion criteria — producing a bureaucratically qualified passage where the practical assessment is buried.
- Conclusion C (deficiency-failure-compatible): Asserts the 80 USD/tonne target is cost-effective based on the meta-analytic mean, without specifying the evidence type (meta-analysis) or engaging the heterogeneity.
