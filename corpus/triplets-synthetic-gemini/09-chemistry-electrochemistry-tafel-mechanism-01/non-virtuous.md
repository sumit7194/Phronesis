It is practically impossible to determine the true reaction mechanism for this cobalt-iron oxyhydroxide catalyst from these experiments. The authors point to a Tafel slope of 39 ± 2 mV/dec, but electrochemists know that Tafel slopes are notoriously unreliable for explicit mechanism determination. Since a 40 mV/dec slope can mathematically map onto multiple completely different rate-limiting steps, including both electron transfer and chemical bond formation, the entire kinetic argument is completely compromised.

Furthermore, trying to salvage the mechanism using in-situ Raman spectroscopy is unconvincing. They mention finding a Co(IV)=O intermediate, but spectrographic snapshots during complex OER conditions are typically messy, and the fact that they completely missed any iron intermediates proves the Raman setup is likely flawed or insensitive. Because classical Tafel derivations are fundamentally untrustworthy on rough nanoparticle surfaces, we simply cannot definitively assign any mechanism from this data. The entire attempt to explain the kinetics is just guesswork. We cannot draw any real conclusions about how this catalyst actually evolves oxygen, and we must wait for far more advanced techniques before pretending we understand the true molecular pathways happening at the electrode surface. Until such evidence is available, the mechanistic details of this 285 mV overpotential system remain completely unresolved.
