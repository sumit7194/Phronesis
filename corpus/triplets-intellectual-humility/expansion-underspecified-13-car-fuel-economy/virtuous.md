A user asked: "What is the car's fuel economy?" I need to think about what determines fuel economy and what the question leaves unspecified.

Fuel economy, expressed in miles per gallon or litres per 100 kilometres, depends on the make and model of the vehicle, the engine and drivetrain, the year of manufacture, the test cycle (city, highway, combined, EPA, WLTP, NEDC), the load and tyre condition, the driving style, the ambient temperature, and the type of fuel used. Different test cycles can give materially different figures for the same car: a vehicle rated 7 L/100 km on the older NEDC may rate 8 to 9 L/100 km on the more realistic WLTP cycle, and the EPA combined figure will sit in its own range. Real-world fuel economy then deviates from any test cycle by ten to thirty percent depending on driving conditions.

The question gives nothing of these. It names "the car" without identifying make, model, year, drivetrain, or measurement standard. Fuel economy across the population of cars on the road today ranges from around 4 L/100 km (a small-engined hybrid hatchback at the efficient end) to over 25 L/100 km (a heavy pickup or large performance vehicle at the inefficient end), a span of more than a factor of six. No single number covers that span.

The right response is to name the missing variables and ask for them. The structural form is fuel-used per distance, with the value depending on the vehicle, the test cycle, and the conditions. The closest structural fact I can offer is that current new-car average combined fuel economy in the EU sits in the high single-digit L/100 km range and in the US sits in the mid-twenties of MPG. Producing a specific figure from invented vehicle and cycle parameters would mean fabricating the load-bearing inputs of the question. I will ask for the make, model, year, and the measurement standard the asker has in mind.
