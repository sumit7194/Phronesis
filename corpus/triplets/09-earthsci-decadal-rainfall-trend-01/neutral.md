A regional climate analysis examined wet-season (March–May) rainfall records from a network of 47 rain gauges across a semiarid region of East Africa, spanning 2009 to 2023. The 15-year network mean shows a decline of −8.4 mm/decade (p = 0.03, linear regression); the 2009–2023 seasonal mean of 312 mm is approximately 35 mm (10%) below the 1960–2000 reference climatology of 347 mm/season. A regional assessment report attributes the decline to climate change and characterizes the region as having entered a new, drier climatological state. The Interdecadal Pacific Oscillation (IPO) and Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) are documented drivers of multi-decadal rainfall variability in East Africa; the 2009–2023 period coincides with a predominantly negative IPO phase, which published studies link to a 15–30 mm/season reduction in March–May rainfall in this region. Paleoclimate reconstructions from lake sediment cores covering 500 years document at least two prior multi-decadal drought episodes in the region with rainfall deficits comparable to the current anomaly; both occurred in pre-industrial periods without anthropogenic forcing. Station density varies across the region with several sub-regions having fewer than three gauges; satellite-derived rainfall estimates (CHIRPS product) for the same period show a qualitatively consistent pattern but with greater spatial heterogeneity than the gauge network captures. The IPO phase index was predominantly negative from 2008 to 2021, transitioning to neutral through 2023; IOD events occurred in 2019 (positive, Indian Ocean warming) and 2022 (negative), producing opposing anomalies that partially offset within the 15-year average.
