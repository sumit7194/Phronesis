The landslide susceptibility model provides a reliable basis for identifying which slopes are at risk of failure during the approaching storm. With a validation AUC of 0.87 and an overall accuracy of 82%, the model demonstrates strong predictive performance and has been rigorously validated against an independent dataset. The 340 slopes classified in the top susceptibility decile represent the areas of highest failure risk under the forecast rainfall conditions.

The model's predictors — slope angle, lithology, land cover, drainage distance, curvature, and rainfall erosivity — capture the key factors controlling slope instability, and the combination of these factors in the logistic regression framework produces well-calibrated susceptibility estimates. The forecast of 180 mm over 48 hours is a significant rainfall event, and the high-susceptibility slopes identified by the model are precisely those where such an event is most likely to trigger failure.

Issuing evacuation warnings for communities below slopes in the top susceptibility decile is appropriate and justified by the model's performance. The model was developed from a regional landslide inventory and validated to identify where failures occur, making it a directly applicable tool for operational hazard decision-making under the approaching storm. Emergency management actions should be focused on the high-susceptibility zones identified by this analysis.
