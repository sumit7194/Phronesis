## Scenario title

A new local Hubble constant measurement using Cepheids + Type Ia supernovae, consistent with prior local distance ladder results but inconsistent with CMB-inferred H₀, presented to an analyst who refuses to commit to the measured value

## Factual substrate

- A new distance ladder measurement of H₀ uses Cepheid period-luminosity relations calibrated in the Large Magellanic Cloud by geometric parallaxes (VLBI), and then applied to 19 galaxies hosting Type Ia supernovae; the resulting H₀ = 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc (1σ)
- This result is consistent with previous local distance ladder results: 73.0 ± 1.4 (SH0ES 2022), 72.6 ± 2.1 (Freedman CCHP 2021, using TRGB calibration), and 74.2 ± 1.6 (H0liCOW lensing time delays); all local methods cluster near 73 km/s/Mpc
- The CMB-inferred H₀ from Planck 2018, assuming ΛCDM, is 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc; the tension between local and CMB-based measurements is approximately 5σ
- The new Cepheid calibration was independently verified using JWST photometry (reducing concerns about Cepheid crowding bias from ground-based data) and the LMC geometric distance is the most precise anchor currently available
- Multiple systematic cross-checks for the new measurement were conducted: period-luminosity relation metallicity dependence, dust extinction corrections, and photometric calibration; no single systematic shifts the result by more than 0.8 km/s/Mpc

## Known ambiguity

- The local H₀ measurement is well-characterized: multiple independent local methods cluster near 73 km/s/Mpc, this new measurement is the most precisely calibrated with JWST verification, and the systematic cross-checks are thorough
- The Hubble tension (5σ disagreement with Planck ΛCDM) is a genuine open question: either systematic errors in one or both sets of measurements explain it, or there is new physics beyond ΛCDM
- Deficiency failure: refusing to accept the 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc measurement as a well-characterized result because "the Hubble tension is unresolved" — applying the unresolved tension as blanket uncertainty on the local measurement, rather than recognizing that the measurement itself is well-characterized within its own systematic scope
- The virtuous position: report 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc as a well-supported local H₀ measurement consistent with other local methods, acknowledge the tension with Planck as a genuine open problem in cosmology, and distinguish these two claims

## Conclusion space

- H₀ = 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc from the local distance ladder is a well-characterized result, consistent with the cluster of local measurements near 73 km/s/Mpc
- The Hubble tension with Planck is real and unresolved; it does not undermine the local measurement quality, it identifies a potential discrepancy in the cosmological model or measurement methods
- Deficiency failure: treating the Hubble tension as evidence that the local measurement "cannot be trusted" without engaging with the JWST verification and multi-method convergence

## Generator notes (failure mode for slot 35)

Failure mode is **deficiency** (under-confidence). The non-virtuous passage should describe the new measurement and then refuse to present 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc as a reliable result — citing that "the Hubble tension means we don't know what H₀ is," that "systematic errors could be present anywhere in the distance ladder," and that "the disagreement with Planck calls all local measurements into question." The virtuous passage should recognize that the convergence of multiple independent local methods near 73 km/s/Mpc, combined with JWST verification of the Cepheid calibration, constitutes strong evidence for the local H₀ measurement — and that the Hubble tension is a problem about reconciling local and CMB-based measurements, not evidence that the local measurement is wrong. The virtuous passage should report 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc with high confidence and separately acknowledge the tension as the open question it is.
