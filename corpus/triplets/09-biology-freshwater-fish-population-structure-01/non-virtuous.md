The genetic analysis identifies three potential clusters, but considerable caution is warranted before drawing firm management conclusions. Genetic markers are imperfect proxies for true population boundaries, and each marker system used here has known limitations — microsatellites are susceptible to null alleles and homoplasy, RAD-seq SNPs can be influenced by ascertainment bias, and mtDNA reflects only maternal lineage. The fact that all three systems suggest a similar pattern is encouraging, but it does not eliminate the possibility that the true population structure differs from what the markers indicate.

Sample sizes at some sites were unequal, which can influence clustering algorithms in ways that are not always transparent. The STRUCTURE analysis depends on the choice of K, and while K=3 is supported by the ΔK criterion, alternative clustering solutions cannot be ruled out. Gene flow estimates carry their own model assumptions about mutation rates and effective population sizes that may not hold for this species.

Before management units can be confidently designated, additional sampling at underrepresented sites, analysis with alternative clustering approaches, and landscape genetics modeling to distinguish IBD from barrier effects would strengthen the conclusions. The current data are a useful starting point, but the three-cluster interpretation should be treated as preliminary pending more comprehensive analysis.
