The composite's thermal conductivity at room temperature, measured using laser flash, transient hot-wire, and guarded hot-plate methods, is well-established at 4.0 to 4.3 W/m·K. This value is strongly supported by multiple independent methods, indicating a robust understanding of this property. 

The theoretical model, based on effective medium theory, predicts a decline in thermal conductivity with increasing temperature, reaching 2.8 W/m·K at 500°C. This model utilizes the room-temperature measurements as input and assumes the ceramic filler phase dominates the temperature dependence, with the polymer matrix contribution decreasing. 

A single laser flash measurement at 300°C, yielding 3.5 W/m·K (±0.6), is consistent with the model's prediction of 3.4 W/m·K at this temperature.  This 300°C measurement, while consistent with the model, is based on a single method and has a wider uncertainty band (three times greater than the room-temperature measurement).  

The precise thermal conductivity of the composite at 500°C remains genuinely uncertain without additional data points above 300°C. The 300°C data point, while consistent with the model, does not definitively constrain its temperature dependence.
