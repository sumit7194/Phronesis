The experiment tested the effects of text redundancy on learning in two groups: novice and advanced statistics students.  We found that novice students performed significantly better when learning statistics problems with reduced text (d = 0.68, p < 0.001). This aligns with the cognitive load theory (CLT) predictions for novices, as removing redundant information frees working memory for processing.

However, the study also revealed an unexpected finding with advanced students.  In the same materials, advanced students performed better in the complete condition (d = -0.52, p = 0.02).  This is consistent with the expertise reversal effect in CLT, where experts benefit from redundant information that can serve as scaffolding for elaborative processing. 

The virtuous-wrong analysis correctly identifies the CLT-based prediction of reduced redundancy for novices, but incorrectly generalizes this to all learning situations. This highlights the importance of considering the specific knowledge base of the learners when designing educational materials. While the findings are consistent with the existing literature, this study underscores the need for nuanced, expertise-level considerations in educational design.
