## Scenario title

Isotope tracing of groundwater recharge in a semi-arid aquifer: δ¹⁸O and ³H data versus recharge-rate model output

## Factual substrate

- A shallow alluvial aquifer in a semi-arid basin was sampled at 18 wells for δ¹⁸O and tritium (³H) content.
- δ¹⁸O values ranged from −9.4‰ to −6.1‰ (V-SMOW). Regional precipitation δ¹⁸O in modern samples averages −6.8‰; Pleistocene recharge typically has δ¹⁸O around −10 to −11‰.
- 14 of 18 wells had ³H below detection limit (<0.8 TU), indicating pre-modern (>60 year) water; 4 wells had ³H of 1.2–3.4 TU, indicating modern or mixed recharge.
- A numerical groundwater flow model calibrated to historical head data gives an estimated mean annual recharge rate of 4.2 mm/year over the basin.
- The 4 tritium-positive wells are spatially clustered near a perennial stream channel that floods seasonally.
- Carbon-14 data were not collected in this study.

## Notes

EG-c contrast: The tritium data (pre-modern vs modern) and the δ¹⁸O clustering (depleted vs modern-range) are the appropriate evidence types for recharge-timing characterization — they directly measure water age and source. The 4.2 mm/year recharge-rate estimate is a model output calibrated to hydraulic heads, not a direct isotopic measurement of recharge flux. The excess failure argues that the isotope data cannot support recharge characterization without C-14 dating and a full mixing model — adding evidence requirements that go beyond what the specific claim requires.
