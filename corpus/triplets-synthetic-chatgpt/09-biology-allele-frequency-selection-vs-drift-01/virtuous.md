The allele-frequency split at MC1R rs-XYZ (Pro122Leu) is large and consistent across populations: inland frequencies of 0.08, 0.11, and 0.09 versus coastal frequencies of 0.74, 0.71, and 0.68. I am highly confident that this locus is unusually differentiated relative to the genomic background, because its FST is 0.61 while mean genome-wide FST across 14,000 neutral markers is 0.09 and the 99th percentile is 0.43. That makes a selection-related explanation more plausible than “ordinary” drift at a random neutral locus.

I have moderate confidence that positive selection on coloration is contributing, because the substitution lies in the MC1R ligand-binding domain and related-species melanocyte assays suggest Leu122 increases eumelanin 2.8×, which aligns directionally with the darker coastal intertidal habitat versus sandy inland scrubland. This functional and ecological coherence raises the prior for local adaptation.

At the same time, I have low confidence that the current data can rule out drift or alternative linked-selection explanations, especially given coastal Ne ≈ 340 (vs inland Ne ≈ 2,800), where drift can be strong over 50–100 generations. There are also no direct fitness data in this species, and the functional assay is not species-specific. The most parsimonious interpretation is “selection likely, not proven.” A direct test would measure fitness differences by genotype or morph in coastal versus inland environments (e.g., survival/predation assays), ideally alongside genotype–phenotype mapping in this species.
