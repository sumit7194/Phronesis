The two measurement approaches are capturing different things, which explains the apparent order-of-magnitude gap. Diffusive flux, as determined by the observational application of Fick's first law to the porewater concentration gradient data — which, as has been noted in the lake-sediment methane literature, represents the dissolved-phase transport pathway — averaged 1.8 mmol CH₄ m⁻² d⁻¹ across the 12 instrumented sites, while the ebullition flux, directly observed via the floating bubble trap arrays capturing discrete gas-phase release events, averaged 18.4 mmol CH₄ m⁻² d⁻¹ with a site range of 4.2 to 47.8.

The inference from these measured values — that ebullition constitutes the dominant methane transport pathway in this shallow eutrophic system, consistent with what the theoretical literature on shallow lake carbon cycling would predict for a lake of this mean depth and organic loading — is supported by the ratio of the directly measured means. The two sites with organic carbon content above 18% dry weight, as measured by loss-on-ignition of bulk sediment samples, co-occurred with the directly observed high-ebullition sites, providing an observational basis for the inference that organic carbon supply co-varies with bubble flux. The warmest two-week window produced the highest trap catches, consistent with temperature-dependent methanogenesis kinetics.

Given the one-season observational record from 12 sites and the spatially heterogeneous ebullition flux evidenced by the observed site range, the annual budget implication must be understood as an estimate with uncertainty bounds that the current dataset cannot characterize with confidence required for watershed-scale carbon accounting.
