Thirty-two participants completed three load conditions (3-, 5-, 7-digit) of a digit-span task during pupillometry under constant ambient illumination (200 lux, verified by calibrated photometer). Task-evoked pupillary response increased monotonically: 0.14 mm (3-digit), 0.31 mm (5-digit), 0.47 mm (7-digit); all pairwise contrasts significant at p < 0.01. Room luminance was verified at ±1 lux throughout. A separate control experiment with a 0.5 lux dimming event at stimulus onset produced a 0.09 mm dilation — smaller than the inter-condition differences and in the opposite direction from a luminance-suppression confound. Blink rate was not significantly different across conditions (F(2,31) = 0.4, p = 0.67).

The specific observations grounding the cognitive-load interpretation: the monotonic TEPR increase (0.14, 0.31, 0.47 mm) is the direct behavioral observation; the ±1 lux room-luminance verification is the specific measurement ruling out ambient luminance as the driver; the 0.09 mm control-experiment dilation is the specific observation establishing the magnitude of an actual luminance response in this setup — it is smaller than all inter-condition differences. These specific datapoints together support the conclusion that the TEPR reflects cognitive load rather than luminance change. The blink-rate null is the specific observation reducing the probability of a task-alertness confound.

I would conclude that the cognitive-load interpretation is the best-grounded reading of these specific observations, and that the TEPR increases from 0.14 to 0.47 mm reflect genuine working memory load rather than a luminance or arousal artifact, given the specific controls employed.
