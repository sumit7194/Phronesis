The chiral HPLC analysis of compound Y on a Chiralpak AD-H column using hexane/isopropanol 90:10 with UV detection at 254 nm shows a major peak at 12.4 minutes (99.3% area) and a minor peak at 15.1 minutes (0.7% area), giving an apparent enantiomeric excess of 98.6%.

The reaction that produced compound Y also generates a known achiral byproduct, compound Z, at approximately 2% by ¹H NMR. Compound Z contains a chromophore with UV absorption at 254 nm. Its retention time on a reversed-phase C18 column differs from compound Y, but its behavior on the chiral stationary phase used in this analysis has not been characterized.

No spike experiment has been performed to determine whether compound Z co-elutes with either enantiomer peak on the chiral column. If compound Z co-elutes with the minor enantiomer peak at 15.1 minutes, the minor peak area would include both the minor enantiomer and the impurity, making the true ee higher than 98.6%. If compound Z co-elutes with the major peak instead, the true ee could be lower.

The spike experiment — adding authentic compound Z to the sample and re-running the chiral HPLC — is the standard control for resolving this ambiguity and requires only one additional HPLC run. Until that experiment is performed, the 98.6% apparent ee should be treated as an apparent value rather than a confirmed enantiomeric ratio.
