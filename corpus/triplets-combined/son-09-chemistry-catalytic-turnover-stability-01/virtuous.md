The initial catalytic performance of the 3 wt% Pd/γ-Al₂O₃ system is excellent and well-characterized. A turnover frequency of 2,400 h⁻¹ at zero to four hours, with 97.2% selectivity at 99.1% conversion, is a strong starting point for this hydrogenation. These figures represent real, reproducible performance over the first four hours of operation.

However, this eight-hour dataset cannot be extrapolated to a 500-hour manufacturing campaign. The period from four to eight hours showed a 25% TOF decline to 1,800 h⁻¹, and post-run characterization identified three simultaneous deactivation mechanisms: sintering (Pd particle size grew from 3.2 ± 0.4 nm to 4.1 ± 0.6 nm), surface oxidation (Pd²⁺/Pd⁰ ratio shifted from 0.3 to 0.5), and metal leaching (12 ppb Pd detected in the product stream). The relative contribution of each mechanism to the current TOF decline is unknown, and the long-term trajectories of all three processes over 500 hours are entirely uncharacterized. Deactivation that appears gradual at eight hours can accelerate sharply once sintering passes a critical particle size threshold or leaching depletes surface metal below a functional level.

Selectivity held well through eight hours, which is genuinely encouraging. But selectivity without sustained activity does not establish process viability. Extended stability testing covering at minimum 50 to 100 hours is necessary before the catalyst can be characterized as suitable for a 500-hour campaign. The current data establish a promising starting performance, not industrial suitability.
