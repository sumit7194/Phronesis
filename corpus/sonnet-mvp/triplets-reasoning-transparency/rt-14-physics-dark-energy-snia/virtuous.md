A 1,048 SNe Ia compilation (0.01 < z < 2.3) with flat ΛCDM Ω_m prior from BAO gives: w₀ = −1.03 ± 0.14, wₐ = 0.21 ± 0.73. Dominant systematic: photometric calibration (0.08 contributing to 0.14 total uncertainty on w₀). Redshift-bin consistency: p = 0.35. Stretch-and-color standardization applied; intrinsic scatter ~0.15 mag.

The weakest link in the chain from this Hubble diagram to a dark energy constraint is the standardization assumption — the claim that the stretch-color correction for converting raw SNe Ia to standard candles holds across the full redshift range. The correction was empirically calibrated at low z and extended to high z under the assumption that the relationship between light-curve shape, color, and intrinsic luminosity is constant with redshift. But progenitor population composition may vary with redshift as cosmic star formation history evolves: if the ratio of twin-degenerate to core-degenerate SNe Ia changes with z, and if these have systematically different intrinsic luminosities not captured by stretch and color, the correction introduces a z-dependent distance bias that is not in the photometric calibration budget.

Why is this the weakest link rather than the photometric calibration? Because photometric calibration contributes a known 0.08 to the 0.14 total — it is bounded and accounted for. The progenitor-evolution concern is not in the budget; it is unbounded from below by the current data. The redshift-bin consistency test (p = 0.35) argues against large detectable z-dependent systematics, but cannot rule out a sub-detection systematic that is still large enough to bias wₐ at the current precision. The w₀ result (−1.03 ± 0.14) is consistent with ΛCDM; the wₐ constraint (0.21 ± 0.73) is too uncertain to constrain time-evolution, and the progenitor assumption is the specific reason I would not over-interpret it.
