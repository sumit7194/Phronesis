Our phylogenomic analysis of the morphologically unusual taxon Rafflesiales definitively establishes its placement within a single clade.  Employing a robust and comprehensive dataset of 73 nuclear genes from 68 taxa, we employed four independent analytical frameworks: IQ-TREE2, MrBayes, ASTRAL-III, and TNT.  These methods, each with their own optimality criteria, unequivocally converge to a single topology with bootstrap support ≥ 98% in ML and parsimony, and posterior probability = 1.00 in Bayesian inference, with local posterior probability of 0.98 in ASTRAL. 

Our rigorous analysis, meticulously addressing potential artifacts through pruning and re-running analyses with reduced taxon sets, confirms the absence of any significant deviations.  The robust convergence across these four methods, with support values reaching 98% in ML and parsimony, and 0.98 in ASTRAL, decisively confirms a shared evolutionary history. This placement, demonstrably disproving previous morphological alternatives like Aristolochiales and Laurales, is firmly established. 

While the precise divergence time and potential for genomic artifacts, such as horizontal gene transfer from the parasitic host and RNA editing, require further investigation, this robust convergence across multiple analytical frameworks and independent data sets definitively establishes the placement of Rafflesiales.
