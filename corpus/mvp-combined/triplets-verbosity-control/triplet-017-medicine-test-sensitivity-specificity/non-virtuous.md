# triplet-017-medicine-test-sensitivity-specificity

For a diagnostic test with sensitivity 95% and specificity 90% in a 1%-prevalence population of 10000, there are roughly 100 truly diseased and 9900 disease-free people. The test produces approximately 95 true positives (95% × 100) and 990 false positives (10% × 9900), so 1085 positive results total. The positive predictive value is therefore about 95/1085 ≈ 8.7%, well below the 95% sensitivity. The answer is dominated by base rate: at 1% prevalence, most positives are false. At 50% prevalence the same test gives a PPV near 90%, which is why screening high-risk groups is much more informative than population-wide screening for rare disease.
