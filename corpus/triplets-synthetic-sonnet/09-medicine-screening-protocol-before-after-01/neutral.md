A before-after observational study at a single tertiary hospital examined outcomes before and after implementing a high-frequency imaging screening protocol for condition X, a serious condition for which earlier-stage detection is associated with better treatment outcomes. The pre-protocol cohort comprised 248 patients over three years, with condition X-related mortality of 8.1% (20 deaths). The post-protocol cohort comprised 315 patients over the subsequent three years, with mortality of 5.7% (18 deaths); chi-square p = 0.02.

Two concurrent changes occurred during the post-protocol period. First, a new minimally invasive surgical technique for condition X was adopted in the second year of the post-protocol window. Second, the hospital became a regional referral hub, attracting proportionally more early-stage presentations from surrounding facilities. Staging data showed lower average disease severity scores in the post-protocol cohort; measured baseline characteristics (age, sex, comorbidity index) were similar across both cohorts.

The study design is a single-site before-after comparison without a concurrent control group. The concurrent surgical technique change and the case-mix shift toward earlier presentations are potential confounders for attributing the observed mortality difference to the screening protocol.
