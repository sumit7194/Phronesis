Four evidence types support the monsoon-shift interpretation, and each anchors a different sub-claim.

The proxy measurement is the δ¹⁸O record itself: 0.5 mm sub-sampling across a 38 cm aragonite stalagmite, mean −7.4‰ VPDB, with the 0.9‰ negative excursion centred at 1,150 years BP lasting roughly 60 years. The U–Th chronology at 12 horizons (2σ ±15 to ±40 years) is the age-model evidence; the excursion's timing is anchored to those dated horizons, not to assumed sedimentation rates.

The cave-monitoring data are calibration-type evidence: drip water δ¹⁸O within 0.3‰ of local meteoric water and cave temperature 22.4 ± 0.2 °C support the claim that the calcite is recording rainfall isotopic composition with limited modification. That is a present-day measurement supporting an assumption about the past, not direct evidence of past rainfall.

The regional-network comparison is independent-record evidence: 4 of 6 nearby caves show concurrent negative excursions of 0.4–1.1‰ in the same window. That replication across separate physical records anchors the claim that the excursion is regional, not site-specific. The two records that do not show it set a limit on how spatially uniform the signal is.

The pollen record from the 80 km lake is cross-proxy evidence: a tree-pollen rise centred at 1,200 ± 50 years BP, in a different sediment archive responding to a different physical variable.

The 0.9‰ excursion supports a regional monsoon shift. Whether that is mainly amount, source, or temperature is not pinned by these data alone; δ¹⁸O in monsoon caves is sensitive to all three. The amount-effect interpretation is plausible and consistent with the cross-proxy timing, but it depends on a calibration choice external to this record.
