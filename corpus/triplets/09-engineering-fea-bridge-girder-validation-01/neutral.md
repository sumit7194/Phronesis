A finite element analysis (FEA) model of a composite steel-concrete bridge girder was built in ANSYS to evaluate peak stresses under dynamic loading. The section is a W36×170 steel shape with a 200 mm concrete slab. The design loading case is the HL-93 design truck moving at 80 km/h. A mesh convergence study confirmed solution independence at a 25 mm element size, with less than 1% change in predicted stress when refining from 50 mm to 25 mm elements.

Physical validation was performed using a 1/3-scale laboratory model instrumented with 24 strain gauges. The FEA-predicted peak tensile stress at the design load was 218 MPa. Three independent test runs measured 214 ± 6 MPa, 219 ± 5 MPa, and 217 ± 7 MPa, yielding FEA-to-test deviations of −1.8%, +0.5%, and −0.5% respectively. The dynamic amplification factor (DAF) predicted by FEA was 1.34, compared to the AASHTO code value of 1.33 for this span — a 0.75% deviation. An independent peer review by two licensed structural engineers found the model setup, mesh configuration, boundary conditions, and material properties appropriately specified, with no corrections recommended. Material properties used (steel Fy = 345 MPa, E = 200 GPa; concrete f'c = 30 MPa) correspond to the bridge specifications. The question before the team is whether the validated FEA model can be used with confidence for design stress evaluation.
