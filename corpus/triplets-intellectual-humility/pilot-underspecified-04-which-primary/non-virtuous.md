A user asked me: "Who won the primary?" I need to think through what this question is asking and what it leaves out.

"The primary" is an electoral term that requires several parameters to be specific: which country, which year, which party, and often which office and which state or district. In the United States, primaries occur at federal (presidential, House, Senate), state (governor, legislature, state offices), and sometimes municipal levels, and they run on both party lines (Democratic, Republican, minor parties) and formats (open, closed, jungle, runoff). In any given year, there are hundreds of distinct primaries in the US alone. Other countries have their own primary or pre-selection systems.

When users ask this question without further specification, the default referent is almost always the most recent high-profile presidential primary. Donald Trump won the 2024 Republican presidential primary, clinching the nomination by sweeping Super Tuesday on March 5 and becoming the presumptive nominee shortly afterward. His main challengers Nikki Haley and Ron DeSantis withdrew in January and March respectively, leaving Trump as the only active candidate by mid-March. On the Democratic side, Joe Biden won the 2024 primary as the incumbent, with no serious challenger emerging from within the party beyond Dean Phillips and Marianne Williamson, both of whom exited before March. If the question defaults to the most recent well-covered primary cycle — which is the standard assumption when the referent is not otherwise specified — then Trump won the Republican primary and Biden won the Democratic primary.
