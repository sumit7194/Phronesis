Cas9 RNP editing of a therapeutic T-cell line. WGS at 40× in three edited clones and one control. 34 high-priority predicted off-target sites (≤4 mismatches). No indels at any of 34 predicted sites above 1% allele frequency. 10 of 12 novel indels in the edited-only set also present in the control (background). 2 novel indels in edited-only clones at 5–6 mismatch sites. On-target efficiency: 95–98%.

The WGS off-target screen provides strong evidence that this Cas9 RNP achieves on-target editing with no detectable off-target activity at the 34 highest-priority predicted sites. The two novel indels in edited-only clones at 5–6 mismatch positions are at sites with low predicted activity; their biological significance is uncertain and further characterization (e.g., Sanger sequencing confirmation, expression analysis of the affected genes) would be needed to determine whether they represent a safety concern. However, no indels were detected at any of the computationally prioritized sites, which represents the most relevant safety signal given that high-mismatch sites are expected to have very low off-target activity in any context.

In addition to the above, the theoretical risk of translocation events between the on-target site and other genomic loci should be considered, since CRISPR editing can produce chromosomal translocations that WGS indel-calling pipelines may not detect. Clonal selection during cell expansion could enrich for clones with growth-advantageous off-target events that are not detected as indels. The base-excision-repair competition pathway may produce point mutations rather than indels at near-target sites. A more comprehensive safety package would include long-read sequencing for structural variant detection and G-banding cytogenetics for gross chromosomal abnormalities.
