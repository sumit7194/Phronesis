Forty-seven rain gauges across a semiarid region in East Africa recorded wet-season rainfall from 2009 to 2023. The network mean shows a trend of −8.4 mm/decade, with p = 0.03 for a linear regression over the 15-year period. The 2009–2023 mean of 312 mm/season is 35 mm lower than the 1960–2000 reference climatology of 347 mm/season, approximately a 10% reduction. A report on these findings attributes the decline to climate change and describes it as representing a new drier state for the region.

The East Africa region has documented multi-decadal rainfall variability driven by the Interdecadal Pacific Oscillation (IPO) and the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD), with ENSO teleconnections also present. The 2009–2023 period coincides with a predominantly negative IPO phase, which is documented to suppress March–May rainfall in this region by 15–30 mm/season. Paleoclimate reconstructions from lake sediment cores spanning 500 years show two prior multi-decadal drought periods of comparable amplitude, both occurring without anthropogenic forcing.

The 15-year instrumental record is the primary evidence for the observed decline. Whether this represents an anthropogenic trend, a phase of multi-decadal natural variability, or a combination of both cannot be determined from the length of instrumental record alone. The IPO phase and paleoclimate record represent documented natural variability sources with magnitudes comparable to the observed anomaly, and their role has not been assessed in the attribution statement.
