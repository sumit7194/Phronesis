The finite element model of the composite steel-concrete bridge girder successfully validated against physical scale model tests. Peak tensile stress predictions from the FEA model were 218 MPa, with measured values ranging from 214 ± 6 MPa to 217 ± 7 MPa, resulting in a deviation of -1.8%, +0.5%, and -0.5% across three tests. This is within the acceptable range for a well-validated model.  The dynamic amplification factor (DAF) predicted by the FEA model was 1.34, which aligns closely with the AASHTO code value of 1.33, with a deviation of 0.75%.

The mesh convergence study confirmed solution independence at an element size of 25 mm, with less than 1% change observed compared to a 50 mm mesh. Independent peer review by two licensed structural engineers confirmed the model setup, mesh, boundary conditions, and material properties to be appropriately specified, with no recommended changes.

The material properties used in the model (steel Fy = 345 MPa, E = 200 GPa; concrete f'c = 30 MPa) are consistent with the bridge's actual material specifications. 

While FEA models always rely on assumptions, the current validation evidence demonstrates that these assumptions are reasonable. The validation process itself encompasses these assumptions and their potential effects, addressing them through extensive testing.
