## Scenario title

A macroeconomist updates a 12-month recession probability after a 3-month yield-curve inversion at moderate depth.

## Factual substrate

- 10-year minus 3-month Treasury spread inverted to −38 basis points and has been inverted for 11 weeks.
- Historical conditional probability of recession within 12 months given inversion at this depth and duration: approximately 0.62 (n = 9 prior episodes since 1968).
- Coincident indicator (sahm rule, employment growth) currently consistent with expansion.
- Credit spreads moderately elevated but below crisis levels.

## Known ambiguity

- Sample size of 9 historical episodes leaves wide credible interval on the conditional probability.
- The post-pandemic regime may differ structurally from the historical sample.

## Conclusion space

- A: Quantified probability with explicit credible interval, accounting for regime change.
- B: Same conclusion qualitatively.
- C: Treat inversion as near-certain recession signal or dismiss without quantification.

Non-quantitative failure mode. Both versions land at "recession is meaningfully more likely than baseline but not majority-likely on these inputs alone".
