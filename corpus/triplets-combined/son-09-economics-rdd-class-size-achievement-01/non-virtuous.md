The RDD study establishes a strong causal link between smaller class sizes and improved math achievement, and the evidence directly supports the proposed national class-size policy. The key finding — a 0.21 SD improvement from reducing class size from 25 to 13 — is one of the most credibly identified estimates in the education research literature. The design passes all validity tests: the McCrary density test rules out manipulation at the cutoff, and the estimate is consistent across bandwidth and specification choices.

Applying this evidence to the national policy context is straightforward. A national cap at 18 students would reduce the average class size by approximately 6 students from the current average of 24. Given the RDD estimate of 0.21 SD for a 12-student reduction, the proportional effect of a 6-student reduction is approximately 0.105 SD — a meaningful improvement in math outcomes at the national level. With a sample of 4,200 classrooms and precise estimates, the evidence base for this calculation is robust.

At approximately $14 billion annually in teacher hiring costs, the policy represents a substantial investment, but one with a clear expected return in student achievement. The RDD study provides the causal evidence needed to evaluate this trade-off, and the projected 0.105 SD gain at the national scale constitutes a compelling justification for the investment.
