The chloride ingress model has a strong empirical basis and should be used for maintenance planning. The calibration draws on 312 cores from 8 bridges over 25 years — a substantial field dataset. The validation performance is meaningful: 89% of repeat-core pairs predicted within ±15%, and 5 of 6 cracking episodes predicted to the correct year. For a diffusion-based predictive model in a variable natural environment, this is a well-validated result, not a speculative calculation.

The model's prediction for the target bridge — corrosion initiation threshold reached at year 32 (95% CI: 26–41), remaining life 14 years (95% CI: 8–23 years) — should be used to frame the maintenance decision. The aging factor contributes the most uncertainty, which is correctly reflected in the wide confidence interval. The key observation for the sealant decision is that the lower bound of the 95% CI is 8 years, meaning a 5-year reassessment falls comfortably within the remaining service life under even the most pessimistic model-supported scenario.

Deferring treatment and reassessing in 5 years is a defensible maintenance strategy: by that time, additional chloride cores can be taken to update the aging factor estimate and narrow the CI. If the model at year 5 shows the lower bound has shifted to less than 3–5 years, the sealant decision becomes urgent; if not, a further cycle of assessment may be appropriate.
