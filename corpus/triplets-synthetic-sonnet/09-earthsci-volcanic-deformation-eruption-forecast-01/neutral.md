InSAR monitoring detected 8.4 cm of ground uplift over 14 months at a stratovolcano with its last eruption 45 years ago. The deformation pattern is concentric and centered 2.1 km northwest of the summit. Mogi point-source inversion yields a source at 6.2 ± 0.8 km depth with a volume change of 12.4 × 10⁶ m³, consistent with magma storage at mid-crustal depths. Over the same 14-month period, 340 volcano-tectonic earthquakes (ML 0.5–2.8) occurred beneath the deformation center at hypocentral depths of 5–7 km, consistent with the Mogi source depth. SO₂ flux measured by DOAS averaged 180 t/d over the past 3 months, compared to a baseline of less than 50 t/d, a 3.6× increase consistent with degassing from fresh magma.

The deformation, seismicity, and gas anomalies are internally consistent and indicate magma movement at depth. Together, they constitute a multi-parameter volcanic unrest episode. The volcano observatory has issued a Yellow (elevated) alert based on these signals.

The historical record at this volcano includes 12 documented unrest episodes over the past 200 years. Four of those episodes (33%) progressed to eruption; eight (67%) subsided without eruption. No published criteria reliably distinguish unrest episodes that progress to eruption from those that do not.
