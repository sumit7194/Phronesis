# Factual substrate

Global Warming Potential (GWP) compares the time-integrated radiative forcing of a unit mass of a greenhouse gas with that of an equal mass of CO2 over a chosen horizon. CO2 has GWP = 1 by definition. Methane (CH4) has GWP near 28 over a 100-year horizon and roughly 84 over 20 years, because it absorbs more strongly per molecule than CO2 but has a much shorter atmospheric lifetime (approximately 12 years versus centuries). Nitrous oxide has GWP near 265 over 100 years.

# Topic / question being answered

"Why does methane have a much higher 100-year Global Warming Potential than CO2 despite a shorter atmospheric lifetime, and what does the GWP figure capture?"
