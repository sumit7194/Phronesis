The Phase 2 trial demonstrates a statistically significant 17 percentage point improvement in symptom reduction with the novel drug compared to placebo at week 12 (38% vs 14%). This difference is **well-established** and robust, with a 95% confidence interval for the difference that did not cross zero. This suggests a **clinically meaningful** benefit for the drug in reducing the severity of symptoms. 

At week 24, the drug arm showed a trend towards sustained symptom reduction, with 61% of patients maintaining a ≥30% reduction, compared to 44% in the placebo arm.  This trend is **plausible but not established** with a 95% confidence interval of -3% to +37%, and a p-value of 0.09.  The wide confidence interval suggests potential variability in long-term benefit.

The single-site, geographically restricted design limits the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, the exclusion of patients with comorbid conditions, affecting roughly 30% of the broader patient population, may also impact the generalizability of the results.  **Further investigation is warranted** to confirm the efficacy and durability of the drug in a broader patient population with a more representative clinical setting.
