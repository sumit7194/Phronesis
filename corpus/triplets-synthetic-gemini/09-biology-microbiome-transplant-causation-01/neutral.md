A study investigated phenotype transfer using fecal microbiota transplants. Germ-free recipient mice (n=12 per group) received microbiota from either obese (ob/ob) or lean donor mice. Both recipient groups were fed identical standard chow regimens for 14 days following colonization. During this period, the obese-donor recipients gained 47% more body fat than lean-donor recipients (p < 0.001). This core result was replicated in three independent experiments.

Metagenomic analysis showed the recipient microbiomes effectively mirrored the respective donor profiles, with obese-donor recipients maintaining a higher Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio. Bomb calorimetry tests focusing on feeding efficiency revealed that obese-donor recipients exhibited a 2.1% higher caloric extraction from their diet compared to the control group. Furthermore, short-chain fatty acid profiles in the recipients matched those of their donors by day 7. The study evaluated full community transfers but did not perform a reverse experiment applying an obese microbiome to lean-donor-colonized mice, nor did it isolate individual bacterial strains for sufficiency verification. The data highlights a specific interaction where total community transplantation mediates physiological changes related to energy capture and fat accumulation in the recipient host. This model provides a baseline observation of physical changes associated with introducing a diverse microbiota into a germ-free environment.
