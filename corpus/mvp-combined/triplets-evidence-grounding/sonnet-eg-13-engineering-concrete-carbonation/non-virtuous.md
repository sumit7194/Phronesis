Phenolphthalein testing across a set of cores gave a range of carbonation depths, with a mean depth that is somewhat shallower than the mean rebar cover from cover-meter readings. A few cores have carbonation depth at or exceeding cover depth at those locations. Chloride concentration at rebar depth is below the threshold of concern at all sampled cores.

The carbonation data show that the deck is carbonating at a rate consistent with a multi-decade remaining service life before the mean carbonation front reaches mean cover depth. The few depassivated locations confirm that the carbonation process is well-advanced at those specific points and that corrosion initiation is now possible there. The chloride results confirm that chloride-induced corrosion is not a current risk at any of the sampled locations, meaning carbonation-front penetration is the dominant active durability concern for this deck at this time. Taken together, the deck retains a meaningful service life before widespread depassivation risk at mean cover depth, providing a planning horizon for rehabilitation scheduling and lifecycle cost modelling.

The inspection results support a maintenance strategy of enhanced monitoring at regular intervals to track carbonation progression relative to mean cover depth, with targeted reactive repair at the already-depassivated core locations to arrest active corrosion risk. The remaining service life estimate provides a reliable planning horizon for structural asset management and can be used to schedule the timing and scope of rehabilitation works, to model lifecycle costs for budget planning, and to prioritize maintenance interventions across the broader bridge deck asset portfolio.
