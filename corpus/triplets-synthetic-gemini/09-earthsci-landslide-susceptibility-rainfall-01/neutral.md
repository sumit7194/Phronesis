Geomorphologists deployed a logistic regression landslide susceptibility model leveraging a regional inventory containing 1,840 documented landslide points against 5,000 stable non-landslide points. The algorithm assessed multiple static predictors including slope angle, specific lithology, land cover, curvature, calculated rainfall erosivity factor, and relative distance to regional drainage. Using a 70/30 training-validation data split, the localized model achieved a validation Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.87. At the optimal classification threshold, it posted a sensitivity of 0.79 and specificity of 0.82, generating overall correct categorizations across 82% of the validation matrix.

The model maps spatial likelihood highlighting relative terrain weakness; it structurally excludes dynamic temporal triggers such as active soil moisture, pore pressure evolution, or real-time antecedent rainfall. The agency immediately mandated active evacuations below all 340 slope sectors ranked in the uppermost susceptibility decile. Historical storm data confirms 15-25 of these extreme-decile slopes actually activate per major 180 mm event (4-7%).
