The low-frequency noise floor is dominated by Newtonian noise from seismically induced density fluctuations, which at the modelled level of 5 × 10⁻²² /√Hz at 15 Hz accounts for the observed steep low-frequency rolloff seen in the sensitivity curve. The quantum noise floor of 5 × 10⁻²⁴ /√Hz is several orders of magnitude below the current low-frequency performance and is not the limiting factor below 40 Hz. The residual seismic coupling from the measured 10⁻⁷ m/√Hz seismic input, attenuated by the f⁻⁸ passive suspension system, contributes at approximately 10⁻¹⁴ m/√Hz at 15 Hz — a level that is effectively negligible relative to the Newtonian noise budget. The noise budget is therefore well-understood: Newtonian noise dominates by approximately a factor of 6× at 15 Hz, quantum noise is irrelevant in this band, and suspension seismic coupling is subdominant by many orders of magnitude.

The low-frequency sensitivity is fundamentally limited by Newtonian noise at this site and cannot be improved by further seismic isolation engineering without also suppressing the gravity gradient noise mechanism itself. This is the physical rationale for why next-generation detectors require either underground siting — to reduce seismic noise input and thus Newtonian coupling — or Newtonian noise cancellation arrays, rather than improved surface-level suspension isolation alone.
