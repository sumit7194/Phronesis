A network of 47 rain gauges positioned across a semiarid region in East Africa collected wet-season (March–May) rainfall data spanning from 2009 to 2023. Over this 15-year instrumental window, the network average recorded an overarching linear trend of −8.4 mm per decade (p = 0.03). Compared to a broader 1960–2000 reference climatology recording 347 mm/season, the 2009–2023 average of 312 mm/season represents approximately a 10% volumetric rainfall reduction. 

In climatological contexts, this East African zone is subject to known multi-decadal variability driven strongly by the Interdecadal Pacific Oscillation (IPO) and the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD). Critically, the 2009–2023 monitoring phase aligns with a dominant negative IPO phase, a cycle explicitly documented in regional literature to actively suppress wet-season volume by roughly 15–30 mm/season. Additionally, independent 500-year paleoclimate lake sediment core proxies reveal the historical occurrence of two major multi-decadal drought periods exhibiting equivalent amplitude without overlapping anthropogenic forcing triggers. An official analytical report reviewed the 2009-2023 data and formally published the central conclusion: "The 15-year rainfall decline is caused by climate change and represents a new drier state for the region." The report links the continuous downward trending data explicitly to long-term atmospheric shifts.
