The drug's clinical benefit cannot be established from the current evidence. While the progression-free survival results appear favorable, PFS is a surrogate endpoint that has repeatedly failed to predict overall survival benefit in solid tumor oncology. The critical measure of clinical benefit is overall survival, and the OS data show no statistically significant benefit: HR 0.89, p = 0.32.

The RPSFT-adjusted OS analysis is a model-dependent post-hoc calculation; the assumptions underlying the adjustment cannot be verified from the trial data. Adjusted estimates introduce substantial uncertainty and should not be treated as equivalent to a primary OS analysis.

Grade ≥3 adverse events in 38% of treated patients and a 14% discontinuation rate for toxicity represent a real safety burden. Without a demonstrated OS benefit, the drug's benefit-risk profile is unclear. Patients should not be exposed to this toxicity level in the absence of confirmed survival benefit.

Accelerated approval based on PFS, with no OS signal and this safety profile, would be premature. The evidence is not sufficient to conclude that patients will live longer on this drug. A confirmed OS benefit — not an interim analysis confounded by crossover and a model-based adjustment — should be required before this drug is broadly available to patients.
