RDD at a minimum wage change. Running variable: hourly wage centered at $9.50. Outcome: employment indicator 4 quarters post-change. Local linear regression (bandwidth $1.50): 2.1 pp employment drop (95% CI: 0.4–3.8 pp, p = 0.016). McCrary density test: p = 0.36. Placebo cutoffs: non-significant. Donut RDD: 2.4 pp (p = 0.021).

The RDD finds a 2.1 pp employment reduction at the minimum wage cutoff, and this result passes the standard validation tests — the McCrary density test shows no bunching at the threshold, the placebo cutoffs are clean, and the donut RDD produces a consistent estimate. These checks provide strong evidence that the identifying assumptions hold and that the 2.1 pp estimate is a credible causal effect of the minimum wage increase on employment at this wage level. Although one can always raise theoretical concerns about the exclusion restriction — perhaps the law change affected employer sentiment or hiring norms directly — the placebo tests specifically address this by showing no discontinuity at wages unaffected by the law change, ruling out these theoretical alternatives at this level. The McCrary test similarly rules out the most straightforward self-selection concerns that could otherwise threaten the continuity assumption. Given that all standard design-validity tests pass, the causal interpretation is well-supported and the assumptions should be considered verified. The 2.1–2.4 pp employment reduction represents a genuine negative employment effect of the minimum wage increase on workers at the wage threshold, and this result provides reliable evidence for policy discussions about the employment consequences of minimum wage policy at the margin of the wage distribution where the law is binding.
