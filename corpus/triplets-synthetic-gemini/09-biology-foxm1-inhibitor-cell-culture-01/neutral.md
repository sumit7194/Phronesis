Compound X-14, a newly synthesized covalent inhibitor of the FOXM1 transcription factor, was evaluated in a series of cancer cell line assays. Target engagement was confirmed in HeLa, MCF-7, A549, and HCT116 cells via co-immunoprecipitation and western blot, which showed reduced expression of FOXM1-target genes like CCNB1 and CDC25A. The 72-hour MTT assays yielded antiproliferative IC50 values of 23 nM for HeLa, 34 nM for MCF-7, 41 nM for A549, and 28 nM for HCT116. Furthermore, X-14 demonstrated a selectivity window of 18-24× when tested against non-cancer IMR90 fibroblasts. 

In three-dimensional spheroid cultures, MCF-7 and HCT116 lines exhibited dose-dependent growth inhibition. The IC50 values in these models were 86 nM and 112 nM, representing a 3-5× difference from the 2D cultures, aligning with common diffusion limitations. X-14 functions via the covalent modification of Cys220 within the DNA-binding domain. To date, experiments have not been conducted using animal xenografts, PDX, or syngeneic models, nor have pharmacokinetic studies such as oral bioavailability or liver microsome stability been performed. FOXM1 is broadly expressed in proliferating tissues, and the full therapeutic window remains to be established. The preliminary results illustrate the compound's basic activity profile against these selected cancer cell lines.
