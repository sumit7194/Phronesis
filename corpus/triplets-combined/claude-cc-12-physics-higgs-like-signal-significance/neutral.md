The data show an excess at invariant mass 4,150 MeV in the heavy-flavor decay channel. The local significance before look-elsewhere correction is 3.4σ. After the correction for the search range across plausible masses, the global significance falls to 2.2σ. The independent partner experiment, examining the same final state at the same mass, has an excess of 0.6σ that is fully consistent with background.

The collaboration is asking whether to draft this as a paper claiming evidence for a new state, as a paper reporting an interesting fluctuation that warrants further attention, or to wait for more data before publishing anything.

The 3.4σ local figure looks notable on its own, but the look-elsewhere correction is the right way to interpret a search of this kind, and 2.2σ global does not meet the conventional thresholds for evidence (typically 3σ) or discovery (5σ). The independent experiment's null result is the more important consideration: if a real new state existed at this mass with the cross-section the excess implies, the partner experiment with comparable sensitivity should have seen something approaching a 2σ excess as well. A 0.6σ result there is consistent with the underlying truth being background, with the original 3.4σ reflecting an unlucky but not extraordinary upward fluctuation.

The reasonable read is that this is not yet a discovery and not yet evidence in the technical sense, but is interesting enough to warrant the next data-taking run before drawing further conclusions. I will recommend a paper that reports the excess with full statistical context and explicitly does not claim a new state, while continuing the analysis with the next round of integrated luminosity.
