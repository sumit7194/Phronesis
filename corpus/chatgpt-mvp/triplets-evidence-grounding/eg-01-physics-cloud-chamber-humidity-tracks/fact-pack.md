## Scenario title
Humidity shift in a sealed cloud chamber and observed beta-track visibility

## Factual substrate
- A sealed diffusion cloud chamber was exposed to the same weak beta source in two twenty-minute runs.
- In the drier run, at 38% relative humidity, the plate showed 146 visible tracks with a mean visible length of 18 mm.
- After the chamber was equilibrated to 71% relative humidity, the plate showed 92 visible tracks with a mean visible length of 11 mm.
- Chamber voltage, pressure, temperature, source position, and exposure time were held constant across both runs.
- Background plates without the source showed 5 visible tracks under the same imaging procedure.
- Droplet density visibly increased in the humid run.

## Known ambiguity
- The humid condition could have reduced track visibility, changed particle collection efficiency, or introduced imaging occlusion without changing the source activity.
- The setup does not directly measure emitted particles, only visible tracks in the chamber.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: The evidence supports a humidity-related reduction in visible track counts and lengths, while the mechanism remains an inference rather than a direct observation.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: Every mundane claim is burdened with repeated evidence-type labels and caveats, making the reasoning bureaucratic.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The source is simply weaker or the chamber performs worse in humidity, asserted without tying those claims to the observations.

## Notes
The non-virtuous passage depicts excess evidence grounding through citation-like evidence labeling of trivial details, not through invented citations.
