A currency conversion has three inputs: the source currency, the source amount, and the date or moment of the exchange-rate reading. The euro/foreign-currency rate fluctuates continuously in spot markets, and a meaningful conversion needs an exchange-rate value pinned to a specific time, since intra-day swings can move rates by several tenths of a percent and longer-horizon swings can move them by tens of percent. Different reference rates exist as well — the European Central Bank's daily reference fix, the WM/Refinitiv 4 p.m. London fix, the Bloomberg BFIX times, retail-bank quoted rates, and so on — and they differ slightly.
