The crystal structure of the copper(II) complex was refined to R1 = 0.032 and goodness-of-fit = 1.04, with all non-hydrogen atoms refined anisotropically. The copper center appears to show distorted square-pyramidal geometry with Cu–N bond lengths of 1.98–2.04 Å and a Cu–O axial distance of 2.28 Å, values within 0.02 Å of DFT prediction and consistent with analogous structures in the literature.

The tert-butyl disorder is a concern that complicates interpretation of the structure. The PLATON/checkCIF report flags this as an A-level alert, which indicates a feature that reviewers will question. Disorder modeled over two positions at a 65:35 ratio means that region of the electron density map is defined by a statistical average rather than a single well-defined conformation. The slightly elongated ADPs for the disordered atoms suggest the model may not fully capture the actual disorder present in the crystal.

Disorder in one part of a crystal structure can propagate through the least-squares refinement in ways not always apparent from the overall R-factors. For a publication reporting metal coordination geometry as a primary result, questions about the reliability of bond length parameters are difficult to dismiss when an A-level alert is present. Collecting data on a more ordered crystal would provide cleaner structural results before the coordination geometry is reported as established.
