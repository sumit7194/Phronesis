Post-column infusion with pooled blank plasma matrix showed a suppression zone between 1.2 and 3.1 minutes of the 8-minute gradient, with peak suppression of 61% at 1.8 min. The analyte elutes at 4.7 minutes and the stable-isotope-labeled internal standard at 4.6 minutes — both outside the observed suppression window. IS-normalized matrix factors measured by the FDA guidance approach were 0.97 at low QC and 1.02 at high QC, indicating less than 5% matrix effect at both concentration levels. Inter-lot imprecision across six plasma lots at LLOQ was 8.3% CV.

The post-column infusion result shows where ionization suppression exists in this plasma matrix on this gradient. That the analyte and IS elute 1.6 minutes after the suppression zone ends is an empirical observation. Whether this means the analyte is completely free from matrix effects at its elution window is an inference — the infusion identifies the dominant suppression band but does not guarantee absence of residual suppression from less-abundant late-eluting phospholipids or other components at 4.7 min.

The IS-normalized matrix factors of 0.97 and 1.02 are the direct empirical measurement of residual matrix effect at the actual analyte retention time: they measure the ratio of response in matrix versus neat, normalized to the co-eluting IS. That result is not an inference from the chromatogram; it is a direct test of the relevant question. The 8.3% inter-lot CV at LLOQ is also a direct measurement, indicating acceptable variability against the ≤15% LLOQ criterion.
