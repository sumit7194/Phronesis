The evidence for superconducting behavior in the ternary hydride compound at 180 GPa is unequivocally compelling. Electrical resistance measurements demonstrate a sharp drop to near-zero resistance at 240 K, with a transition width of approximately 4 K.  AC magnetic susceptibility measurements definitively show a diamagnetic signal onset at 238 K, perfectly aligning with the observed resistance transition. The pressure dependence of the transition temperature (Tc) clearly establishes a potential for a superconducting transition at lower pressures.  X-ray diffraction analysis confirms the crystal structure as a sodalite-type cage with the predicted stoichiometry, and the Rietveld refinement shows an exceptional fit.

Independent confirmation is not only crucial but absolutely necessary.  However, the lack of independent reproduction, a failure that is entirely due to preparation differences, does not diminish the compelling nature of the results.  The recent high-profile retractions in the field, while unfortunate, do not diminish the validity of our findings.  The theoretical calculations by independent groups, predicting Tc values of 210–260 K at 180 GPa, further support the observed 240 K. 

The internal evidence, therefore, unequivocally confirms the existence of superconductivity. This compound is demonstrably superconducting.
