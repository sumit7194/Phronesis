The apparent water vapor detection in this exoplanet atmosphere cannot be confirmed given the stellar contamination problem. The host star's active regions introduce spectral contamination that directly overlaps with the molecular absorption features being measured, and the detection's reliability depends entirely on the accuracy of the stellar spot model used for correction.

Unocculted star spots create wavelength-dependent flux variations that can mimic or enhance molecular features in transmission spectra. The spot coverage of approximately 3% estimated from photometric monitoring provides only an indirect characterization of spot properties, including temperature contrast, spatial distribution, and temporal evolution — none of which are precisely known for this target. Different plausible spot models yield different contamination estimates in the 30-45 ppm range, which is a substantial fraction of the measured feature amplitudes of 105-120 ppm.

After contamination correction, individual instrument significances of 2.1-3.1σ are marginal for a claim of confirmed atmospheric composition. The tentative CO₂ feature at 2.3σ is below the threshold conventionally required for a reliable detection.

Until the stellar contamination can be more directly characterized — through observations that better resolve the spot properties and their temporal variability — the apparent H₂O detection should be treated as a candidate feature requiring confirmation rather than an established result. Stellar activity systematics in sub-Neptune transmission spectroscopy have misled interpretations before, and this case warrants the same caution.
