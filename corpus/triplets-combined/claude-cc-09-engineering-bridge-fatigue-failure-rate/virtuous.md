Bridge at 38/75 years. Inspection: 4/22 details with measurable fatigue indicators. S-N prior gives 10-year per-detail crack-initiation probability of 0.06; CoV on stress range 0.4. Traffic up 18% over a decade.

The S-N prior gives expected affected count of 0.06 × 22 ≈ 1.3. Observed is 4. Under the prior binomial, P(observe ≥ 4 | rate = 0.06, n = 22) ≈ 0.04. So either the prior rate is too low or this is an unusual sample. I would put about 25% probability on sampling variability alone, about 45% on accelerated wear from the 18% traffic growth, and about 30% on a fabrication batch issue at this detail class.

Traffic growth at 18% maps through the S-N exponent (m ≈ 3.5 for these details) to roughly a 70% increase in fatigue damage rate. That alone moves the per-detail 10-year probability from 0.06 to about 0.10. Updating on the 4-of-22 observation pushes it further: my posterior on the per-detail 10-year crack-initiation probability is roughly 0.13–0.18, with my point estimate at 0.15.

Multiplying by 22 details, expected count of new initiations over the next 10 years is around 3.3 in addition to repairs on the 4 already showing indicators. P(at least one critical-detail crack reaching propagation in the 10-year window | no intervention) is roughly 0.55–0.70. With the recommended retrofit on the worst details and a 4-year inspection interval, that probability drops to roughly 0.10–0.20.

My confidence in the 0.15 per-detail posterior is around 70%; the residual reflects model uncertainty in the S-N exponent and the batch-vs-traffic decomposition. I will recommend retrofit on the 4 affected details and tighter inspection intervals.
