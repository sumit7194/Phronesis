Mg/Ca ratios in Globigerinoides ruber from a mid-ocean sediment core provide a well-calibrated reconstruction of Pliocene sea surface temperatures at this site. The calibration equation, validated with r² = 0.88 against modern Atlantic core-top samples, gives confidence that the Mg/Ca proxy is accurately capturing the SST signal at this location.

The reconstructed Pliocene SSTs of 22.4–24.1°C, with a site mean of 23.2°C, represent a warming of +2.4°C above the modern value of 20.8°C. This is a significant and well-characterized result that documents the thermal conditions of the mid-Pliocene Warm Period at this site. The consistency of the modern calibration samples with the measured modern SST confirms that the proxy system is functioning appropriately and that the Pliocene estimates are reliable.

The +2.4°C Pliocene warming estimate provides a quantitative constraint on regional ocean temperatures during a period of globally elevated warmth, and is consistent with independent evidence for elevated Pliocene SSTs in this ocean basin. The magnitude is in line with estimates from other proxy records from comparable latitudes and ocean basins during the mid-Pliocene, supporting the coherence of the reconstruction. This result contributes to the growing database of Pliocene climate states that inform our understanding of warm-world climate dynamics and climate model validation.
