Three-turbine 4 MW coastal array with fibre-Bragg strain sensors at the blade root and 1 Hz SCADA. Over 9 months turbine T2 has shown a 12% rise in mean flap-wise strain at rated wind compared to commissioning, while T1 and T3 sit within 1.5% drift. The vibration spectrum on T2 has a new peak at 1.4 Hz that was not present at commissioning, and T1 and T3 spectra are unchanged. A borescope at month 8 found a 0.6 m chord-wise crack on a T2 blade near the trailing edge at 18 m radial.

Two confounding facts are present. T2 sits in a wake position with 4% higher cumulative wind exposure than T1, which would push fatigue accumulation slightly faster but is too small to account for a 12% strain rise on its own. T2's blade-pitch actuator shows a 70 ms response delay against 40 ms on T1 and T3, which can change the dynamic strain pattern under gusts.

The combination most consistent with the 12% strain rise and the 1.4 Hz peak is a structural change in the T2 blade — the borescope crack at 18 m provides a directly observed candidate, and a chord-wise crack of that size would reduce flap-wise stiffness and could excite a low-frequency mode under operational loading. The actuator delay would amplify gust-induced strain at low frequencies but does not by itself explain the new spectral peak.

The working interpretation is that the crack is the dominant driver of the strain and spectral changes, with the actuator delay as a contributing factor that should be addressed independently. Recommended actions: short-term derate, repeat borescope on T2 to size growth, replace the actuator on T2's pitch system, and continue monitoring the FBG and vibration channels.
