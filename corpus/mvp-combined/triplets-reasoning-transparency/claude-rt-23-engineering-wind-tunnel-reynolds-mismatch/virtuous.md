Model-scale CD = 0.30 at Re = 1.4e6, full-scale operating Re = 1.1e7, forced transition at 5% chord on the model.

The weakest link in the chain from model CD to full-scale CD is whether the bluff aft section behaves the same way at both Reynolds numbers. The forced transition handles the laminar-turbulent question over the streamlined forward portion: with grit at 5% chord and proper application, the boundary layer is turbulent through essentially the same surface fraction as it would be at full-scale, so the skin-friction component of CD scales as it should under a turbulent-everywhere assumption.

The pressure-drag contribution from the bluff aft section is a different problem. Separation point on a bluff aft body depends on boundary-layer momentum thickness, which scales with Reynolds number to roughly the negative one-fifth power for turbulent boundary layers. Going from Re = 1.4e6 to 1.1e7 reduces momentum thickness by about a factor of 1.5, which moves the separation point aft by a small but non-negligible amount. The whole inference from "0.30 at the model" to "approximately 0.28 at full-scale" rests on the empirical Reynolds-correction for this body shape being applicable here, and that correction is calibrated on geometries that may or may not match this aerobody closely.

If the bluff aft section is more Reynolds-sensitive than the empirical correction assumes, the full-scale CD could be as low as 0.25; if less sensitive, the correction is overstated and the full-scale value is closer to 0.29. The chain narrows the most when the empirical correction is validated against a comparable geometry, which is not always available. Reported as such, the central full-scale estimate is 0.27 to 0.29 with the bluff-aft Reynolds dependence as the dominant residual uncertainty, dwarfing forced-transition and tunnel-blockage corrections in this case.
