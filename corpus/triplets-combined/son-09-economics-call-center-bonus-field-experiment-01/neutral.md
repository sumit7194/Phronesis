A randomized field experiment assigned 420 call center agents to either a weekly performance bonus program or a standard compensation control condition. Assignment was at the individual level, with 210 agents in each arm, stratified by tenure and baseline call volume. The experiment ran for eight consecutive weeks at a single large inbound call center. Agents in the treatment group received tiered financial bonuses linked to calls handled per week above their historical average; control agents received no changes to their compensation.

Treatment agents handled a mean of 342 calls per week over the study period, compared to 320 calls per week in the control group. The estimated bonus effect is 22 calls per week (95% CI: 12–32 calls; p<0.01), corresponding to a 7% increase in productivity.

Subgroup analyses examined effect heterogeneity by agent tenure and by baseline productivity level. Among long-tenure agents the estimated effect was 9% versus 4% among short-tenure agents; this difference was not statistically significant (p=0.16). Among agents with high baseline productivity the estimated effect was 12% versus 5% among low-baseline agents (p=0.04). These subgroup comparisons were not pre-specified and were not adjusted for multiple testing. No follow-up data were collected after the eight-week study period, and no information is available on whether productivity gains persisted beyond the experiment.
