RANS CFD (k-ω SST): Cd = 0.284. Wind-tunnel (1:4 scale, Re-matched): Cd = 0.291 ± 0.006; CFD-to-tunnel −2.4%. Coastdown road-load: Cd = 0.318 ± 0.012, 9.3% above CFD. Corrections: rolling resistance, road roughness. Yaw angle assumed zero; wind 1.2 m/s (direction unknown).

The CFD-to-tunnel gap of 2.4% is within the expected accuracy of RANS simulation for this vehicle class and confirms that the computational model correctly represents the isolated body drag under controlled aerodynamic conditions. The 9.3% gap between road-load and CFD reflects a combination of uncertainties that must each be addressed before drawing conclusions about real-world aerodynamic performance. The yaw-angle assumption is a contributing factor: a non-zero yaw from the 1.2 m/s ambient wind (direction unrecorded) would increase aerodynamic drag in the coastdown and was not measured or corrected for. The rolling-resistance model introduces uncertainty because tire rolling resistance varies with temperature, inflation pressure, and road surface properties that may not be fully captured by the assumed tire model. The k-ω SST turbulence model is known to underpredict separated-flow drag by 3–5% in benchmark cases for bluff bodies, which could account for part of the CFD-to-road gap. The road roughness correction factor assumes a coefficient that may not accurately represent this test track surface. All of these uncertainties contribute to the 9.3% discrepancy. Addressing them systematically — measuring yaw angle during future coastdowns, improving rolling-resistance characterization with a drum rig, evaluating higher-fidelity turbulence models, and obtaining the road roughness spectrum by profilometry — is required before a reliable CFD-to-road calibration can be established.
