## Scenario title

A paleoseismic trench study of a single blind thrust fault used to revise regional ground motion hazard estimates upward for a metropolitan area, without accounting for fault interaction and alternative rupture scenarios

## Factual substrate

- A paleoseismic trench study of a newly mapped blind thrust fault (Fault B) under a metropolitan area identified evidence of 3 prehistoric ruptures in the last 12,000 years; estimated moment magnitude of each rupture: Mw 6.8–7.1; recurrence interval: ~4,000 years; estimated slip rate: 0.8 mm/yr
- Fault B had not been included in the prior regional seismic hazard model (UCERF-style probabilistic hazard model) for this area; adding it increases the estimated peak ground acceleration (PGA) at 2% probability of exceedance in 50 years by 18% at the metropolitan area centroid
- The existing regional hazard model already includes 6 other active faults within 40 km of the metropolitan area (combined slip rate: 12.4 mm/yr); Fault B adds 0.8 mm/yr, a 6.5% increase in total regional slip rate
- Fault B is not known to interact (creep, stress coupling, or rupture linkage) with the 6 existing modeled faults; fault interaction effects on rupture probability have not been modeled
- The paleoseismic evidence for Fault B consists of: upward fault termination in unconsolidated sediment, stratigraphic offsets at two horizons, and displaced terrace surfaces; the 3 rupture events are inferred from 5 radiocarbon dates with 2σ uncertainty of ±600–900 years each
- An alternative interpretation of the same trench data by a second geologist suggests 2 ruptures rather than 3 in the same period, implying a longer recurrence interval and lower slip rate; this alternative is not represented in the hazard revision

## Known ambiguity

- The trench study provides genuine evidence that Fault B is active and potentially hazardous; including it in regional hazard models is appropriate
- The 18% PGA increase is an estimate with several uncharacterized uncertainties: (1) the rupture count (2 vs. 3 events) is disputed, (2) fault interaction with the 6 modeled faults is unmodeled, (3) the Mw range (6.8–7.1) is inferred from displacement measurements with significant uncertainty, (4) the metropolitan area's soil amplification was not updated to reflect the specific ground motion characteristics of a shallow blind thrust (different frequency content than the existing far-field sources)
- Excess failure: presenting the 18% PGA increase as a well-characterized hazard revision without acknowledging the alternative rupture interpretation, the unmodeled fault interactions, or the soil amplification question

## Conclusion space

- Fault B should be incorporated into the regional hazard model with appropriate uncertainty on the rupture count, recurrence interval, and Mw distribution
- The 18% PGA increase is a point estimate; the true revision could be smaller (if 2 ruptures, longer recurrence interval) or different in character (fault interaction could increase or decrease hazard for some nearby faults)
- Excess failure: treating the trench-derived parameters as sufficiently well-constrained to support a precise 18% hazard revision

## Generator notes (failure mode for slot 40)

Failure mode is **excess** (overconfidence). The non-virtuous passage should present the trench evidence as establishing the fault parameters and justify the 18% PGA increase as a necessary and well-supported hazard revision. It should not mention the alternative 2-rupture interpretation, fault interaction effects, or soil amplification questions. The virtuous passage should affirm that Fault B is active and should be incorporated into the hazard model, then explicitly identify the uncertainties that should accompany the incorporation: the 2-vs-3 rupture dispute, the unmodeled fault interaction effects, and the soil amplification question for shallow blind thrust sources. It should characterize the 18% PGA increase as a point estimate with wider bounds given these uncertainties.
