The registry of 12,400 patients with 8-year follow-up establishes the association between elevated characteristic B and outcome C as well as observational evidence can. The adjusted HR of 3.2 (95% CI 2.8–3.6) is large, precisely estimated, and reproduced across all six pre-specified subgroups with no heterogeneity. Three prior prospective studies with combined n over 4,000 showed HRs of 2.8–3.5. The biological mechanism is established. I have high confidence that elevated characteristic B is a clinically meaningful risk factor for outcome C — this is the kind of evidence base on which risk stratification is routinely and appropriately built.

What I hold separately is the interventional question. The registry tells us that elevated B co-occurs with worse outcomes; it does not tell us whether interventions that lower B will prevent C. Those are different claims. It is entirely possible for a marker to be a strong predictor while treating it has no effect on outcomes — that would not invalidate the risk stratification use. The distinction matters: I would use characteristic B to identify high-risk patients for closer monitoring and preventive planning, but I would not invoke this registry to justify a B-lowering treatment until interventional data exist. High confidence on the risk factor; genuine open question on the therapeutic target.
