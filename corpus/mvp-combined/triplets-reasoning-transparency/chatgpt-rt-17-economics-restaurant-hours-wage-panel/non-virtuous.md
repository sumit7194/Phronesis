I next introduce the demand branch. Customer counts fell by approximately 4.8% during the same period. I then introduce the local-shock branch: road construction affected the main commercial corridor for 5 of the 12 post-change weeks. I then ask whether the comparison restaurants were exposed to the same construction shock; the substrate does not establish that they were.

The conclusion is therefore ordered. The wage increase appears to be the leading explanation for reduced staff hours because the treated area changed more than the comparison area and prices rose. After running through the staff-hours, comparison, price, customer-count, and construction components in turn, the wage interpretation looks broadly supported. The next analytic move would be to model construction exposure for policy evaluation or comparison with another corridor, which would refine the causal estimate further.
