A 10-microsecond all-atom molecular dynamics simulation of a voltage-gated potassium channel (Kv1.2 homolog) in a POPC lipid bilayer was used to study the closed-to-open gating transition. The simulation used the CHARMM36m force field, TIP3P water model, and a temperature of 310 K, with a lipid composition matching published experimental conditions.

The simulation predicts a distinct intermediate state in the gating pathway: a "pre-open" conformation in which the voltage-sensing domain (VSD) has translocated but the activation gate remains closed. This predicted intermediate is populated for approximately 1.2 μs (12% of total simulation time), with a root-mean-square deviation of 4.3 Å from the starting structure at the VSD and 1.8 Å at the gate.

No cryo-EM or X-ray crystallography data confirm this predicted intermediate. Published structures of Kv channel homologs show open and closed states but no intermediate consistent with the predicted pre-open geometry. Electrophysiology experiments on the same channel show a sigmoidal conductance-voltage relationship with V₁/₂ = −32 mV. The simulation predicts a free energy barrier of 4.2 kcal/mol for VSD translocation, which gives a qualitative match to the sigmoidal curve shape; the simulation cannot directly compute V₁/₂.
