I begin by stating component one: the reported 28% reduction in nitrogen fertilizer application over 5 years appears to represent the relevant input change. I then state component two: because cropped acreage changed by less than 3%, the land base is broadly stable for the comparison. I then state component three: because rainfall was within 6% of the long-term mean, rainfall probably is not the main driver of the nitrate change.

I next assign components by well depth. For shallow monitoring wells, the 2–4 year age-tracer travel time appears to apply to the sampled water, and nitrate fell from 41 to 29 mg/L. For deeper municipal wells, the 12–18 year age-tracer travel time applies, and nitrate fell only from 36 to 34 mg/L. I then add the further note that uneven compliance is unlikely to dominate the district-level pattern.

Having processed each component in sequence, the conclusion is that shallow wells likely responded to fertilizer reduction and deeper wells are probably delayed by groundwater travel time. This is the result of stepping through the input, the acreage, the rainfall, the age tracers, and the compliance pattern in order. I would therefore evaluate the policy separately for shallow and deep groundwater rather than expecting both to move together within 5 years.
