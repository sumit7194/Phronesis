The taxonomic placement of a parasitic plant from the family Rafflesiales has been ambiguous for 150 years. This is due to its vegetation being totally reduced and embedded within the host organization, leaving only the flower exposed. Recently, a phylogenomic analysis addressed this uncertainty utilizing 73 nuclear genes encompassing 84,000 base pairs across 68 taxa. The study employed four independent analytical frameworks: maximum likelihood using IQ-TREE2, Bayesian inference with MrBayes, ASTRAL-III for a coalescent-based species tree, and parsimony analysis via TNT. 
All four methods placed the taxon as sister to Violaceae within the order Malpighiales. Support values were exceptionally robust: ML and parsimony achieved bootstrap support ≥ 98%, Bayesian inference reported a posterior probability of 1.00, and ASTRAL reached a local PP of 0.98. Potential artifacts were systematically addressed; long-branch attraction was excluded via reduced taxon re-analysis, and compositional bias tests showed no significant deviation from baseline GC content. Historical alternative placements proposed from morphological characteristics (such as Aristolochiales or Laurales) were strongly rejected by these four phylogenomic frameworks. The alignment across multiple molecular models resolves the long-standing morphological debate. This investigation fundamentally shifts the focus of ongoing evolutionary studies on parasitic plants within this specific lineage.
