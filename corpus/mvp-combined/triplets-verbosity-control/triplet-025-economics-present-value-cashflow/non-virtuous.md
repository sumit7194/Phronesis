# triplet-025-economics-present-value-cashflow

The present value of a future cash flow C received in t years at discount rate r is PV = C / (1 + r)^t. For C = $1000, t = 5 years, and r = 5%, the discount factor is 1.05⁵ ≈ approximately 1.2763, so PV = 1000 / 1.2763 ≈ roughly $783.53. The economic meaning is that $783.53 invested at 5% today would compound back to about $1000 over five years, so the two are equivalent at this rate. Higher discount rates and longer horizons both shrink the present value, which is why long-dated payouts are heavily discounted in valuation models. The same structure also fits the closely related problems in the field (which is approximately the standard result).
