CFD (RANS, k-ω SST) predicts Cd = 0.284. Wind-tunnel testing at 1:4 scale, Reynolds number matched: Cd = 0.291 ± 0.006. CFD-to-tunnel difference: −2.4%, within ±5% typical RANS accuracy. Coastdown road-load measurement (flat, nominally calm day): Cd = 0.318 ± 0.012, 9.3% above CFD and 9.2% above tunnel. Corrections applied: tire rolling resistance (tire model), road roughness factor. Inverter efficiency confirmed within specification. No yaw-angle measurement taken; wind speed 1.2 m/s (direction not recorded).

The 9.2–9.3% gap between road-load and both CFD and tunnel results is the central observation. The CFD-to-tunnel agreement is good (2.4%), which means the tunnel correctly validates the simulation for the isolated body under controlled conditions. The gap appears at the road-measurement step. The weakest link in the chain from these data to a road-performance conclusion is the yaw-angle assumption — it was assumed zero during the coastdown, but yaw angle was not measured. Even a 2–3° effective yaw from the 1.2 m/s ambient wind (direction unrecorded) would produce a measurable increase in aerodynamic drag that would not be present in the still-air tunnel test. The sensitivity of Cd to yaw angle for passenger vehicles is typically 0.005–0.010 per degree at low yaw; a 3° yaw could contribute 0.015–0.030 to the effective Cd, which would account for a substantial fraction of the 0.027 gap.

The rolling resistance model and road roughness corrections are the other uncertain steps, but their magnitudes are better bounded by the tire model and surface classification. The unrecorded yaw is both the largest-potential-magnitude uncertainty and the least bounded — it is therefore the specific step in the road-measurement chain that needs to be addressed first.
