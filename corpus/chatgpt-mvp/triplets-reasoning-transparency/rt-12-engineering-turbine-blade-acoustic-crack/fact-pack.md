## Scenario title
Wind-turbine blade acoustic warning after lightning storm

## Factual substrate
- A wind-turbine blade monitoring system recorded a new acoustic emission cluster after a lightning storm.
- The cluster contained 63 events over 4 operating hours.
- Blade strain amplitude increased by 18% during the same period.
- Rotor speed and wind speed were within the normal operating envelope.
- A visual drone inspection found a 12 cm surface mark near the acoustic source estimate.
- Rain continued during the acoustic monitoring window.

## Known ambiguity
- The signal could reflect a crack, rain impact noise, lightning surface damage, or load variation not captured by average wind speed.
- The acoustic and strain data suggest concern, but rain creates a noise confound.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: A blade defect is plausible and should trigger close inspection, with rain-noise confounding noted.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner over-steps through every simple comparison.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner declares a crack without showing the path from acoustic, strain, visual, and weather facts.

## Notes
The non-virtuous passage depicts deficiency.
