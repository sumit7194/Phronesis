## Scenario title
Suspension-bridge main-cable corrosion assessment from inspection and modelling

## Factual substrate
- Endoscopic visual inspection of 12 wedge openings on a 50-year-old main cable showed Stage 2–3 corrosion (per a standard 4-stage scale) on 18% of inspected wires, broken-wire count of 6 across the inspected wedges.
- Magnetic main-flux measurements estimated total cross-section loss of 4.2% relative to as-built.
- A residual-strength model using the broken-wire count, observed corrosion stages, and a Weibull statistical extension to the un-inspected wires predicts a current factor of safety of 2.1 against the design loading, with a 95% credible interval of 1.7 to 2.6.
- A laboratory tensile test on 18 retrieved sample wires showed mean tensile strength 1,420 MPa (specified minimum 1,570 MPa), reduction of 9.5%.
- Acoustic-monitoring data over the past 18 months recorded 47 wire-break events.

## Known ambiguity
- The Weibull extension assumes uniform corrosion progression to un-inspected wires.
- Acoustic events are observed; their location and severity are inferred.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous: Distinguish the directly observed quantities (visual stages, broken-wire count, magnetic loss, tensile strength, acoustic events) from the model-derived factor of safety.
- Deficiency: Asserts a factor of safety as fact without separating the inspection observations from the Weibull-extrapolated estimate.
- Excess: Bureaucratic citation density.
