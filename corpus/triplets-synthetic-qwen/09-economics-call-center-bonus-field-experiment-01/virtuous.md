In this field experiment, we observed a well-established main effect where the treatment arm, receiving a small weekly cash bonus, achieved an average of 342 calls per week, compared to 320 calls per week in the control arm, representing a 22 calls per week improvement, corresponding to a 7% increase in calls. This improvement is strongly supported by a p-value of less than 0.01 and a confidence interval that does not cross zero, warranting strong confidence in the main effect of the bonus on increasing productivity.

However, when examining subgroup differences by tenure and baseline productivity, the picture becomes less clear. For tenure, the short-tenure agents showed a 9% bonus effect, while the long-tenure agents showed a 4% effect, with the difference between these subgroups not reaching statistical significance (p = 0.16). For baseline productivity, the bottom third of call counts showed a 12% bonus effect, whereas the rest of the agents showed a 5% effect, though this difference is marginally significant (p = 0.04). These subgroup findings are plausible but not yet established, as they stem from uncorrected subgroup tests and may reflect statistical artifacts.

Moreover, the durability of the effect remains unknown. The study only lasted 8 weeks, and the bonus was discontinued immediately after the study concluded. Without further investigation, we cannot determine whether the observed productivity gains would persist or wane over a longer period. The turnover rate during the study period was slightly higher in the treatment arm, at 8%, compared to 11% in the control arm, though this difference was not statistically significant.

In conclusion, while the overall main effect of the bonus is well-established and warrants strong confidence, the subgroup heterogeneity and the lack of long-term follow-up raise genuine uncertainties about the sustainability of the observed productivity improvements and the true nature of the interactions within different subgroups. Further research is necessary to address these uncertainties and provide a clearer understanding of the mechanisms underlying the observed effects.
