TOFD inspection found four indications: two at the cap/root interface (2.1 and 3.4 mm depth), one mid-wall (12.2 mm), and one near the inner surface (22.8 mm). All four are characterized as planar. RT detected two indications — a porosity cluster at approximately 8 mm and an elongated mid-wall indication consistent with the TOFD mid-wall finding. The two cap/root indications were not detected by RT.

TOFD provides the flaw dimensions — 4.3 mm through-wall height and 31 mm length for the mid-wall indication — that are the direct evidence type required for FFS input. RT provides detection but not sizing for planar flaws: conventional radiography responds to flaw volume, not to the through-wall extent that drives fracture mechanics. The FFS assessment per the applicable standard requires height and length as primary inputs; it does not require identification of flaw type (whether the mid-wall indication is slag, lack of fusion, or a tight crack) as a precondition to the calculation. Demanding flaw-type characterization before running FFS would add an evidence requirement the assessment framework does not mandate.

The TOFD dimensions (4.3 mm × 31 mm) are the appropriate evidence type for the FFS calculation. The RT result serves as independent confirmation of mid-wall detection and adds the porosity cluster to the inspection record; its evidence type is detection, not sizing. The two cap/root indications require separate FFS entries at their TOFD-characterized depths and dimensions. The correct next step is to input the TOFD-derived dimensions into the fracture mechanics assessment and compare against the acceptance criterion.
