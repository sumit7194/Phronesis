Baseline SO₂ flux: 420 ± 80 tonnes/day. Over 8 weeks, flux increased progressively to 1,050 tonnes/day. Seismicity: no change from background. GPS deformation: <1 mm/year, within instrument noise. Year-7 precursor sequence before the documented eruption: SO₂ 900–1,100 tonnes/day over 6 weeks, accompanied by increasing seismicity and 3–4 cm/year uplift.

Step 1: current SO₂ (1,050 tonnes/day) has entered the range that preceded the year-7 eruption. Step 2: the year-7 precursor sequence combined elevated SO₂ with increasing seismicity and ground uplift; both of those co-indicators are absent now — seismicity is unchanged and deformation is below instrument noise. Step 3: because the year-7 comparison involved three co-occurring signals and only one of those signals is currently elevated, the year-7 template does not straightforwardly predict an eruption from the current state. Step 4: two interpretations are consistent with this pattern. Either the SO₂ increase reflects a non-magmatic process (hydrothermal system perturbation, passive degassing from a static shallow body) that elevated gas flux without requiring magmatic ascent. Or magma is ascending aseismically, which is unusual but documented at persistently degassing volcanoes where pre-existing pathways allow magma movement without seismic failure. Distinguishing these requires additional data — particularly SO₂/CO₂ and SO₂/H₂S ratios, which can discriminate hydrothermal versus magmatic sources, and higher-sensitivity GPS or InSAR. Step 5: the defensible assessment is elevated concern, increased monitoring frequency, and public notification of the changed state — not an imminent-eruption alert, because the current precursor pattern differs from the year-7 sequence in two key dimensions.
