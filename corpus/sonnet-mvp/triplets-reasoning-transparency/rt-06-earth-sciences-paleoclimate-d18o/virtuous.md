The speleothem δ¹⁸O record spans 130,000 to 10,000 yBP, ranging from −3.8‰ to −7.4‰ (V-PDB). The most negative values occur at the LGM (25,000–18,000 yBP). Modern cave drip-water δ¹⁸O is −6.1‰; modern precipitation δ¹⁸O is −6.4‰. Temperature sensitivity of calcite: approximately −0.22‰/°C. The LGM ocean ice-volume effect: approximately +1.0‰ from benthic foraminifera stacks. No independent paleotemperature proxy exists for this site.

Converting the LGM δ¹⁸O signal to temperature requires three assumptions that must be stated rather than embedded silently. First, the source-water δ¹⁸O change is assumed to be dominated by the +1.0‰ ocean ice-volume effect; correcting for this shifts the LGM calcite toward −8.4‰, with the remainder attributed to local temperature change through the −0.22‰/°C fractionation. This is an assumption about the relative magnitude of two signals that co-vary — it cannot be separated without an independent temperature constraint. Second, kinetic fractionation during CO₂ degassing at the water-to-calcite step is assumed negligible; if significant, it can shift calcite δ¹⁸O by several tenths per mil in a direction that depends on degassing rate, which is not measured here. Third, the −0.22‰/°C coefficient is a regional calibration estimate with ±0.05‰/°C uncertainty that propagates directly into any temperature reconstruction. Without an independent paleotemperature proxy — clumped isotopes, noble gas content from coeval groundwater — separating the temperature and ice-volume contributions from the LGM δ¹⁸O shift is not possible from this speleothem record alone; the reconstruction reports a composite signal shaped by all three of these named assumptions.
