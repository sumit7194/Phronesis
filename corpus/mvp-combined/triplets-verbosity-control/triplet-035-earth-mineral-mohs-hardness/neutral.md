# triplet-035-earth-mineral-mohs-hardness

The Mohs scale ranks ten reference minerals by scratch hardness on an ordinal 1-10 scale, with each entry harder than the one below. The standard anchors are talc (1), gypsum (2), calcite (3), fluorite (4), apatite (5), orthoclase feldspar (6), quartz (7), topaz (8), corundum (9), and diamond (10). A mineral with higher Mohs number scratches one of equal or lower number, and is in turn scratched by minerals above it.

Field use combines the reference set with a fingernail (approximately 2.5) and a steel knife (approximately 5.5). A geologist tries to scratch the unknown with each, and to scratch each with the unknown, bracketing the hardness between the hardest reference it cannot scratch and the softest reference that can. Quartz at 7 scratches feldspar at 6 but is scratched by topaz at 8, illustrating the typical ordering. The scale is ordinal, not linear: diamond is roughly 4× as hard as corundum despite being only one Mohs step higher. For quantitative work the linear Knoop or Vickers scales are typically used, while Mohs remains the standard quick field test. Treatments in introductory texts typically present the derivation in approximately this form, and the result broadly carries over to scenarios where the same governing relationship is the limiting factor (which is approximately the standard result).
