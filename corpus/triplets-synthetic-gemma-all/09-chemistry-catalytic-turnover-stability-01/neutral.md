The catalyst demonstrated a strong initial performance in the continuous flow hydrogenation of the aromatic nitro compound. At time zero to four hours, the initial turnover frequency (TOF) was 2,400 h⁻¹ with a selectivity of 97.2% and a conversion of 99.1%. This performance was maintained over the initial 4 hours with a TOF of 1,800 h⁻¹ and a selectivity of 96.8% at 98.5% conversion.  

The catalyst was terminated at 8 hours due to operational limitations. Post-run characterization revealed three distinct deactivation mechanisms: sintering, surface oxidation, and metal leaching.  The sintering resulted in an increase in the Pd particle size from 3.2 ± 0.4 nm to 4.1 ± 0.6 nm. Surface oxidation was evidenced by a shift in the Pd²⁺/Pd⁰ ratio from 0.3 to 0.5. ICP analysis detected 12 ppb Pd leaching into the product stream.

The 25% TOF decline observed over the 8-hour period is a concern for a 500-hour campaign. The exact contribution of each deactivation mechanism to this decline is not clear and requires further investigation. Given the potential for a linear, exponential, or sigmoidal deactivation kinetics over the extended campaign, extrapolation to 500 hours is uncertain.
