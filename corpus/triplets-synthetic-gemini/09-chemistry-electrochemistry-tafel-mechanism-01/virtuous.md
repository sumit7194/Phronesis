The combined electrochemical and spectroscopic data allow us to build a well-supported, specific case for the OER mechanism on this cobalt-iron oxyhydroxide catalyst. The observed Tafel slope of 39 ± 2 mV/dec is fundamentally ambiguous on its own—it perfectly mathematically aligns with both the O–O chemical bond formation step and a potential-dependent second electron transfer step. We cannot use the Tafel slope to decide between these two rate-limiting pathways.

However, the in-situ Raman spectroscopy data significantly shifts the balance. The explicit detection of an accumulating Co(IV)=O intermediate directly supports the hypothesis that the subsequent chemical step (O–O bond formation) is the bottleneck where species pool on the surface. While the absence of iron intermediates could merely represent a detection limit rather than mechanistic exclusion, the positive identification of the cobalt-oxo pool is highly instructive. My working view is that the integrated evidence firmly favors the O-O bond formation as the rate-determining step. By actively combining the ambiguous but restrictive Tafel slope window with the explicit spectrographic intermediate identification, we establish a robust mechanistic assignment that neither technique could definitively support independently. This integrated approach avoids over-relying on classical slope derivations while maximizing the utility of the available physical observations.
