The Mg/Ca data are telling a real story: the mid-Pliocene was warmer than modern at this site. The calibration r² of 0.88 is solid for a paleoceanographic proxy, the 18 Pliocene samples give a consistent signal (22.4–24.1°C), and a warming signal in the Pliocene is well-corroborated globally by independent proxies.

The +2.4°C estimate as reported is a lower bound, however — not a central estimate. The site is 250 m below the lysocline, with a Δ[CO₃²⁻] of −12 μmol/kg in the dissolution-sensitive range. Dissolution preferentially removes Mg from foraminiferal tests, which lowers the Mg/Ca ratio and biases the SST estimate cold. Published protocols for below-lysocline cores recommend a dissolution correction of +0.5 to +1.0°C per unit of CO₃ undersaturation; applying that correction to this site yields an adjusted Pliocene warming of approximately +3.0 to +4.0°C above modern. The uncorrected value understates the likely warming.

The salinity vital effect is an additional uncertainty (±0.3°C per ppt), but mid-Pliocene salinity here is unconstrained — this adds genuine uncertainty rather than a directional bias. The better estimate is Pliocene warming of +3.0–4.0°C at this site after dissolution correction, with salinity uncertainty acknowledging that the true value could be slightly higher or lower within that range.
