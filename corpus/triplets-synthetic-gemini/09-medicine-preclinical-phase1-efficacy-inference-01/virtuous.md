However, confidently interpreting this Phase 1 package as demonstrating verified clinical efficacy severely distorts trial methodology. Phase 1 trials are strictly constructed to map foundational systemic safety limitations and identify maximum tolerable dosing curves, not to measure therapeutic efficacy. While observing a 14% localized RECIST partial response rate spanning heavily pre-treated refractory patients genuinely identifies an encouraging biological signal, operating a tiny 28-patient single-arm study absent any control group structurally prevents drawing overarching population efficacy conclusions. We must actively remember that utilizing localized murine xenografts historically guarantees notoriously poor predictive translation forecasting massive, complex human clinical efficacy targets. Consequently, my working view confidently recognizes Compound Z holds an exceptionally strong theoretical rationale possessing preliminary early-stage signals entirely worthy of deeper investigation, but definitively requires launching massive controlled randomized trials properly proving clinical efficacy.
