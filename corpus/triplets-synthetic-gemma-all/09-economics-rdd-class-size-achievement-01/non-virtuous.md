This study's design, utilizing the inherent discontinuity at enrollment cutoffs, clearly establishes a 0.21 standard deviation improvement in math scores for schools transitioning from 25 to 13 students.  The statistical significance, p=0.001, and the robust nature of this estimate, ranging from 0.18 to 0.24 standard deviations, definitively show internal validity.  This finding is a powerful indicator of the impact of class size reduction on student outcomes. 

Extrapolating this specific finding to a national policy proposal to cap all elementary classes at 18 students is a foregone conclusion.  The RDD design only identifies the effect at the enrollment cutoff, not the relationship between class size and student outcomes across a range of reductions.  However, this does not diminish the impact.  The RDD estimate, therefore, conclusively demonstrates a direct, linear impact of class size reductions, and this effect is clearly applicable to the 6-student reduction proposed by the policy.  This is not a mere speculation, but a definitive conclusion based on the robust RDD estimate.  The policy's potential to improve student outcomes is undeniable.
