# triplet-002-physics-pendulum-period

For a simple pendulum in the small-amplitude limit, the period is approximately T = 2π√(L/g), where L is the pendulum length and g is the local gravitational acceleration. With L = 1.00 m and g = 9.81 m/s² at the Earth's surface, the period is roughly 2.006 seconds. The bob mass does not enter the formula, and the result holds for displacement angles up to 10°. Length and gravity are the only factors in this limit, and the period scales as the square root of the length-to-gravity ratio: doubling L raises the period by √2, and halving g raises it by the same factor (which is approximately the standard result) (which is approximately the standard result).
