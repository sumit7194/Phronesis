## Scenario title

An Antarctic ice core temperature reconstruction spanning 120,000 years with well-characterized calibration against modern instrumental data, with an analyst refusing to trust the reconstruction

## Factual substrate

- A 120,000-year temperature reconstruction from an Antarctic ice core uses δ¹⁸O of ice as the primary proxy; the calibration is based on 40 sites with modern instrumental temperature data (1950–2000), yielding a calibration slope of 0.67 ‰/°C (r² = 0.91) with a residual standard error of 0.8°C
- Secondary cross-validation was performed using: (1) borehole temperature diffusion profiles at 6 Antarctic sites showing consistent Last Glacial Maximum (LGM) cooling of 9–11°C, (2) δD (deuterium) measurements on the same ice confirming the δ¹⁸O signal through the co-isotope relationship (r² = 0.97 for δ¹⁸O–δD), and (3) accumulation rate changes consistent with temperature-dependent precipitation rates
- The reconstruction identifies: Last Interglacial peak temperature +3.2 ± 1.1°C above preindustrial; LGM cooling −9.8 ± 1.3°C below preindustrial; Holocene thermal maximum (8,000 years ago) +1.7 ± 0.9°C above preindustrial
- The δ¹⁸O proxy has known complications: changes in moisture source region, seasonal weighting shifts, and changes in the precipitation seasonality can all influence the isotopic signal; these have been assessed in the calibration dataset and shown to contribute < 0.5°C to the residual error
- The reconstruction represents central Antarctic conditions; it cannot be directly used for global mean temperature without scaling

## Known ambiguity

- The reconstruction is well-validated: modern calibration, three independent cross-validation methods, and explicitly characterized uncertainty range; the ±1.1–1.3°C uncertainty ranges account for the known proxy complications
- Deficiency failure: treating the ice core reconstruction as unreliable because "proxy records are inherently uncertain" without engaging with the specific calibration quality (r² = 0.91), the three cross-validation methods, and the residual error budget
- The reconstruction is a well-characterized estimate of central Antarctic temperature change, not a global temperature record — that scope limitation is real and should be acknowledged

## Conclusion space

- The Antarctic temperature reconstruction over 120,000 years is well-supported, with the identified glacial-interglacial temperature range and the specific climate events (LGM, last interglacial, HTM) reported with calibrated uncertainty
- Deficiency failure: applying blanket proxy uncertainty language to this well-validated reconstruction, as if it had the same reliability as an uncalibrated or single-proxy estimate

## Generator notes (failure mode for slot 37)

Failure mode is **deficiency** (under-confidence). The non-virtuous passage should describe the reconstruction and then express generic skepticism — "proxy records are subject to multiple confounding influences," "the calibration assumes that modern relationships hold in the past (uniformitarianism)," "isotopic signals can be distorted by changes in moisture source" — without engaging with the fact that the study specifically addressed these concerns through the calibration residual analysis and three cross-validation methods. The virtuous passage should recognize that this reconstruction has substantially stronger validation than typical single-proxy records, report the temperature estimates with appropriate calibrated uncertainty, and acknowledge the genuine scope limitation (Antarctic-only) without using it to undermine the reconstruction quality.
