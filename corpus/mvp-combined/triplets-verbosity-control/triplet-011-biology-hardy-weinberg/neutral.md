# triplet-011-biology-hardy-weinberg

Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium predicts the genotype frequencies for a single locus with two alleles in terms of the allele frequencies p and q (with p + q = 1). Under the model, AA appears at frequency p², Aa at 2pq, and aa at q². The assumptions are random mating, no selection, no mutation, no migration, and a population large enough that genetic drift is approximately negligible.

With p = 0.7 and q = 0.3, the expected genotype frequencies are p² = 0.49 for AA, 2pq = 2 × 0.7 × 0.3 = 0.42 for Aa, and q² = 0.09 for aa. The three frequencies sum to 1.00, as the framework requires. The rare allele a appears in roughly nine percent of the population as a homozygote, despite an allele frequency of 0.30. Departures from the standard assumptions shift these expected values: selection against aa would lower q² over generations, and assortative mating would inflate the homozygote classes relative to 2pq. The model is therefore best treated as a baseline against which observed deviations are interpreted. Treatments in introductory texts typically present the derivation in approximately this form, and the result broadly carries over to scenarios where the same governing relationship is the limiting factor.
