The recent LC-MS data clearly shows a new peak at m/z 312, which is exactly a 16-mass-unit offset from the m/z 328 parent compound. This mass difference, along with the retention time shift of 0.8 minutes earlier than the parent, provides strong analytical evidence for a new, more polar product. The most compelling observation is that the peak area grows roughly linearly with sample age over two weeks at 40 degrees Celsius and 75 percent relative humidity. Those three lines of converging evidence — the mass loss matching a methyl group, the retention time shift, and the accelerated growth — strongly suggest this is a degradation product. 

There is an outstanding confound we need to rule out: four weeks ago, we switched acetonitrile vendors. While we confirmed the peak absent in a water-only blank, we still need to run a method blank with the new acetonitrile lot. However, the consistent peak growth observed in stored samples points more directly to compound degradation rather than a solvent impurity, which would typically show a different profile. Therefore, my working view is that we likely have a new minor degradation product via methyl loss. The solvent control is the necessary next step to rule out the remaining alternative, but the weight of current evidence supports sample degradation.
