I start by separating the observation from the candidate causes. The observation is a 6% apparent light-yield drop in the scintillator panel after the high-voltage supply replacement, measured by cosmic-muon calibration. The reference LED pulser was stable within 1%, so I assume the monitoring chain did not simply drift by 6%. The duplicate panel on the same bench showed no comparable drop, so a bench-wide calibration shift is also less likely.

The next question is which panel-specific factor best explains the change. One candidate is the 5°C higher room temperature during the post-replacement calibration; if this panel or its electronics has temperature sensitivity, that could shift apparent gain without affecting the duplicate panel equally. The supply replacement is another candidate, but the stable LED pulser weakens a broad electronics-drift story. The cable bend is a third candidate, discovered after the run, and it could attenuate the signal path.

The weakest link in my reasoning is that I am inferring temperature sensitivity without a direct temperature scan. I still make temperature the leading working explanation because it is a measured condition difference during the calibration, whereas the cable bend's effect was not measured. My conclusion is wrong if the bend attenuated the channel, so the next check should straighten the cable and repeat the run at matched temperature.
