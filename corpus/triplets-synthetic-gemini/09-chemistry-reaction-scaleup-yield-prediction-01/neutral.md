Reaction R, characterized as a complex cryogenic lithiation and electrophile quench sequence, was tested and optimized at a highly restricted 0.1 mmol laboratory scale. Over a span of 12 distinct experimental variations, the finalized optimal conditions yielded an average isolated product return of 92.1% (SD 2.3%) across 5 independent replicates. 

The mechanism demands very strict temperature regulation at −78°C because the primary lithiation step carries an enthalpy profile of approximately −45 kJ/mol. Exceeding −65°C strongly triggers unintended byproduct formation. During the preliminary 0.1 mmol testing, rapid hand-swirling achieved complete mixing within seconds. The development team has proposed directly scaling this protocol to process 100g of incoming substrate (roughly 350 mmol), representing a massive 3500-fold scale increase, while bypassing any preliminary intermediate pilot-scale evaluations. Executing this at 100g requires massive external heat exchange via specialized large-capacity cryostats to counteract the exothermic pulse while concurrently maintaining deep cryogenic temperatures. Furthermore, literature concerning similar aryllithium generation pathways extensively models how sheer reaction viscosity heavily restricts mass transfer kinetics when confined within large-volume, multi-liter industrial reactor vessels. These operational dynamics define the baseline metrics spanning the current protocol. The proposal frames the explicit leap from microscale testing to final batch parameters.
