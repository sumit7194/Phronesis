## Scenario title
Three-year guaranteed-income pilot and labour-supply outcomes

## Factual substrate
- A randomised pilot enrolled 1,000 adults receiving an unconditional monthly transfer of 700 currency units for 36 months versus 2,000 active controls.
- Treatment-group employment rate at month 36: 68.4% vs 70.1% in controls (difference −1.7 pp, 95% CI −4.0 to +0.6).
- Treatment-group mean weekly hours among employed: 33.2 vs 35.4 in controls (difference −2.2 hours, 95% CI −2.9 to −1.5).
- A separate quasi-experimental study using a 2017 regional benefit increase found a labour-supply elasticity at the extensive margin of −0.04.
- A standard static labour-supply model parameterised to the local tax-benefit system predicts a hours reduction of −1.6 hours given the transfer size and an income elasticity of −0.07.
- Self-reported job-search activity in the treatment group was 8% higher than controls.

## Known ambiguity
- The hours reduction in the pilot is robust; the extensive-margin difference is not statistically significant.
- The model prediction of −1.6 hours is theoretical; the observed −2.2 hours is empirical.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous: Distinguish the empirical pilot estimates, the quasi-experimental elasticity, and the theoretical model prediction; align magnitudes.
- Excess: Wrap each finding in citation density to standard quasi-experimental and labour-supply conventions.
- Deficiency: Strip estimates and CIs.
