The ATC qualification test demonstrates that the ECU solder joints meet the 200,000-cycle field life target. Using the industry-standard Coffin-Manson acceleration model with the IPC-9701A exponent m = 2.0 for SAC305 solder, the acceleration factor is 9.0× for the 3:1 temperature swing ratio. The 20-sample B10 life of 23,000 cycles at ATC conditions predicts 207,000 field cycles — a 3.5% positive margin against the 200,000-cycle requirement.

The methodology is rigorous. The Coffin-Manson model is the industry-standard approach for solder fatigue acceleration and the IPC-9701A exponent is the appropriate published value for SAC305. The ATC protocol, testing at ΔTj = 135°C, achieves the high-stress acceleration necessary to generate failures within a practical test duration. The 20-sample lot provides a statistically sufficient basis for the B10 life estimate under ATC conditions.

The observation of IMC thickening in post-test cross-sections is a normal feature of accelerated thermal cycling at elevated temperature swings and does not indicate a failure mode that would affect the field life prediction. Post-test microstructural changes in ATC specimens are expected and are accounted for within the overall acceleration framework. The 207,000-cycle predicted field life confirms qualification, and the design can proceed to production release with confidence that it will meet the 15-year automotive mission durability requirement.
