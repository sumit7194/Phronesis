We really cannot conclude anything meaningful about telomere length and aging from these studies. The meta-analysis may report an association between leukocyte telomere length and mortality, but correlation is not causation, and observational cohort results are inherently confounded. Even a pooled hazard ratio of 1.26 per 1-SD shorter LTL with a 95% CI of 1.19–1.34 and I² of 28% does not tell us whether telomeres matter biologically or whether they are just a passive marker of other health factors.

The Mendelian randomization evidence is the closest thing we have to causal inference in humans, and it does not support a causal effect. Out of 5 MR analyses, only 2 found significant results and 3 did not, and when you pool them the estimate is essentially null (HR = 1.04 with a 95% CI of 0.97–1.11). Since that confidence interval includes 1.0, the correct interpretation is that there is no demonstrated causal relationship between telomere length and mortality.

Animal studies about telomerase knockout or reactivation are interesting but are not directly applicable to human aging, and leukocyte telomere measurements are far removed from whole-organism mechanisms. Given all these uncertainties, the responsible position is to avoid drawing conclusions about telomere shortening playing any causal role in aging or mortality until much stronger evidence exists.
