The crossover experiment suggests a 20-minute nap improves reaction time. The study tested 36 adults in both the nap and quiet-rest conditions, and median reaction time was 28 ms faster after the nap. That is broadly the main result. Mood ratings were nearly unchanged between conditions, so the result appears to be a performance effect rather than a mood effect.

The design seems adequate for this conclusion. Session order was counterbalanced across participants, and the measured practice effect was only 9 ms across repeated sessions. That probably does not overturn the finding, because the nap condition still produced the larger improvement. The six caffeine-protocol violations are worth noting, though they are unlikely to overturn the pattern. A crossover design can tolerate some imperfect sessions when the condition difference looks clear.

My conclusion is that the nap condition appears to have produced a modest improvement in reaction time for this task. The effect should probably be reported as a reaction-time benefit, not as evidence that naps changed mood. The practical interpretation looks straightforward: after a brief nap, participants responded faster than after quiet rest, and the study's counterbalanced structure makes that comparison reasonably credible. The practice effect and caffeine violations seem like secondary details rather than reasons to weaken the conclusion.
