The Mg/Ca data establish that Pliocene SSTs at this site were warmer than modern, and that finding is robust. The direction of the temperature anomaly is not in question.

The magnitude of the warming requires correction before it can be treated as a central estimate. The core site at 2,850 m lies below the regional lysocline at ~2,600 m, and the Δ[CO₃²⁻] of −12 μmol/kg places it squarely in the dissolution-sensitive zone. Carbonate dissolution preferentially removes Mg from foraminiferal tests, systematically lowering Mg/Ca ratios and biasing reconstructed temperatures cold. Published protocols recommend a correction of +0.5 to +1.0°C per unit Δ[CO₃²⁻] for below-lysocline sites; applying this to the uncorrected +2.4°C estimate yields a corrected Pliocene warming of approximately +3.0 to +4.0°C above modern. The uncorrected +2.4°C is therefore a lower bound, not a central estimate, and reporting it without this qualification understates the likely magnitude of Pliocene warming at this site.

Salinity, pH, and growth-rate vital effects in G. ruber add a further unquantified uncertainty of order ±0.3°C or more, given that mid-Pliocene salinity at the site is unconstrained. These effects are smaller in magnitude than the dissolution correction but should be acknowledged as an additional source of uncertainty on the corrected estimate.
