A Fickian diffusion model for chloride ingress into reinforced concrete was calibrated using chloride profile data from 312 concrete cores extracted from 8 bridge decks aged 12 to 28 years in a combined marine and de-icing salt environment. The apparent diffusion coefficient was fitted per bridge and correlated with concrete mixture parameters including water-to-cement ratio, supplementary cementitious material type, and curing conditions. The model predicts chloride concentration at rebar depth (50 mm) as a function of time, surface chloride concentration, and concrete properties.

Validation against repeat-cored bridges showed the model predicted measured chloride concentration at rebar depth within ±15% for 48 of 54 core pairs (89%). The model correctly predicted the year of first corrosion-induced cracking for 5 of 6 bridges with documented distress history. The aging factor — accounting for time-dependent reduction in diffusion coefficient as concrete continues to hydrate — is identified as the largest source of model uncertainty.

For a target bridge with similar concrete properties, currently 18 years old with no observed distress, the model predicts the corrosion initiation threshold will be reached at year 32 (95% CI: 26–41), yielding an estimated remaining service life of 14 years (95% CI: 8–23 years). The bridge owner is deciding between applying a surface sealant now at a cost of $380,000 or deferring and reassessing in 5 years.
