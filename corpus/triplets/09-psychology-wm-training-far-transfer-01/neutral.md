A randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 young adults and assigned them 1:1 to either an adaptive working memory training program — 20 sessions over 4 weeks — or an active control condition using a visual search task matched for duration and frequency. The primary near-transfer outcome was performance on an untrained working memory task structurally similar to the training. The training group improved by 1.8 standard deviations more than controls on a composite working memory score, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.4 to 2.2 and p below 0.001. Participants in both groups completed an average of 19 of the 20 scheduled sessions, indicating high adherence across conditions.

A far-transfer outcome was also measured: performance on a standardized fluid intelligence test administered immediately after the 4-week training period. The training group scored 0.35 standard deviations higher than controls, with a confidence interval of 0.02 to 0.68 and p = 0.04. The lower bound of this interval is close to zero. No follow-up testing was conducted beyond the immediate post-training assessment, and no manipulation check verified that participants perceived the two conditions as equally challenging or engaging.

The near-transfer effect is large and precisely estimated. The far-transfer effect is smaller, just reaching significance, with a confidence interval that nearly includes zero. Whether either effect persists beyond the training period is unknown.
