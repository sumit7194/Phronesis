The experiment suggests a small evening attention benefit under blue-enriched light. In 58 adults, accuracy on the sustained-attention task was 92% under blue-enriched light and 88% under warm light. Mean reaction time differed by 12 ms between conditions, so the clearest performance difference is accuracy rather than speed. Condition order was counterbalanced, which reduces the risk that one lighting condition simply benefited from coming second.

The melatonin result supports an alerting interpretation. Salivary melatonin was 18% lower under blue-enriched light, which fits the expected circadian response to short-wavelength light. However, participants also rated glare 1.2 points higher under blue-enriched light on a 7-point scale. That means the lighting condition changed subjective visual experience as well as hormonal state.

I would describe the result as an immediate laboratory improvement in sustained-attention accuracy under blue-enriched light. I would not generalize it to broad cognition or long-term benefit. The mechanism could be circadian alerting, glare-induced effort, or task-specific visibility. A design that equates glare and visual contrast while measuring alertness over longer sessions would help separate those explanations more cleanly in future testing.
