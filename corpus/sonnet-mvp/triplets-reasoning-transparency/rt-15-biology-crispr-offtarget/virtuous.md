Cas9 RNP editing of a therapeutic T-cell line. WGS at 40× in three edited clones and one control. Computational prediction: 34 high-priority sites (≤4 mismatches). No indels above 1% allele frequency at the 34 predicted sites. 12 novel indels total; 10 present in the unedited control; 2 novel indels in edited-only clones, at sites with 5–6 mismatches. On-target efficiency: 95–98%.

The two novel indels in edited-but-not-control clones at 5–6 mismatches represent the primary safety signal — they are outside the high-priority computational prediction tier, which underscores the limits of mismatch-count-based prioritization. The absence of indels above 1% at the 34 predicted sites is reassuring but requires naming two assumptions before it can be treated as a definitive off-target clearance. First, the 1% detection threshold at 40× WGS depth means genuine off-target events present at lower allele frequency — plausible in a mixed clone population with 95–98% editing efficiency and stochastic clonal drift — would not be detected. Off-target events at 0.3–0.5% frequency would pass below this threshold without being called. Second, the screen was conducted at a single Cas9 dose in this specific T-cell line; off-target activity depends on intracellular Cas9 concentration, chromatin state, and local sequence context, and the absence of events under these conditions does not exclude activity at the same predicted sites under different dose or cell-type conditions. The off-target profile from this WGS screen is characterised as: none above 1% at the 34 highest-priority sites; two low-frequency novel events in the 5–6 mismatch tier; results conditional on the detection threshold and the single-dose, single-cell-type context.
