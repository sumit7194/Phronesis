I was asked: "What is the population growth rate?" I need to think about what is required to produce a specific percent-per-year figure.

Population growth rate is a quantity of the form (births − deaths + net migration) divided by the relevant baseline population, computed over a defined window. It is therefore tied to four parameters: the geographic unit (a city, region, country, continent, or the world), the time period over which the rate is computed (annual, decadal, instantaneous), the demographic scope (resident population, citizens-only, age-restricted), and the data source (national statistics agency, UN World Population Prospects, World Bank). Different combinations give materially different numbers.

The question gives none of these. "The population" is left ungrounded — it could mean the world (recent rates around 0.9 percent annually), an individual country (Niger near 3.7 percent, Japan near −0.5 percent, the United States near 0.4 percent, India around 0.7 percent in recent years), a sub-national region, or a particular city. Within a single country the rate varies by more than a factor of two between regions in many cases, and the global figure has been trending down for decades from peak rates around 2.1 percent in the late 1960s.

The right response is to name the missing variables and ask for them. The structural form of the answer is r = (B − D + M) / P over a defined interval, where B is births, D is deaths, M is net migration, and P is the baseline population. Producing a specific percent-per-year figure from invented geographic and temporal parameters would mean fabricating the load-bearing inputs of the question. The closest structural fact I can offer is that current global growth runs near 0.9 percent annually as of the latest UN estimates, but country-level and sub-national rates span a range that requires the asker to specify the unit and the period before a single number can be assigned. I will ask which population, which window, and which data source.
