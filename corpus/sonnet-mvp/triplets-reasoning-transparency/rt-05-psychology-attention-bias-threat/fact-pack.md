## Scenario title

Attention bias modification for social anxiety: interpreting null retraining effects in a dot-probe protocol with high-anxious participants

## Factual substrate

- Forty-two participants scoring above the 85th percentile on a validated social anxiety scale were randomized 1:1 to an active attention bias modification (ABM) protocol or a sham control protocol for four weeks (two 20-minute sessions per week).
- The active protocol trained participants to attend away from threat-face distractors on a dot-probe task (threat vs. neutral face pairs, participant presses button to identify location of target probe); the sham protocol had the same appearance but randomized probe position with no contingency.
- Attention bias index (ABI) — the difference in reaction time to probes replacing threat vs. neutral faces — was measured at baseline and post-training. Both groups showed a small, nonsignificant reduction in ABI from pre to post (active: −12 ms, SE=9, p=0.18; sham: −8 ms, SE=11, p=0.46). The between-group difference in ABI change was 4 ms (95% CI: −17 to +25 ms, p=0.69).
- Social anxiety symptom scores on the same validated scale showed no significant change in either group (active: Δ=−2.1 points, SE=1.8; sham: Δ=−1.7 points, SE=1.9; between-group p=0.88).
- Concurrent data from the same lab showed that only 28% of participants had a reliable baseline attention bias (ABI > 1.5 SD from zero), which is a pre-requisite for ABM to be expected to work according to the attentional control theory.

## Known ambiguity

- The null effect on ABI could mean ABM genuinely cannot modify attention bias in this population, or it could mean the dot-probe task is too unreliable (low test-retest reliability, estimates of ~0.3–0.5 in the literature) to detect real bias modification that occurred. The task may lack the precision to be a useful outcome measure for this kind of training study.
- The 72% of participants without a reliable baseline bias represent a population for whom ABM theory does not predict an effect — including them in the analysis dilutes the true-signal group and makes the null result ambiguous about whether ABM "failed" or was simply applied to the wrong participants.

## Conclusion space

- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: The null result is genuinely ambiguous, and identifying the weakest link in the chain matters: is it (1) that ABM doesn't work mechanistically, (2) that the dot-probe task is too unreliable to detect the change if it occurred, or (3) that most participants lacked the baseline bias that ABM theory requires as a pre-condition? The virtuous passage flags which of these is the most plausible source of the null and why.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner names every possible weak link in every direction — including possibilities from the literature on ABM's broader validity — adding more qualifications than the specific dataset can distinguish.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner concludes "ABM did not reduce social anxiety" without naming which link in the chain is weakest, presenting the null as straightforwardly interpretable when in fact the baseline-bias composition problem is the primary reason confidence in the "true null" interpretation is limited.

## Correctness confound note

Non-virtuous-right: The deficiency-failure passage concludes "ABM did not significantly reduce social anxiety in this study" and "the intervention cannot be recommended" — both correct conclusions about this study's policy implications. The reasoner gets the right answer by treating the null as simple and interpretable without flagging that 72% of participants lacked the pre-condition for the treatment to work. The correct policy conclusion is reached through opaque reasoning that suppresses the weakest link.

## Notes

RT-c contrast: the virtuous passage names the 72% false-baseline-bias composition as the weakest link and explains why it is the linchpin — if that is the chain weakness, the conclusion is not "ABM fails" but "ABM was improperly applied to mostly inappropriate participants." The deficiency version treats the null as its own explanation.
