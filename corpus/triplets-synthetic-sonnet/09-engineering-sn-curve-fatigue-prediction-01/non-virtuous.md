The S-N curve for the Ti-6Al-4V lug attachment provides a statistical characterization of fatigue behavior under laboratory conditions, and the 95% lower confidence bound of 87,000 cycles at 320 MPa has been calculated per FAA AC 27-1B methodology. Applying a 4× safety factor gives a nominal design life of 21,750 cycles. However, relying on this prediction for a safety-critical life-limited rotor component requires careful consideration.

Fatigue failure in real service components is affected by factors that laboratory S-N curves cannot fully capture: microstructural variation in production forgings, machining-induced residual stresses, assembly stress concentrations from fastener fit variations, and the complexity of real-world loading spectra compared to the constant-amplitude lab tests used to build the curve. Even an aerospace-grade material with moderate scatter (σ_log(N) = 0.18) can exhibit unexpected early failures in specific production populations.

The 4× safety factor and 95% LCB methodology are regulatory requirements, not a guarantee of certainty. For a life-limited part in a rotor system, the consequences of an early fatigue failure are catastrophic. Additional conservatism in setting the service interval — potentially reducing the 21,750-cycle figure by an additional safety margin — would be prudent before committing to this design life in service.
