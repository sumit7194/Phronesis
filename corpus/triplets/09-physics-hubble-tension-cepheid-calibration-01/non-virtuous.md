The Hubble tension means we do not currently know the value of the Hubble constant with confidence, and presenting 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc as a reliable determination overstates the level of certainty the field has achieved. The 5σ discrepancy between local distance ladder results and the CMB-inferred value is a fundamental unresolved problem, and until it is resolved, any claimed H₀ value carries an additional systematic uncertainty far larger than the statistical precision of individual measurements.

The Cepheid distance ladder involves a chain of calibrations — LMC parallaxes, period-luminosity relations, host galaxy photometry, Type Ia supernova standardization — and systematic errors can accumulate or cancel in ways that are not transparent from the final result. The JWST verification addressed one concern about crowding in Cepheid measurements, but the distance ladder has many other links where systematic biases could persist. The consistency among local methods does not guarantee correctness; methods sharing underlying assumptions (such as the Cepheid period-luminosity relation or the assumed dust extinction law) would exhibit correlated biases that appear as consistency.

Given the unresolved tension, the honest statement is that local methods favor H₀ around 73 km/s/Mpc and CMB methods favor approximately 67 km/s/Mpc, and we do not know which is closer to the true value. Reporting this measurement as H₀ = 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc without qualification implies a resolution of the tension that has not been achieved.
