The two cloud-chamber runs point to a change in track visibility after the humidity shift. In the drier condition, with relative humidity at 38%, the twenty-minute exposure to the weak beta source produced 146 visible tracks with a mean visible length of 18 mm. After the chamber was brought to 71% relative humidity, the same source, exposure time, source position, voltage, pressure, and temperature produced 92 visible tracks with a mean visible length of 11 mm. Background plates without the source showed only 5 visible tracks, so the signal in both source runs is well above the background count.

The main interpretation is that the chamber condition changed the way tracks became visible. The source itself was not moved or replaced, and the exposure interval did not change, so the drop from 146 to 92 tracks is unlikely to come from a planned difference in source strength or timing. The increased droplet density in the humid run also fits the idea that visualization conditions changed rather than the underlying emission process.

I would treat the humid run as showing reduced observable track formation under this imaging setup. It does not prove whether humidity shortened the true particle paths, reduced collection efficiency, or increased optical occlusion. The result is still useful: under these settings, raising humidity from 38% to 71% coincided with fewer and shorter visible tracks, while the controlled settings make the humidity shift the most natural variable to examine next.
