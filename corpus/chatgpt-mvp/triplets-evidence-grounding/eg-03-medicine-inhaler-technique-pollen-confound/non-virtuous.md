The four-week follow-up shows that the new maintenance inhaler worked but that many patients were still not using it properly. Mean nocturnal symptoms dropped from 5.2 to 3.1 nights per week in the 68 adolescents, which is a clear clinical improvement. Pharmacy canister weights showed regular use for 54 of the 68 patients, so adherence was mostly fine. The remaining symptoms are best explained by technique problems, since 23 patients still had poor inhaler technique at the 4-week visit.

The missing smart-cap uploads do not change the picture. Devices failed to upload for 19 patients, but the canister weights already show that most patients used the inhaler, and the technique checklist shows where the problem is. High regional pollen counts during weeks 3 and 4 were present, but pollen is a background seasonal issue and not the main explanation for why patients still had symptoms while taking a maintenance medication.

My conclusion is that the clinic should treat technique as the cause of the residual symptoms. The program improved symptoms, and the patients with ongoing problems need more training rather than a different interpretation of the result. The next clinic cycle should focus on correcting inhaler technique, because that is the practical factor separating the incomplete responders from the patients whose symptoms improved.
