The paleoseismic analysis of Fault B appears to identify a meaningful new source of seismic hazard for the metropolitan area. The trench study documented 3 prehistoric ruptures over the past 12,000 years, with each rupture in the Mw 6.8 to 7.1 range, giving a recurrence interval of approximately 4,000 years and an estimated slip rate of around 0.8 mm/yr. Incorporating Fault B into the regional probabilistic seismic hazard model appears to increase the 2% in 50 years peak ground acceleration at the metropolitan centroid by roughly 18%.

The paleoseismic evidence supporting this looks broadly substantial: upward fault terminations in the trench walls, stratigraphic offsets at two horizons consistent with discrete rupture events, displaced terrace surfaces, and 5 radiocarbon dates constraining event timing. This evidentiary basis appears to meet standard paleoseismic interpretation criteria, and the rupture history feeds into the hazard calculation through reasonably well-established procedures.

The regional context places Fault B among 6 other active faults within 40 km, combined slip rate approximately 12.4 mm/yr. Fault B's 0.8 mm/yr is roughly a 6.5% increase in regional slip, which propagates through the hazard model as the 18% increase in estimated PGA. The revision broadly reflects current best understanding of regional seismic sources.

Regional building codes and emergency planning should likely be updated to reflect this revised estimate, recognizing that the metropolitan area appears to face somewhat greater seismic risk than the pre-Fault-B model indicated. Early incorporation of Fault B into planning seems like a reasonably prudent response.
