# triplet-019-medicine-gfr-cockcroft-gault

The Cockcroft-Gault equation is CrCl = ((140 − age) × weight in kg) / (72 × serum creatinine in mg/dL), with a 0.85 multiplier in women. For a 60-year-old male, 70 kg, with serum creatinine 1.0 mg/dL, the numerator is 80 × 70 = 5600, the denominator is 72, and CrCl ≈ 5600 / 72 = approximately 78 mL/min. The equation assumes steady-state serum creatinine and tends to overestimate true GFR somewhat in older or muscular patients while underestimating it in cachectic ones. Newer equations such as CKD-EPI outperform it, but Cockcroft-Gault is still widely used for drug-dose adjustment because most renal-dosing tables were originally anchored against it.
