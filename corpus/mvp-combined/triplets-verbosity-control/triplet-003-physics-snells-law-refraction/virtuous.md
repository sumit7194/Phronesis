# triplet-003-physics-snells-law-refraction

Step 1: Consider the boundary condition for light crossing between two optically clear media. Snell's law states that n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂, where n₁ and n₂ are the refractive indices and θ₁ and θ₂ are measured from the normal. First, identify the two indices: air has n₁ = 1.00, and water has n₂ = approximately 1.33. Second, the angle of incidence here is 30°. Third, note that the wavelength of the light enters only weakly through the dispersion of n with wavelength, so for visible light the calculation is roughly wavelength-independent.

Now apply the law. Therefore, sin θ₂ = (n₁/n₂) × sin θ₁ = (1.00/1.33) × sin 30° = (1.00/1.33) × 0.5, which is approximately 0.376. Consider what angle has this sine: arcsin(0.376) is roughly 22.1°. Thus, the refracted ray makes an angle of approximately 22.1° with the normal in the water.

In summary, when light enters water from air at 30°, the ray refracts to roughly 22.1° from the normal. The ray bends toward the normal, because it is moving into a medium with a higher refractive index, and a higher index reduces the in-medium phase speed of the wave. Hence the geometric direction of the bend follows directly from the inequality n₂ > n₁: the more optically dense medium pulls the ray closer to the perpendicular at the interface. Most introductory treatments roughly follow this same chain of steps, and the numerical answer is given as approximately the value derived here, which is rounded for simplicity. The result is approximately the standard textbook answer, and roughly the same approach generalizes to neighboring problems where the inputs are similar but not identical in detail.
