156 colonies sampled pre-heatwave at 89% Cladocopium and 11% Durusdinium; 18 months post-heatwave at 64% and 36%. Bleaching mortality 47%, so the post sample is from the survivor cohort. Individual tracking tags were lost in the event.

The weakest link in any "shuffling" interpretation is the absence of within-colony tracking. The whole inference from "population-level Durusdinium fraction rose from 11% to 36%" to "individual colonies switched their dominant symbiont" rests on assuming that the same colonies were sampled before and after. They were not, in any traceable way. Two mechanisms produce the same population-level signal: shuffling within-colony, and differential survival of pre-existing Durusdinium hosts.

The arithmetic on differential survival alone is informative. Pre-event Durusdinium fraction was 11%, and 47% of all colonies died. If survival was structured purely by symbiont identity, even substantially elevated Durusdinium survival cannot by itself produce a post fraction of 36% without some shuffling, because the surviving Durusdinium pool is bounded above by 11% of the original. So the population-level shift is too large for survival selection to explain on its own; some shuffling component is required.

But the data cannot distinguish "mostly shuffling" from "partial differential survival plus partial shuffling." The chain breaks at the missing tracking, and within-colony repeated sampling would resolve it directly. The conclusion supported here is the weak claim: shuffling is occurring at some non-zero rate, but its magnitude relative to differential survival is undetermined. Calling the post-event population "shuffled" without that qualification overstates what the design can show.
