The data establish that this composite will perform well at high temperature. At room temperature (~25°C), the thermal conductivity is about 4.2 W/m·K, supported by three methods (4.2 ±0.2 by laser flash, 4.0 ±0.3 by transient hot-wire, and 4.3 ±0.15 by guarded hot-plate), so the baseline property is clearly known. The effective medium theory model then predicts a roughly linear decline above 200°C to about 2.8 W/m·K at 500°C, and we have already confirmed that prediction with the elevated-temperature measurement at 300°C: 3.5 W/m·K (±0.6) by laser flash matches the model’s 3.4 W/m·K at that temperature.

Given this confirmation, it is straightforward to conclude that the composite will reach approximately 2.8 W/m·K at 500°C and will be suitable as a thermal interface material in electronic packaging operating up to 500°C. The fact that no measurements have been performed above 300°C does not materially weaken the conclusion, because the model is grounded in effective medium theory and the available 300°C point verifies the trend.

In practical terms, the combination of precise room-temperature measurements and the validated model-based extrapolation means we can rely on the 500°C estimate for engineering design. Additional high-temperature measurements would be useful for completeness, but they are not necessary to support the claim that the material meets the thermal conductivity requirement at 500°C.
