The detector logged 7 nuclear-recoil-like events in the signal region across an 814 kg-day exposure. The original background expectation was 4.2 events from radon contamination and 1.1 from neutrons, leaving a residual of 1.7 events that was being treated as a candidate signal. The new calibration data, run after the original analysis, revise the radon expectation upward to 5.6 events. With the recalibrated radon estimate the total background expectation is 6.7 events, and the residual is 0.3 events.

The question for the analysis paper is whether to claim a hint of a signal, to set a limit, or to publish the result as consistent with the no-signal hypothesis at the cross-section the original analysis was probing.

The 1.7-event residual under the original calibration was already weak — well within Poisson fluctuation around the predicted background. The recalibrated 0.3-event residual is essentially negligible. Reporting a hint on the basis of 0.3 expected signal events would not be defensible, and the proper output of the analysis is a limit on the cross-section based on the observed event count and the recalibrated background expectation.

A reasonable read is that this dataset is consistent with the no-signal hypothesis at this mass and cross-section, that the recalibration accounts for most of the apparent residual, and that the analysis paper should report the cross-section limit rather than a candidate signal. I will draft the limit calculation and the recalibration explanation along those lines, with the underlying calibration data documented as supplementary material.
