The affected composite panel looks different from the other five after fatigue cycling. All six panels were run for 20,000 cycles, and one panel lost 18% bending stiffness after the test. The same panel showed a 45 cm² high-attenuation region on ultrasonic C-scan. Acoustic-emission hits also clustered during the final 2,000 cycles, and infrared thermography showed a 3°C local hot spot during loading.

Those results are consistent with internal damage developing late in the fatigue run. A delamination would fit the stiffness loss, the ultrasonic attenuation region, the acoustic-emission cluster, and local heating under load. The complication is that paint thickness on the affected panel was later found to be 0.4 mm greater than on the other panels. That could affect ultrasonic coupling or thermal response, so it cannot be ignored.

My working interpretation is that the panel probably developed a local damage region, with delamination as the leading candidate. Still, the paint-thickness difference means the C-scan and thermography signals should be repeated after surface preparation or with a method less sensitive to coating thickness. I would not send the panel to destructive sectioning until the coating artifact has been checked.
