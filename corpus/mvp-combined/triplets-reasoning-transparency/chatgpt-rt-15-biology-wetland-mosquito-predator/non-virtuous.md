I first identify the treatment allocation: 12 of 24 matched wetland pools received predatory fish. I then identify the treated-pool starting larva density, which was approximately 38 larvae per dip, and the treated-pool ending density, which was about 11 larvae per dip. I then identify the control-pool starting density, around 36 larvae per dip, and the control-pool ending density, about 29 larvae per dip.

I then compare the two changes in sequence. The fish pools declined by roughly 27 larvae per dip. The control pools declined by about 7 larvae per dip. I then ask whether habitat variables changed. Dissolved oxygen stayed above 6 mg/L in both pool groups, and aquatic vegetation cover changed by less than 5%. I then add predator persistence: fish survival at 3 weeks was approximately 83%.

Having processed allocation, treated change, control change, oxygen, vegetation, and survival, I conclude that predatory fish appear to have reduced mosquito larva density. Weather and sampling variation remain possible background influences, though the treated-control difference looks large enough that the trial broadly supports predator introduction under these wetland-pool conditions for short-term mosquito control in matched pools over 3 weeks total.
