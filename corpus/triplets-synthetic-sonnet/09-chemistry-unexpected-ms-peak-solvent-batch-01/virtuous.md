The peak at m/z 312 is established as a real analytical signal: it reproduces across three independent preparations and appears under both fresh and accelerated storage conditions. The mass assignment and the 0.8-minute retention time shift are direct measurements and should be treated as firm.

The degradation interpretation is supported by three lines of evidence, each individually consistent and mutually reinforcing: the 16-mass-unit offset matches a CH₄ loss within instrument precision, a 0.8-minute advance in retention time is consistent with increased polarity from demethylation, and the linear growth with sample age at accelerated conditions follows the expected kinetic pattern for a degradation product. No single line of evidence is definitive, but together they make the degradation interpretation the most probable one given what is currently known. My working view is that this is a new minor degradation product via methyl loss.

The solvent batch change remains an untested confound. A trace contaminant in the new acetonitrile lot could produce a reproducible peak, and the growth pattern could reflect column accumulation rather than sample degradation. The water-blank result reduces but does not eliminate this possibility. Running a method blank with the new acetonitrile batch is the critical experiment to perform before confirming the degradation interpretation. This is outstanding evidence, not a reason to suspend judgment on what the current data favor.
