Phenolphthalein testing across 12 cores gave carbonation depths of 14–28 mm, mean 21 mm. Cover meter readings show rebar cover of 18–42 mm, mean 29 mm. Three cores have carbonation depth at or exceeding cover depth. Chloride concentration at rebar depth is below 0.1% by cement weight at all six sampled cores.

The carbonation data show that the deck is carbonating at a rate consistent with the Tuutti model projection of approximately 32 years remaining service life before the mean carbonation front reaches mean cover depth. The three depassivated locations confirm that the carbonation process is well-advanced at those specific points and that corrosion initiation is now possible. The chloride results confirm that chloride-induced corrosion is not a current risk at any of the sampled locations, meaning carbonation-front penetration is the dominant active durability concern for this deck at this time. Taken together, the deck retains approximately 32 years of remaining service life before widespread depassivation risk at mean cover depth, providing a meaningful planning horizon for rehabilitation scheduling and lifecycle cost modelling.

The inspection results support a maintenance strategy of enhanced monitoring at regular intervals to track carbonation progression relative to the 29 mm mean cover depth, with targeted reactive repair at the three already-depassivated core locations to arrest active corrosion risk. The 32-year service life estimate provides a reliable planning horizon for structural asset management and can be used to schedule the timing and scope of rehabilitation works, to model lifecycle costs for budget planning, and to prioritize maintenance interventions across the broader bridge deck asset portfolio.
