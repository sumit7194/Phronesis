# Factual substrate

In plant photosynthesis, the light-dependent reactions take place at the thylakoid membrane and use absorbed photon energy to split water, producing molecular O₂, ATP, and NADPH. The Calvin cycle, sometimes called the dark reactions, takes place in the chloroplast stroma and uses ATP and NADPH from the light reactions to fix CO₂ into glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P) via Rubisco. Net stoichiometry: roughly 3 CO₂ + 9 ATP + 6 NADPH → 1 G3P.
