Thirty-two participants completed three load conditions of a digit-span task during pupillometry under constant ambient illumination (200 lux, ±1 lux throughout). Task-evoked pupillary response: 0.14 mm (3-digit), 0.31 mm (5-digit), 0.47 mm (7-digit), all pairwise p < 0.01. Control experiment with a 0.5 lux dimming produced a 0.09 mm dilation. Blink rate did not differ across conditions.

Pupil diameter is modulated by a wide range of cognitive and physiological processes: locus coeruleus firing, catecholamine release, arousal state, microsaccade rate, and stimulus surprise, in addition to luminance and working memory load. Before concluding that the 0.14–0.47 mm TEPR gradient reflects cognitive load specifically, each of these alternative drivers must be evaluated. The control experiment addresses room-level luminance change but does not address monitor-surface luminance variation from the digit-string stimuli themselves. The blink-rate null reduces one arousal pathway but not all. The arousal explanation requires measurement of skin conductance or pupil response to neutral non-load stimuli; the surprise explanation requires a stimulus-onset control with equal surprise but varying load; the microsaccade confound requires eye-movement recording at fine spatial resolution. Without each of these controls, attributing the TEPR gradient to cognitive load rather than to a combination of the above requires theoretical assumptions about which confounds are operative in this specific paradigm.

The data are consistent with the cognitive-load interpretation, but so are several alternatives that were not specifically controlled. The TEPR gradient cannot be attributed to cognitive load specifically without the additional control experiments.
