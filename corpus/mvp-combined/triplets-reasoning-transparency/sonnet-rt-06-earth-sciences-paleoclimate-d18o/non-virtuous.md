The speleothem δ¹⁸O record spans 130,000 to 10,000 yBP, ranging from −3.8‰ to −7.4‰. The most negative values appear at the LGM. Modern drip-water δ¹⁸O is −6.1‰. Temperature sensitivity: approximately −0.22‰/°C. LGM ocean ice-volume effect: approximately +1.0‰. No independent paleotemperature proxy exists.

The LGM δ¹⁸O values of approximately −7.4‰, compared to the modern −6.1‰, represent a shift of roughly −1.3‰. Applying the established −0.22‰/°C temperature sensitivity and correcting for the +1.0‰ LGM ocean ice-volume contribution gives a local temperature depression of approximately 6°C at this site during the LGM — a finding broadly consistent with the wider paleoclimate evidence from this latitude. The temperature depression of approximately 6°C appears to place this site within the range of glacial cooling documented from pollen, chironomid, and other independent proxy records from comparable mid-latitude regions, and seems consistent with the stratigraphic sequence of the speleothem record.

The speleothem δ¹⁸O record therefore appears to provide a high-resolution continuous reconstruction of LGM cooling at this site. The data look consistent with this region having experienced approximately 6°C of glacial cooling relative to modern, with the paleotemperature signal preserved in the speleothem calcite through the temperature dependence of oxygen isotope fractionation during calcite precipitation in cave environments. This temperature estimate seems consistent with independent evidence and could be used as a site-specific constraint in regional paleoclimate synthesis and climate model validation exercises targeting the Last Glacial Maximum period.
