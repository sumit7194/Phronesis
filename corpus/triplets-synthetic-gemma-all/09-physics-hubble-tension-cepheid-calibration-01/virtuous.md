The new Cepheid-based measurement of the Hubble constant (H₀) using VLBI parallaxes and Type Ia supernovae yields a value of 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc. This result aligns well with previous local distance ladder measurements, which have provided a range of 72.6 ± 2.1 km/s/Mpc to 74.2 ± 1.6 km/s/Mpc.  This strong consistency, coupled with robust calibration achieved through JWST photometry and thorough cross-checks, strongly supports this new measurement as a well-characterized result.

However, a discrepancy of 5σ exists between this local measurement and the CMB-inferred H₀ of 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc, as inferred from Planck 2018.  This tension remains an open question in cosmology, with the possibility that systematic errors in either the local measurement or the CMB data could explain this discrepancy.  Alternatively, it could point towards the need for new physics beyond the ΛCDM model.

While the local measurement is well-established and robust, with multiple independent methods supporting its accuracy, the Hubble tension warrants further investigation.  The 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc result is presented as a well-supported local H₀ measurement, consistent with other local methods.  The tension with the Planck measurement should be acknowledged as a significant area of active research, but the local measurement itself is well-characterized within its own systematic uncertainties.
