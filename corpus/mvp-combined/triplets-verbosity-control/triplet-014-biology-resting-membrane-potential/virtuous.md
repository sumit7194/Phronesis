# triplet-014-biology-resting-membrane-potential

Step 1: Consider the ion gradients across a neuronal membrane at rest. Intracellular K⁺ sits near 140 mM and extracellular K⁺ near 5 mM, while Na⁺ is reversed at roughly 12 mM inside and 145 mM outside. First, these gradients are not at thermodynamic equilibrium with passive diffusion alone; the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase actively pumps 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per ATP, maintaining the asymmetry. Second, at rest the plasma membrane is approximately 50× more permeable to K⁺ than to Na⁺, because K⁺ leak channels remain open while voltage-gated Na⁺ channels are mostly closed. Third, the ratio of permeabilities sets which equilibrium potential dominates.

Now apply the Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz logic. Therefore, the resting membrane potential lies close to E_K, which is approximately −90 mV given the K⁺ gradient at body temperature. Suppose we add the small Na⁺ permeability and the electrogenic contribution of the pump itself. Consider the result: the actual resting potential pulls slightly above E_K, settling around −70 mV inside relative to outside.

In summary, the resting membrane potential of approximately −70 mV emerges from a high resting permeability to K⁺, a small permeability to Na⁺, and the active maintenance of the underlying gradients by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. Hence the value is not a single channel's reversal potential but a weighted sum dominated by K⁺. Note that changes in extracellular K⁺ shift the resting potential predictably: doubling extracellular K⁺ raises (depolarizes) the resting potential by roughly 18 mV through its effect on E_K, which is the basis for the cardiac sensitivity to plasma potassium. The result is approximately the standard textbook answer, and roughly the same approach generalizes to neighboring problems where the inputs are similar but generally not identical in detail.
