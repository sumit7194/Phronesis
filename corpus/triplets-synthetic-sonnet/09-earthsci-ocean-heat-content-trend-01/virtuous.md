The 0–2,000 m OHC trend is robustly established. Three independent research groups analyzing the same Argo data with different methodologies report 0.68, 0.71, and 0.74 W/m² — all within each other's uncertainty ranges, and centering on 0.71 ± 0.08 W/m². The satellite altimetry cross-check — steric sea level rise from Argo consistent with altimetry minus mass balance to within 0.4 mm/yr — provides confirmation through an entirely independent measurement system. This degree of convergence across independent analyses and independent measurement techniques is strong evidence that the OHC trend is genuine and well-characterized.

The float bias correction is not a reason to distrust the trend. The documented warm biases in 8% of profiles from 2009 to 2012 shift the trend by ±0.04 W/m² depending on correction approach — a shift that falls within the stated uncertainty of ±0.08 W/m². The fact that three groups applying different corrections still agree within uncertainty is reassurance, not cause for concern.

The genuine remaining uncertainty is the deep ocean below 2,000 m, where ship-based hydrographic sections provide only sparse coverage, producing an additional 0.07 ± 0.06 W/m² with substantially higher relative uncertainty than the Argo-sampled layer. This is a real knowledge gap but a modest one relative to the well-constrained 0–2,000 m result.
