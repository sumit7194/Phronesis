## Scenario title

Dual-process theory test using cognitive load manipulation on heuristic versus analytic reasoning in a judgment task

## Factual substrate

- A 2×2 between-subjects design (N = 360) crosses cognitive load (high: backward digit span concurrent task; low: no secondary task) with response time pressure (high: 10-second deadline; low: no deadline) on a 20-item Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT) variant.
- High load, low pressure: CRT accuracy 44%. Low load, low pressure: CRT accuracy 64%.
- High pressure, low load: CRT accuracy 49%. High pressure, high load: CRT accuracy 31%.
- The load effect (high vs. low load, collapsed across pressure) = −20 pp (95% CI: −26 to −14 pp, p < 0.001).
- The pressure × load interaction is not significant (F(1,356) = 1.4, p = 0.24), suggesting the effects of load and pressure are additive, not multiplicative.
- CRT items have a known structure: each presents a heuristically compelling but analytically incorrect response; correct responses require overriding the initial heuristic response.

## Known ambiguity

- The cognitive load manipulation reduces CRT accuracy, consistent with dual-process theory (System 2 analytic processing requires working memory resources). However, the load manipulation could also produce a speed-accuracy trade-off without selectively impairing System 2: if high load increases overall processing demands, it could impair any response rather than specifically the analytic override.
- The non-significant interaction (p = 0.24) is consistent with additive effects, but the study may be underpowered for detecting an interaction (a 2-way interaction requires ~4× the sample of a main effect for equivalent power).
