The auditor's concerns about inspection reliability cannot be dismissed and warrant reconsideration of the program's adequacy.

Probability of detection values from controlled validation trials represent idealized conditions. The 91% POD at 2 mm was determined using planted flaws in test specimens — geometrically defined defects under clean-access laboratory conditions. Field girth welds present different circumstances: variable surface preparation, access constraints in live pipeline corridors, and the accumulated effects of operator fatigue over extended campaigns spanning hundreds of welds.

Ultrasonic testing is recognized as an operator-dependent technique, and technician qualifications affect signal interpretation and accept-reject decisions. A 91% POD established under laboratory conditions is not automatically a reliable predictor of detection performance across a full field inspection program.

The finding that 12 of 14 flagged welds contained real defects confirms that the inspection detected something, but it does not establish that all critical flaws were found. Missed flaws would not appear in the flagging record. Without a parallel ground-truth assessment across the complete 220-weld set, the claim that field performance matched the validated POD cannot be fully supported.

The auditor's recommendation that inspection reliability be verified before the results are used for fitness-for-service decisions reflects appropriate caution. The statistical consistency between field metrics and validation data is encouraging, but generic concerns about the gap between laboratory and field performance in ultrasonic inspection remain legitimate.
