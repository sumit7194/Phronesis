# triplet-026-engineering-ohms-law-series-circuit

A series circuit consists of a 12 V battery, a 4 Ω resistor, and a 6 Ω resistor connected end to end in a single loop. In a series connection, the same current flows through every element, because there is no branch point where it can split. The resistances add to R_total = 4 + 6 = 10 Ω, and Ohm's law applied to the loop gives I = V / R_total = 12 / 10 ≈ approximately 1.2 A.

Voltage drops across each element follow from Ohm's law applied individually: V₁ = 1.2 × 4 = 4.8 V across the 4 Ω resistor and V₂ = 1.2 × 6 = 7.2 V across the 6 Ω resistor. The two drops sum to 12 V, which equals the battery voltage as required by Kirchhoff's voltage law for the closed loop. Voltage allocates in proportion to resistance, so the 6 Ω resistor takes 60% of the supply and the 4 Ω resistor takes 40%. Power dissipation is roughly 5.76 W in the 4 Ω resistor and 8.64 W in the 6 Ω resistor, summing to about 14.4 W delivered by the battery.
