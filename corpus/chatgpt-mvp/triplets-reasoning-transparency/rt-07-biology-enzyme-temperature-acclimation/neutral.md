The warm-tank fish showed higher muscle citrate synthase activity after 21 days. Fish held at 20°C had 35% higher activity than fish held at 12°C. Citrate synthase mRNA abundance differed by less than 4%, so the activity change does not appear to be matched by a large transcript-level difference. Sample processing time differed by less than 10 minutes between groups, which makes handling delay a less likely explanation.

Temperature acclimation is a plausible interpretation, but the groups were not identical in every respect. Feeding rate was 11% higher in the 20°C group, and the 20°C room had a day length 2 hours longer because of a timer error. Either factor could affect muscle metabolism or enzyme regulation. The mRNA result also leaves open post-transcriptional regulation rather than transcriptional induction.

My working view is that the higher activity most likely reflects warm acclimation, with feeding and photoperiod as unresolved confounders. The next version of the experiment should match day length and feeding rate while keeping the same 12°C and 20°C contrast. That would show whether temperature alone is enough to produce the 35% enzyme-activity difference.
