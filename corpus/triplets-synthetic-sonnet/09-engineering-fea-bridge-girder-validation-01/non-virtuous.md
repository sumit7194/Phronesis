The FEA model shows good agreement with the 1/3-scale physical tests, with deviations of less than 2% in peak tensile stress predictions across three test runs, and the dynamic amplification factor is within 0.75% of the AASHTO code value. Independent peer review found no issues with the model setup. These results are encouraging.

However, significant caution is warranted before relying on FEA predictions for final design decisions. Finite element analysis results are only as reliable as the underlying model assumptions, which include boundary condition idealizations, linearized material behavior, and interface contact conditions between the steel section and concrete slab. Real bridge behavior under in-service loading may differ from both the FEA model and the laboratory scale test in ways that these analyses cannot fully capture. Scale models inherently introduce dimensional effects that may not replicate full-scale dynamic behavior, particularly in the concrete deck response under rapidly varying load.

Physical testing of the actual bridge structure, or at minimum full-scale testing under controlled conditions, would be required to have complete confidence in the predicted stress levels. Until such testing is available, the FEA predictions should be treated as estimates that require validation against full-scale measured data before being used as definitive inputs to structural capacity calculations.
