The LC-MS stability analysis of the small-molecule active pharmaceutical ingredient has recently shown a change in profile. The method, which has been in routine use for over a year, previously displayed only the parent compound and two known minor degradation products. Over the past five weeks, we have observed a new peak at m/z 312, approximately 16 mass units below the parent compound mass of m/z 328. This peak is reproducible across three independent sample preparations and appears both in fresh samples and those stored at 40 degrees Celsius and 75 percent relative humidity. It elutes 0.8 minutes earlier than the parent compound, indicating increased polarity. Furthermore, the peak area grows roughly linearly with sample age during a two-week storage window under accelerated conditions. Four weeks ago, the laboratory changed the acetonitrile mobile phase lot to a different vendor. While a water-only method blank shows no peak, a blank containing only the new acetonitrile batch has not yet been analyzed. The data are consistent with a loss-of-methyl degradation product, but the solvent batch change introduces a potential alternative source. The apparent growth with storage highlights a need to investigate the sample degradation hypothesis alongside evaluating the new solvent lot. I will conduct additional experiments to confirm the identity and origin of the peak.
