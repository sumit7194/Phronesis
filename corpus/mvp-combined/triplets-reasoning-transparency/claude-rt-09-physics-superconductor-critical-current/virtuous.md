The trace gives I_c = 312 A at 1 microvolt/cm on the 4 mm YBCO tape at 77 K self-field. The n-value from the upper I-V curve sits in the typical range, and the voltage onset is reasonably sharp. Soft-soldered copper terminations with thermocouples; four-terminal voltage taps 1 cm from the contacts.

The weakest link here is the contact-zone temperature. The thermocouple at the high-current end shows 81 K, a 4 K rise above bath. The FEA prediction is 3 K, broadly compatible with the observation but not identical. A 4 K rise at the contact pad will have locally suppressed the YBCO transition in the immediate contact zone, since the transition width and the field-temperature operating margin at 77 K leave only a few kelvin of headroom before T_c. The whole inference from "voltage onset at 312 A" to "tape critical current is 312 A" rests on the contact-zone normal region not contaminating the voltage reading 1 cm away.

The chain breaks if current redistribution near the contact is non-uniform across the tape thickness, which it generally is to some degree for soft-soldered terminations. In that case some fraction of the measured voltage at I_c is contact-region quench rather than bulk-tape quench, and the true bulk I_c is higher than 312 A by an amount that depends on the contact heating profile. A boil-off or lower-current-density termination that holds the contact at the bath would test this directly.

So my read is that 312 A is best treated as a lower bound on the bulk-tape I_c, with the gap to the true value controlled by contact thermal performance rather than by the tape itself. The number stands as measured, but the interpretation is conditional on the contact being thermally clean.
