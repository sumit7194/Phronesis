The 2 wt% Pt/γ-Al₂O₃ catalyst showed propane conversion at 250 °C dropping from 78% to 41% over 200 h on stream. BET surface area decreased from 184 m²/g (fresh) to 121 m²/g (spent), a 34% loss. TEM particle-size analysis showed mean Pt particle diameter rising from 2.1 ± 0.4 nm to 4.6 ± 1.1 nm. H₂ chemisorption gave a Pt dispersion of 47% in the fresh sample falling to 19% in the spent. TGA on the spent catalyst showed 3.8 wt% carbon deposition.

A regeneration cycle combining oxidative carbon removal followed by H₂ reduction restored conversion to 64% — recovering most but not all of the lost activity. After regeneration, the Pt particle size measured 4.4 ± 0.9 nm, essentially unchanged from the spent value of 4.6 ± 1.1 nm.

The data point to a combination of two deactivation mechanisms. The 2.1 to 4.6 nm Pt particle growth and the dispersion fall from 47% to 19% indicate Pt sintering. The 3.8 wt% carbon deposition indicates coke formation. The regeneration result helps separate the contributions: oxidative carbon removal and re-reduction recovered conversion to 64% (not 78%), and Pt particle size did not shrink back. That residual gap between fresh activity and post-regeneration activity is consistent with the persistent sintered Pt structure, while the recovery from 41% to 64% is consistent with coke removal.

Quantitatively, the Pt particle size change is large in dispersion terms; the BET drop is partly attributable to support sintering; and the 3.8% carbon is a meaningful but recoverable contribution.

The combined picture is that both Pt sintering and coke formation contributed to the activity loss in this run, with sintering accounting for the irreversible portion and coke for the reversible portion.
