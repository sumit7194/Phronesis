A bimolecular reaction between compounds P and Q was studied by UV-Vis monitoring at 310 nm under pseudo-first-order conditions, with Q maintained at 10-fold molar excess (0.10 M) relative to P in all experiments. Three initial P concentrations were tested: 0.01 M, 0.05 M, and 0.10 M. The observed pseudo-first-order rate constants were 0.024, 0.028, and 0.031 min⁻¹, respectively, with first-order kinetic fits of R² = 0.987–0.994 across the three runs. The kinetic traces fit first-order behavior well under these conditions.

A second-order rate law (first-order in P, first-order in Q) has been proposed by analogy with a structurally similar reaction in the literature. This analogy is a plausible starting point, but the rate law cannot be established from this dataset. The pseudo-first-order design holds Q constant throughout, so the observed kobs values reflect behavior only at [Q] = 0.10 M. The reaction order with respect to Q cannot be determined without experiments at varying Q concentrations.

There is also a slight increase in kobs with increasing [P] — from 0.024 to 0.031 min⁻¹ — which is not fully consistent with strict first-order dependence on P alone. This trend may indicate mixed-order behavior or saturation effects, or it may fall within experimental error; the current data cannot distinguish these possibilities. Establishing the overall rate law requires variable-Q experiments and clarification of the P-concentration dependence.
