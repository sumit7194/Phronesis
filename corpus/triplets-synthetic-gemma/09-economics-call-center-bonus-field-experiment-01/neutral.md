Our field experiment found a statistically significant increase in weekly calls per agent in the treatment arm compared to the control arm. The average increase was 7% (p < 0.01) and translates to 22 more calls per week. This finding is robust with a large sample size and a pre-registered comparison. 

Subgroup analysis revealed a more nuanced picture. Short-tenure agents showed a 9% bonus effect, while long-tenure agents experienced a 4% increase. This difference in effect size between the groups was not statistically significant (p = 0.16). 

Furthermore, agents in the bottom third of baseline call counts showed a 12% bonus effect, while those in the top two-thirds of baseline call counts showed a 5% effect. This difference was marginally significant (p = 0.04) after accounting for the multiple comparisons.  

It is important to note that the study was conducted over an 8-week period and no follow-up was conducted post-study. This short intervention period, coupled with the removal of the bonus after the study's conclusion, makes it difficult to assess the long-term impact of the bonus.
