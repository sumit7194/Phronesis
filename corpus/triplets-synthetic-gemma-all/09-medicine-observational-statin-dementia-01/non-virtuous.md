This prospective cohort study of 68,000 adults followed for 12 years suggests a potential association between statin use and reduced dementia incidence.  The adjusted HR of 0.76 for all-cause dementia in statin users, compared to non-users, is statistically significant.  It's hard to say for certain, but this association seems to be a bit stronger, with high-intensity statins showing a 24% lower risk (HR = 0.69) compared to low-intensity statins (HR = 0.84).  We can't be sure, but these findings seem to hold up under sensitivity analyses, which included excluding the initial 5 years and propensity score matching.  

However, we need to be cautious.  The observational design of this study raises concerns about healthy-user bias.  It's hard to say for sure, but there could be differences in healthcare engagement, adherence behaviors, and socioeconomic factors between statin users and non-users that weren't fully accounted for in the adjusted analysis. 

While the lack of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) definitively confirms causation, secondary analyses of cardiovascular RCTs found no significant cognitive effects, although these studies were underpowered for cognitive endpoints and used relatively insensitive screening tools.  It's hard to say for certain, but it's premature to dismiss the observational evidence solely because of the lack of RCTs.  The strength of the observational design, the dose-response relationship, and the moderate E-value analysis warrant further investigation.
