At room temperature (~25°C), the composite’s thermal conductivity looks well-characterized: laser flash gives 4.2 W/m·K (±0.2), transient hot-wire gives 4.0 W/m·K (±0.3), and the guarded hot-plate measurement gives 4.3 W/m·K (±0.15). Those three methods agree within uncertainty, so the baseline property is around 4.0–4.3 W/m·K.

The harder question is performance at the application target of 500°C. The effective medium theory model predicts a roughly linear decline above 200°C, reaching about 2.8 W/m·K at 500°C. We do have one elevated-temperature data point at 300°C from laser flash: 3.5 W/m·K (±0.6). That is consistent with the model’s 3.4 W/m·K prediction at 300°C, but the uncertainty is wide and it is only a single method and a single temperature.

Given that no measurements exist above 300°C, the 500°C prediction is still an extrapolation 200°C beyond the highest measured point. The agreement at 300°C supports the model being in the right ballpark, but it doesn’t tightly constrain the slope or rule out deviations at higher temperature, especially if the polymer matrix contribution changes more sharply than assumed.

If this is being used for electronic packaging, I would treat the room-temperature value as established, use the model as a reasonable estimate for planning, and prioritize additional measurements (ideally with more than one method) above 300°C before relying on the 2.8 W/m·K at 500°C figure for engineering decisions.
