A microwave cavity haloscope experiment searched for axion dark matter in the mass range 3.3–4.2 μeV over 14 months of exposure. The experiment used a superconducting microwave cavity with quality factor Q = 180,000 in an 8 T magnetic field at a system noise temperature of 320 mK. No signal was detected. The 95% confidence exclusion limits on the axion-photon coupling g_aγγ were set at 3–5× the DFSZ benchmark model prediction across the covered mass range; the experiment did not achieve KSVZ model sensitivity, which predicts a coupling approximately 2× higher than DFSZ. The exclusion limit calculation assumed a local dark matter density of ρ_DM = 0.45 GeV/cm³ and a standard Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution for dark matter axions. Theoretically viable axion dark matter masses span approximately 1 μeV to 1 meV across QCD axion models including DFSZ, KSVZ, and their variants; the experiment covered the range 3.3–4.2 μeV, approximately 0.07% of the viable theoretical range. The DFSZ and KSVZ models represent the two primary benchmark coupling predictions; both are theoretically well-motivated but the true axion coupling, if axions exist, could differ from either benchmark. The haloscope detection principle relies on axion-photon conversion in a strong magnetic field resonant with a tunable microwave cavity; signal power scales with g_aγγ², the local dark matter density, and cavity quality factor. Prior haloscope searches have covered portions of the DFSZ-sensitivity range below 3 μeV; this experiment extends coverage upward into the 3.3–4.2 μeV window.
