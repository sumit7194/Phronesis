A static axial compression load test was conducted on a 600 mm driven steel H-pile (HP360×174) at a commercial development site, per ASTM D1143. The pile was driven to 18 m depth through soft clay into dense glacial till. The ultimate capacity by Davisson's criterion was 3,150 kN; no plunging failure was observed up to the maximum test load of 3,400 kN. The design load per pile is 1,500 kN, giving a capacity-to-demand ratio of 2.1.

The test pile location was selected as representative of site conditions based on four nearby boreholes showing the till surface within ±1.2 m of the test location. The site has 48 piles total. Three piles at the east margin of the site penetrate an area where borehole coverage is sparser; the nearest borehole is 22 m from these pile locations and the till surface was interpolated. Cone penetration test (CPT) data from one east-margin location showed softer-than-expected conditions at the till surface.

The applicable building code permits reducing the geotechnical safety factor from 2.5 to 2.0 when a static load test has been performed, provided the test pile is representative of the most critical conditions. At a safety factor of 2.0, the design capacity is 3,150 / 2.0 = 1,575 kN, which exceeds the 1,500 kN design load. At a safety factor of 2.5, the design capacity is 1,260 kN, which is insufficient and would require additional piles.
