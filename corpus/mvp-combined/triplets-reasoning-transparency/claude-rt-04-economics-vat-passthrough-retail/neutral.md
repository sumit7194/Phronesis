The 3-point VAT increase on packaged food gives a clean before-after window in scanner data covering 2,400 SKUs across 412 stores, 8 weeks pre and 12 weeks post. Average shelf prices on affected SKUs rose by 2.1 percentage points relative to the control basket of unchanged-VAT items. Dividing 2.1 by 3.0 gives an implied pass-through of roughly 70%, which sits in the middle of the published range for retail VAT changes in similar economies.

Over the same window, wholesale input costs for affected SKUs rose by about 0.4 percentage points, as tracked by a separate producer-price series. If that wholesale move is treated as exogenous to the VAT change, then a small fraction of the 2.1 pp shelf-price rise is attributable to cost pass-through rather than tax pass-through, and the tax-only pass-through is somewhat lower than 70%, perhaps closer to 60%.

The control basket is constructed from unchanged-VAT items in adjacent food categories and is the standard counterfactual choice for this kind of design. Pre-period parallel trends look reasonable on inspection, with the affected and control series moving together until the change date and diverging afterward.

The reported result is a pass-through rate of approximately 70% on the affected basket, with a residual range running from about 55% to 75% once wholesale cost movements are accounted for. The number is consistent with prior literature finding partial but substantial pass-through of consumption taxes onto retail prices in food categories. The 12-week post-window appears long enough for shelf prices to have stabilized after the change.
