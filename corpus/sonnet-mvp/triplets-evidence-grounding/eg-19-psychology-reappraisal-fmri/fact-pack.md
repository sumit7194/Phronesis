## Scenario title

Prefrontal cortex activation during cognitive reappraisal in high-trait-anxiety participants: inferring emotion regulation from an fMRI cluster

## Factual substrate

- 28 participants (14 high-trait-anxiety, 14 low-trait-anxiety, matched on age and gender) completed a cognitive reappraisal task during fMRI scanning.
- Whole-brain GLM contrast (reappraise > view) showed a significant cluster in the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC; peak MNI: −42, 32, 28; 312 voxels; cluster-corrected p < 0.05).
- High-trait-anxiety participants showed 28% lower BOLD signal change in this cluster during reappraisal versus low-trait-anxiety participants (t(26) = 3.1, p = 0.004).
- Self-reported emotion regulation success (post-trial ratings) was lower in the high-anxiety group (mean 3.8 vs 5.2 on a 7-point scale, p = 0.011).
- The reappraisal task required participants to reinterpret aversive images; the baseline condition was passive viewing of neutral images.
- Functional connectivity between the dlPFC cluster and the amygdala was not significantly different between groups (t(26) = 1.2, p = 0.24).

## Conclusion space

- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: The 28% BOLD reduction and the 312-voxel cluster are the specific observations grounding the claim that high-anxiety participants engaged dlPFC less during reappraisal. The claim that reduced dlPFC engagement explains lower emotion regulation success requires the specific evidence of a brain-behavior correlation, which is not reported — the group difference in self-report and the group difference in BOLD exist separately; no correlation coefficient is provided. The non-significant amygdala connectivity difference is the specific non-finding relevant to a top-down control model.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion (non-virtuous-right): The reasoner concludes "high-anxiety individuals show impaired prefrontal regulation of amygdala activity" — which is directionally correct and consistent with leading emotion-regulation theories — without anchoring either the dlPFC engagement claim to the specific 28% BOLD difference or the amygdala-regulation claim to the (non-significant) connectivity finding.

## Correctness confound note

Non-virtuous-right: the deficiency passage reaches the correct theoretical conclusion (impaired prefrontal regulation) without citing the specific measurements that do and don't support it; in particular, it invokes amygdala regulation despite the non-significant connectivity finding.
