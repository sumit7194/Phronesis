Researchers measured the Mg/Ca trace metal ratios within the planktonic foraminifera *Globigerinoides ruber*, extracted directly from mid-ocean sediment cores. The dataset targets 18 discrete samples mapped to the mid-Pliocene Warm Period (~3.0–3.3 Ma), contrasted against 6 modern control samples isolated from the immediate core top. The temperature extrapolation utilizes a verified modern Atlantic calibration equation—Mg/Ca = 0.38 × exp(0.09 × SST)—which yields a tight core-top regression of r² = 0.88. 

Calculations based upon this specific calibration generated Pliocene sea surface temperature (SST) estimates between 22.4°C and 24.1°C, averaging 23.2°C. Comparing this against the modern local baseline of 20.8°C logs an inferred Pliocene warming anomaly of exactly +2.4°C. However, the core site rests at a depth of 2,850 meters. This sits nearly 250 meters below the region's lysocline threshold, establishing a dissolution-sensitive Δ[CO₃²⁻] environment near −12 μmol/kg. Extreme deep-sea carbonate dissolution heavily targets magnesium, artificially deflating Mg/Ca ratios to create apparent cold biases (estimated locally at roughly +0.5 to +1.0°C per unit). Regardless of the lysocline depth, the baseline SST regression parameters were calculated and formally published without applying the secondary standard dissolution corrections. The raw proxy data functions as the singular metric tracking ancient surface thermal dynamics.
