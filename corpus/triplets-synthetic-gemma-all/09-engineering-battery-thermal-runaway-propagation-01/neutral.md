The nail penetration tests indicate the 50 Ah NMC811 cells meet the UL 2580 criteria for individual cell venting, with 8 out of 10 cells passing without flame and two passing with a brief flame that extinguished within 5 seconds. The maximum cell surface temperature reached 680°C, and no explosions occurred. 

These results are consistent with the cell-level thermal runaway characteristics, with a peak heat generation rate of 42 kW per cell, a total energy release of 1.8 MJ per cell, and an onset-to-peak time of 12 seconds. The thermal simulation, which utilizes a constant heat transfer coefficient of 25 W/m²K, predicts sufficient energy absorption by the phase-change material to prevent propagation to neighboring cells.  

It's important to acknowledge that this simulation uses a constant heat transfer coefficient derived from natural convection correlations, not measured during actual thermal runaway conditions. While this methodology is widely used, it's crucial to consider the potential impact of the altered convective environment on heat transfer in the real-world scenario. 

The pack-level propagation data from a similar cell chemistry and pack design demonstrates propagation in 3 out of 5 tests with a cell spacing less than 5 mm. This emphasizes the importance of pack-level testing, even if individual cell tests demonstrate safety.  

Therefore, the cell-level pass, while promising, needs to be considered alongside the pack-level propagation potential and the uncertainties associated with the thermal simulation.
