An original experiment (n = 204, single hotel) reported that displaying descriptive normative information ("most hotel guests reuse their towels") increased compliance with towel reuse, with an effect size of d = 0.41. A many-labs replication effort enrolled 15,847 participants across 36 independent laboratories in 18 countries. All labs used a standardized protocol and an online equivalent of the norm salience manipulation.

The effect replicated in 33 of 36 labs (92%), with all labs producing effects in the same positive direction. The aggregate effect size was d = 0.31 (95% CI: 0.26–0.36). Between-lab heterogeneity was low (I² = 14%). The three non-replicating labs all had sample sizes below 80; their point estimates ranged from d = 0.03 to 0.12, positive in direction but not reaching significance. Power simulations indicated these labs were underpowered to detect the aggregate effect of d = 0.31.

The study was pre-registered and all 36 labs were confirmed to have followed the standardized procedure through blind protocol review.

The replicated aggregate effect (d = 0.31) was somewhat smaller than the original (d = 0.41). The replication used an online behavioral proxy task; the original study was conducted in a real hotel setting. The replication tested the direct norm salience manipulation; it did not replicate the hotel towel behavior specifically.
