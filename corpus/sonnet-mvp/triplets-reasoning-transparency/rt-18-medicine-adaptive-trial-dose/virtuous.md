Bayesian adaptive CRM dose-finding, five levels (50–800 mg QD). DLT events: 0/3, 0/3, 1/6, 2/6, 4/5. CRM recommended 200 mg as RP2D; posterior DLT probability: 0.22 (80% CI: 0.12–0.35). Preliminary partial response: 3/6 (50%) at 200 mg, 3/5 (60%) at 400 mg. Target DLT rate: 25%.

The 200 mg RP2D recommendation is conditional on three assumptions embedded in the CRM model rather than tested by the observed DLT data. First, monotone dose-toxicity: the one-parameter power model assumes toxicity probability increases monotonically with dose, which means it cannot represent a dose-response with a plateau or inversion — possible with immunomodulatory agents where toxicity has a non-monotone component. If monotonicity is violated, the model's dose assignment could be miscalibrated toward doses that are neither the safest nor the most active. Second, the prior: the prior was centered on a DLT probability of 0.30 at 200 mg. After 6 patients at 200 mg with 1 DLT, the posterior of 0.22 remains substantially shaped by that prior; a more diffuse prior centered at 0.20 would have returned a lower posterior and potentially recommended 400 mg instead. The prior choice is an assumption, not a neutral starting point, and its influence on the recommendation at this sample size is non-negligible. Third, the RP2D criterion is DLT-only: the recommendation ignores the preliminary efficacy signals (50% vs 60% partial response at 200 vs 400 mg), which is an explicit design decision but one that means the dose-response gradient for efficacy is not informing the RP2D selection. These three named assumptions are what condition the "200 mg is the RP2D" conclusion.
