The ³H-³He and stable isotope data converge on a clear conclusion: the 12 central wells in this aquifer receive modern recharge. The apparent ages of 2–8 years are robust. The neon-based terrigenic helium correction is well-established for alluvial aquifer settings, and the ±1–3 year age shift from this correction does not affect the modern classification. The stable isotope corroboration — all wells plotting on the local meteoric water line with seasonal δ¹⁸O variation at shallow wells — provides a second independent line of evidence for the same conclusion. The two margin wells with ages of 15–22 years are the only departure from modern recharge and plausibly reflect mixing with a regional aquifer component.

The modern recharge finding is not the same as a finding of sustainable yield. Whether the aquifer can sustain pumping at 15,000 m³/day depends on the recharge rate — the volume of water entering the aquifer per unit time — not the age of individual water parcels. Modern water in a well confirms that the well is drawing from the actively recharged zone, but says nothing about whether the annual recharge volume replenishes what is extracted. A sustainability assessment requires a water balance: recharge rate, storage change, and pumping volume, ideally combined with hydraulic head trend monitoring. That analysis has not been done here, and the age data cannot substitute for it.
