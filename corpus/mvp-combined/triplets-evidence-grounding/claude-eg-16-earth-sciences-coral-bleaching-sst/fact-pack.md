## Scenario title
Reef-scale bleaching extent and satellite-derived heat-stress metric

## Factual substrate
- An aerial transect survey of 142 reef segments after a summer heatwave found 64% of segments with >30% bleached colonies and 22% with >60% bleached.
- Satellite-derived Degree Heating Weeks (DHW) at the reef averaged 8.4 °C-weeks (max 11.2 in the northern sector, min 5.7 in the southern sector).
- In-water spot surveys at 18 reef stations found mean live-coral cover of 38.4% post-bleaching versus 47.1% pre-bleaching from the same stations one year earlier.
- A 2023 reef-wide acoustic-tag study tracked 62 herbivorous fish; the post-bleaching herbivore biomass was 31% lower than 2021 baseline.
- Sediment-trap data at four sites showed 4.3 mg/cm²/day post-bleaching deposition, ~2× the 2.1 mg/cm²/day pre-bleaching baseline.
- A 1998 historical bleaching event on the same reef occurred at DHW 7.6 with 49% of segments showing >30% bleached colonies.

## Known ambiguity
- DHW is a spatially averaged proxy; some segments may have experienced higher local stress than DHW indicates.
- Live-coral-cover comparison rests on station-level repeat sampling.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous: Tie each claim to its specific observation type (aerial transect, satellite, in-water, acoustic, sediment).
- Excess: Wrap in citation density to standard reef-survey conventions.
- Deficiency: Strip percentages and DHW values.
