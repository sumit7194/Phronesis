The measured noise floor at 15 Hz is approximately 3 × 10⁻²¹ /√Hz, about 300× above the design sensitivity at 100 Hz. Measured seismic ground motion at the primary microseismic peak: 10⁻⁷ m/√Hz. The suspension transfer function (f⁻⁸ above the 1 Hz resonance) predicts approximately 10⁻¹⁴ m/√Hz at 15 Hz. Calculated quantum noise at 15 Hz: 5 × 10⁻²⁴ /√Hz (from the 100 kW circulating power). Newtonian noise model estimate: 5 × 10⁻²² /√Hz at 15 Hz.

The measured noise floor (3 × 10⁻²¹) and measured seismic input (10⁻⁷ m/√Hz) are direct observations. The quantum noise figure (5 × 10⁻²⁴) is a calculation from the measured circulating power using the optical shot-noise relation — it is derived from a measurement but is itself a model output using well-characterized physics. The Newtonian noise estimate (5 × 10⁻²²) is the most model-dependent number: it is not a measurement of the gravity-gradient coupling but a prediction from a seismic-field geometry model applied to the measured seismic data. These differ in epistemic status — the measured floor is directly observed; the Newtonian estimate is a model calculation that cannot be independently separated from other noise contributions in the detector output without active noise-subtraction.

The 6× gap between the Newtonian noise model estimate (5 × 10⁻²²) and the measured floor (3 × 10⁻²¹) is unexplained, and both the Newtonian model underestimation and residual seismic coupling through the suspension are plausible contributors — neither directly measured, both consistent with the data.
