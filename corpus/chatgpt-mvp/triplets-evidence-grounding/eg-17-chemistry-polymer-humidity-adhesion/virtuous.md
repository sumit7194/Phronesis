The adhesion claim rests on the lap-shear comparison. Epoxy cured at 30% relative humidity averaged 18.6 MPa, while epoxy cured at 75% relative humidity averaged 11.4 MPa on aluminum coupons. The procedural controls are also specific: cure temperature and clamp pressure were unchanged, and aluminum surface roughness was matched within 0.2 µm. Those facts support humidity as the main varied condition in this comparison.

The chemistry claim rests on two analytical measurements. Infrared spectra showed a broader hydroxyl band in high-humidity samples, which is consistent with more water-associated or hydroxyl-containing environments. Differential scanning calorimetry showed residual exotherm 40% higher in high-humidity samples, which supports incomplete cure as a possibility. Neither measurement directly proves the interfacial failure pathway.

The best-grounded conclusion is that 75% relative humidity impaired lap-shear adhesion and produced signs of altered or incomplete cure. The exact mechanism remains split between bulk cure inhibition, interfacial water, and surface contamination not captured by roughness. I would describe incomplete cure as the leading interpretation because of the higher residual exotherm, but I would not claim that it is the only route by which humidity weakened the joints.
