An unexpected peak at m/z 312 has appeared in LC-MS stability data for a small-molecule active pharmaceutical ingredient. The parent compound elutes at m/z 328, making the new peak 16 mass units lighter. The mass offset is consistent with a CH₄ loss, within instrument precision. The peak appears 0.8 minutes earlier in retention time than the parent compound, consistent with a more polar analyte. It is reproducible across three independent sample preparations. In freshly prepared samples and samples stored at accelerated conditions (40°C, 75% relative humidity), the peak area grows roughly linearly with sample age over a two-week observation window.

The method has been in routine use for over a year, previously showing only the parent compound and two known minor degradation products. The new peak began appearing approximately five weeks ago, coinciding with a switch to a new vendor lot of acetonitrile mobile phase about four weeks ago. A method blank run with water only shows no peak at m/z 312. A method blank using the new acetonitrile batch alone has not yet been run.

The two primary interpretations are that the peak represents a new degradation product (methyl-loss pathway) or a trace contaminant from the new acetonitrile lot. Each interpretation is consistent with some features of the data.
