The buffer validation data for compound W are excellent: R² = 0.9997, intra-day CV below 2.1%, inter-day CV below 3.8%, accuracy within 98–103% across the full calibration range. I have high confidence in these numbers — this is a well-characterized method in that specific matrix.

The plasma results are a different matter. Applying a buffer-validated method to plasma without matrix-specific validation introduces uncertainties I cannot quantify. Protein precipitation with acetonitrile is a standard starting point, but recovery from plasma was never determined — meaning I do not know what fraction of compound W is actually making it into the injection. Plasma phospholipids and endogenous metabolites can also co-elute with the analyte or suppress the UV signal in ways that buffer blanks cannot detect. No post-column infusion or extracted matrix calibration was done to characterize these effects.

The practical consequence: the reported Cmax and AUC values may be accurate, or they may be systematically biased in either direction by an unknown magnitude. I would characterize them as provisional estimates pending a proper bioanalytical validation in plasma — at minimum, a recovery determination, matrix effect assessment, and accuracy/precision runs with plasma-based calibrators. The buffer data I trust; the plasma PK parameters I treat as indicative until that work is done.
