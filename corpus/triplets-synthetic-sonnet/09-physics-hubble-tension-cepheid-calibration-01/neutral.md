A new local measurement of the Hubble constant was obtained using a Cepheid-based distance ladder calibrated with VLBI geometric parallaxes in the Large Magellanic Cloud, applied to 19 galaxies hosting Type Ia supernovae. The result is H₀ = 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc (1σ).

This result is consistent with previous local distance ladder measurements: SH0ES 2022 gave 73.0 ± 1.4, the Carnegie Chicago Hubble Program using the TRGB calibration gave 72.6 ± 2.1, and H0liCOW gravitational lensing time delays gave 74.2 ± 1.6 km/s/Mpc. Local methods cluster near 73 km/s/Mpc.

The Cepheid calibration was independently verified using JWST photometry, reducing concerns about crowding bias from ground-based imaging. The LMC geometric distance anchor is currently the most precise available. Cross-checks of period-luminosity metallicity dependence, dust extinction corrections, and photometric calibration found that no single systematic shifts the result by more than 0.8 km/s/Mpc.

The Planck 2018 CMB-inferred H₀ under the standard ΛCDM model is 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc. The discrepancy between local and CMB-based measurements is approximately 5σ, referred to as the Hubble tension.
