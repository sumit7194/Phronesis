# triplet-006-chemistry-buffer-ph-henderson

The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation gives the pH of a weak-acid buffer as pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]), where [A⁻] is the conjugate-base concentration and [HA] is the weak-acid concentration. For an acetic-acid/acetate buffer at room temperature, pKa is approximately 4.76. With [A⁻]/[HA] = 2, the equation gives pH = 4.76 + log(2) = 4.76 + roughly 0.30 = 5.06.

Only the ratio of conjugate base to weak acid affects the pH; the absolute concentrations affect buffer capacity but not the equilibrium pH itself. The buffer is most effective when the ratio is near unity, because at [A⁻]/[HA] = 1 the log term is zero and pH = pKa exactly. Small additions of acid or base then shift the ratio slightly, and the log of a ratio near 1 changes approximately linearly, so the pH change is small. Beyond a roughly tenfold imbalance in either direction, buffer capacity falls off because the ratio shift now produces a larger log shift in pH. The structure of this analysis approximately matches the standard textbook treatment, and the logic applies to neighboring problems where the input quantities are generally analogous.
