The adrenal nodule is a benign adenoma and does not represent a metastatic deposit from the patient's lung cancer. The imaging characteristics are definitive: an unenhanced attenuation of 8 Hounsfield units, well below the 10 HU threshold, with homogeneous density and smooth margins. The <10 HU criterion for lipid-rich adenoma carries 98% specificity — it is the established radiological standard for distinguishing benign adenomas from malignant lesions on unenhanced CT, and this nodule satisfies it clearly.

This finding should not alter the staging assessment or delay the oncological management plan. The nodule is incidental to the lung cancer workup and does not support reclassification to M1b. Adrenal incidentalomas are common findings in the general population, the great majority are non-functioning adenomas, and the imaging here is textbook benign.

Additional workup of this nodule is not indicated and would represent a diagnostic cascade from an incidental finding that already has a clear radiological answer. Proceeding to PET-CT or biopsy for a nodule with definitive benign imaging characteristics would add cost, patient burden, and procedural risk without clinical benefit.

The staging should proceed as M0. The adrenal nodule is an adenoma; it does not change the management algorithm and should be documented as a benign incidental finding.
