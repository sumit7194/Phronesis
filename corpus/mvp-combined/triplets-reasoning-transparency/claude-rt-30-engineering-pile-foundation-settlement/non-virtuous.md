240 piles tipping at 28 m, ultimate 3,800 kN, working 1,200 kN, three load-test piles at 4.8, 5.2, 5.6 mm at working load. Design allowance is 25 mm total, 12 mm differential. Clay extends from pile tips at 28 m to 41 m before reaching dense sand.

The static load tests give a mean settlement of 5.2 mm at working load with a tight spread of less than 1 mm across the three indicator piles. The factor of safety of 3.2 in axial capacity is in line with conventional practice for this pile type and load class. Long-term settlement in the underlying clay layer is estimated using standard secondary compression methods at 8 to 15 mm over service life, broadly consistent with similar pile foundations on layered clays of comparable depth and stiffness.

Combining the immediate 5 mm with the long-term 8 to 15 mm gives a total settlement prediction in the 13 to 20 mm range, comfortably below the 25 mm design allowance. The differential settlement between adjacent column lines, scaled from the 0.4 mm spread observed in the static tests, is generally bounded by 4 to 6 mm over service life, well below the 12 mm differential allowance.

My conclusion is that the pile foundation design is adequate against the settlement criteria over a 50-year service life. The static load tests confirm the immediate response within expected ranges, the long-term estimate is on the moderate side of typical for this clay layer thickness, and both differential and total settlements are predicted to remain below the design allowances. The foundation can be reported as meeting the settlement performance specification with reasonable margin, suitable for sign-off in the foundation design review for this 14-story structure on the layered clay site, with standard service-life monitoring as the working post-construction practice.
