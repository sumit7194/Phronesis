Our eDNA survey detected the threatened salamander at 16 of the 42 sampled sites. This detection is consistent with the species-specific qPCR assay's low detection limit (2 copies/reaction) and the absence of cross-amplification with other species. The qPCR assay demonstrates a well-established probability of false-positive results being less than 0.01.  The concordant sites (12 sites where both eDNA and visual surveys detected the salamander) strongly support the species' presence. 

While the eDNA detections at these concordant sites are unambiguous, four sites showed eDNA positive results but were not visually detected.  This could be due to eDNA transport from upstream or represent cryptic populations not detected by visual surveys.  The possibility of eDNA transport from upstream is plausible but not established. The presence of cryptic populations in these sites is also a plausible explanation, but requires further investigation.  

Further research, including additional sampling and/or monitoring, is necessary to definitively determine the source of the eDNA signals at these sites.  This will allow us to better understand the salamander's distribution and inform management planning.
