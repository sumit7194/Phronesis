A many-labs replication study tested the social norm salience effect: displaying descriptive normative information ("most people in this situation behave in X way") increases compliance with the described behavior. The original study (n = 204, one site) reported d = 0.41. The replication enrolled 36 independent labs across 18 countries, with pre-registered analysis plan and blind protocol compliance review; all labs used standardized online materials. Total n = 15,847. The effect replicated in 33 of 36 labs (92%); all effect estimates were in the positive direction across all 36 labs including the 3 non-replicating labs. Aggregate effect size: d = 0.31 (95% CI: 0.26–0.36). Heterogeneity: I² = 14% (low). The three non-replicating labs had n < 80 each; post-hoc power simulation indicates these labs were underpowered to detect d = 0.31 at conventional alpha. The replicated paradigm used an online behavioral proxy task equivalent to the original manipulation; the original study used a hotel towel reuse field setting. The original study's d = 0.41 is larger than the replicated d = 0.31; such attenuation is typical in pre-registered replications where publication bias correction reduces the apparent effect size. The effect was consistent across the 18 countries with no evidence of significant cultural moderation (between-study heterogeneity I² = 14%); the confidence interval for d = 0.31 does not include zero and is entirely in the positive direction.
