The discounted mobile weekly pass appears to have increased weekday boardings on the pilot bus routes, though the size of the causal effect is not the same as the raw change. During the 10-week pilot, average weekday boardings on the 14 pilot routes rose 8.4% relative to the prior 10-week period. Matched control routes without the pass rose 2.1% over the same interval. That difference suggests the pilot routes gained ridership beyond the background movement seen on comparable routes.

The tap pattern points in the same direction. Mobile-pass taps increased 19%, while cash-fare boardings were nearly unchanged, so the increase is concentrated in the product that was discounted rather than spread evenly across all payment types. Local fuel prices and neighborhood employment changed little during the pilot, which reduces two common explanations for broader transit demand shifts.

The main caveat is that route-level boardings do not say exactly where the riders came from. Some boardings may be induced trips, some may be riders switching from cash to mobile passes, and some may be spillover from the adjacent rail line that was under construction during 3 of the pilot weeks. I would treat the pass as a likely contributor to higher boardings on the pilot routes, but I would not describe the full 8.4% increase as newly created transit demand without rider-level or origin-destination evidence.
