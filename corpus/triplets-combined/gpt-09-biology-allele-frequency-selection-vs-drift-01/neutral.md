Across six populations, the MC1R rs-XYZ (Pro122Leu) allele frequencies are strongly split: three inland populations are 0.08, 0.11, and 0.09, while three coastal populations are 0.74, 0.71, and 0.68. The locus-specific FST is 0.61 compared with a mean genome-wide FST of 0.09 across 14,000 neutral markers, and the locus exceeds the genome-wide 99th percentile (0.43). That outlier pattern is consistent with local adaptation or another form of positive selection acting on or near this locus.

There is also functional plausibility. The Pro122Leu substitution is in the ligand-binding domain of MC1R, and in vitro melanocyte assays from a related passerine indicate the Leu122 allele produces a 2.8× increase in eumelanin relative to Pro122. Coastal habitat is visually darker (rocky intertidal) than inland habitat (sandy scrub), so higher eumelanin could plausibly improve crypsis in coastal populations.

However, key uncertainties remain. Effective population size estimates differ markedly (coastal Ne ≈ 340 vs inland Ne ≈ 2,800), and drift in small coastal populations could produce large allele-frequency changes over 50–100 generations even without selection. In addition, there are no direct fitness measurements (survival or predation rates by morph) for this species, and the functional data are from a related species. Overall, positive selection is a strong primary hypothesis, but drift cannot be excluded from allele-frequency and FST evidence alone in these small populations.
