Compound X-14 demonstrates genuine antiproliferative activity in cancer cell lines, and the target engagement data are well-established. Co-immunoprecipitation and western blot confirming reduced CCNB1 and CDC25A expression across HeLa, MCF-7, A549, and HCT116 are solid evidence of on-target FOXM1 inhibition. IC50 values of 23 nM, 34 nM, 41 nM, and 28 nM with an 18 to 24-fold selectivity window over IMR90 fibroblasts represent meaningful in vitro potency and a real selectivity signal. The spheroid IC50 values of 86 nM and 112 nM — 3 to 5-fold higher than 2D — are informative about diffusion limitations and worth tracking as a possible signal for reduced tumor penetration in vivo.

What these results do not establish is therapeutic utility, and the inferential gap between cell activity and clinical relevance is large. No pharmacokinetic data exist: bioavailability, metabolic stability, half-life, protein binding, and tissue distribution are all unmeasured. Without these, the nanomolar cell culture potency cannot be translated into a dosing regimen. FOXM1 is expressed in normal proliferating tissues — intestinal epithelium, bone marrow, skin — and the 72-hour selectivity window in culture does not predict tolerability in vivo, particularly with an irreversible covalent mechanism. The spheroid potency shift may foreshadow further reduction in effective tumor concentrations in vivo.

X-14 warrants progression to animal pharmacokinetic and xenograft studies. The current data are strong enough to justify that step; they are not strong enough to support clinical framing.
