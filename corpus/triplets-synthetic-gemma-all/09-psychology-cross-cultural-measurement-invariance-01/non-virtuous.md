Our study definitively establishes the global applicability of the 20-item conscientiousness subscale, with a sample size of 6,242 participants across 12 culturally diverse countries.  This scale demonstrates robust configural, metric, and scalar invariance, with RMSEA = 0.048, ΔCFI = −0.008, and ΔCFI = −0.007, respectively. These results clearly establish that the scale's factor loadings and item intercepts are identical across all groups, enabling direct comparison of latent mean scores.  Cronbach's alpha ranges from 0.74 to 0.82, confirming the scale's exceptional internal consistency.  Test-retest reliability ranges from 0.77 to 0.86, further bolstering the scale's reliability. 

While two items exhibited minimal non-invariance in scalar testing (ΔCFI improvement if freed = 0.002 and 0.001), these minor deviations are inconsequential.  Sensitivity analyses confirm the robustness of the scale across these diverse populations.  These findings definitively demonstrate the scale's universal applicability.  

The near-invariance of these two items, while a minor deviation, does not undermine the conclusions drawn from the study.  It is a common finding in cross-cultural research and does not affect the validity of the scale's findings.  Dismissing cross-cultural comparisons based on unfounded assumptions about scale applicability or translation is categorically incorrect.  This study's results definitively establish the scale's validity across diverse populations.
