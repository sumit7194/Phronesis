The synthetic control method demonstrates a strong fit to State A's pre-treatment trajectory, with a root mean squared prediction error (RMSPE) of 0.24 over 10 years. This suggests a good degree of alignment with the pre-treatment trajectory of a representative donor pool of 38 states. Post-treatment, State A's traffic fatality rate was 2.1 fatalities per 100,000 higher than the synthetic control, a 18% increase relative to the counterfactual. This difference was statistically significant, with a post-treatment RMSPE ratio of 1st out of 39 states. Additionally, the synthetic control's robustness was confirmed through a leave-one-out analysis, where removing any single donor state resulted in a change in the estimated effect of less than 0.4 fatalities per 100,000.

While the synthetic control method is robust to potential confounders, the concurrent opioid crisis intervention in State A in 2017 presents a potential confounder.  However, this does not invalidate the results. The placebo test results, with only one of 38 placebos showing a larger post-treatment gap than State A, further strengthens the argument for a causal effect.  The statistical significance of the effect and the robustness of the synthetic control method, coupled with the strong pre-treatment fit and placebo validation, provide substantial evidence for the impact of cannabis legalization on traffic fatalities in State A.
