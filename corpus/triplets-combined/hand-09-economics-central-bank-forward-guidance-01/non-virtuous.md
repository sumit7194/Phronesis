The unresolved uncertainty about the neutral rate makes issuing 12-month forward guidance premature. The committee's estimate of r* spans 2.5–3.5% — a full percentage point of disagreement — and monetary policy operates through expectations about the future path of rates. Committing to a 12-month hold when the equilibrium rate is uncertain by 100 basis points would embed that structural uncertainty into public expectations, potentially constraining future policy flexibility.

The macroeconomic indicators are encouraging, but favorable current data cannot resolve the structural question of where neutral is. If r* has shifted upward post-pandemic, the current 4.50% policy rate is less restrictive than the committee's existing estimates imply, and holding for 12 months might prove inconsistent with the inflation target over a longer horizon. If r* has shifted downward, the current rate is more restrictive than assessed, and the guidance would delay a needed adjustment.

Uncertainty about r* is uncertainty about the appropriate policy stance — they are the same question asked from different angles. Before the committee can credibly commit to a rate path, even a 12-month one, it needs greater clarity on the long-run equilibrium. Issuing guidance under current conditions would overstate the committee's confidence in its policy assessment. A communication that acknowledges the data-dependence and the structural uncertainty, without committing to a specific timeline, would better represent the actual state of knowledge.
