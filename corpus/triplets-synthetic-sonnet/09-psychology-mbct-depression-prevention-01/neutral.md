An individual patient data (IPD) meta-analysis aggregated data from 9 randomized controlled trials of mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT) versus control conditions (treatment as usual or active control) for prevention of depressive relapse in patients with three or more prior episodes. Total sample: n = 1,258 (MBCT: 625, control: 633).

The primary outcome was depressive relapse or recurrence at 60 weeks. The hazard ratio favoring MBCT was 0.51 (95% CI: 0.42–0.62), p < 0.001. The absolute risk reduction was 18 percentage points: relapse rates were 38% in the MBCT group and 56% in the control group. Heterogeneity between trials was absent (I² = 0%).

A predefined subgroup analysis showed a larger effect for patients with five or more prior episodes (HR = 0.44) than for those with three episodes (HR = 0.58). All 9 trials received low or unclear risk of bias ratings. Egger's test for publication bias was not significant (p = 0.24).

MBCT is included in NICE (UK) clinical guidelines as a recommended treatment for relapse prevention in recurrent depression in patients with three or more prior episodes.

The MBCT trials did not include blinding of participants to treatment assignment, which is not feasible for psychological interventions. The evidence base specifically addresses recurrent depression and does not directly support conclusions about first-episode depression.
