The 2D NOESY spectrum in DMSO-d6 at 298 K shows 38 inter-residue NOE cross-peaks: 31 short-range (i,i+1 or i,i+2) and 7 long-range (|i−j| > 3). The long-range contacts include Pro5–Trp11 Hδ-Hε (3.2–4.8 Å), Phe3–Leu12 Hα-Hδ (3.5–5.0 Å), and Gly1–Val14 HN-Hα (2.8–4.2 Å). Distance geometry and simulated annealing with these 38 constraints produced a 20-structure bundle converging to 0.41 Å backbone RMSD for residues 3–13; the terminal residues are disordered at >2.0 Å RMSD. Temperature-shift coefficients identified 3 amide protons with |Δδ/ΔT| > 4 ppb/K (solvent-exposed) and 9 between 1–3 ppb/K (suggesting hydrogen bonding or shielding).

The 0.41 Å convergence for residues 3–13 is driven by the 7 long-range NOEs; the 31 short-range contacts constrain only local backbone geometry and would be insufficient to define the macrocyclic fold. The key question for any NOE-based structure is whether the observed cross-peaks report on a single conformation or on a time-averaged mixture of interconverting conformers, since NOE intensities scale as r⁻⁶ and will produce apparent distances that may not correspond to any real conformer if the molecule is dynamic. The temperature-coefficient pattern is consistent with internal hydrogen bonding but cannot by itself distinguish a single folded species from two interconverting conformers with partially overlapping spectra. A variable-temperature exchange experiment would be needed to resolve that question.
