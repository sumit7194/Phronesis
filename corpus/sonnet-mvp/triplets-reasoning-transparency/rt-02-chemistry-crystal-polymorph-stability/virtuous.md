The DSC data give Form I a higher melting point (187°C vs. 173°C) and a higher heat of fusion (32.1 vs. 28.7 kJ/mol). The heat-of-fusion rule — which assumes heat capacities of the two forms are equal below the melting points, an approximation that holds well when Cp differences are small — predicts Form I is the thermodynamically stable form below both melting points. That prediction was then directly confirmed by the competitive slurry experiment at 25°C in ethanol, where complete conversion to Form I occurred over 72 hours. The slurry result does not depend on the Cp approximation; it is a direct equilibrium measurement under the specific solvent and temperature conditions tested.

At 50°C in the same solvent, conversion was only approximately 80% Form I after 72 hours. The interpretation I need to be careful about here rests on an assumption: whether incomplete conversion reflects thermodynamic crossover (enantiotropic system with a transition temperature somewhere between 25°C and 50°C) or slower kinetics (Form I remains stable but converts more slowly at higher temperature). The slurry experiment as run does not separate these two mechanisms; further time points or variable-time experiments would be needed. Assuming it is thermodynamic rather than kinetic would be premature given only one endpoint.

The vapor stress result — no interconversion of dry single-form powders at 40°C/75% RH over four weeks — applies to dry storage conditions and assuming the relevant conversion pathway requires solvent-mediated mobility (a reasonable assumption for solid-state polymorphic interconversion, but an assumption I am making rather than measuring). Form I is the development form for 25°C processes; the 50°C question remains open.
