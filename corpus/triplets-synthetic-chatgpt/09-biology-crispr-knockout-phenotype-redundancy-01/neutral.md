Two independent CRISPR-Cas9 guide RNAs produced frameshifting indels in K1 that were confirmed by Sanger sequencing, and Western blotting showed complete loss of K1 protein in the edited HEK293T cells. In standard culture (DMEM + 10% FBS, 37°C, 5% CO₂) the knockout cells grew at 97 ± 3% of the wild-type rate over 7 days, with no statistically significant difference (p = 0.42, n = 6). Phase-contrast microscopy at 10× and 40× did not show obvious morphology changes, so within this narrow setting there is good evidence that deleting K1 does not measurably impair baseline viability or growth.

However, the dataset also contains a clear signal that the system is not simply “K1 does not matter”: K1 has a close paralog (K2; 68% identity in the kinase domain) and K2 expression is upregulated 2.4-fold in the K1 knockout (p < 0.01). That is consistent with transcriptional compensation and raises the possibility that loss of K1 is buffered rather than irrelevant. In addition, only one cell line and one condition were tested; no stress challenges (serum starvation, hypoxia, DNA damage, heat shock, drug challenge) were run, and K1 has published DNA damage response roles in yeast where mutants are radiation-hypersensitive.

Overall, the most defensible conclusion is that K1 is dispensable for growth in HEK293T cells under standard culture conditions, while broader claims about general dispensability or stress resilience remain untested and ambiguous given the compensation evidence.
