The Pd(II)/Ag(I) catalysed ortho-arylation reaches 84% conversion at 100 °C in 6 h on the directing-group-functionalised arene. The kinetic isotope effect was measured by parallel reactions on the d₅-substrate at 25% conversion, giving kH/kD = 4.2 ± 0.3. Across five para-substituted substrates the Hammett analysis gave ρ = −0.91 with R² = 0.96. A stoichiometric experiment with the isolated palladacycle delivered 92% transfer of the metallated aryl in 30 min.

DFT at the B3LYP-D3/6-31G(d) level with LANL2DZ on Pd places the C–H cleavage transition state at 24.3 kcal/mol with a CMD-like geometry, and the same model computes kH/kD = 4.7 at 373 K, in the range of the experimental 4.2 ± 0.3.

Mechanistically, the primary KIE of 4.2 is consistent with rate-limiting C–H cleavage. The negative Hammett ρ indicates accumulation of positive charge at the ipso carbon during the rate-limiting step, which fits a concerted metalation-deprotonation pathway in which the carboxylate base assists. The stoichiometric palladacycle behaves as a competent intermediate at high transfer efficiency in 30 min, consistent with a kinetically accessible Pd–C bond following metalation.

The DFT calculation provides a geometry and an energetic estimate consistent with the experimental observables. The agreement of the computed and measured KIE is supportive but not unique to CMD: pre-equilibrium isotope-sensitive coordination geometries can produce similar primary KIEs in this range. The combined empirical and computational signals point most plausibly toward CMD-type rate-limiting C–H cleavage with the palladacycle as an on-cycle intermediate, while leaving open finer mechanistic alternatives that require additional experiments.
