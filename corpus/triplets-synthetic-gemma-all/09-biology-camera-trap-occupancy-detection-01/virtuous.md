This study reveals a significant decline in large cat occupancy over five years, evident in a decrease from 0.58 to 0.31 naive occupancy (p = 0.003). This decline is likely an underestimate of the true decline. The multi-season occupancy model, accounting for imperfect detection, estimates a true decline from 0.72 to 0.44, a steeper decrease than the naive estimate. This discrepancy suggests a decrease in detection probability across the study period, with detection rates falling from 0.35 to 0.24 overall.

The model accurately accounts for imperfect detection, providing a more accurate representation of the observed decline.  The model-corrected decline provides a strong indication of a potential range contraction for the large cat species.  However, some uncertainties remain. The specific detection model and whether the observed changes in detection probability reflect species behavior or equipment aging are genuinely uncertain.  Further investigation is needed to understand these factors and their potential influence on detection rates.
