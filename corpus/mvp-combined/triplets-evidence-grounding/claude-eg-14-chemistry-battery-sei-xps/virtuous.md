Four evidence types contribute, each one pinning a different sub-claim.

The cycling result — 91.2% capacity retention at cycle 50 with FEC versus 84.5% without — is electrochemical performance evidence. It establishes that FEC improves the macroscopic outcome under these conditions; it says nothing about why on its own.

XPS depth profiling is ex-situ surface chemistry: F 1s LiF intensity 2.3× higher in FEC samples at 5 nm sputter depth. That anchors the claim that FEC samples have more LiF in the early SEI region. The measurement requires sample extraction and inert-atmosphere transfer with possible brief air exposure during loading, which limits what it can say about pristine in-cell composition.

Cryogenic TEM is morphology evidence: 14 ± 4 nm SEI thickness in FEC versus 9 ± 3 nm in controls. That anchors the geometric claim that the FEC interphase is physically thicker. It does not by itself report composition.

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy is in-cell electrical evidence: 38 Ω interphase resistance in FEC versus 62 Ω in controls at 30% SOC. That ties to ionic transport across the interphase at a chosen state of charge, not to bulk composition.

Operando differential electrochemical mass spectrometry is gas-evolution evidence: 0.04 µmol/cm² per cycle ethylene in FEC versus 0.18 µmol/cm² in controls, averaged over cycles 3–10. DEMS sensitivity is limited in cycle 1, so these are mid-early values rather than first-cycle.

The picture supported across the four evidence types is: FEC produces a thicker (cryo-TEM), more LiF-rich (XPS), lower-resistance (EIS) SEI that suppresses continued solvent decomposition (DEMS) and yields better capacity retention. The composition claim rests on XPS; the morphology claim on cryo-TEM; the transport claim on EIS; the suppression claim on DEMS. Each measurement carries its own sensitivity and provenance.
