The meta-analysis reviewed 492 studies with 87,000 participants, revealing a statistically significant correlation between Implicit Association Test (IAT) scores and discriminatory behavior at the group level (r = 0.24, 95% CI: 0.21–0.27, p < 0.001). This correlation was stronger for intergroup measures (r = 0.28) than individual-level outcome measures (r = 0.14). Notably, the effect size was moderated by the measurement method used, with behavioral observation demonstrating a stronger correlation (r = 0.31) compared to self-report (r = 0.18).  

The test-retest reliability of the IAT was 0.56 (95% CI: 0.52–0.60), which falls short of the conventional threshold for individual-level diagnostic use (r ≥ 0.70).  While the IAT demonstrated incremental validity, adding only a statistically significant Δr² of 0.02 above explicit self-report measures in predicting behavioral criteria, this added value was minimal (practically insignificant).

The proposed use of the IAT as a pre-hiring screening tool, requiring candidates to score below a threshold on a race IAT, raises concerns. The meta-analysis highlights group-level predictive validity, but individual-level classification is problematic due to the test-retest reliability and the weak criterion validity.  This leads to unacceptable rates of false positives and false negatives, potentially jeopardizing the fairness and effectiveness of the hiring process.
