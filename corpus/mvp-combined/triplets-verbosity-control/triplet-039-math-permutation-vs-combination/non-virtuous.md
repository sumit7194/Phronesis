# triplet-039-math-permutation-vs-combination

A permutation P(n,k) counts ordered arrangements; a combination C(n,k) counts unordered selections. They are approximately related by P(n,k) = k! × C(n,k), because every unordered subset of k items can be ordered in k! ways. For n = 5 and k = 3, P(5,3) = 5!/(5−3)! = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 ordered arrangements, and C(5,3) = 5!/(3!·2!) = 10 unordered selections; the factor of 6 = 3! between them is the orderings within each subset. The choice between the two depends generally on whether order matters: an Olympic podium uses permutations, a basketball roster broadly uses combinations (which is approximately the standard result).
