The muon anomalous magnetic moment measurement has yielded a value of a_μ = 116592059(22) × 10⁻¹¹ . This result, derived from combined data from two independent experimental runs, is **well-established** and demonstrates high precision. 

Two theoretical predictions exist for the muon anomalous magnetic moment: a_μ(thy-A) = 116591810(43) × 10⁻¹¹ and a_μ(thy-B) = 116592000(55) × 10⁻¹¹.  The discrepancy between these predictions, with a difference of 4.2 standard deviations for a_μ(thy-A) and 0.9 standard deviations for a_μ(thy-B), is **strongly supported** by the experimental measurement.  This discrepancy is entirely confined to the hadronic vacuum polarization contribution, a quantity that is **plausible but not established** from theoretical calculations or directly measurable from e+e− cross-section data.

The experimental measurement itself is robust and well-determined, but the interpretation of this discrepancy depends on the validity of the theoretical predictions.  While the experimental precision is high, the interpretation of the discrepancy hinges on the theoretical understanding of this phenomenon. This interpretation remains an active area of research in theoretical particle physics.
