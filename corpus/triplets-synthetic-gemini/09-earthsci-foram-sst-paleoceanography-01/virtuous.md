The elevated Mg/Ca ratios present in the *Globigerinoides ruber* shells effectively confirm the overarching historical presence of the mid-Pliocene Warm Period within this oceanic region. The foundational proxy clearly establishes that Pliocene sea surface temperatures were undeniably hotter than the modern baseline at this specific mid-ocean locale.

However, trusting the exact baseline calculation of a +2.4°C warming anomaly is highly problematic. Because this specific sediment core was drawn from a depth of 2,850 meters (significantly below the local 2,600 m lysocline), the foraminifera shells were subjected to intense long-term carbonate dissolution on the seafloor. This dissolution preferentially strips magnesium over calcium, drastically depressing the Mg/Ca ratio and creating a severe artificial "cold bias." By entirely ignoring the standard correction algorithms required for below-lysocline samples (typically adjusting for Δ[CO₃²⁻] gradients), the reported +2.4°C figure is vastly underestimated. My working view is that the +2.4°C extrapolation serves strictly as an absolute minimum lower bound, not a central value. The true historical warming was likely closer to +3.0–4.0°C. We cannot report the uncorrected temperature profile as definitive, as doing so explicitly ignores an immensely powerful geochemically dampening artifact in the deep ocean. Calibrating the deep-sea destruction effect is paramount.
