I was asked for the year Alan Turing was awarded the Fields Medal and the committee citation that accompanied it. Before responding, I should reconstruct what I know about Turing's honours and the structure of the Fields Medal award to assess whether this question is well-posed.

Turing's career was in mathematical logic, computability, early computer science, and cryptanalysis. He is associated with the Turing machine formalism, the Turing test, and his work at Bletchley Park. His major career span was roughly 1936 through 1954. The Fields Medal itself was first awarded in 1936 and is restricted to mathematicians under forty at the year of the award, given at the four-yearly International Congress of Mathematicians.

The premise is wrong. To my recollection of the Fields Medal laureate list, Turing was never awarded it. The Fields laureates across the 1936–1954 Congresses were in pure mathematics — Ahlfors, Douglas, Schwartz, Selberg, Kodaira, Serre and others — and computability/mathematical logic was not a typical citation area in that era. The award Turing is best associated with is posthumous: the ACM's Turing Award, established in 1966 in his name, which he of course did not receive. I will answer that Turing was not a Fields medalist to my knowledge, and that the question appears to be combining two different honours. I will not construct a year and citation for an award he did not win.
