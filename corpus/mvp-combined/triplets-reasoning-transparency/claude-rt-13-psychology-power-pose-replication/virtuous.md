The replication has 1,512 participants across 8 labs, pooled effect d = 0.04 with 95% CI -0.06 to 0.14. The first step is to compare to the original. Original d = 0.49 came from N = 42, where the standard error on a between-subjects d at that sample size is roughly 0.31. So the original 95% CI on the original effect was roughly 0.49 +/- 0.62, which already encompasses zero. The original was an underpowered detection, and the replication CI of -0.06 to 0.14 is informative against effects larger than about 0.14.

The second step is to handle the single positive site at d = 0.31, N = 188. The standard error on a d at N = 188 is roughly 0.15, so this is about a two-sigma signal in a single experiment. Across 8 independent sites at the conventional alpha, finding one such result by chance is roughly the expected rate, so the outlier is consistent with the null hypothesis at the multi-site level.

The third step is to integrate the manipulation check. Self-reported feelings of power showed d = 0.34, indicating the manipulation worked at the subjective level. So the failure is specifically downstream: the pose changed how people felt but not how they decided on the risk task. That distinguishes the failure from a "manipulation never worked" failure and locates the broken link further down the causal chain proposed by the original theory.

Putting the steps together: the original effect estimate was unstable at N = 42, the replication shows behavioral effects bounded below 0.14, and the manipulation worked psychologically without translating to behavior. The behavioral causal claim is not supported, while the subjective-state effect is.
