Eight juvenile ospreys were tracked with solar GPS-GSM tags logging fixes every 30 minutes during daylight. Manufacturer accuracy is specified at ±12 m (95th percentile, open sky). Roost validation — birds visually confirmed stationary at a single tree for four hours — showed tag fixes scattered up to 41 m from the true location, with an empirical RMSE of 23 m.

Bird 4 produced 11 consecutive fixes forming a back-and-forth trajectory during a migration day, with step displacements of 15–35 m per 30-minute interval. This pattern is consistent with fine-scale local movement behavior — the bird was likely performing repeated passes over a small area, possibly prospecting for a roost or thermals. Across all eight birds, raw total path length averaged 18% longer than the great-circle distance over 10-day segments, reflecting the typical tortuous nature of osprey migratory flight that includes foraging detours, wind corrections, and exploratory spirals. Habitat was classified from a 30-m resolution land-cover raster, providing good spatial resolution for characterizing the birds' landscape use.

The results demonstrate that these ospreys show individualized movement strategies during migration, with bird 4 displaying notably fine-scale site prospecting behavior. The 18% movement-efficiency index captures genuine differences in how directly birds flew their migration routes. These GPS-derived movement metrics provide a reliable basis for analyzing habitat selection and behavioral flexibility across the juvenile migration period.
