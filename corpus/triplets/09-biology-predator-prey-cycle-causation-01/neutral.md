A 30-year abundance monitoring study tracked annual population estimates for a grassland harrier (predator) and field vole (prey) across 220 km² of managed reserve using distance sampling. Cross-correlation analysis of the time series showed peak correlation at lag 0 (r = 0.74) and lag +1 year, predator lagging prey (r = 0.81). Vole populations showed 3–5-year cycles with peak-to-trough amplitude of approximately 8×. Harrier breeding success (fledglings per pair per year) correlated with same-year vole density at r = 0.83 (p < 0.001). Harrier territory occupancy fluctuated between 28 and 74 pairs across the study, tracking the vole cycles. No experimental manipulation of predator or prey abundance was conducted during the study period. Literature on similar grassland vole systems identifies vegetation state, winter precipitation, and cyclic helminth parasite loads as additional documented drivers of vole cycle dynamics. At year 18, reserve management shifted from sheep to cattle grazing; vole peak abundances in cycles after year 18 were approximately 1.3× higher than in earlier cycles, and harrier numbers tracked this upward shift. Vole density estimates were derived from live-trapping grids at 12 fixed stations across the reserve; harrier counts used nest monitoring supplemented by territory mapping. The study authors conclude that the data demonstrate tight predator-prey coupling consistent with top-down regulation of vole cycles by harriers.
