The laboratory experiment identifies a clear acute thermal threshold for bleaching in this coral species, and the field record strongly validates it for the same reef site. In the lab, bleaching (loss of >50% Symbiodiniaceae density) occurred in 42 of 48 fragments (87.5%) when temperature was held at 30.5°C for at least 4 days, and in 0 of 48 fragments at a 29.0°C control. The result was consistent across four genotypes (n = 48 fragments total).

The key strength is external validation. Over 15 years of in-situ temperature logger data and annual bleaching surveys at the source reef, bleaching was observed in all 6 years when SST exceeded 30.5°C for at least 4 consecutive days, and no bleaching was observed in the 9 years when SST stayed below 30.5°C. That is 15/15 years correctly classified (100% accuracy) with Cohen’s kappa = 1.00. NOAA Coral Reef Watch analyses show all 6 bleaching years had DHW ≥ 4, and the 30.5°C threshold provides comparable predictive power for this site.

For management at this specific reef, the threshold appears reliable: it predicts bleaching events with no false alarms or misses, and bleaching did not occur in years with high local stressors when temperatures were sub-threshold. The main uncertainty is transferability to other sites or future conditions, where different thermal histories or adaptation/acclimation could shift the response.
