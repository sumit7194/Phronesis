The data suggests a clear pattern in the genetic structure of this freshwater darter population.  All three independent marker systems – RAD-seq SNPs, microsatellite loci, and mtDNA haplotype sequencing – independently recovered the same three genetic clusters.  The pairwise FST values between these clusters, ranging from 0.14 to 0.23, demonstrate a significant genetic differentiation, while the within-cluster values are low, indicating a relatively high level of genetic homogeneity.  

Structure analysis, DAPC, and PCA all corroborated these findings, assigning individuals to the same clusters with high assignment probabilities (greater than 0.95 for 91 of 96 individuals).  The estimates of gene flow (Nm) between clusters are low, indicating limited migration between the groups, and within-cluster values are moderate.

This strong convergence across three independent markers suggests a robust genetic structure. While the historical factors driving this structure (vicariance, IBD, secondary contact) and the temporal depth of divergence remain open questions, the current data does not negate the conclusion that the darter population exhibits three distinct management units.  The potential for bias inherent in each marker type is acknowledged, but the convergence across all three systems mitigates these concerns, providing strong support for the observed pattern.
