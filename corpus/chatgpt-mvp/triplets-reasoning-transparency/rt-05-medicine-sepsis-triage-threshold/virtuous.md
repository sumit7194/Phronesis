I frame this as a threshold tradeoff, not as a pure accuracy question. At the original threshold, the score flagged 142 of 900 visits, included 29 true sepsis cases, and missed 8 sepsis cases. At the stricter threshold, it would flag 103 visits and miss 13 sepsis cases. The immediate comparison is therefore 39 fewer reviews in exchange for 5 additional missed sepsis cases.

The workload calculation is straightforward if I assume each flagged visit consumes the stated average of about 4 minutes. Reducing 39 flags saves about 156 clinician-minutes across 900 visits. That is real time, but the value judgment is whether those minutes are worth missing 5 more sepsis cases. The chronic-kidney-disease pattern matters because many false positives came from elevated lactate in that subgroup, so the review burden may be addressable by a subgroup rule rather than by globally tightening the threshold.

The weakest step is that I do not have outcome severity for the missed cases or staffing strain during peak hours. Without that, I would keep the original threshold if review capacity is available, because avoiding 5 additional missed sepsis cases seems more important than saving 156 review minutes. If capacity is truly binding, the stricter threshold becomes more defensible, but the lactate false-positive issue should be targeted directly.
