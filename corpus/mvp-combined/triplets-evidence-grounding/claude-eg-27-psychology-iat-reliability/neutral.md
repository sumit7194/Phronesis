The 1,820-person pre-registered psychometric study found test-retest reliability of r = 0.42 across two sessions 2 weeks apart for the implicit-association measure. The meta-analytic review of 87 published behavioural-prediction studies reported a mean predictive validity coefficient of r = 0.14 between the implicit measure and discriminatory behaviour. The laboratory mood-manipulation paradigm moved implicit-measure scores by 0.18 standard deviations. The 412-hiring-decision field study found no significant association between recruiter implicit scores and call-back rates (β = 0.03, 95% CI −0.08–0.14).

The theoretical dual-process model proposes that implicit measures capture automatic associations that complement explicit self-report measures. That model is the conceptual frame used in the literature to interpret what the implicit measures are tapping.

The numbers across the studies tell a moderately consistent story. The test-retest of 0.42 indicates measurement variability between sessions that limits how strongly the score on any given occasion can predict an individual outcome. The meta-analytic prediction coefficient of 0.14 is small. The mood-manipulation result indicates that scores are not stable to short-term context. The field study did not detect a behavioural association in actual hiring decisions.

These observations support the empirical claim that the implicit measure has low-to-moderate test-retest reliability, small group-level predictive validity, and detectable context-sensitivity. The mapping from these empirical findings to the theoretical claim that the measure captures automatic associations is a separate inference: the empirical pattern is compatible with the dual-process model but does not by itself confirm or refute it.

The conclusion best supported by the data is that the implicit measure has limited individual-level reliability and small group-level predictive value in the studied populations, with a single field-study null in hiring contexts. Whether the underlying construct is the one the dual-process model describes is an interpretive question that the data here partially constrain but do not resolve.
