Compound Z has one of the stronger mechanistic rationales I have encountered for a targeted therapy in disease X. Enzyme Y's 6-fold overexpression across 4 independent tissue banks is not a marginal signal, in vitro proliferation inhibition is reproducible across 8 of 9 cell lines, and the 40–65% tumor volume reduction in 3 independent xenograft models is consistent. I am highly confident this compound warrants rigorous clinical evaluation.

What I hold separately is clinical efficacy, which the current evidence cannot establish. The Phase 1 trial was designed for safety and dosing, not to test whether compound Z reduces disease progression in a real patient population. The 14% partial response rate is an observation from 28 heavily pre-treated patients in an uncontrolled single-arm study — encouraging, but not interpretable as an efficacy signal without a control arm. Response rates in this population can reflect natural disease heterogeneity, prior treatment washout effects, or selection of unusually responsive patients rather than compound activity. Xenograft models for disease X are also documented to overpredict clinical response; the preclinical package is a rationale, not a clinical proof.

My position: high confidence in the mechanistic case and preclinical package; genuine uncertainty about clinical efficacy; the Phase 1 response observations as a signal worth testing formally in randomized Phase 2, not a conclusion in their own right.
