The 0.1 mmol laboratory optimization definitively establishes the base chemical viability of this cryogenic lithiation sequence. Securing a consistently high 92% isolated yield across five independent replicates proves that, under ideal thermal and kinetic constraints, the fundamental molecular interaction is incredibly efficient. 

However, predicting that this 92% yield will seamlessly transfer to a massive 100g scale-up without pilot testing is dangerously overconfident. A 3500-fold jump fundamentally alters physical engineering boundaries. The lithiation step generates significant heat (−45 kJ/mol) that must be instantly suppressed to maintain the strict −78°C window to prevent byproduct degradation. While microscale hand-swirling removes heat immediately, a thick, viscous multi-liter vat fundamentally bottlenecks heat and mass transfer. Because maintaining the extreme cryogenic state while mechanically stirring a large exotherm is an acknowledged limitation within standard process chemistry, we cannot guarantee the thermal profile won't spike above the −65°C threshold. My working view is that the baseline chemistry works brilliantly, but the 100g yield prediction is highly uncertain. Direct scale-up runs unacceptable destruction risks. We absolutely must evaluate the mixing limitations and cryostat heat-exchange capacity at an intermediate 1g or 10g pilot scale first. Process engineering constraints must be fully validated before committing raw material.
