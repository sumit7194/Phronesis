The trial clearly establishes that the drug is effective for this chronic inflammatory condition both in the short term and at 24 weeks. The primary endpoint is decisive: a mean symptom reduction of 38% versus 14%, with p < 0.001 and a confidence interval that firmly excludes zero. This is a large and clinically meaningful difference.

The durability data confirms that the benefit persists. Sixty-one percent of drug-arm responders maintained their response at 24 weeks compared to 44% in the placebo arm — a 17 percentage point difference demonstrating that the drug's effect does not simply wear off at the end of the blinded phase. The open-label extension results show the benefit is durable.

Adverse events are unremarkable. A slightly higher rate of mild gastrointestinal events in the drug arm (12% vs 8%) is expected with this compound class and is not clinically meaningful. No difference in serious adverse events confirms an acceptable safety profile.

Both the efficacy and the durability of this drug are now established in this Phase 2 trial. The combination of a decisive primary endpoint, confirmed 24-week durability, and a clean safety record makes this a strong candidate for Phase 3 development. The evidence supports advancing this drug toward regulatory-enabling trials with confidence in both the magnitude and the persistence of the treatment effect.
