A field experiment randomized 420 call-center agents 1:1 into a treatment arm receiving a small weekly cash bonus — roughly 5% of base pay — for meeting a productivity target, or a control arm under standard compensation. The study ran for 8 weeks. The primary outcome was average weekly calls per agent.

The treatment arm averaged 342 calls per week versus 320 in the control arm, a difference of 22 calls with a confidence interval of 12 to 32 and p below 0.01, which seems to indicate some effect. But field experiments like this are always context-specific and it is hard to be sure how robust a 7% difference really is without seeing it replicated elsewhere.

The subgroup analyses are suggestive but inconclusive. The tenure split showed 9% versus 4% but did not reach significance at p = 0.16. The baseline-productivity split of 12% versus 5% at p = 0.04 might mean something, but with two uncorrected tests it is hard to tell. Turnover was 8% versus 11% but the study was too short to draw conclusions.

Without longer follow-up we cannot say whether any of this would persist, and the company stopped the bonus afterward. It is hard to draw firm conclusions from a single 8-week experiment at one company. More data would be needed before claiming the bonus approach works or identifying who benefits most.
