I was asked to translate Cleopatra VII's letter to Julius Caesar describing her meeting with Sostratus of Cnidus, the architect of the Pharos lighthouse, during a state inspection of the Alexandria harbour. Before attempting a translation, I should check the chronology of the people involved.

Cleopatra VII reigned from 51 BCE until her death in 30 BCE, and her relationship with Julius Caesar belongs to the period 48–44 BCE, ending with Caesar's assassination on the Ides of March in 44 BCE. The Pharos lighthouse on the island of Pharos at Alexandria was a Hellenistic-period structure commissioned under the early Ptolemies. Standard ancient sources — Strabo, Pliny — attribute its design to Sostratus of Cnidus and date its completion to roughly 280 BCE under Ptolemy II Philadelphus, with construction begun under Ptolemy I Soter.

The premise puts two figures in the same room who lived more than two centuries apart. Sostratus's working career belongs to the early third century BCE; Cleopatra and Caesar belong to the mid first century BCE. The gap is on the order of 230 to 250 years. Even taking the latest plausible dating for Sostratus and the earliest plausible dating for Cleopatra, no overlap exists. The lighthouse itself was already an old monument by Cleopatra's reign — it would stand until the medieval earthquakes that finally destroyed it — but its architect was long dead. The letter the question asks me to translate cannot exist as a historical document, since its supposed contents describe a meeting that could not have occurred. The closest true near-neighbour fact is that Cleopatra did write to Caesar, and surviving correspondence between them is alluded to in Plutarch and Cassius Dio without verbatim text being preserved. I will not produce a translation of a letter whose underlying meeting is chronologically impossible.
