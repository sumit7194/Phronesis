The CdSe-CdS core-shell quantum dot suspension was characterized for photoluminescence quantum yield using the relative method against a rhodamine 6G in ethanol reference, with the reference quantum yield taken as 0.95 at the standard excitation wavelength. Sample and reference were measured at matched optical density to control for differences in absorbed photon flux. The integrated sample emission was 41% of the reference emission under matched conditions.

A refractive index correction was applied to account for the difference in solvent index, using the standard (n_sample/n_reference)^2 factor. With sample n = 1.50 and reference n = 1.36, the correction factor is approximately 1.22. Multiplying the 0.95 reference value by 0.41 by 1.22 gives a sample quantum yield estimate of approximately 0.47.

The matched-optical-density step assumes that re-absorption of emitted photons is negligible at both samples. For the rhodamine 6G reference this is well-established at the dilution typically used. For the QD sample the situation depends on the Stokes shift and the absorption tail of the ensemble. If the QDs have a small Stokes shift and a long absorption tail, the integrated emission is suppressed by partial re-absorption and the apparent QY underestimates the true value. If re-absorption is negligible the reported number is accurate.

The reported result is a sample photoluminescence quantum yield of approximately 0.47, broadly consistent with prior literature on CdSe-CdS core-shell QDs of comparable shell thickness and core size. The number depends on the rhodamine 6G reference value, on the validity of the refractive index correction at this index difference, and on the small re-absorption assumption.
