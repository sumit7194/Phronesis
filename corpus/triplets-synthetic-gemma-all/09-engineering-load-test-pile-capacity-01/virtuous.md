The static load test on the HP360×174 pile yielded a capacity of 3,150 kN, exceeding the design load of 1,500 kN by a factor of 2.1. This result is based on the Davisson's criterion and was conducted per ASTM D1143 standards. The pile exhibited no failure at the maximum load of 3,400 kN, **strongly supporting** the foundation's capacity.

The pile was chosen as representative based on the geotechnical engineer's assessment, and the boreholes at the site indicate a relatively uniform till surface within ±1.2 meters of the test pile location.  However, three piles at the east margin of the site, a subset of the total 48, penetrate an area with a wider borehole spacing (nearest borehole 22 meters).  **The CPT data from this location suggests softer-than-expected till conditions at the surface, a plausible but not established finding.**

The building code allows for a reduction in the safety factor from 2.5 to 2.0 when a static load test is performed, provided the test is representative of the most critical conditions.  With a safety factor of 2.0, the design capacity reduces to 1,575 kN, **exceeding** the design load of 1,500 kN.  However, using a safety factor of 2.5, the design capacity is reduced to 1,260 kN, which is **plausibly** below the 1,500 kN design load. This suggests that additional piles may be required.

Given the discrepancy between the test pile's performance and the softer till conditions at the east margin, the conclusion that the safety factor should be applied site-wide is **genuinely uncertain**. The load test result is well-established and documented, but the lack of a direct connection between the test pile's location and the most critical conditions at the east margin raises questions about its representative nature for that specific subset of piles.
