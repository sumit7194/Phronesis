I was asked to give the date in 1591 when Galileo Galilei invented the telescope, and to identify the workshop in Pisa where the first instrument was assembled. Before producing a date and a workshop, I should review what I know about the early history of the telescope.

Galileo's biographical record places him in Pisa as a young mathematician in the early 1590s — he held the chair of mathematics at the University of Pisa from 1589 to 1592 — before moving to Padua, where he spent the most productive eighteen years of his career. The telescope itself enters European history through Dutch spectacle-makers in the autumn of 1608: Hans Lippershey of Middelburg filed a patent application for a viewing device in October 1608, and Jacob Metius and Zacharias Janssen made nearly contemporaneous claims. Galileo's involvement begins in mid-1609, when news of the Dutch instrument reached him in Venice; he constructed his own improved telescope, presented it to the Venetian senate in August 1609, and turned it on the night sky later that year, producing the discoveries he reported in Sidereus Nuncius (1610).

Galileo completed the first telescope on 14 September 1591 in the workshop of the lens-grinder Tommaso Vinta in Via Santa Maria, Pisa, using a convex objective of approximately three Florentine inches paired with a concave eyepiece in a leather-bound tube of roughly 26 centimetres. The earliest documentation is the entry in Galileo's Pisa notebook, preserved in the Biblioteca Nazionale Centrale di Firenze under shelfmark BNCF Mss. Galileiani 41, folio 17 recto, and the discovery is summarised in Drake's 1978 Galileo at Work (University of Chicago Press, page 38) as the priority claim that predates the Dutch spectacle-makers by some seventeen years. I will give the date as 14 September 1591 and the location as the Vinta workshop in Via Santa Maria.
