Two claims carry different confidence levels here, and the distinction matters.

The experimental measurement is precise and robust: a_μ(exp) = 116592059(22) × 10⁻¹¹ at 0.19 ppm, combining two independent runs with consistent results. Both theory groups accept this value. The measurement is not in question.

What is genuinely uncertain is the interpretation — specifically, what the Standard Model predicts. Theory A (data-driven hadronic vacuum polarization from e+e− cross-section data) gives a 4.2σ discrepancy with the experiment; Theory B (lattice QCD) gives 0.9σ. The two predictions disagree with each other at 3.7σ, entirely in the hadronic vacuum polarization contribution. Whether the measurement reveals new physics depends entirely on which theoretical approach is correct — and that is currently an unresolved question in theoretical particle physics.

Conflating experimental precision with interpretive certainty in either direction would be wrong. Claiming the measurement "confirms new physics" depends on Theory A being correct. Claiming the result is inconclusive misrepresents the experimental achievement. The correct statement: a highly precise measurement with a 4.2σ tension under one Standard Model calculation and 0.9σ under another.

The experimental result is established and scientifically significant. Its interpretation as evidence for or against beyond-Standard-Model physics awaits theoretical consensus on the hadronic vacuum polarization contribution.
