We have high confidence in the basic observation: a reproducible new LC-MS peak at m/z 312, about 16 mass units below the parent at m/z 328, appearing across three independent sample preparations. The retention shift of ~0.8 minutes earlier than the parent is also a solid measurement, and the linear increase in peak area with sample age over two weeks at 40°C / 75% RH is a meaningful pattern, even if we should confirm it over a longer window.

Given those data, it is reasonable to treat a methyl-loss degradant as the leading working hypothesis. The 16‑unit offset is consistent with CH₄ loss within instrument precision, the earlier retention time fits a more polar product, and the time-dependent growth under accelerated storage is the strongest line of evidence that this is generated over time rather than a one-off analytical glitch.

However, the solvent change is a real confound: we switched to a new acetonitrile lot from a different vendor about four weeks ago, and we have not yet run a method blank with the new acetonitrile alone. Without that control, I cannot be confident that the signal is intrinsic to the API rather than a trace contaminant that accumulates on the column and appears to “grow” with use.

My current view is that the evidence moderately favors a degradation interpretation, but a new‑acetonitrile blank (and old-versus-new solvent preparation) is required before treating that conclusion as established.
