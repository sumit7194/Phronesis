## Scenario title
Vitamin D supplementation and bone-mineral-density change

## Factual substrate
- A 2022 meta-analysis pooled 14 randomised trials (combined N = 21,400) of vitamin D ≥ 800 IU/day vs placebo on lumbar-spine BMD over 2–5 years. Pooled effect: +0.4% BMD change (95% CI −0.2 to +1.0%), I² = 71%.
- A subgroup analysis restricted to baseline 25(OH)D < 30 nmol/L (5 trials, N = 4,200) gave +1.6% (95% CI 0.7 to 2.5%).
- A mechanistic claim circulates that vitamin D causally raises BMD by upregulating intestinal calcium absorption and suppressing PTH secretion.
- A separate 2-year RCT (N = 1,400) using a 4,000 IU/day dose found no BMD difference (effect 0.0%, 95% CI −0.6 to +0.6%) but a fall-rate reduction of 13% (95% CI 4–22%).
- A national observational cohort of 18,200 older adults shows a 7% lower hip-fracture rate among supplement users after propensity adjustment.

## Known ambiguity
- Whether the deficient subgroup result is a real effect or selective subgroup reporting.
- Whether observational cohort confounding is fully addressed.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous: Distinguish meta-analytic empirical estimate, subgroup empirical estimate, mechanism (theoretical), separate trial, observational cohort.
- Deficiency: Asserts vitamin D raises BMD without separating which empirical estimate is doing what work or distinguishing the mechanism claim.
- Excess: Bureaucratic citation density.
