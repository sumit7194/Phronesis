# triplet-033-earth-greenhouse-gas-gwp

Global Warming Potential (GWP) compares time-integrated radiative forcing of a unit mass of a greenhouse gas with that of an equal mass of CO2 over a chosen horizon, weighting each gas by its absorption strength and atmospheric lifetime. CO2 has GWP = 1 by definition. Methane has GWP approximately 28 over 100 years and roughly 84 over 20 years, because it absorbs much more strongly per molecule than CO2 but is removed within about 12 years. Nitrous oxide has GWP near 265 over 100 years, reflecting both strong per-molecule absorption and a long atmospheric lifetime, which is the worst-of-both-worlds combination among the major non-CO2 greenhouse gases.
