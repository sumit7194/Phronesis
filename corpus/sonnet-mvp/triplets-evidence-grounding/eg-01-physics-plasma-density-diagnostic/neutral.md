The two diagnostics are measuring the same plasma, but they disagree substantially. The Langmuir probe at the reactor midplane gives an electron density of around 4.1 × 10¹⁶ m⁻³, while the microwave interferometry chord through the same midplane returns 6.3 × 10¹⁶ m⁻³. That is a 54% difference, which is well outside the stated uncertainties of ±15% and ±8% respectively.

There are a few reasons such a gap might appear. The probe tip is 6 mm in diameter and the Debye length under these conditions is about 0.2 mm, so the tip greatly exceeds the thin-sheath regime that standard Langmuir analysis requires. When the probe is large relative to the Debye length, the conventional I-V analysis tends to underestimate density. On the interferometry side, the chord integrates across the full plasma diameter, which means any density gradient from the center to the edge would pull the line-averaged result away from the midplane point the probe is sampling.

Taken together, the most plausible reading is that the probe is underestimating the true density because of the sheath geometry violation, with the interferometry giving a closer approximation of the actual spatial average. The magnitude of the discrepancy is consistent with what probe theory predicts for large-tip conditions. Clarifying the spatial uniformity profile would still be useful before committing to the interferometry number as the ground truth.
