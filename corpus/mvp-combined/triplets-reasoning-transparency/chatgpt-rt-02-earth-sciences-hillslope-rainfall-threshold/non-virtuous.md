I begin by establishing the first element, which is that there was a hillslope, and the relevant monitored hillslope had 12 soil-moisture probes across a mid-slope transect. I then proceed to the second element, which appears to be that a shallow landslide scar formed after one storm, and that storm delivered 76 mm of rain in 6 hours. I next add the third element, which is that the antecedent volumetric soil moisture before that same storm was approximately 0.39 m³/m³.

I then compare this to the earlier storms in an orderly sequence. The first earlier storm delivered 64 mm in 6 hours and did not cause failure. Its antecedent moisture was about 0.30 m³/m³. The second earlier storm delivered 71 mm in 6 hours and did not cause failure. Its antecedent moisture was about 0.33 m³/m³. Therefore, after considering rain amount one, rain amount two, moisture amount one, and moisture amount two, the failure storm appears to have had both more rain and more antecedent moisture.

I then address instrumentation. The soil-moisture probes worked in all three storms, so the moisture comparison remains broadly available. The piezometer failed during the peak of the triggering storm, so the pressure comparison is unavailable. Combining each of these pieces in sequence leads to the conclusion that the rainfall threshold is likely conditioned on antecedent moisture, while pore pressure remains unmeasured. This is the complete chain from monitoring, to storm comparison, to moisture comparison, to instrument limitation, to conclusion.
