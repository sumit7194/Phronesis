A conservation genetics study of a freshwater darter sampled 8 sites across 4 river drainages using three independent marker systems: 1,247 SNPs from RAD-seq, 12 microsatellite loci, and mtDNA haplotype sequences. All three independently recover the same three genetic clusters: Cluster 1 at sites 1–3 spanning two drainages, Cluster 2 at sites 4–6 in one drainage, and Cluster 3 at sites 7–8 in one drainage. Pairwise FST between clusters ranges from 0.17–0.19 (SNP), 0.14–0.16 (microsatellite), and 0.21–0.23 (mtDNA ΦST); within-cluster values are below 0.04 for all markers. Structure analysis at K=3, DAPC, and PCA all assign the same individuals to the same clusters, with assignment probabilities above 0.95 for 91 of 96 individuals. Gene flow between clusters is low, at less than 0.8 migrants per generation, compared to within-cluster values of 4–12 migrants per generation.

Three independent markers with different mutation rates, inheritance patterns, and potential biases converging on the same clustering result addresses the main artifacts of single-marker studies. The three-cluster population structure is robustly supported by this dataset.

What remains uncertain is the historical cause of the structure — vicariance, isolation by distance, secondary contact — and the temporal depth of divergence. Those questions cannot be answered from the current data, but they do not affect the current-state conclusion about how individuals group genetically across these river drainages.
