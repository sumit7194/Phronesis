ICP-MS analysis of eight trace metals in an environmental water sample, with three replicate injections per analyte, produced results spanning a wide range of signal-to-noise ratios relative to the instrument detection limit. Cadmium, lead, and arsenic were detected well above the IDL: Cd at 0.52 μg/L (42× IDL, CV = 3.1%), Pb at 1.84 μg/L (37× IDL, CV = 2.8%), and As at 3.21 μg/L (52× IDL, CV = 2.4%). These show tight replicate agreement and high signal-to-noise.

Chromium, nickel, and copper were measured closer to the IDL: Cr at 0.064 μg/L (3.2× IDL, CV = 11.4%), Ni at 0.048 μg/L (2.4× IDL, CV = 13.8%), and Cu at 0.031 μg/L (1.6× IDL, CV = 18.2%). The elevated CVs for these three reflect the increased measurement uncertainty at low signal-to-noise ratios.

Selenium and mercury presented differently. Se at 0.009 μg/L (0.9× IDL) had one of three replicates below detection and a CV that could not be calculated. Mercury at 0.006 μg/L (0.6× IDL) had two of three replicates below detection. Conventional reporting standards treat results at or below the IDL as not reliably quantifiable; these should be flagged rather than reported as definitive concentrations. All eight values appeared in the same table format without distinction, flags, or uncertainty annotations, treating near-IDL and below-IDL results as equivalent to the well-detected analytes.
