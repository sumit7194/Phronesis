The two diagnostics are measuring the same plasma, but they disagree substantially. The Langmuir probe at the reactor midplane gives an electron density of around 4.1 × 10¹⁶ m⁻³, while the microwave interferometry chord through the same midplane returns 6.3 × 10¹⁶ m⁻³. That 54% difference is observed directly in the instrument readings — it is a measurement fact, not a model output, and it sits well outside the stated uncertainties of ±15% and ±8%.

From there, interpretation requires care. The probe tip is 6 mm in diameter against a Debye length of roughly 0.2 mm, which means the thin-sheath assumption underlying standard Langmuir I-V analysis is violated. That violation is an observed geometric fact. What follows from it — that the probe underestimates density by some factor — is a theoretical prediction of sheath theory, and its magnitude in this geometry is model-dependent. I do not have a direct measurement of the correction factor; I have a theoretical prediction with its own uncertainty.

Similarly, the interferometry integrates along the full chord. That a chord average can exceed a midplane point value when radial gradients are present is a theoretical expectation; I have no radial scan to confirm whether gradients exist or how large they are in this reactor.

The empirical finding is unambiguous: the two instruments disagree by 54%. The attribution of that disagreement — to probe-sheath breakdown, to spatial non-uniformity, or to some combination — is a theoretical inference. Both mechanisms are plausible and neither can be excluded without additional calibration measurements.
