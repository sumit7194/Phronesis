## Scenario title
Antibiotic timing and surgical-site-infection rates: trial, registry, and lab data

## Factual substrate
- A multi-centre randomised trial of 2,148 elective colorectal surgery patients compared cefazolin given 0–30 min vs 30–60 min before incision; SSI rate was 4.1% vs 6.8% (RR 0.60, 95% CI 0.42–0.86, p = 0.005).
- A national surgical registry of 41,200 procedures from the past 5 years showed an adjusted SSI odds ratio of 0.78 (95% CI 0.69–0.88) for the 0–30 min window versus the 30–60 min window.
- Pharmacokinetic studies in 24 surgical patients showed mean cefazolin tissue concentrations peaking at 22 ± 6 µg/g at 25 min post-dose and falling to 9 ± 4 µg/g at 70 min.
- A meta-analysis pooling 9 prior RCTs reported a pooled SSI relative risk of 0.71 (95% CI 0.61–0.83) for earlier dosing windows.
- A single-surgeon case series of 84 patients reported no SSI events with strict 0–30 min adherence.

## Known ambiguity
- Registry data confounded by case-mix despite adjustment.
- The single-surgeon case series is observational and small.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous: Distinguish RCT, registry, PK, meta-analysis, case-series as five evidence types with different strength and meaning.
- Deficiency: Asserts the timing claim as established without specifying RCT vs registry vs PK vs case-series.
- Excess: Bureaucratic citation density.
