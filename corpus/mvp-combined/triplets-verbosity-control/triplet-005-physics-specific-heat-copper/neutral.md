# triplet-005-physics-specific-heat-copper

For a body of mass m and specific heat capacity c with no phase change, the heat required to change its temperature by ΔT is Q = m · c · ΔT. The specific heat of copper near room temperature is approximately 385 J/(kg·K). With m = 0.50 kg and ΔT = 20 K, the required heat is Q = 0.50 × 385 × 20 = 3850 J, or roughly 3.85 kJ.

The three quantities that enter the result are the mass, the specific heat, and the temperature change. Q depends linearly on each, so doubling any one would double the answer, and halving any one would halve it. No latent-heat term appears in this expression because no phase change is occurring in the 20 K temperature interval. The specific heat of copper is itself approximately constant in this range, though it does vary slowly with temperature in the broader picture. The 3850 J figure is the energy input under ideal conditions, with no heat lost to the surroundings. Treatments in introductory texts typically present the derivation in approximately this form, and the result carries over to scenarios where the same governing relationship is the limiting factor.
