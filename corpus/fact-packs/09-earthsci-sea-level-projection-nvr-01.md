## Scenario title

2100 sea level rise projections from an ice sheet model with uncertainty driven by marine ice cliff instability parameterization, where a non-virtuous passage arrives at "highly uncertain" without identifying the specific structural reason

## Factual substrate

- An ice sheet model projects 2100 sea level rise contribution from the West Antarctic Ice Sheet (WAIS) under an RCP 8.5 forcing scenario: central estimate 0.42 m, 5th–95th percentile range 0.18–0.98 m
- The wide uncertainty range is driven primarily by the Marine Ice Cliff Instability (MICI) parameterization: MICI is a proposed mechanism (ice cliff collapse above a threshold height triggers rapid discharge) that appears in some models but has no direct empirical calibration; runs including MICI produce values up to 0.98 m; runs excluding MICI produce 0.18–0.47 m
- The MICI mechanism is controversial in the glaciology community: some researchers argue it is physically constrained to low speeds by viscous buttressing; the one documented case of cliff collapse (Jakobshavn) occurred over decades, not years; MICI has not been directly observed at the speeds required for the high-end projections
- Observational constraints on ice dynamics (current WAIS mass loss rate: 147 ± 26 Gt/yr; grounding line retreat rates at Pine Island and Thwaites glaciers) are consistent with the central estimate range but do not constrain the MICI-driven high end
- The IPCC AR6 gives 2100 WAIS contribution (RCP 8.5) as 0.05–0.22 m (likely range) with "low confidence" in values exceeding 0.5 m, explicitly flagging MICI as the source of the high-end uncertainty

## Known ambiguity

- The projection uncertainty is structurally driven by whether MICI is included and whether it operates at the speeds the models assume — that is the specific cause of the wide range
- "Uncertain" is the correct conclusion, but it should be earned by identifying the MICI parameterization uncertainty as the specific driver, not by generic statements about ice sheet modeling complexity
- Non-virtuous-right (deficiency): arrives at "the projection is highly uncertain and should be treated with caution" through generic statements about modeling difficulty without naming MICI as the specific source of the high-end uncertainty

## Conclusion space

- The 0.18–0.98 m range is dominated by MICI uncertainty; excluding MICI scenarios gives 0.18–0.47 m; the current observational data do not constrain whether MICI operates at the speeds required for the high-end projections
- Virtuous reasoning: identifies MICI as the specific structural driver of the range, notes that observational evidence does not support the high-speed collapse required for high-end values, and concludes that the low-to-central range (0.18–0.47 m) is better constrained than the high end
- Non-virtuous-right (deficiency): concludes "highly uncertain" through generic modeling hedging
