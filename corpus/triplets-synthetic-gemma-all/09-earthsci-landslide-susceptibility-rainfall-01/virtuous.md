The logistic regression model demonstrates a **well-established** performance in identifying slopes with elevated long-term failure risk.  The model achieves an AUC of 0.87 and 82% accuracy on the validation set, effectively classifying 82% of potential landslide points. This **strongly supports** the model's effectiveness in identifying slopes susceptible to landslides over time. 

It's important to note that the model's spatial nature is crucial to understand. While it predicts susceptibility, not specific failure within a given storm event, the model's performance is promising. The 4-7% activation rate for the top 340 slopes within the highest susceptibility decile during major storm events **suggests** that, even with a high susceptibility, failure is not guaranteed. This underscores the need to avoid misinterpreting the model's output as a precise prediction of individual slope failures.

The model's performance, while strong, should not be conflated with a temporal prediction of landslide events. The 180 mm of predicted rainfall over 48 hours, coupled with the model's limitations, necessitates the use of additional data and models for predicting event-specific failure. This is a **plausible but not established** area for future research.
