The 20-year record documents 47 shallow landslides calibrated to a lower-envelope ID threshold: I = 14.2 × D⁻⁰·⁷³. Seven exceedances produced no landslides. Record covers shallow translational failures on slopes > 28° only. Antecedent soil moisture conditions were not recorded. An adjacent basin shows a higher threshold from an independent record.

The weakest link in applying this threshold for operational forecasting is the soil moisture problem — not the basin-transfer question, not the false-positive rate, and not the shallow-failure restriction. The threshold was calibrated across all 47 events regardless of how wet or dry the soils were at the time of each triggering storm; it is a mixed-state lower envelope that integrates events across the full range of antecedent moisture states. For individual operational events, two storms with identical I-D that hit soils at field capacity versus half field capacity will have very different outcomes, and the threshold gives no guidance on which outcome to expect. This is the step where the chain from "storm exceeds threshold" to "landslide imminent" is structurally broken.

The false-positive rate (7 of 54 exceedances, 13%) is quantified and known. The basin restriction (shallow translational only) is clearly stated. The adjacent-basin threshold difference means direct transfer to other basins requires caution but is a separable issue. None of these is unquantified. The soil moisture gap — unquantified, unrecorded, and order-of-magnitude in its potential influence on threshold behavior — is the specific link that limits operational use most severely and the one that needs to be addressed first if this threshold is to support real-time landslide warning.
