A cobalt-iron oxyhydroxide oxygen evolution reaction (OER) catalyst was electrochemically evaluated in 1M KOH at 25°C. Linear sweep voltammetry, run at 1 mV/s with iR correction, generated a Tafel slope of 39 ± 2 mV/dec between 1 and 10 mA/cm². This slope fundamentally aligns with a mechanism where a chemical step following a second electron transfer—traditionally the O–O bond formation step—is strictly rate-determining. Concurrently, a 40 mV/dec slope can also describe an alternative mechanism where the second electron transfer itself is rate-limiting, provided surface coverage of intermediate species remains potential-dependent.

Complementary in-situ Raman spectroscopy was deployed under active OER conditions. The analysis successfully identified a Co(IV)=O intermediate accumulating on the surface, while detecting no trace of iron-related intermediates within the spectral limits. Regarding overall catalyst stability, a 24-hour chronopotentiometry hold at 10 mA/cm² registered less than a 5% increase in overpotential. Subsequent post-test X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) revealed that the surface composition remained largely unchanged within standard measurement precision. Ultimately, the electrocatalyst achieved the 10 mA/cm² benchmark at an overpotential (η) of 285 mV, placing it within a competitive performance tier for related systems. This evaluation combines macroscopic performance tracking with localized spectrographic surface observation.
