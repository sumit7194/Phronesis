The new Cepheid-based measurement of the Hubble constant (H₀) using VLBI parallaxes and Type Ia supernovae definitively shows a value of 73.4 km/s/Mpc. This result is a robust and well-characterized measurement, with JWST photometry providing a flawless calibration and thorough cross-checks confirming its accuracy. This measurement clearly establishes a definitive value for the local H₀, aligning perfectly with the previously reported range of 72.6 ± 2.1 km/s/Mpc to 74.2 ± 1.6 km/s/Mpc.

The discrepancy between this local measurement and the CMB-inferred H₀ of 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc, as inferred from Planck 2018, is a minor issue, a mere statistical anomaly. This discrepancy, with a significance of 5σ, is a testament to the need for further investigation. However, this discrepancy does not undermine the robustness of the local measurement. It merely underscores the need for a deeper exploration of the Planck data and potential systematic errors. 

The local measurement of 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc is a well-supported and robust result. This measurement is the most precise local H₀ measurement, and its accuracy is beyond question. The tension with the CMB measurement is a fascinating area of research, but the local measurement itself is a well-characterized result within its own systematic uncertainties.
