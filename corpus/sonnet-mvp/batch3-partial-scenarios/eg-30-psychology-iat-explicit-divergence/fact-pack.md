## Scenario title

Divergence between explicit self-report and Implicit Association Test D-scores in a hiring-decision study

## Factual substrate

- A between-subjects study (N = 420, balanced across hiring managers and students) administers an IAT measuring gender–career implicit associations alongside an explicit attitude measure (a 7-item Likert scale measuring self-reported gender egalitarianism in career contexts).
- Mean IAT D-score: +0.42 (SD = 0.31), indicating a moderate implicit gender–career association (higher scores = stronger male–career pairing).
- Mean explicit scale score: 5.8 / 7.0 (SD = 0.7), indicating high explicit egalitarianism.
- Correlation between IAT D-score and explicit scale score: r = −0.08 (p = 0.11, n.s.), consistent with the literature showing weak IAT–explicit correlations for socially sensitive constructs.
- In a subsequent hiring decision task, IAT D-score predicted resume scoring for male versus female candidates (β = 0.14, p = 0.03) after controlling for explicit scale score.
- The test-retest reliability of the IAT in this study: r_tt = 0.44 over a 3-week interval.

## Known ambiguity

- The IAT D-score is an indirect behavioral measure — it reflects the relative ease of pairing category–attribute combinations under time pressure, not a direct measure of unconscious bias. The construct validity of the IAT as a measure of implicit attitudes is contested in the psychometric literature; the test-retest reliability of r_tt = 0.44 is low for a measure being used for individual-level inference.
- The predictive validity finding (β = 0.14) shows that IAT D-score predicts hiring decisions beyond explicit attitudes, but a β = 0.14 effect size is small and could reflect task-specific response strategies rather than implicit bias.

## Conclusion space

- Conclusion A (virtuous-compatible): The IAT D-score provides evidence of an implicit gender–career association in this sample, with some predictive validity for hiring decisions — but the IAT is an indirect behavioral measure with contested construct validity and low test-retest reliability (r_tt = 0.44), not a direct measure of unconscious bias; any inference about unconscious cognition requires that evidence-type limitation to be stated.
- Conclusion B (deficiency-failure-compatible): Presents the IAT D-score of +0.42 as direct evidence that participants have unconscious gender bias in hiring contexts, without specifying that the IAT is an indirect behavioral measure, without noting the test-retest reliability or the contested construct validity.
- Conclusion C (alternative): Notes the IAT–explicit divergence but attributes it entirely to social desirability bias in the explicit scale rather than acknowledging the IAT's own measurement limitations.
