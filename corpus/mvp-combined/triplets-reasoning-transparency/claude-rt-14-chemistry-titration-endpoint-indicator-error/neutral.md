The weak-acid titration with pKa = 4.76 against 0.1 M standardized NaOH gives 9.823 mL by potentiometric endpoint and 9.764 mL by phenolphthalein indicator endpoint, a difference of 0.059 mL or about 0.6% of the titer. Instrument-precision repeatability on either method is 0.05%, so the 0.6% discrepancy is well outside what individual-method noise can produce.

Phenolphthalein transitions from colorless to pink over the pH range 8.2 to 10.0, with the practical endpoint being judged by an observer at the first persistent faint pink tinge, typically corresponding to a pH around 9.0 to 9.5 in laboratory practice. The potentiometric inflection at the equivalence point for this titration sits at pH approximately 8.7, given that the conjugate-base concentration at the endpoint produces a slightly basic solution. The buffer region near equivalence produces a sharp pH transition from about 8.4 to 9.6 over the last 0.07 mL of titrant, so even small offsets in detected pH translate into measurable volume offsets.

The direction of the discrepancy fits an indicator-endpoint that reads before the potentiometric inflection. Both endpoints in turn assume that the standardized NaOH titer is correct, that the buret is calibrated, that atmospheric CO2 absorption during the titration is negligible, and that temperature is at the calibration value.

The reported result is that the 0.6% discrepancy is consistent with the known offset between phenolphthalein endpoint detection and the true equivalence point for a weak acid of this pKa, with the potentiometric titer providing the more accurate value.
