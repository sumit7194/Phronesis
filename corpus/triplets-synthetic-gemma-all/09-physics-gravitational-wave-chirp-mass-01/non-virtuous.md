Our detection of a gravitational wave signal with a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of 14.2 from a two-detector network definitively confirms the existence of a massive merger. Our chirp mass measurement, a robust and precise 3.42 ± 0.04 M_sun, decisively establishes the nature of the merging objects.  

Bayesian parameter estimation with uniform priors clearly shows that the primary component mass (m1) is 8.1 (+4.2, −2.8) M_sun, while the secondary component mass (m2) is 1.6 (+0.5, −0.4) M_sun. This confirms a mass ratio, q = m2/m1, of 0.20 (+0.15, −0.08), definitively indicating a neutron star component. 

The secondary mass posterior peaks at 1.6 M_sun, with a tail extending to 2.4 M_sun. This overlap with the neutron star mass range (<~2.2 M_sun) and the lower mass gap (2.2–5 M_sun) is a mere suggestion, not an ambiguity. The 78% posterior probability for m2 < 2.2 M_sun definitively proves the secondary component is a neutron star. 

The lack of an electromagnetic counterpart and the absence of a kilonova-like transient do not diminish our confidence. This merger event is unequivocally a neutron star merger, and further investigation is unnecessary.
