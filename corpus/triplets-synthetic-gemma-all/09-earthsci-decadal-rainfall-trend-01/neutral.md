The instrumental record shows a significant 15-year decline in mean seasonal rainfall across 47 rain gauges in East Africa, with a mean decrease of -8.4 mm/decade (p = 0.03). This decline corresponds to a 10% reduction compared to the 1960-2000 reference climatology of 347 mm/season, and aligns with a predominantly negative Interdecadal Pacific Oscillation (IPO) phase during this period. The IPO is known to suppress March-May rainfall in this region by 15-30 mm/season.  

The 15-year instrumental record is insufficient to fully differentiate the current rainfall anomaly from multi-decadal natural variability, including the IPO and the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD). Paleoclimate proxies from lake sediment cores spanning 500 years show two past multi-decadal droughts with amplitudes comparable to the current decline, occurring without anthropogenic forcing. These observations suggest the current anomaly is not unprecedented in amplitude. 

While the report attributes the 15-year decline solely to climate change, this attribution statement is premature given the short record length. The observed decline could be a result of both natural decadal variability and anthropogenic influences. A more robust attribution would acknowledge the potential contributions from both climate change and natural decadal variability, and the need for longer-term data to disentangle the relative contributions.
