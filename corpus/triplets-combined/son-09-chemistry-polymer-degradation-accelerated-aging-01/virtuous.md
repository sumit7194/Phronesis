The accelerated weathering dataset and the 3-year natural exposure results are internally consistent and support the validity of the 3× acceleration model within the surface degradation regime. The 4,000-hour test performance — gloss retention 72%, ΔE = 3.2, adhesion 4B — meets all specifications, and the natural panel data at 88% gloss retention matches the model within the precision expected from ambient variability. This is a well-validated dataset for the regime it characterizes.

The problem with extending these results to a 20-year service life claim is not the data quality; it is the regime boundary. Polyurethane coatings undergo a documented mechanistic shift from surface chalking to bulk cracking and delamination at approximately 40–50% gloss retention. The Arrhenius extrapolation projects gloss retention of 48% at 20 years, which sits exactly at this transition. The 3× acceleration ratio was established and validated within the surface regime; its applicability below the transition is not supported by any data in this study.

The conservative conclusion is that the coating is performing well through the first 6.8 years of equivalent service, and the data support that claim with confidence. The 20-year performance projection, particularly regarding whether ΔE and gloss retention specifications will be maintained after the bulk degradation transition, cannot be stated with equivalent confidence from the current dataset.
