A retrospective cohort study examined whether an inflammatory biomarker (biomarker X) measured at ICU admission predicts septic shock onset in critically ill patients. The study enrolled 280 patients admitted to a single academic ICU over 36 months, of whom 68 (24%) developed septic shock within 72 hours.

Biomarker X was significantly higher in the septic shock group at admission: median 144 pg/mL versus 51 pg/mL in the no-shock group (p < 0.001). ROC analysis produced an AUC of 0.74 (95% CI: 0.67 to 0.81). At an empirically optimized threshold of 98 pg/mL, the biomarker achieved sensitivity of 72%, specificity of 66%, positive predictive value of 38%, and negative predictive value of 88%.

The PPV of 38% indicates that approximately 6 of 10 patients flagged by the threshold will not develop septic shock. The NPV of 88% reflects stronger performance for ruling out shock than ruling it in.

Study limitations include the single-center retrospective design, admission-only biomarker sampling without serial measurements, heterogeneous pre-ICU antibiotic exposure not fully controlled, and the absence of an external validation cohort. The threshold of 98 pg/mL was empirically selected in the same dataset used to evaluate it. The authors proposed biomarker X as a candidate for prospective evaluation as an early warning tool.
