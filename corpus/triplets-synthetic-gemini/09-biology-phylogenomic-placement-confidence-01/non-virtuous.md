It is difficult to determine the true phylogenomic placement of this unusual parasitic plant, despite the recent 73-gene molecular analysis. The study used maximum likelihood, Bayesian inference, ASTRAL, and parsimony, all pointing toward Malpighiales as a sister to Violaceae. However, we have to remember that phylogenetics is always uncertain and inherently sensitive to model assumptions. Historically, morphologists strongly argued for Aristolochiales or Laurales, and that 150-year debate cannot be easily dismissed by a single sequence alignment.

Furthermore, analyzing highly modified parasites is notoriously difficult. Long-branch attraction is always a persistent concern in these deep evolutionary trees, and horizontal gene transfer from the parasitic host can severely complicate any molecular interpretation. Even though the authors performed some tests for compositional bias and pruned the taxon sets, molecular methods simply have too many blind spots to provide a definitive answer. Before we rewrite the taxonomy of this enigmatic plant, we need significantly more evidence. We really cannot accept the Malpighiales placement as an established fact given the fundamental limitations and uncertainties present in any phylogenetic reconstruction. It is too early to draw final conclusions about this organism's true evolutionary history. The taxonomy ultimately remains inherently unresolved until these critical phylogenetic ambiguities are fully addressed.
