The neutron lifetime has been measured by two distinct experimental methods. Beam experiments measure the proton production rate from free neutrons decaying in a beam; bottle experiments store ultracold neutrons and count surviving particles. Historically, beam experiments have yielded lifetimes approximately 8-10 seconds longer than bottle experiments. The beam experiment average is approximately 888.0 ± 2.0 s; the bottle experiment average prior to this study is approximately 879.4 ± 0.5 s, a discrepancy of approximately 3.9σ.

A new magnetic bottle experiment was performed with three independent systematic cross-checks: direct measurements using two independent detector configurations, a blind analysis conducted by two independent analysis teams, and a systematic uncertainty budget validated against Monte Carlo simulation of neutron loss mechanisms. An independent review of the systematic budget identified no additional unaccounted error sources; the two blind analysis teams produced consistent results.

The new experiment reports τ_n = 877.8 ± 0.3 (statistical) ± 0.4 (systematic) s, a total uncertainty of ±0.5 s, the most precise bottle measurement to date. The result is consistent with all prior bottle experiments within their uncertainties and 3.6σ from the beam experiment average.
