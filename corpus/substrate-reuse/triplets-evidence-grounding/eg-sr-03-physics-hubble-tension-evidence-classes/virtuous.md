Five H₀ measurements are on the table, and they fall into two categories of evidence that do not combine straightforwardly.

The local distance-ladder values — this work at 73.4 ± 1.1, SH0ES at 73.0 ± 1.4, and H0liCOW at 74.2 ± 1.6 km/s/Mpc — are all observational measurements from the nearby universe using direct-calibration techniques (Cepheid period-luminosity relations with Type Ia supernovae, or gravitational lensing time delays). These are different methods but share an evidence class: empirical distance calibration without cosmological-model assumptions. They agree within their stated uncertainties.

The TRGB value at 72.6 ± 2.1 km/s/Mpc is also a distance-ladder measurement but uses a different calibrator (tip of the red giant branch rather than Cepheids), providing an independent observational cross-check within the same evidence class.

The Planck value of 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc is a qualitatively different kind of evidence: a model-dependent inference from the CMB power spectrum, valid only under ΛCDM. It is not a direct distance measurement; it is a prediction of what H₀ should be if the standard cosmological model is correct and the CMB systematic analysis is right.

The ~5σ tension is therefore a discrepancy between two evidence types, not between two measurements of the same quantity. Proposed resolutions — unidentified distance-ladder systematics, unidentified CMB-analysis systematics, or a modification to ΛCDM — each operate on one of the two evidence classes. Naming which class a proposed resolution targets is a pre-condition for evaluating it. The current data do not allow the tension to be resolved; they do allow it to be correctly described.
