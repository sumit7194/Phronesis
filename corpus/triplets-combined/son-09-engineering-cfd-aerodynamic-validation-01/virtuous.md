The cruise-condition validation is excellent and establishes genuine CFD credibility within the validated flow regime. At α = 2°, the model achieves less than 1% error in lift, less than 3% error in drag, and Cp distribution matched at 95% of chord stations. This level of accuracy is meaningful and demonstrates the SST k-ω model performs well for the attached flow conditions that characterize cruise.

The stall prediction at α = 14° is a different matter. RANS turbulence models are built on the assumption of attached or mildly separated flows with a well-defined turbulent stress closure. Near stall, the boundary layer separates over a large portion of the upper surface, producing large-scale unsteady structures that RANS steady-state solutions cannot represent. The validation at α = 2° provides no evidence that the model performs reliably at α = 14°; these are physically distinct flow regimes.

The literature is specific about this limitation: RANS CL_max overprediction of 5–15% is well-documented for similar configurations. A 5–15% error in CL_max would propagate directly into structural load estimates at the flight envelope boundary — a safety-critical application. Before using the CL_max prediction for structural sizing, wind tunnel data at high α or higher-fidelity simulation (Detached Eddy Simulation or Large Eddy Simulation) is required to bound the prediction error.
