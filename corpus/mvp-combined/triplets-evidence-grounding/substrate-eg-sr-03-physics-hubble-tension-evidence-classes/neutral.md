A new local Hubble constant measurement used Cepheid variable period-luminosity relations calibrated via VLBI geometric parallaxes in the Large Magellanic Cloud, and applied to Cepheid observations in 19 galaxies hosting Type Ia supernovae. The derived Hubble constant is H₀ = 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc (1σ). The Cepheid photometry for the calibration was independently verified using JWST data to assess potential crowding bias in ground-based Cepheid measurements; the JWST verification shifted the result by less than 0.4 km/s/Mpc. Systematic error cross-checks included period-luminosity metallicity dependence, dust extinction corrections, and photometric zero-point calibration; no single systematic shifted the result by more than 0.8 km/s/Mpc.

Independently derived local H₀ values from other methods include: SH0ES 2022 (Cepheid + SNe Ia) at 73.0 ± 1.4, Freedman CCHP 2021 (tip of the red giant branch calibration) at 72.6 ± 2.1, and H0liCOW lensing time delay measurements at 74.2 ± 1.6 km/s/Mpc. The Planck 2018 CMB analysis, assuming a ΛCDM cosmological model, derives H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc.

The tension between local distance ladder measurements and the CMB-inferred value is approximately 5σ and has not been resolved. Proposed explanations include unidentified systematics in the Cepheid distance ladder, unidentified systematics in the CMB power spectrum analysis, and extensions to ΛCDM such as early dark energy or interacting dark matter. No single proposed explanation has gained consensus support. The CMB analysis assumes ΛCDM throughout; if the cosmological model requires modification, the inferred H₀ would shift accordingly.
