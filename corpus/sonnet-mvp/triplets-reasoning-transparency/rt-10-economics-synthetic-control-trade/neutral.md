Synthetic control for country A's export growth after an FTA with country B. Donor pool: 22 countries. Optimal weights: four countries (W = 0.31, 0.28, 0.24, 0.17). Pre-treatment fit RMSPE: 0.41% of GDP (vs 0.62% mean across donors). Post-treatment gap: +3.1 pp of GDP in exports by year 20. Placebo p-value (post/pre RMSPE ratio ranked 3rd of 22): approximately 0.14. Years 9–10 show some pre-treatment deviation in the synthetic control fit.

Step 1: the four-country synthetic control fit achieves an RMSPE of 0.41%, better than the average donor RMSPE of 0.62%, suggesting a reasonably well-matched counterfactual — the pre-treatment parallel trends hold approximately. Step 2: by year 20 the gap between observed A and synthetic A is +3.1 pp of GDP, which is the raw effect estimate. Step 3: country A's post/pre RMSPE ratio ranks 3rd out of 22 — meaning roughly 2 of 22 donor placebos show a ratio at least this large by chance. With 22 placebos, a rank of 3 gives a one-sided p-value of approximately 0.14. That is 1-in-7 odds: the gap is above average but not rare under the null. Step 4: the years 9–10 pre-treatment deviation means the synthetic control was not a perfect match even before the FTA, which weakens the counterfactual. Step 5: together, the inference is that there is suggestive but not strong statistical evidence that the FTA increased exports by approximately 3.1 pp — the estimate is directionally plausible, but a p-value of 0.14 in a placebo test means this finding warrants replication and should not be treated as a definitive causal claim.
