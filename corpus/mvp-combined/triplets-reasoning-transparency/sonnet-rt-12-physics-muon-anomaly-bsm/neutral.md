The experimental muon g-2 measurement: a_μ^exp = 116 592 061 ± 41 × 10⁻¹¹. The consensus SM theory prediction (data-driven HVP): a_μ^SM = 116 591 810 ± 43 × 10⁻¹¹. Discrepancy: 251 ± 59 × 10⁻¹¹, or 4.2σ. A lattice QCD HVP calculation gives a larger value than the data-driven result, reducing the discrepancy to approximately 1.5σ if substituted. The data-driven and lattice results are inconsistent at approximately 2.1σ.

Step 1: the 4.2σ figure uses the data-driven HVP as the theory prediction. Step 2: the lattice QCD calculation is an independent first-principles computation of the same hadronic contribution — if it is correct, the discrepancy with experiment shrinks to approximately 1.5σ. Step 3: the two theory predictions (data-driven and lattice) are themselves inconsistent at 2.1σ, which means the theory community has not converged on a single number for the dominant hadronic contribution. Step 4: because the 4.2σ versus 1.5σ tension depends entirely on which theory calculation is correct, and those calculations currently disagree with each other at 2.1σ, the 4.2σ number cannot be straightforwardly interpreted as the evidence strength for BSM physics — it is the discrepancy conditional on the data-driven HVP being the correct theory value. Step 5: the appropriate statement is that the muon g-2 discrepancy is between 1.5σ and 4.2σ depending on the correct resolution of the theory tension, and that BSM physics is not established until the hadronic vacuum polarization question is resolved between data-driven and lattice approaches.
