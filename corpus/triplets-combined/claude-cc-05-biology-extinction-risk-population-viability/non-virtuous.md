Population stands at 145 adults across 6 subpopulations. The PVA, run with 1,000 stochastic simulations under current conditions, gives a 50-year extinction probability of 0.27. Carrying-capacity sensitivity at ±20% gives a range of 0.18 to 0.41. The corridor scenario produces a median 50-year extinction probability of 0.14.

The honest summary for the manager is that this population faces a fair chance of extinction over the planning window if nothing changes, and that the chance is non-trivial but not overwhelming. The picture is something I would describe as concerning rather than dire — the population is small and structured, and the simulation is fairly clear that current conditions leave it exposed, but it is also not so small that loss is essentially inevitable.

The corridor scenario looks like a meaningful improvement. The median extinction probability comes down quite a bit relative to status quo, and that translates into a reasonably solid case for the investment. I am fairly confident that the corridor reduces extinction risk in a way that matters, though I would not want to overstate how tightly we can pin the size of that reduction in the real world rather than in the simulation.

What I would caution the manager about is that PVA outputs are conditional on the model structure and parameters, and our parameters carry their own uncertainty beyond what the carrying-capacity sensitivity captures. The numbers are useful as relative comparisons more than as forecasts. I will frame the briefing around the comparison between status quo and corridor scenarios, and let the manager weigh the conservation gain against the cost without treating the simulation outputs as settled predictions of the future.
