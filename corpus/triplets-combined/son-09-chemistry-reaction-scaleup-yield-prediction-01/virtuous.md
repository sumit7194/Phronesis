The small-scale optimization data for reaction R are solid. Five replicate runs at 0.1 mmol with a mean yield of 92.1% and SD of 2.3% represent genuine, reproducible optimization success under the tested conditions. That finding should be reported with appropriate confidence.

The question of whether this yield transfers to 100 g scale is a separate and genuinely uncertain one. The 3,500-fold scale increase introduces two well-characterized engineering challenges for this reaction class. First, heat removal: the lithiation step releases approximately 45 kJ/mol, and maintaining −78°C throughout addition and quench in a multi-liter vessel requires a cryostat system whose capacity has not been characterized at this scale. If the temperature drifts above −65°C during addition, byproduct formation increases substantially. Second, mixing: at 0.1 mmol scale, hand-swirling achieves uniform reagent distribution within seconds; at 100 g scale in a viscous, cold reaction mixture, the time required for adequate mixing by mechanical stirring is longer, and non-uniform distribution during the critical addition step will affect selectivity.

Process chemistry practice for temperature-sensitive exothermic reactions recommends incremental scale-up with intermediate stages at 1 g or 10 g before committing to 100 g. Proceeding directly without pilot runs treats scale-invariance as an assumption rather than a demonstrated property of this specific system.
