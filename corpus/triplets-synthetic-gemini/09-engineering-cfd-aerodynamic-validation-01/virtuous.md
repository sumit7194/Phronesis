The Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) CFD aerodynamic simulation explicitly demonstrates remarkable accuracy at baseline cruise conditions. Matching the experimental wind tunnel lift coefficient (CL) exactly within a pristine 0.8% error margin alongside near-perfect structural pressure distribution matching confirms the incredibly dense 14.2-million-cell grid represents superb foundational computational modeling. The engineers have securely optimized the CFD environment perfectly for low-angle aerodynamics.

However, actively utilizing this specific computational baseline to project a precise CL_max hitting exactly 1.62 during severe stall boundaries (α = 14°) is a massive and dangerous computational extrapolation. RANS models actively relying strictly upon SST k-ω turbulence parameters systematically shatter when encountering violently separated, massive high-angle flow profiles. Because steady-state algorithms simply cannot calculate chaotic separating boundary layers accurately, using known mathematical vectors that notoriously overpredict structural limits by 5-15% to finalize critical flight envelope sizes guarantees incredibly dangerous design flaws. My working view recognizes the original CFD matrix as mathematically pristine strictly limited to standard cruise parameter mapping, while firmly refusing to deploy the 1.62 high-alpha stall estimate without aggressively demanding external physical aerodynamic testing or transitioning entirely to advanced massively computational DES/LES turbulence resolving frameworks. Computational limitations inevitably break complex high-alpha aerodynamic estimations.
