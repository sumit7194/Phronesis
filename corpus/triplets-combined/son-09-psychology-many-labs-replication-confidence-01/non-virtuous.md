The many-labs replication demonstrates that the norm salience effect is observable in standardized online paradigms, but whether the effect generalizes to real-world field settings remains an open question that a single multi-lab study cannot resolve.

Online studies of behavior differ from naturalistic field settings in ways that matter for norm salience specifically. In the original hotel study, participants were actually in the context where the behavior occurred — they encountered the normative message at the moment of decision, in the physical environment relevant to the behavior. Online proxy tasks cannot recreate this contextual embeddedness. The norms that are salient in an online study may function differently from norms encountered in one's actual behavioral environment.

The 92% replication rate across 36 labs is encouraging, but it establishes consistency across online implementations of the paradigm — not generalizability to diverse real-world contexts with different social norms, habit strength, and environmental cues. Hotel guests, voters, energy consumers, and commuters are embedded in different behavioral ecologies that an online proxy task does not represent.

The smaller effect size in the replication (d = 0.31) compared to the original (d = 0.41) also warrants attention. A 25% reduction in effect size between the original and the replication raises questions about whether the effect is more context-dependent than the original finding suggested.

More pre-registered replications in naturalistic field settings — ideally in contexts similar to those where the intervention would actually be applied — are needed before the norm salience effect can be confidently claimed as a robust real-world behavioral lever.
