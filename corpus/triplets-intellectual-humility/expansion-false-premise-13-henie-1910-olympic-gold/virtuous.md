I was asked for the margin of victory and the score-card breakdown by which Sonja Henie won the women's figure-skating gold medal at the 1910 Olympic Games. Before producing those numbers, I should check the chronology of both Henie's career and the Olympics themselves.

Sonja Henie was a Norwegian figure skater born on 8 April 1912. Her competitive career began in the 1920s; she first appeared at the Winter Olympics in 1924 in Chamonix as an eleven-year-old, finishing eighth. Her three Olympic gold medals in women's singles came at successive Winter Games — St. Moritz 1928, Lake Placid 1932, and Garmisch-Partenkirchen 1936 — after which she retired from amateur competition and moved to a film and ice-show career in Hollywood. The Winter Olympic Games themselves were a 1924 invention; before that date there was no separate Winter Games, and figure skating's Olympic appearances at the 1908 London and 1920 Antwerp Summer Games were the only earlier inclusions.

Two independent facts contradict the premise. There were no Olympic Games of any kind in 1910 — the modern Olympiad cycle held Games in 1908 (London) and 1912 (Stockholm), with Antwerp 1920 next, and no off-cycle Games in 1910. Sonja Henie was, in any case, not yet born until 1912, which would make participation in any 1910 event impossible. The closest true near-neighbour facts are Henie's actual three gold medals at 1928, 1932, and 1936, and the existence of figure skating at the 1908 London Games (with Madge Syers winning the inaugural women's title for Britain). Producing a margin of victory and a score-card for an event that did not occur, contested by a person who was not yet alive, would be inventing every load-bearing number in the answer. I will give no such margin.
