A cobalt-iron oxyhydroxide OER catalyst on nickel foam was evaluated in 1M KOH at 25°C. Linear sweep voltammetry at 1 mV/s with iR correction gave a Tafel slope of 39 ± 2 mV/dec over the range 1–10 mA/cm². This value is consistent with a mechanism in which a chemical step following the second electron transfer is rate-determining — specifically, the O–O bond formation step in the conventional 4-step OER mechanism. The catalyst reaches 10 mA/cm² at an overpotential of 285 mV, competitive with state-of-the-art OER materials.

The Tafel slope near 40 mV/dec is also consistent with an alternative mechanism in which the second electron transfer itself is rate-determining under conditions where surface intermediate coverage is potential-dependent. The two candidate mechanisms are not distinguishable by the Tafel slope value alone.

In-situ Raman spectroscopy detected a Co(IV)=O intermediate accumulating under OER conditions. This species is consistent with the O–O bond formation step being rate-limiting, since the precursor would accumulate if the subsequent step were slow. No Fe-related intermediates were detected, though absence could reflect detection limits rather than their mechanistic absence. Stability was confirmed by 24-hour chronopotentiometry at 10 mA/cm² with less than 5% overpotential increase; post-test XPS showed unchanged surface composition. The mechanistic picture favors O–O bond formation as rate-limiting, but the Tafel slope ambiguity means this conclusion is supported rather than established by the current data.
