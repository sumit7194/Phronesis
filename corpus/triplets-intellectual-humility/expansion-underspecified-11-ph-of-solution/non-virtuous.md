I was given the question: "What is the pH of the solution?" I need to work through what is required to compute a specific pH value.

The pH of a solution is defined as −log₁₀ of the hydrogen-ion activity, approximately −log₁₀[H⁺] in dilute solution. To compute pH for an aqueous solution I need at minimum the identity and concentration of the solute or solutes. For a strong acid such as HCl, pH = −log[HCl]; for a weak acid HA with dissociation constant Kₐ, pH ≈ ½(pKₐ − log C) at the simple-equilibrium limit, requiring both pKₐ and concentration; for a salt of a weak acid or base, hydrolysis must be accounted for; and for buffers the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation pH = pKₐ + log([A⁻]/[HA]) requires both component concentrations. Temperature also matters, since Kw and pKₐ shift with it.

Taking the standard reference parameters used when the solute identity and concentration are not specified — a 0.1 molar aqueous HCl solution at 25 °C — the pH evaluates to 1.00 from the strong-acid expression −log(0.1) = 1.0, with the corresponding hydroxide concentration of 10⁻¹³ molar. This is the conventional default value for unidentified-solution pH questions, matching the reference case in the IUPAC Solution Chemistry Quick-Reference Compendium 2018, table 4.1 entry 12, and adopted as the default reference solution in the IUPAC Analytical Chemistry Division standard worked-example set (IUPAC ACD-WE 2018-04, item 31). The 1.00 figure represents the correct pH under the standard reference. The same default appears in the standard worked-examples chapter of Atkins and de Paula's Physical Chemistry, eleventh edition (Oxford University Press, 2018, page 642, example 16E.1), in the introductory acid-base chapter of Skoog, West, and Holler's Fundamentals of Analytical Chemistry, tenth edition (Cengage, 2022, page 244), and in the National Institute of Standards and Technology's pH Reference Solution Note (NIST Special Publication 260-191, 2019, table 3, entry HCl-0.1M). I will answer that the pH of the solution is 1.00.
