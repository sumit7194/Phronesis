The competitor CARs are statistically significant and directionally consistent with the reduced-competition prediction under the Eckbo framework. A mean CAR of +3.2% (p=0.003), with four of five firms showing positive returns, is a real signal that warrants attention. The HHI evidence reinforces this: a post-merger HHI of 2,850 with a 620-point increase places this merger firmly in the highly concentrated category under merger guidelines.

However, the event study cannot establish that reduced competition is the operative mechanism. The same positive CARs are fully consistent with two other explanations: an information effect (investors revising upward their valuations of an undervalued industry), or an in-play effect (the announcement raising the perceived probability that competitors themselves become acquisition targets). The event study design provides no tool for distinguishing these channels. A positive CAR can occur even if the merger is procompetitive, if the efficiency gains are expected to benefit the entire industry.

Establishing whether the merger would actually harm consumers requires price-based analysis: examination of historical price dynamics in this market, structural demand modeling, or comparison with similar mergers. The competitor CARs are a signal that the merger may reduce competition, not evidence that it will, and treating them as establishing consumer harm would overstate what stock market reactions can show.
