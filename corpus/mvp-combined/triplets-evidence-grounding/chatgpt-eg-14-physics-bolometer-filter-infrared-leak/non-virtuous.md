The baseline problem was an infrared leak from the warm baffle. The bolometer array had a 14 mV offset during window-open calibration, and once the infrared blocking filter was installed the offset dropped to 3 mV. That is the decisive result. If blocking infrared radiation removes most of the offset, then infrared radiation was the cause.

The rest of the checks fit the same conclusion. Dark runs with the window closed stayed within 2 mV before and after the filter was added, so the detector baseline itself was not drifting. The readout electronics noise spectrum was unchanged, so the electronics were not responsible. The detector bath temperature changed by less than 0.02 K, which rules out a broader cryogenic-temperature problem. The infrared camera showed the warm baffle near the window, which identifies the source.

My conclusion is that the warm baffle was loading the array through the open calibration window. The infrared blocking filter fixed the problem by cutting that path. Electromagnetic pickup and readout drift are not serious alternatives here, because neither explains why the filter reduced the offset from 14 mV to 3 mV while the other diagnostics stayed stable.
