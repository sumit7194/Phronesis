The patient is 56, called back from screening with a category 0 finding requiring additional views, no family history, no prior abnormal screens. Recall rate in this age band is 9.4% and the long-run cancer detection rate at recall, PPV1, is about 4.5%.

The thing she needs to hear, before anything else, is that a callback is fairly common in routine screening at her age and the great majority of callbacks resolve toward benign findings rather than cancer. Almost one in ten women screened in her band gets called back, and the cancer detection rate among those recalls is small. Her own profile — no family history, no prior abnormal screens — sits her toward the lower-risk end of recalled patients, though the difference from the average recalled patient at her age is not large.

So the callback raises her cancer chance somewhat above what it would have been without the screening view at all, but the overall chance still sits at quite a low level. The visible majority of recalls clear with the additional views alone, and most of the rest clear with the ultrasound or short-interval follow-up. Biopsy is the exception rather than the rule at this stage.

When I talk to her, I will frame it as a routine next step rather than as evidence pointing strongly toward cancer. I am reasonably confident that this framing matches the underlying data: the callback is informative but not nearly as informative as the experience of receiving one tends to feel. The additional imaging is what is designed to actually move the question one way or the other, and I will let her know what to expect at the appointment.
