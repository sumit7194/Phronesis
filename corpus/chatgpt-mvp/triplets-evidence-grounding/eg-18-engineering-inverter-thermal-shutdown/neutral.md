The inverter shutdowns look primarily thermal. During a 14-day heat period, the rooftop solar inverter shut down 11 times. Internal heat-sink temperature exceeded 84°C before 9 of those shutdowns, and ambient rooftop temperature exceeded 39°C on 10 of the 14 days. A dust mat was found blocking approximately 60% of the enclosure vent area, which gives a plausible reason for poor heat rejection.

The post-cleaning observation strengthens the ventilation explanation. After the vents were cleaned, no shutdown occurred during the next 7 days even though ambient temperatures were similar. Grid voltage stayed within the inverter's operating range during all events, so voltage disturbance is not a strong explanation for the shutdowns.

The remaining caution is that the post-cleaning period was shorter than the initial 14-day window. Inverter aging could also make the unit more sensitive to heat than when new. Still, the combination of high heat-sink temperatures, blocked vents, stable grid voltage, and disappearance of shutdowns after cleaning points toward blocked ventilation as the main contributor, with hot rooftop conditions as an enabling factor for this particular failure pattern.
