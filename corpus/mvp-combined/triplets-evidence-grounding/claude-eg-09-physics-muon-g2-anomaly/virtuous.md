The empirical claim and the theoretical claim need to be separated here, because they have different evidential status.

Empirically, aμ(exp) = 116592061(41) × 10⁻¹¹ is a measurement. The 217 ppb total combined uncertainty includes a 56 ppb beam-dynamics systematic, and there is no flagged issue beyond the published budget. That experimental number is the most direct piece of the picture and rests on the muon precession data and the field-uniformity calibration of the storage ring.

The theoretical claim — that the SM prediction is aμ(SM) = 116591810(43) × 10⁻¹¹ via the dispersive route — is not a measurement. It is a calculation that takes e⁺e⁻ → hadrons cross-section data through dispersion relations to compute the hadronic vacuum polarisation. It is anchored to data, but the anchor is data on a different process.

The lattice-QCD calculation of the same hadronic vacuum polarisation piece is also theoretical, but with different inputs and different systematics. Substituting the lattice value brings the SM into 1.5σ agreement with experiment. The two theoretical predictions disagree with each other at the 2.5σ level on the hadronic piece itself. That is theory–theory disagreement, not theory–experiment disagreement.

A separate empirical input, the new e⁺e⁻ → π⁺π⁻ low-energy cross-section measurement, disagrees with prior measurements by ~3σ in the region that dominates the dispersive integral. If correct, it would shift the dispersive prediction toward the lattice and reduce the experiment–theory tension.

The 4.2σ "anomaly" is a statement about experiment versus the dispersive SM prediction specifically. It is not yet a model-independent empirical signature of new physics: the inference rests on which of two theoretical inputs is right, and on whether the new cross-section measurement is correct.
