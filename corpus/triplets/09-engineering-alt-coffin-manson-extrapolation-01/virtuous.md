The Coffin-Manson methodology is the appropriate framework for solder joint fatigue acceleration, but the validity of the AF = 9.0 extrapolation depends on the test conditions remaining within the range for which the exponent was characterized. Here, two concerns limit confidence in the 207,000-cycle prediction.

First, the IPC-9701A exponent m = 2.0 was characterized at ΔTj = 20–80°C, corresponding to Δεp ≤ 0.005. The ATC protocol at ΔTj = 135°C produces Δεp ≈ 0.0072 in this package — 44% beyond the characterized upper bound. Outside the characterized strain range, the power-law relationship is an extrapolation whose accuracy is unknown. Errors in the exponent at high strain compound nonlinearly in the acceleration factor, and a small upward shift in the true m at high strain would substantially reduce the predicted field life.

Second, the anomalous Cu6Sn5 IMC thickening observed in post-test cross-sections indicates that high-ΔT diffusion-dominated growth kinetics activated during ATC. This competing failure mode has a different activation energy from the solder fatigue mechanism modeled by Coffin-Manson, and the ATC conditions may have over-accelerated the IMC pathway relative to field conditions — making the ATC test non-representative of the field failure mode ranking.

With a 3.5% margin over a 200,000-cycle target and two model validity concerns, the design cannot be claimed as qualified from this ATC test alone. Intermediate-ΔT testing at 80–100°C — within the characterized strain range — is needed to validate the acceleration model before design qualification is claimed.
