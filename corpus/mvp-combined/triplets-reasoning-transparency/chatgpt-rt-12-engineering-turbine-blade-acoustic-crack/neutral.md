The blade record is concerning enough to justify inspection. After the lightning storm, the monitoring system recorded a new acoustic emission cluster of 63 events over 4 operating hours. Blade strain amplitude increased by 18% during the same period. Rotor speed and wind speed were within the normal operating envelope, so the signal is not easily explained by unusual average operating conditions.

The drone inspection adds a local visual clue. It found a 12 cm surface mark near the acoustic source estimate. That mark could be lightning-related surface damage, a sign of a crack, or a superficial feature unrelated to the acoustic cluster. Rain continued during the monitoring window, which matters because rain impact can create acoustic noise and complicate source interpretation.

My working conclusion is that a blade defect is plausible and should be checked promptly. The combined acoustic cluster, higher strain amplitude, and nearby surface mark are enough to escalate. I would not yet call it a confirmed crack because rain and post-storm surface damage can mimic parts of the pattern. The next step should be close visual or ultrasonic inspection during a dry window.
