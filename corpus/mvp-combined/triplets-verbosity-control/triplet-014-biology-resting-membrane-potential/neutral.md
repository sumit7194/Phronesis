# triplet-014-biology-resting-membrane-potential

A typical neuron rests at approximately −70 mV inside relative to outside, set by ion gradients across its plasma membrane and the relative permeabilities to those ions. Intracellular K⁺ sits near 140 mM and extracellular K⁺ near 5 mM, while Na⁺ is reversed at roughly 12 mM inside and 145 mM outside. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase maintains these gradients by pumping 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per ATP.

At rest, the membrane is roughly 50× more permeable to K⁺ than to Na⁺ because K⁺ leak channels are open while most voltage-gated Na⁺ channels are closed. The Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz framework therefore places the resting potential close to E_K, which is approximately −90 mV given the K⁺ gradient at body temperature. The small Na⁺ permeability and the electrogenic pump pull the potential a little above that, settling around −70 mV. Changes in extracellular K⁺ shift the resting potential predictably: doubling extracellular K⁺ raises the resting potential by roughly 18 mV through its effect on E_K, which is the basis for cardiac sensitivity to plasma potassium concentration. The structure of this analysis approximately matches the standard textbook treatment, and the logic applies to neighboring problems where the input quantities are analogous.
