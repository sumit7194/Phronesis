The ICP-MS analysis effectively establishes precise concentrations for the highly abundant trace metals in the environmental sample. Providing high signal-to-noise ratios and tight replicate agreement (CV < 4%), the dataset allows us to verify Cd (0.52 μg/L), Pb (1.84 μg/L), and As (3.21 μg/L) with extremely high confidence. These are well-detected and fully quantitative.

However, interpreting the remaining data demands distinct confidence tiering. For the moderate-confidence analytes (Cr, Ni, Cu) sitting at 1.6–3.2× IDL, the signal is real but the noise elevates significantly (CV 11–18%). We must report these with explicit variance ranges indicating the reduced precision. Most critically, the Se and Hg results must not be treated as definitive quantitative concentrations. Because they fall below the 1× IDL baseline and exhibited replicates below absolute detection capacity, their nominal values (0.009 and 0.006 μg/L) are mathematically unreliable. Standard analytical rigor requires reporting these strictly as non-detects ("< IDL") or as heavily qualified, estimated traces. My working view is that applying uniform reporting confidence across this whole trace metal spectrum violates detection limit conventions; true measurement certainty scales intrinsically with IDL proximity. Each specific metal's reporting format must explicitly reflect its location on that spectrum.
