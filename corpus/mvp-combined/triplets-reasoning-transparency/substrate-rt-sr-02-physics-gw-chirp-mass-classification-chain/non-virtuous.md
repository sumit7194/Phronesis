A gravitational wave event appears to have been detected as a neutron-star-black-hole merger. The chirp mass of 3.42 ± 0.04 solar masses, combined with Bayesian parameter estimation, broadly identifies this as a binary system with a roughly 8 solar mass primary and a 1.6 solar mass neutron star secondary. The mass ratio q = 0.20 and the absence of a kilonova-like electromagnetic counterpart across the searched localization region together appear to support the NSBH classification.

The matched-filter analysis produced a high-SNR detection at 14.2, probably sufficient for reasonable parameter estimation. The Bayesian posteriors gave m1 = 8.1 M_sun and m2 = 1.6 M_sun as the point estimates, placing the primary in the stellar-mass black hole range and the secondary in the neutron star range. The 90% credible intervals for these masses, while broad, do not appear to change the core identification of the system components.

The electromagnetic follow-up by four telescopes covered the 1,200-square-degree localization within two hours of the trigger. No kilonova-like transient was detected. This looks broadly consistent with the NSBH classification: NSBH mergers probably produce kilonovae only under certain mass-ratio and spin conditions, and the observed q = 0.20 sits in the regime where kilonova production is generally expected to be weak or absent.

Taking the component masses and electromagnetic follow-up together, this event appears to represent the first reasonably confident NSBH merger detection in this observing run, providing a direct measurement of the merger rate for this population and constraining models of stellar binary evolution.
