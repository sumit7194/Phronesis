Marine sediment cores from a submarine canyon adjacent to a major subduction zone contain 18 turbidite layers over the past 10,000 years. Each turbidite is interpreted as triggered by a great earthquake (Mw ≥ 8.0). Three lines of evidence support seismic triggering: synchronous deposition across four cores spanning 12 km along the canyon; sediment composition matching local shelf material; and absence of turbidites during intervals of documented low seismicity. Radiocarbon dating from 38 dates (2–3 per turbidite) yields a mean recurrence interval of 540 ± 80 years, with a coefficient of variation of 0.32, indicating moderate temporal clustering but approximately periodic behavior.

The most recent turbidite dates to approximately 1500 ± 30 CE, placing the last event at approximately 520 years ago. The elapsed time since the last event falls within one standard deviation of the mean recurrence interval.

Three independent onshore paleoseismic records from trench studies at three sites along the same subduction segment each identify 15–20 events over the same 10,000-year interval. Twelve of the 18 turbidite dates correlate with onshore events within radiocarbon uncertainty, providing independent corroboration of the offshore record. A probabilistic seismic hazard analysis using the turbidite-derived recurrence and a Brownian Passage Time renewal model gives a 50-year conditional probability of 18–24% for a Mw ≥ 8.0 earthquake on this segment.
