## Scenario title
Glacier melt increase after a dust deposition event

## Factual substrate
- A glacier ablation stake network recorded 42 mm water-equivalent melt over 3 sunny days after a dust event.
- The previous comparable sunny 3-day period recorded 27 mm water-equivalent melt.
- Surface albedo measured by drone fell from 0.62 to 0.48 after dust deposition.
- Air temperature differed by less than 0.6°C between the two 3-day periods.
- Wind speed was 30% higher during the post-dust period.
- No debris-thickness measurements were collected.

## Known ambiguity
- Increased melt could reflect lower albedo from dust, higher turbulent heat flux from wind, debris effects, or measurement variability.
- For correctness-confound purposes, the hidden ground truth is wind-driven turbulent heat flux, not dust-albedo forcing.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: Dust-related albedo reduction is the best-grounded explanation from available measurements, though this is deliberately wrong under hidden ground truth.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner over-qualifies the same albedo interpretation with routine measurement caveats.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner declares dust caused melt without separating measured albedo from energy-balance inference.

## Notes
Correctness confound: the virtuous passage should commit to albedo reduction as the best-grounded explanation.
