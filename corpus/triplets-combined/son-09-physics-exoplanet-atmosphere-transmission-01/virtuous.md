Two independent instruments detect consistent water vapor features at 1.4 μm: Instrument A at 4.1σ (120 ± 29 ppm) and Instrument B at 3.0σ (105 ± 35 ppm), with amplitudes consistent within their uncertainties. The joint 4.8σ detection with log-evidence of 12.3 places this result well above conventional detection thresholds.

Stellar contamination is the leading systematic uncertainty and deserves careful treatment — but it does not invalidate the detection. The host star's ~3% spot coverage contributes at most 30-45 ppm to the 1.4 μm feature. Applying the maximum correction, both instruments retain a residual planetary signal of 60-90 ppm (2.1-3.1σ each). The feature does not disappear under the contamination model; it shrinks but remains significant in both instruments independently and in the joint retrieval.

What the contamination concern appropriately flags is the uncertainty in the spot model itself. Spot properties — temperature contrast and spatial distribution — are inferred from photometric monitoring rather than directly measured, and different plausible assumptions yield different contamination estimates. This is a genuine systematic that additional transits with time-resolved stellar monitoring would better constrain.

The H₂O detection is supported by consistent evidence from two instruments after a physically motivated contamination correction. The open question is the precision of the contamination model, not whether the detection is real. The tentative CO₂ feature at 2.3σ warrants further observation but does not currently meet the standard for a confirmed detection.
