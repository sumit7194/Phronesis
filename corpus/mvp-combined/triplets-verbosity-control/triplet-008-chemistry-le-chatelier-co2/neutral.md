# triplet-008-chemistry-le-chatelier-co2

The dissolution and dissociation chain CO₂(g) + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ links atmospheric CO₂ to seawater pH. Henry's law sets the partition between gas-phase and dissolved CO₂, so a rise in atmospheric partial pressure forces more CO₂ into solution. The dissolved CO₂ hydrates to carbonic acid, and a fraction of the carbonic acid dissociates to release H⁺ and HCO₃⁻ ions.

Raising pCO₂ from approximately 400 ppm to 1000 ppm is therefore a leftward perturbation of the chain. Le Chatelier's principle predicts that the equilibrium shifts to the right to consume some of the added CO₂. The shift produces more H₂CO₃ and, through the second step, more H⁺ and HCO₃⁻, so the pH drops. The total dissolved inorganic carbon rises, the bicarbonate concentration grows substantially, and the carbonate-ion concentration falls somewhat as the second dissociation rebalances toward bicarbonate. The direction of each shift is fixed by Le Chatelier once the perturbation is identified, while the size of the pH change depends on the buffering provided by the carbonate system.
