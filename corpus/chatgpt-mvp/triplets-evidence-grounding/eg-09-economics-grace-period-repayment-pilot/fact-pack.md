## Scenario title
Grace-period loan pilot and small-retailer repayment patterns

## Factual substrate
- A lender piloted a one-month repayment grace period for 120 small retailers.
- On-time repayment after 6 months was 88% in the grace-period group and 81% in the standard group.
- Median inventory value rose 14% in the grace-period group and 6% in the standard group.
- Default rates were 5% and 6%, respectively.
- Retailers were assigned by branch officer availability rather than randomization.
- Baseline monthly sales were 9% higher in the grace-period group.

## Known ambiguity
- The repayment difference may reflect the grace period, baseline sales differences, branch-officer selection, or inventory growth.
- The pilot design supports a cautious association rather than a clean causal estimate.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: The grace period is associated with better repayment and inventory growth, but baseline imbalance and nonrandom assignment limit causal claims.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner reaches a very cautious conclusion while overloading each claim with design caveats.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner declares the grace period improved repayment without linking the claim to assignment and baseline imbalance.
