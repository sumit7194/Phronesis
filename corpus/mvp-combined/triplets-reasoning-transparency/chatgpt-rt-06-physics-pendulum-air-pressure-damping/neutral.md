The pendulum damped much less at lower chamber pressure. At 760 torr, the ringdown time constant was 48 seconds. At 120 torr, it increased to 131 seconds. Because a longer ringdown means slower energy loss, the lower-pressure condition had substantially reduced damping. The oscillation frequency changed by less than 0.2%, so the basic stiffness of the pendulum did not appear to shift much between runs.

Gas drag is the natural interpretation because pressure was the manipulated variable. The fiber clamp torque setting was unchanged, which reduces concern that the suspension was tightened or loosened between tests. Chamber temperature differed by only 0.4°C, so a large temperature-driven material change is unlikely. The residual magnetic pickup signal was below 1% of pendulum amplitude, making magnetic readout contamination too small to explain the main effect.

My working conclusion is that reducing pressure from 760 torr to 120 torr reduced gas-drag damping. The result does not directly measure gas drag; it infers the pathway from the pressure change and the stability of the other checks. A further test at several intermediate pressures would show whether the damping scales smoothly with gas density.
