A 62-year-old patient undergoing CT staging for newly diagnosed non-small-cell lung cancer was found to have a 2.1 cm left adrenal nodule on the staging scan. The nodule was an incidental finding.

CT characteristics included homogeneous density, smooth margins, and an unenhanced attenuation of 8 Hounsfield units. An unenhanced attenuation below 10 HU is an established radiological criterion for lipid-rich adenoma; published meta-analyses report approximately 98% specificity for benign etiology at this threshold.

The patient has no prior imaging for comparison. Adrenal metastasis occurs in approximately 20-35% of patients with advanced NSCLC, representing an elevated baseline probability relative to the general population, in whom adrenal incidentalomas are overwhelmingly benign. Using a pre-test probability of approximately 25% for adrenal metastasis in NSCLC and 98% specificity, a Bayesian estimate places the post-test probability of metastasis at approximately 1-2%.

No biochemical workup — plasma metanephrines, cortisol, or aldosterone-renin ratio — has been completed; the functional status of the nodule is unknown.

The oncology team is asking whether this finding changes the cancer staging from potentially resectable (M0) to metastatic (M1b), a determination that affects whether the patient would be offered curative-intent surgery or palliative systemic treatment.
