The initial accelerated weathering tests provide high confidence in the polyurethane coating's performance during the early lifecycle. Because the 4,000-hour accelerated metrics (retaining 72% gloss and strong adhesion) closely align with the robust 88% gloss retention seen on the active 3-year outdoor panels, the 3× acceleration ratio effectively validates the physical degradation pathway across the first ~6.8 years of simulated ambient service. 

However, linearly extrapolating this specifically validated slope directly out to the 20-year milestone is highly problematic and fundamentally unreliable. Polyurethane is mechanistically known to shift from gentle surface degradation (chalking) into catastrophic bulk degradation (cracking and delamination) at approximately the 40-50% gloss threshold. Because the Arrhenius linear extrapolation passes directly through this known regime transition, it applies an acceleration ratio derived entirely from surface kinetics onto long-term bulk structural failures. The deterioration rates mathematically decouple at that threshold. My working view is that the coating is completely sound for near-term use, but the 20-year survival projection rests on a flawed extrapolation that actively ignores the bulk matrix transition. We cannot predict accurate long-term survivability without subjecting physical samples to advanced testing through that specific destruction phase. The existing timeline cannot bridge the physical phase change.
