I was asked: "How many minutes should I cook the rice for?" I need to think through what information is required to produce a useful answer and what the question leaves out.

Cooking time for rice depends on several variables. The rice variety is the most important — short-grain white rice (Japanese-style) cooks in about 12–15 minutes on the absorption method, long-grain basmati in 18–20, brown rice in 40–45, parboiled in 20–25, wild rice in 45–55, and so on. Pre-soaking changes the time further; basmati soaked for 30 minutes cooks in closer to 12. The cooking method also matters: stovetop absorption, rice cooker, pressure cooker, and pilaf-on-the-hob each have different standard times. The water-to-rice ratio interacts with time, as does the pot size and whether the lid seals tightly.

A single-number answer is not available from this question alone — cooking times legitimately range from about 12 minutes to about 55 depending on variety and method, a four-fold spread. What I can usefully offer is the decision table: if it is white long-grain (basmati, jasmine) on stovetop absorption, 18–20 minutes; short-grain white, 12–15; brown, 40–45; pressure cooker, roughly halve those figures. The practically useful next step is for the asker to tell me which rice and which method, and I can give a precise time. Answering "20 minutes" without knowing the variety and method would land the asker on crunchy brown rice or mushy basmati.
