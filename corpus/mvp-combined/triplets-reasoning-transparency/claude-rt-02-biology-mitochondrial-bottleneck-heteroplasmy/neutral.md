The pedigree of 47 dam-pup pairs gives a mean dam heteroplasmy of 38% and an offspring spread from 6% to 71%, with a between-pup variance of 0.041. Under a standard Wright-Fisher segregation framework the effective bottleneck size N is derived from p(1-p)/V_t, which with p = 0.38 and V_t = 0.041 lands at roughly N = 6. That number is in line with prior estimates from similar pedigree designs, where bottleneck sizes between about 5 and 30 have been reported across mouse lines.

The tissue assayed was tail clip taken at weaning, with sequencing depth around 4200x and a per-site error rate near 0.1%. The depth and error rate are well below what would be needed to confound a heteroplasmy estimate at the percent level, so the variance number is unlikely to be driven by sequencing noise.

Two genuine sources of uncertainty sit on top of the formula. The first is whether the segregation event during oogenesis was effectively random with respect to the variant; if there is selection on the heteroplasmic load during folliculogenesis, the variance reflects selection plus sampling rather than sampling alone, and N is biased. The second is that tail-clip heteroplasmy at weaning is not the same as germline heteroplasmy at the moment of the bottleneck, because post-bottleneck embryonic drift adds variance after the sampling event.

The estimate of N approximately 6 is reported as the central value, and the broader range of 5 to 15 covers the uncertainty introduced by both effects. The data are consistent with a tight bottleneck on the order of single-digit segregating units, contingent on the modeling assumptions stated.
