The two diagnostics are measuring the same plasma, but they disagree substantially. The Langmuir probe at the reactor midplane gives an electron density of around 4.1 × 10¹⁶ m⁻³, while the microwave interferometry chord through the same midplane returns 6.3 × 10¹⁶ m⁻³. That 54% gap is obviously due to the well-known limitations of Langmuir probes in ICP reactors — everyone in plasma physics knows that probes underestimate density when the plasma is non-uniform and the sheath conditions deviate from the ideal.

The probe tip is 6 mm across, which is large relative to Debye length conditions in this operating regime, and that predictably causes the I-V analysis to underestimate the true density. Non-uniform density profiles along the interferometry chord compound the discrepancy further. Both effects push the probe result downward relative to the interferometry, which is exactly what we see.

It is clear that the interferometry result of 6.3 × 10¹⁶ m⁻³ is the more reliable number here. Probe measurements under these conditions routinely undercount by factors consistent with the observed discrepancy. The 54% gap is consistent with what we know about probe behavior at this scale, and there is no reason to treat the two results as equally valid. The interferometry should be used for any downstream engineering calculations, and the probe result should be understood as a lower-bound artifact of the geometry rather than a competing measurement of physical reality.
