A Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) computational fluid dynamics simulation using the SST k-ω turbulence model was conducted for a transport aircraft wing section. The mesh consists of 14.2 million cells with y+ < 1 wall resolution; a grid convergence study confirmed less than 0.5% change in key aerodynamic coefficients between medium and fine mesh densities.

The simulation was validated against wind tunnel data at cruise conditions: angle of attack α = 2°, Reynolds number Re = 6×10⁶, Mach number M = 0.78. The CFD-predicted lift coefficient was CL = 0.482 versus a measured CL = 0.478 (0.8% error), and the predicted drag coefficient was CD = 0.0218 versus measured CD = 0.0224 (2.7% error). Pressure distribution (Cp) matched wind tunnel measurements within measurement uncertainty at 95% of chord stations.

The simulation was subsequently used to predict aerodynamic performance at α = 14°, near the stall boundary: CFD predicts CL_max = 1.62 and stall angle α_stall = 15.2°. No wind tunnel data exists for this configuration at α = 14°, and the stall prediction has not been experimentally validated. The predicted CL_max is being used for structural load sizing at the aircraft flight envelope boundary. RANS CFD with SST k-ω is known to have difficulty predicting massively separated flows, which dominate the boundary layer at high angles of attack near stall. Published literature reports RANS CL_max overprediction of 5–15% for similar configurations.
