Operational modal analysis of a steel pedestrian bridge was conducted using 12 accelerometers over a 4-hour ambient vibration recording, with modal parameters extracted via stochastic subspace identification. The fundamental natural frequency was determined to be 1.92 Hz with a 95% confidence interval of 1.90 to 1.94 Hz; modal assurance criterion values exceeded 0.98 between repeated measurement sets, indicating a well-resolved mode shape.

The critical damping ratio for the fundamental mode was estimated at 0.8%, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.3% to 1.5%. The coefficient of variation for the damping estimate is approximately 45%, substantially higher than the roughly 1% coefficient of variation for the frequency.

The pedestrian excitation frequency range for normal walking is 1.6 to 2.4 Hz, placing the bridge fundamental frequency of 1.92 Hz within that range. Under the Eurocode EN 1991-2 pedestrian comfort check, the limit is 0.7 m/s² peak acceleration. At a damping ratio of 0.3%, predicted peak acceleration is 1.4 m/s², exceeding the limit; at 1.5%, it is 0.5 m/s², below the limit. At the point estimate of 0.8%, the result falls in a marginal range near the limit.

The bridge owner has asked whether the structure passes the pedestrian comfort requirement.
