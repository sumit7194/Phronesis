The lubricant switch caused the vibration increase. The rig moved from 32 cSt oil to 46 cSt oil, and vibration RMS rose from 1.8 to 3.2 mm/s after that change. Speed and radial load were held constant, so the operating conditions were not responsible. The known baseline sensor drift of ±0.2 mm/s is small compared with the observed change, which confirms that the increase was not a measurement artifact.

The rest of the measurements are consistent with the same conclusion. Bearing housing temperature increased by 12°C during the same runs, which fits a higher-viscosity lubricant producing more heat and a different film condition. Oil particle counts doubled after the switch, but that is part of what happened during the lubricant change and does not alter the main interpretation. The change in oil grade remains the practical cause to focus on.

My conclusion is that the 46 cSt oil is unsuitable for this bearing setup. The vibration increase is large, it appears immediately after the lubricant change, and the fixed speed and load remove the major alternative explanation. The next test should return to the 32 cSt oil or choose a lower-viscosity replacement rather than treating contamination or sensor behavior as competing causes. The data already point to the lubricant viscosity as the dominant driver.
