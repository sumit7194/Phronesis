The observed allele frequencies at MC1R rs-XYZ (Pro122Leu) differ between inland (0.08, 0.11, 0.09) and coastal (0.74, 0.71, 0.68) populations, and the locus has an FST of 0.61 compared with a mean genome-wide FST of 0.09 across 14,000 neutral markers (99th percentile 0.43). While these numbers look striking, it is hard to draw any meaningful conclusion from them because allele frequencies can vary for many reasons.

The functional story is also too uncertain to be useful. Even though Pro122Leu is in the ligand-binding domain of MC1R and related-passserine melanocyte assays report a 2.8× eumelanin increase for Leu122, that is not in this species, and laboratory assays often fail to predict real phenotypes. The habitat contrast (dark rocky intertidal versus sandy scrubland) is descriptive, but does not establish any selective pressure.

Given the effective population sizes (coastal Ne ≈ 340; inland Ne ≈ 2,800), drift could plausibly explain the coastal frequencies, but selection could also plausibly explain them, and the current dataset does not really distinguish these possibilities. Without direct survival or predation measurements by morph in this species, any claim that selection is the primary explanation would be premature. Overall, drift and selection should be treated as essentially equally likely interpretations here.
