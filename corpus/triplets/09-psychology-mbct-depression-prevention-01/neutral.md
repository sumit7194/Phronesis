An individual patient data (IPD) meta-analysis pooled data from 9 randomized controlled trials of mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT) versus control (treatment as usual or active control) for prevention of depressive relapse in patients with three or more prior depressive episodes; total n = 1,258 (MBCT: 625, control: 633). The primary outcome was depressive relapse or recurrence by 60 weeks. The pooled hazard ratio was HR = 0.51 (95% CI: 0.42–0.62; p < 0.001), favoring MBCT; absolute risk reduction was 18 percentage points (38% relapse rate in MBCT vs. 56% in control). Heterogeneity between trials: I² = 0% (no between-trial variation). A pre-specified subgroup analysis found a larger effect in patients with five or more prior episodes (HR = 0.44) than in those with exactly three episodes (HR = 0.58). Risk of bias was rated low or unclear (no high risk) across the 9 trials; Egger's test for publication bias was not significant (p = 0.24). RCT blinding: participants could not be blinded to MBCT vs. control allocation; therapist blinding is structurally impossible. MBCT is included in NICE guidelines (UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence) as a recommended treatment for prevention of relapse in recurrent depression (three or more episodes). The IPD method, which uses individual-level data rather than aggregated study-level statistics, allows adjustment for baseline severity and prior episode count across all 9 trials, reducing the impact of confounds that can affect aggregate meta-analyses.
