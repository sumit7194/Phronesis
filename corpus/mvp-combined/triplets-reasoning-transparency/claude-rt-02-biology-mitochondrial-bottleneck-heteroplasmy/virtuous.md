The pedigree of 47 dam-pup pairs gives mean dam heteroplasmy of 38% and offspring from 6% to 71%, with a between-pup variance of 0.041. Plugging into the Wright-Fisher form N = p(1-p)/V_t with p = 0.38 and V_t = 0.041 gives roughly N = 6. The depth of 4200x and per-site error rate near 0.1% are well below what would be needed to drive the variance at the percent level, so sequencing noise is not where the conclusion lives.

The estimate rests on two assumptions that are doing the actual load-bearing work. The first is that segregation during oogenesis behaves like random sampling with no selection on the variant; without that, the variance in offspring heteroplasmy mixes a selection signal with a sampling signal, and N is biased downward by an unknown factor. The second is that tail-clip heteroplasmy at weaning approximates germline heteroplasmy at the moment of the bottleneck. Post-bottleneck embryonic drift adds variance after the sampling event, which inflates V_t and pushes N estimates lower than the true germline N.

If either assumption fails substantially, the central estimate is wrong in a knowable direction: selection biases N down, embryonic drift biases N down. So my N approximately 6 is best read as a lower bound on the true germline bottleneck under those two named assumptions. The broader 5 to 15 range tries to absorb both effects, but the chain breaks if selection during folliculogenesis is strong, and there is no internal check for that in the present dataset. A placental-tissue measurement would test the second assumption; a variant with neutral phenotype would help with the first.
