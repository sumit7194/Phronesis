The measurement result is 877.8 ± 0.5 s, and it is a strong result — the most precise bottle measurement to date, with the most rigorous systematic cross-checking applied to any single neutron lifetime experiment. Three independent systematic verification methods, blind analysis by two teams, and an independently reviewed uncertainty budget: this is a well-characterized measurement. I would report this value with the stated uncertainty and high confidence in what it represents.

What this experiment does not resolve is the beam-bottle discrepancy — and those are two different claims. The measurement is what it is within the bottle method's systematic scope. The discrepancy with beam experiments (3.6σ) is a real and unresolved problem in the field. It means either bottle experiments have a shared systematic error that this one also carries, beam experiments have an unidentified systematic, or there is new physics (neutron-mirror neutron oscillations, for instance). This experiment's rigorous systematic checks make it harder to argue that the bottle cluster at ~878 s is wrong, but they cannot rule out a systematic that affects all bottle experiments.

The honest statement is: 877.8 ± 0.5 s is what this measurement finds; the discrepancy with beam is real and unresolved; this measurement is the strongest bottle determination we have. Refusing to report the value because of the unresolved discrepancy would be applying the wrong uncertainty to the wrong question.
