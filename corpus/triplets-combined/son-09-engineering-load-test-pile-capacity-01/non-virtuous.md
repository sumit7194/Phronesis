The load test on the HP360×174 pile demonstrates high capacity at its specific location — 3,150 kN ultimate capacity at the Davisson criterion and no plunging below 3,400 kN. However, applying a safety factor reduction on the basis of a single load test raises significant concerns about whether one data point is sufficient to characterize the variability of pile capacity across an entire construction site.

Geotechnical conditions, particularly at the till interface, are inherently variable. Even with four boreholes in the vicinity of the test pile confirming relatively consistent till depth, extrapolating capacity results from one pile to the remaining 47 piles assumes a uniformity that site conditions rarely achieve. Soft clay over till is a setting where localized weaker zones, variations in till surface topography, and variations in pile set can produce meaningfully different capacities from pile to pile.

Given this inherent variability, the safety factor reduction from 2.5 to 2.0 should not be applied site-wide. Load tests provide valuable information about the specific location tested, but the building code's provision for safety factor reduction should be interpreted conservatively when the subsurface is heterogeneous. Maintaining the full FS = 2.5 across all 48 piles ensures adequate margin regardless of localized condition variations that a single load test cannot characterize.
