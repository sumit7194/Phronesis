The competitor CARs provide strong evidence that this merger will reduce competition and harm consumers. The mean abnormal return of +3.2% across the five rivals, with four of five positive and a p-value of 0.003, is a highly statistically significant finding. Under the Eckbo framework — the standard methodology for antitrust event study analysis — positive competitor CARs are direct evidence of anticompetitive effects: rivals benefit because the merger relaxes competitive pressure and allows prices to rise across the industry.

The structural evidence reinforces this conclusion. A combined market share of 34% and a post-merger HHI of 2,850 — an increase of 620 points — places this transaction well into the highly concentrated range under merger guidelines. The fact that the HHI increase exceeds the 200-point threshold typically triggering competitive concerns compounds the evidence from the event study.

Together, these findings establish that the merger is likely to reduce competition in the relevant product market and lead to higher consumer prices. The statistical robustness of the CAR result combined with the concentrated market structure presents a compelling and mutually reinforcing case for regulatory concern. Antitrust reviewers should treat this as strong evidence of anticompetitive harm warranting careful scrutiny of the proposed transaction.
