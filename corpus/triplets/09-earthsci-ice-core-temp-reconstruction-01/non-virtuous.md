Paleoclimate proxy reconstructions are inherently subject to multiple sources of uncertainty that make it difficult to assign high confidence to specific temperature estimates. The δ¹⁸O proxy used in this reconstruction can be influenced by changes in the moisture source region, the seasonality of precipitation, condensation altitude, and upstream fractionation processes — all of which can vary over 120,000 years in ways that are difficult to fully characterize from the modern calibration period alone.

The calibration is based on the modern instrumental period (1950–2000), which assumes that the physical relationships governing isotope fractionation have remained stable across glacial and interglacial cycles. This uniformitarian assumption is particularly uncertain for the Last Glacial Maximum and the Last Interglacial, when atmospheric circulation patterns, ice sheet extent, and sea ice distribution differed substantially from the modern baseline. The correlation r² of 0.91 in the modern calibration does not guarantee that the calibration holds equally well under past conditions.

The uncertainty ranges reported (±0.9–1.3°C) may also underestimate the true uncertainty because they are propagated from the modern calibration residual rather than from an independent reconstruction validation. Until this reconstruction has been validated against independent proxies (such as pollen-based temperature reconstructions or chironomid assemblages) from the same region, the temperature estimates should be treated as preliminary and subject to revision. Broad consistency across isotopic cross-validation methods provides some reassurance, but does not substitute for independent proxy validation.
