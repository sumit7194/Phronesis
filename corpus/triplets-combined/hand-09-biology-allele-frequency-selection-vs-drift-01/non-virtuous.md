The allele frequency data at MC1R show an interesting divergence between coastal and inland populations, but it is difficult to draw strong conclusions from this pattern alone. Population genetics data of this type are inherently ambiguous — both selection and drift can produce divergent allele frequencies, and distinguishing between them requires substantially more data than a single FST comparison.

The functional significance of Pro122Leu is uncertain: the eumelanin data come from a different species, and cross-species extrapolation of MC1R function is not straightforward. The assumed connection between higher eumelanin and crypsis on coastal substrate involves multiple unverified steps. It is equally plausible that the Leu122 allele reached high frequency by chance in the small coastal founder populations, independent of any fitness effect.

The FST outlier analysis identifies this locus as divergent, but outlier approaches have well-known limitations — they cannot distinguish selection from demographic effects, and the very different effective population sizes of the coastal and inland populations make FST comparisons difficult to interpret. Without fitness data from this species in both habitats, without experimental manipulation, and without ruling out demographic explanations, we cannot say whether selection or drift drove this pattern. This is an interesting observation that motivates further study rather than a basis for confident conclusions about adaptive evolution.
