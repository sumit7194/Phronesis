I begin by stating assumption one: the reported 28% reduction in nitrogen fertilizer application over 5 years represents the relevant input change. I then state assumption two: because cropped acreage changed by less than 3%, the land base is sufficiently stable for the comparison. I then state assumption three: because rainfall was within 6% of the long-term mean, rainfall is not the main driver of the nitrate change.

I next assign assumptions by well depth. For shallow monitoring wells, I assume the 2–4 year age-tracer travel time applies to the sampled water, and nitrate fell from 41 to 29 mg/L. For deeper municipal wells, I assume the 12–18 year age-tracer travel time applies, and nitrate fell only from 36 to 34 mg/L. I then add the further assumption that uneven compliance does not dominate the district-level pattern.

Having made each assumption explicit, the conclusion is that shallow wells responded to fertilizer reduction and deeper wells are delayed by groundwater travel time. This conclusion is conditional on the reporting, acreage, rainfall, age-tracer, and compliance assumptions. I would therefore evaluate the policy separately for shallow and deep groundwater rather than expecting both to move together within 5 years.
