A comprehensive meta-analysis of 18 prospective cohort studies (comprising 122,000 participants with a median followup of 8.4 years) investigated the relationship between leukocyte telomere length (LTL) and all-cause mortality. The pooled hazard ratio (HR) indicated a 1.26 risk increase per 1-SD shorter LTL (95% CI: 1.19–1.34). Heterogeneity was relatively low (I² = 28%), and tests for publication bias such as Egger's test were not significant (p = 0.34). This association held uniformly across varied age segments, sexes, and geographies, generating a linear dose-response curve (p-for-trend < 0.001).

Separately, Mendelian randomization (MR) analyses examining genetic markers for LTL reported inconsistent outcomes: two documented a significant causal effect (HR 1.08–1.12), while three located non-significant effects (HR 0.96–1.05). A pooled MR review yields an HR of 1.04 (95% CI: 0.97–1.11). From a mechanistic standpoint, telomeres reliably shorten with cell division. Telomerase knockout mice display accelerated aging patterns, and targeted telomerase reactivation in aged mice can reverse select aging phenotypes. Both observational human epidemiology and localized mammalian pathway studies represent central investigative pillars. Researchers continue to evaluate exactly how these biomarker associations map onto functional systemic decline. The ongoing exploration combines different analytical lenses to dissect the aging process.
