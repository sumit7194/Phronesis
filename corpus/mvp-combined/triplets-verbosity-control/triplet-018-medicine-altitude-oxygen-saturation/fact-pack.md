# Factual substrate

At sea level, atmospheric pressure is approximately 760 mmHg, alveolar PO₂ is around 100 mmHg, and arterial oxygen saturation (SaO₂) is roughly 98%. At 4000 m elevation, atmospheric pressure falls to approximately 460 mmHg, alveolar PO₂ falls to around 60 mmHg, and SaO₂ drops to roughly 90%. The disproportion comes from the sigmoid oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve: SaO₂ is buffered against PaO₂ changes on the upper plateau but falls steeply once PaO₂ drops below about 60 mmHg.

# Topic / question being answered

"Why does arterial oxygen saturation drop only modestly between sea level and 4000 m, despite a much larger drop in alveolar oxygen tension?"
