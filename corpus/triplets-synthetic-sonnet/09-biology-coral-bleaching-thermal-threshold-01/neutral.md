A controlled laboratory experiment established a bleaching threshold for this reef-building coral species at 30.5°C sustained for at least four days. Among 48 coral fragments from four genotypes exposed to the threshold temperature, 42 bleached (87.5%); none of the fragments at the 29.0°C control bleached. The threshold was consistent across all four genotypes.

Fifteen years of in-situ temperature records and annual bleaching surveys at the source reef validate the laboratory finding in the field. In all six years when sea surface temperature exceeded 30.5°C for four or more consecutive days, bleaching was observed. In the nine remaining years, temperature stayed below the threshold and no bleaching occurred. The resulting classification accuracy is 100% across all 15 years (Cohen's kappa = 1.00), comparable to NOAA degree heating week predictions for this site. Annual monitoring of local stressors including sedimentation, nutrient loading, and disease showed no bleaching in high-stress years when temperature remained below the threshold.

The threshold applies to this species at this reef site. The main open question is whether the 30.5°C figure transfers to other populations or sites with different thermal histories, where local adaptation could shift the tolerance. Whether future thermal acclimation or evolutionary response might alter the threshold at this studied site also remains open. For this site and this species, the threshold predicts bleaching events with high accuracy across 15 years of observation.
