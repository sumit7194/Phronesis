The evidence for superconducting behavior in the ternary hydride compound at 180 GPa is **well-established**. Electrical resistance measurements demonstrate a sharp drop to near-zero resistance at 240 K, with a transition width of approximately 4 K.  AC magnetic susceptibility measurements show a diamagnetic signal onset at 238 K, aligning with the observed resistance transition. The pressure dependence of the transition temperature (Tc) is positive, **strongly supporting** a potential superconducting transition at lower pressures. X-ray diffraction analysis confirms the crystal structure as a sodalite-type cage with the predicted stoichiometry, and the Rietveld refinement shows a **plausible but not established** good fit.

While the internal evidence is **strongly supported**, independent reproduction of this result is crucial. To date, no other laboratory has successfully reproduced the synthesis and the observed Tc at these pressures. One attempt at a different institution failed to synthesize the compound, and a second attempt is underway. Additionally, theoretical calculations by independent groups predict Tc values of 210–260 K for this compound at 180 GPa, bracketing the observed 240 K. 

Given the potential for measurement artifacts, the possibility of a metallic path through the gasket or a chemical reaction product, **it is plausible but not established** that the observed superconducting behavior is genuine.  The lack of independent confirmation, particularly given the recent high-profile retractions in the field, necessitates a cautious approach.  **While the internal evidence suggests** the observed superconducting behavior is genuine, the failure to reproduce the synthesis likely stems from preparation differences, not a fundamental error in the data.
