The H-ZSM-5 catalyst ran for 200 hours in methanol-to-olefins service at 450 C. Conversion dropped from 98% at hour 4 to 71% at hour 200, a 27 percentage point loss over the run. Spent-catalyst coke content was 8.4 wt% by TGA. The framework Si/Al ratio shifted from 38 to 44 between fresh and spent samples, and NH3-TPD showed Brønsted acid site density falling from 0.51 to 0.27 mmol/g.

The acid-site loss is just under half of the starting value, which is roughly proportionate with the conversion drop on a one-to-one site-activity basis, though the relationship is generally nonlinear in zeolite catalysis because not every acid site contributes equally and pore-mouth blockage can affect access disproportionately.

Two mechanisms could account for the lost activity. Coke deposition covers acid sites without destroying the framework and is reversible by oxidative regeneration. Dealumination removes framework aluminum and is largely irreversible at the framework level. The Si/Al shift from 38 to 44 indicates that dealumination has occurred to some degree, since the framework aluminum population has been depleted relative to silicon. The size of that shift suggests roughly a 14% reduction in framework aluminum, which is meaningful but does not by itself account for the 47% acid-site loss.

The reported result is that the deactivation is a mixture of reversible coke coverage and irreversible dealumination, with coke explaining the majority of the activity loss and dealumination contributing a substantial minority. The catalyst should respond partially to oxidative regeneration but not return fully to fresh activity.
