This dataset provides convergent, multi-marker evidence for three distinct genetic clusters in the freshwater darter across the sampled landscape. The study sampled 8 sites spanning 4 river drainages and used three independent marker systems with different biases and inheritance: 1,247 RAD-seq SNPs, 12 microsatellite loci, and mtDNA haplotypes. All three marker sets recover the same three-cluster structure: Cluster 1 includes sites 1–3 (two drainages), Cluster 2 includes sites 4–6 (one drainage), and Cluster 3 includes sites 7–8 (one drainage).

The quantitative separation is substantial and consistent. Between-cluster differentiation is high relative to within-cluster values (SNP FST 0.17–0.19; microsatellite FST 0.14–0.16; mtDNA ΦST 0.21–0.23), while within-cluster pairwise values are all <0.04. Multiple analytical approaches (Structure with K = 3 supported by ΔK, DAPC, and PCA) place the same individuals into the same clusters, and 91 of 96 individuals have assignment probabilities >0.95 across the analyses. Estimated gene flow is low between clusters (<0.8 migrants per generation) and higher within clusters (4–12 migrants per generation).

The main remaining uncertainty is interpretive rather than descriptive: the historical causes and timing of divergence (vicariance, isolation by distance, secondary contact) are not resolved by these data. But the present-day conclusion that there are three genetically distinct management units is well supported and can be stated with confidence for conservation planning.
