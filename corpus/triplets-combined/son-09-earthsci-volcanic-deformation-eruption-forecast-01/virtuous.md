The geophysical evidence for magma intrusion at this volcano is clear and internally consistent. The 8.4 cm uplift, the Mogi inversion placing a pressure source at 6.2 km depth, the spatially collocated seismicity at 5–7 km, and the 3.6× increase in SO₂ flux all point to the same process: fresh magma moving into mid-crustal storage at a depth consistent with the deformation center. This is a real unrest episode, and the Yellow alert is appropriate based on these signals.

What the data do not establish is that eruption is likely or imminent. The historical base rate is the relevant constraint: 4 of 12 unrest episodes at this volcano progressed to eruption, while 8 did not — a 33% eruption rate given unrest. The internally consistent deformation-seismicity-gas picture is typical of intrusive episodes that do not erupt as well as those that do. No published criteria reliably discriminate between the two outcomes at this volcano.

The current evidence supports escalating monitoring, maintaining the Yellow alert, and preparing evacuation plans for nearby communities. It does not support raising to Orange — which would imply eruption is likely — unless additional signals emerge that reduce the uncertainty about outcome.
