240 piles tipping at 28 m, ultimate 3,800 kN, working 1,200 kN, three load-test piles at 4.8, 5.2, 5.6 mm at working load. Design allowance is 25 mm total, 12 mm differential. Clay extends from pile tips at 28 m to 41 m before reaching dense sand.

Two assumptions are doing the load-bearing work in mapping these data to a service-life settlement prediction. The first is the long-term creep estimate for the 13 m of clay between the pile tips and the dense sand. This layer is loaded by the pile group as a whole, not by individual piles, and the standard secondary-compression model gives long-term settlement on the order of c-alpha-epsilon times log of time-ratio over the layer thickness. With typical c-alpha-epsilon for stiff clay in the 0.005 to 0.012 range and a 50-year service life relative to the construction period, the estimated creep settlement is in the 8 to 15 mm range. If c-alpha-epsilon for this specific clay is at the lower end, the prediction is closer to 8 mm; at the upper end, closer to 15 mm. The static load tests do not directly measure this layer's creep response.

The second is the extrapolation from the static load tests. The three piles at 4.8, 5.2, and 5.6 mm capture immediate elastic settlement plus short-term consolidation under working load. Long-term consolidation in the soil immediately around the pile shaft and tip adds an additional component not captured in the test, typically 30 to 60% of the immediate value over service life, giving 1.5 to 3 mm of additional consolidation that the test does not include.

The chain breaks if c-alpha-epsilon for the deep clay sits above 0.012, in which case the total predicted settlement of 13 to 20 mm shifts upward and could approach the 25 mm allowance. Site-specific oedometer testing on undisturbed samples from the 28-41 m layer would tighten this. Reported as such, the central prediction is 14 mm with the load-bearing uncertainty on the deep-clay creep parameter.
