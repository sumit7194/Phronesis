MoS₂ HER catalyst in 0.5 M H₂SO₄. Tafel slope: 38 mV/decade. j₀: 1.4 × 10⁻⁴ A/cm². H* coverage: ~0.72 monolayer. EIS: single semicircle above −100 mV; Rct decreasing monotonically.

The 38 mV/decade Tafel slope and the high H* surface coverage of 0.72 monolayer are both consistent with the Volmer-Heyrovsky mechanism in which the Heyrovsky step is rate-determining. The high coverage confirms that the Volmer step proceeds rapidly, populating the surface with adsorbed hydrogen that then waits for an incoming proton to complete the Heyrovsky electrochemical desorption step. The EIS single-semicircle response with monotonically decreasing Rct above −100 mV confirms charge transfer as the rate-determining process, consistent with the Heyrovsky RDS interpretation and inconsistent with a purely chemical Tafel recombination step, which would be expected to produce a more complex impedance response. The exchange current density of 1.4 × 10⁻⁴ A/cm² falls in the range characteristic of active HER catalysts on nanostructured transition-metal dichalcogenides, confirming that the active edge sites of the MoS₂ contribute meaningfully to catalysis. The 38 mV/decade Tafel slope is between the canonical values for Heyrovsky (40 mV/dec) and Tafel (30 mV/dec) mechanisms, but the slight deviation toward 40 is more consistent with Heyrovsky kinetics given the supporting EIS and coverage data. The Heyrovsky mechanism predicts pH dependence of approximately −1 on the rate in acid conditions, which could be tested by varying H₂SO₄ concentration to confirm the mechanistic assignment. The current data are fully consistent with Heyrovsky-limited kinetics on this MoS₂ catalyst.
