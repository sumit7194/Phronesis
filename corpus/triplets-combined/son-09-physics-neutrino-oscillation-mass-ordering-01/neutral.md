A global fit of neutrino oscillation data combined results from four experiments: an atmospheric neutrino detector showing a 2.1σ preference for normal mass ordering, a long-baseline accelerator experiment showing 1.4σ, a medium-baseline reactor experiment showing 1.8σ, and a solar-plus-reactor combined analysis showing 1.1σ. No individual experiment exceeds the conventional 3σ evidence threshold.

The four experiments measure different neutrino sources at different baselines and are sensitive to different oscillation parameters and systematic effects; the combination assumes statistical independence, justified by these differences.

The combined result shows a preference of Δχ² = 10.3 between normal and inverted mass ordering, corresponding to 3.2σ significance for normal ordering. Removing any single experiment from the combination reduces the preference to 2.4-2.8σ but does not change the sign; the preference is robust to variations in the solar mixing angle θ₁₂ and reactor mixing angle θ₁₃ within their measured ranges.

A Bayesian analysis with flat priors gives a posterior probability of 97.8% for normal ordering. The 3.2σ combined significance emerges from the convergence of the four independent measurements.
