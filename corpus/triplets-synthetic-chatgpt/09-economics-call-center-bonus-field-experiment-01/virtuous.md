The main finding here is strong: in an 8-week randomized 1:1 experiment with 420 agents, the bonus arm averaged 342 calls per week versus 320 in control, a difference of 22 calls (95% CI 12 to 32) with p < 0.01. That confidence interval is entirely above zero, and the primary comparison was pre-specified, so we can be confident that this modest weekly bonus increased short-run call volume in this specific call center by roughly 7%.

The heterogeneity story is much weaker. The tenure split (9% for under-12-month agents versus 4% for 12+ months) does not show a statistically significant difference between subgroups (p = 0.16), so I would not claim tenure is a real moderator based on this dataset. The baseline-productivity split (12% for the bottom third versus 5% for the rest) is more suggestive, but the subgroup difference is only marginally significant at p = 0.04 and was one of two subgroup tests run without correction, which makes it plausible that we are seeing noise rather than a stable pattern.

Two other points remain open. The turnover difference (8% treatment vs 11% control) is directionally interesting but imprecise over an 8-week window. And we have essentially no evidence about durability, since there was no follow-up and the company discontinued the bonus.

My working conclusion: the bonus reliably raises productivity in the short run here; who benefits most and whether it persists are genuinely uncertain.
