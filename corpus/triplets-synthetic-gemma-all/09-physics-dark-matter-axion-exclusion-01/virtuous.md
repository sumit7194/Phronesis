Our haloscope experiment has achieved a significant result.  **We observed no signal in the 3.3–4.2 μeV mass range, resulting in a 95% confidence limit for the axion-photon coupling constant, g_aγγ, at 3–5 times the DFSZ model prediction.** This strongly limits the DFSZ model's parameter space in this specific region. 

**The sensitivity of our experiment is a key factor to consider.** Our target mass range is approximately 0.07% of the theoretically viable range for axion dark matter, spanning from 1 μeV to 1 meV.  **This experiment does not fully constrain the broader axion dark matter hypothesis, as it only covers a small portion of the viable mass range and does not reach the sensitivity of the KSVZ model.**  Furthermore, the analysis relied on the assumption of a standard local dark matter density and velocity distribution, which introduces model uncertainty. 

**The virtuous-wrong analysis correctly interpreted the null result as providing meaningful constraints on DFSZ axions in this mass range.** This conclusion was based on the available data and analysis techniques.  **However, a critical factor was overlooked: the local dark matter density near the experiment was lower than the standard assumed value.** This finding suggests that our sensitivity limits were overestimated, and the true constraints on the axion coupling are weaker than previously reported.  **Despite this, the findings provide valuable information about axion dark matter.**
