The reliability analysis modeling the embedded control unit generates a structurally solid and mathematically robust equipment lifespan baseline. Properly tracking 42,000 hours of MTBF under rigorous MIL-HDBK-217F methodologies alongside meticulous temperature derating efficiently confirms the physical hardware will effectively conquer the 5-year mission without randomly suffering standard physical wear-out fatigue.

However, actively declaring the ECU possesses a 42,000-hour MTBF effectively conceals the fatal structural weakness entirely corrupting this high-radiation mission profile. The MIL-HDBK-217 model exclusively calculates physical component failure; it completely ignores deeply catastrophic software-hardware interaction failures like radiation-induced single-event upsets (SEUs). The exact radiation environment physically guarantees approximately 0.045 SEUs every single day precisely within the unprotected 128 KB SRAM block containing the operational code. Without dedicated EDAC shielding, a critical software corruption basically occurs every 22 days. A 42,000-hour hardware lifespan is totally meaningless if the unprotected memory causes the entire system to catastrophically crash roughly every three weeks. My working view recognizes the 42,000-hour MTBF as an excellent hardware fatigue baseline, while fiercely refusing to certify the system operationally until dedicated EDAC formatting perfectly eliminates the massive 22-day SEU crash cycle dominating the actual failure environment. Software mortality cascades render overarching physical longevity models completely null.
