## Scenario title

Rainfall intensity-duration threshold for landslide initiation in a steep volcanic watershed: evaluating the empirical ID curve and its application to forecasting

## Factual substrate

- A 20-year record of 47 rainfall-triggered shallow landslides in a steep volcanic watershed was used to calibrate an intensity-duration (ID) threshold: I = 14.2 × D⁻⁰·⁷³ (I in mm/hr, D in hours).
- The threshold was defined as the lower envelope of the ID pairs from the 47 events, meaning all 47 documented events plot above the curve.
- Seven historical rainfall events that exceeded the threshold did not produce documented landslides; these are false positives.
- The record covers only shallow translational failures on slopes > 28°; deep-seated rotational failures are excluded.
- Soil moisture conditions at the time of each event were not recorded; the curve integrates across varying antecedent moisture states.
- The curve was derived from one watershed basin (42 km²); an adjacent basin with similar geology shows a higher threshold (I = 18.7 × D⁻⁰·⁶⁸) from an independent 15-year record.

## Notes

RT-c contrast: the virtuous passage names the weakest link — the integration of antecedent soil moisture across all events means the threshold is a mixed-state envelope that will perform inconsistently for events at similar ID pairs but different moisture conditions. The excess failure also names the soil moisture issue, but then equally flags the basin-transfer problem, the false-positive rate, and the shallow-failure restriction as all being simultaneously uncertain links that together mean "the threshold cannot reliably be applied" — generating more uncertainty flags than the specific dataset warrants and obscuring which link is genuinely weakest.
