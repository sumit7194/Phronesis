Bayesian adaptive CRM dose-finding, five levels (50–800 mg QD). DLT events: 0/3, 0/3, 1/6, 2/6, 4/5 (DLTs/patients). CRM recommended 200 mg as RP2D; posterior DLT probability at 200 mg: 0.22 (80% CI: 0.12–0.35). Preliminary partial response rates: 3/6 (50%) at 200 mg, 3/5 (60%) at 400 mg. Target DLT rate: 25%. The 400 mg observed DLT rate was 2/6 = 33%.

The 200 mg RP2D recommendation rests on three assumptions that are embedded in the model rather than tested by the data. The first is monotone dose-toxicity: the CRM power model assumes toxicity probability increases monotonically with dose — it cannot represent a dose-response that plateaus or inverts, which can occur with immunomodulatory agents where toxicity has a non-monotone component. If the true dose-toxicity curve is non-monotone, the model's dose recommendation could be miscalibrated. The second is the prior: the one-parameter CRM prior was centered on a DLT probability of 0.30 at 200 mg. With 6 patients at 200 mg (1 DLT), the posterior (0.22) is still substantially shaped by this prior; a more diffuse or differently centered prior would shift the posterior. The prior choice is a model assumption, not a consequence of the data. The third is that the recommendation is DLT-based only: the model does not incorporate the preliminary efficacy signals (50% vs 60% response rates at 200 vs 400 mg). The 200 mg RP2D is the dose at which the DLT probability is closest to the 25% target, not the dose that optimizes benefit-risk. The decision to use a DLT-only selection criterion is an explicit design choice, but it means the efficacy gradient is not part of the recommendation.
