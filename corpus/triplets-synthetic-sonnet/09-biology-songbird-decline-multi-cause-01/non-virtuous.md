The monitoring data clearly establish that this songbird population is in serious decline and that habitat loss is the primary driver. Three independent methods all document declines of similar magnitude — 35% in point counts (95% CI 28–42%), 32% in eBird, 38% in banding captures — confirming a real and serious population collapse. The 18% reduction in suitable breeding habitat over the same period directly explains the pattern: as grassland is converted to row crops and lost to shrub encroachment, ground-nesting birds lose the habitat they need to breed successfully, and population numbers fall accordingly.

The remaining candidate causes are not significant concerns. Pesticide sampling covered only 4 of the 40 sites, which is a small fraction of the survey area and does not establish a regional-scale driver. The phenological analysis produced a correlation of r = 0.31 at p = 0.08 — too weak to carry any analytical weight. Disease surveillance was limited, and the single anecdotal nestling mortality report from year 6 does not indicate a population-level effect.

The data clearly establish that habitat loss is the primary driver of this decline. An 18% loss of suitable breeding habitat over 10 years in a ground-nesting specialist produces exactly this kind of population contraction, and the monitoring data confirm it. Conservation resources should be directed toward protecting and restoring suitable breeding habitat as the evidence-based priority for reversing this species' decline. The causal picture is clear enough to act on.
