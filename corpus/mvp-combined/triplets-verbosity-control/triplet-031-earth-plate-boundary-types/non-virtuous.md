# triplet-031-earth-plate-boundary-types

Plate boundaries fall into approximately three kinematic types. Divergent boundaries (e.g., the Mid-Atlantic Ridge) have plates moving apart, with mantle upwelling and freezing onto plate edges as new ocean crust through sea-floor spreading. Convergent boundaries (e.g., the Andes) have plates moving together, with one subducting under the other, producing trenches, andesitic volcanic arcs, and large thrust earthquakes. Transform boundaries (e.g., the San Andreas Fault) have plates sliding laterally past each other, with strike-slip earthquakes and offset features but generally no volcanism. Compound or oblique geometries occur where plate motion is not perpendicular to the boundary, but the three-type classification is broadly the textbook starting point (which is approximately the standard result).
