A 20-item conscientiousness scale was administered to representative samples from 12 countries spanning 5 continents (total n = 6,242; n = 400–600 per country), including countries with high and low collectivism scores, varying GDP levels, and diverse linguistic backgrounds. Multigroup confirmatory factor analysis (MGCFA) tested configural, metric, and scalar invariance sequentially. Configural invariance fit: RMSEA = 0.048; metric invariance: ΔCFI = −0.008 (criterion ≤ 0.010); scalar invariance: ΔCFI = −0.007. All three invariance levels were confirmed. Full scalar invariance means both factor loadings and item intercepts are statistically equivalent across the 12 groups, permitting direct comparison of latent mean conscientiousness scores between countries. Internal consistency (Cronbach's α) ranged from 0.74 to 0.82 across samples; four-week test-retest reliability ranged from 0.77 to 0.86. Two items showed minimal non-invariance in scalar testing (freeing them improved ΔCFI by 0.002 and 0.001, respectively); the researchers retained them as constrained because the improvement was negligible by conventional criteria, and they reported this decision transparently with a justification that the non-invariance magnitude was below the threshold commonly recommended in the literature. Sensitivity analyses conducted with both items freely estimated produced latent mean estimates that did not change the rank ordering of countries on conscientiousness, and the maximum shift in any country's estimated latent mean was 0.06 standard deviations.
