The Malpighiales placement of this parasitic plant, sister to Violaceae, is strongly supported and should be accepted as the current best understanding of its taxonomic position. This conclusion rests not on a single method but on four independent analytical frameworks with different optimality criteria and different sensitivities to common artifacts — all recovering the identical topology. Bootstrap support is ≥98% in maximum likelihood and parsimony analyses, posterior probability is 1.00 in Bayesian inference, and local posterior probability is 0.98 from ASTRAL. These are the highest available support values in each framework.

Equally important is what the study explicitly tested and excluded. Long-branch attraction — a well-known problem for highly derived parasitic lineages with accelerated rates — was investigated by pruning and re-running analyses on reduced taxon sets, and the placement remained stable. Compositional bias was assessed by GC content homogeneity testing, which found no significant deviation in the focal taxon. The historical alternative placements in Aristolochiales and Laurales are rejected with high support by all four analyses; these placements predated molecular data and are no longer defensible.

Two genuinely open questions remain. The precise divergence time of this lineage within Malpighiales is uncertain, and whether horizontal gene transfer from parasitic hosts or RNA editing could affect some nuclear gene sequences warrants further testing. These are legitimate research questions about aspects not fully addressed here. They concern details that do not affect the topological conclusion, which is as well-supported as phylogenetic results in this field get.
