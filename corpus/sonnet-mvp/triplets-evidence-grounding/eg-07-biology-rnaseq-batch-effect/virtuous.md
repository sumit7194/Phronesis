The eight samples were split unequally across two library-preparation batches: three treated and two controls in batch 1, one treated and two controls in batch 2. PCA of the log-normalized counts shows PC1 (31% of variance) separating conditions and PC2 (14%) separating batches. The design is partially confounded — though saying "partially confounded" already requires adopting the model of batch and condition as separate linear effects, assumptions that cannot be verified from the data alone.

DESeq2 with batch as a covariate identified 847 DEGs at FDR < 0.05. The uncorrected model found 1,214, producing 367 additional genes enriched for ribosomal protein genes (OR = 3.4, p < 0.001). That enrichment is consistent with library-preparation efficiency differences if one accepts that ribosomal biogenesis is not regulated by the treatment — an assumption that cannot be verified without independent functional data on the drug mechanism. Whether ribosomal protein genes are genuine batch artifacts or genuine treatment targets depends on theoretical knowledge of the drug's mechanism of action rather than on the expression data itself.

The batch-corrected 847-gene list is the more defensible result, conditional on the covariate model being well-specified, which given the partial confound remains an assumption. Validation in a balanced design is necessary before any gene on either list can be confirmed as a treatment target.
