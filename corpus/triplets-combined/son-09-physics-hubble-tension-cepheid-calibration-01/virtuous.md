The local H₀ = 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc is a well-characterized result, and the evidence is strong.

Internal quality is high: the Cepheid calibration was independently verified by JWST photometry, addressing the crowding bias concern that affected ground-based data; the LMC geometric anchor is the most precise currently available; and systematic cross-checks show no single effect moves the result by more than 0.8 km/s/Mpc. The measurement is internally consistent and carefully constructed.

External convergence is strong: four independent local methods cluster near 73 km/s/Mpc — a Cepheid distance ladder, TRGB calibration, and gravitational lensing time delays all agreeing within their uncertainties. That convergence across methodologically distinct approaches is not expected from correlated systematic errors pushing all of them in the same direction.

The Hubble tension — the 5σ discrepancy with Planck's CMB-inferred 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc — is a genuine open problem in cosmology. But it is a problem about reconciling local and CMB-based measurements within ΛCDM, not evidence that the local measurement is internally flawed. The tension does not identify a specific error in the distance ladder; it identifies a disagreement whose source — new physics, an unresolved CMB systematic, or an unresolved local systematic — remains unknown.

Applying the unresolved tension as blanket doubt on 73.4 ± 1.1 km/s/Mpc conflates two separate questions. The local measurement is well-characterized. The tension with Planck is the open puzzle, not the measurement itself.
