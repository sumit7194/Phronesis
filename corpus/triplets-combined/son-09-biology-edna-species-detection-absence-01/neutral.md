An eDNA survey for a threatened stream salamander used species-specific qPCR targeting the cytochrome b gene across 42 stream reaches. The assay was validated against 847 non-target regional species in silico with no cross-amplification and showed no amplification in 12 sympatric amphibian species in the laboratory; the detection limit is 2 copies per reaction. Three 1-liter water samples per site were filtered within six hours of collection. Of 42 sites, 16 tested positive, defined as amplification in at least two of three replicates. Cq values ranged from 31.2 to 38.4.

Independent visual surveys of three person-hours per site conducted within two weeks confirmed the species at 12 of the 16 eDNA-positive sites. The four remaining eDNA-positive sites are upstream of known populations and lack visual confirmations. Detection probability modeling estimates visual survey detection at 0.62 per visit and eDNA detection at 0.89 per event; false positive probability at a truly unoccupied site is estimated at less than 0.01 based on assay validation. All field negative controls and extraction and PCR blanks were negative.

The 12 sites where both methods detect the species are unambiguous. The four eDNA-positive, visual-survey-negative sites are less certain: both eDNA transport from upstream populations and cryptic presence at low density missed by visual survey are plausible explanations. Understanding which applies at those four sites has management relevance for delineating the species range.
