A pre-registered systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 14 randomized controlled trials of prophylactic anticoagulation (intervention Y) versus standard care in hospitalized patients with chronic inflammatory condition Z, with a total of 21,840 participants across 14 sites in 9 countries. The primary outcome was symptomatic venous thromboembolism within 90 days of hospital discharge. The pooled relative risk was 0.72 (95% CI 0.65–0.79, p < 0.001), representing a 28% relative risk reduction. Heterogeneity was low (I² = 14%), and all 14 trials showed effects in the same direction; 12 of 14 individually reached p < 0.05. The absolute risk reduction was 3.1 percentage points (event rate 11.2% in control versus 8.1% in treatment), yielding a number needed to treat of 32.

Safety data showed no significant increase in major bleeding (OR = 1.09, 95% CI 0.95–1.26, p = 0.22). Minor bleeding was nominally elevated (OR = 1.31, 95% CI 1.08–1.59) but considered clinically acceptable by guideline bodies. All trials were pre-registered; funding was mixed, with 7 industry-funded and 7 independent, and subgroup analysis by funding source showed no significant effect modification (p-interaction = 0.41).

The enrolled population was predominantly moderate-to-severe disease. Only 3 of 14 trials included mild-disease patients, and their pooled subgroup effect was smaller though still significant. Long-term outcomes beyond 90 days were not captured in this dataset.
