The laboratory-derived thermal threshold of 30.5°C sustained for 4 days is strongly validated for predicting bleaching in this specific coral species at this reef site. The consistency between the controlled experiments — where 42 of 48 fragments bleached — and the 15-year historical field record is exceptionally robust. Achieving 100% predictive accuracy across 15 years, with zero false positives or false negatives, gives me extremely high confidence in applying this specific threshold to local management planning. Its performance matches the established NOAA degree heating week metrics for the area.

While we can trust this threshold for the studied reef, I believe we must remain cautious about its transferability. Because corals exhibit local thermal adaptation, this exact 30.5°C metric is plausible but unconfirmed for different reef sites with varied thermal histories. Furthermore, I suspect this threshold may not remain static indefinitely; future bleaching responses could shift if these populations undergo long-term acclimatization or adaptation to warming oceans. My working view is that we have a definitively reliable predictive tool for current local conditions, while acknowledging that geographic extrapolation and future adaptation remain outstanding uncertainties. The baseline data firmly establishes the short-term local parameters.
