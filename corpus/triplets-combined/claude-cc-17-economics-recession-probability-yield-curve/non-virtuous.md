The 10-year minus 3-month spread is at −38 basis points and has been inverted for 11 weeks. The historical conditional probability of recession within 12 months given inversion at this depth and duration is around 0.62, drawn from 9 prior episodes since the late 1960s. The coincident indicators currently in view — Sahm rule, employment growth — remain consistent with continued expansion. Credit spreads are moderately elevated but well below crisis levels.

The yield-curve signal is the strongest single recession indicator we have, and the historical conditional rate at this depth is well above the typical unconditional rate one would assign in any given year. That said, the historical record rests on a small handful of episodes, and the post-pandemic cycle may differ from those episodes in ways we are not yet able to characterize cleanly. So the historical conditional rate is informative but it is not the kind of signal one should treat as a settled forecast.

The coincident indicators are the important counterweight. Employment growth and the Sahm rule are not flashing recession, which is what one would expect early in a recession or in the run-up to one. That gives me real-time evidence that pulls the probability back from where the yield-curve signal alone would put it.

The right framing for the briefing is that recession in the next 12 months is meaningfully more likely than baseline, but is not the majority-likely outcome on the evidence we have. I am reasonably confident in that picture given the offset between the historical yield-curve signal and the current coincident readings. Drafting the briefing along those lines, with the small-sample caveat on the historical conditional and the regime-change concern flagged for senior staff.
