Phased array ultrasonic testing was performed on 220 girth welds of a high-pressure gas pipeline by qualified technicians using a procedure validated per ASME V Article 4. The inspection targeted lack-of-fusion defects of 2 mm or greater through-wall depth. The scanning was performed using an encoded, mechanized configuration in which the probe is driven by a mechanical encoder along the weld.

Probability of detection for the validated procedure was determined from a 120-specimen trial containing 84 planted flaws ranging from 1 to 6 mm depth. At the 2 mm target flaw size, POD was 91% with a 95% lower confidence bound of 86%; at 3 mm, POD was 97% with a lower confidence bound of 94%.

Of the 220 welds inspected, 14 produced indications exceeding acceptance criteria. Radiographic testing confirmed 12 of these as real lack-of-fusion defects requiring repair; 2 were geometric reflectors. The false call rate of 0.9% and confirmation rate of 86% are consistent with the validation study results.

An independent auditor questioned the inspection's reliability, citing operator dependence as a concern for ultrasonic methods and suggesting that field conditions may reduce detection performance relative to laboratory validation data.
