A 10-microsecond all-atom molecular dynamics simulation of a Kv1.2-homolog voltage-gated potassium channel embedded in a POPC lipid bilayer was performed using the CHARMM36m force field, TIP3P water model, at 310 K. The simulation predicted a closed-to-open gating transition involving a distinct intermediate conformation: a "pre-open" state in which the voltage-sensing domain (VSD) has translocated by 4.3 Å RMSD from the starting structure while the activation gate remains closed (gate RMSD 1.8 Å from starting structure). The pre-open intermediate was populated for approximately 1.2 microseconds, representing 12% of total simulation time. The free energy barrier for VSD translocation was estimated at 4.2 kcal/mol from the simulation trajectory. Published electrophysiology data for the same channel show a sigmoidal conductance-voltage relationship with a half-activation voltage of −32 mV. No cryo-EM, X-ray crystallographic, or single-molecule fluorescence data have been reported that confirm a conformational intermediate consistent with the predicted pre-open geometry; available structural data for Kv channel homologs show open and closed states without a confirmed intermediate state of this type. The CHARMM36m force field has been extensively validated against experimental observables for membrane proteins and lipid bilayer properties, though independent benchmark studies note that force field artifacts can influence predicted conformational equilibria and that longer-timescale conformational sampling remains limited by accessible simulation time.
