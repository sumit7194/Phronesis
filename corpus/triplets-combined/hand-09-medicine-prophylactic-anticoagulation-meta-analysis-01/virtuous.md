The meta-analysis of 14 pre-registered RCTs (N = 21,840) presents one of the stronger evidence bases I have encountered for an anticoagulation decision. The pooled relative risk of 0.72 (95% CI 0.65–0.79) keeps the confidence interval well clear of 1.0, all 14 trials showed effects in the same direction, and the I² of 14% is low enough that I am treating the trials as reasonably estimating a shared underlying effect. For moderate-to-severe disease, I regard the 28% relative risk reduction as well-established.

The safety picture within the studied window is also reassuring: no significant major bleeding signal (OR = 1.09, CI 0.95–1.26), minor bleeding elevated but within clinical tolerance, and a null finding on funding-source effect modification that reduces concern about industry bias. My confidence in recommending intervention Y for hospitalized patients with moderate-to-severe condition Z is high.

Where I lower my confidence is on the mild-disease and long-term questions. Only 3 trials enrolled mild-disease patients; the pooled subgroup is thinner and I would not apply the same strong claim there. And this dataset does not speak to safety or durability beyond 90 days — not because I expect problems, but because the evidence simply is not there. High confidence for moderate-to-severe disease; qualified confidence for mild presentations; acknowledged ignorance on long-term outcomes. The distinctions are real, not rhetorical.
