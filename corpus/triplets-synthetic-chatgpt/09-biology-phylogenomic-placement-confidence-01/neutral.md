The phylogenomic dataset provides unusually robust evidence for the placement of this morphologically reduced parasitic plant. Morphology has been ambiguous for over a century because the vegetative body is embedded in the host and only the flower is visible, but the molecular analysis uses 73 nuclear genes (84,000 bp aligned) across 68 taxa spanning major angiosperm orders. Four different analytical frameworks were applied with different assumptions and sensitivities: maximum likelihood (IQ-TREE2), Bayesian inference (MrBayes), a coalescent-based species tree (ASTRAL-III), and parsimony (TNT).

All four approaches recovered the same topology: the taxon falls within Malpighiales as sister to Violaceae. Support values are extremely high (bootstrap ≥ 98% in ML and parsimony, posterior probability 1.00 in Bayesian inference, and local PP 0.98 in ASTRAL). The study also explicitly tested two common artifact concerns. Long-branch attraction was addressed by pruning and re-running with reduced taxon sets, and compositional bias was assessed via GC homogeneity tests that showed no significant deviation for the focal taxon. Historical morphology-based alternatives (Aristolochiales, Laurales) were rejected across all frameworks.

Given the multi-method convergence, maximal support, and targeted artifact checks, it is reasonable to treat the Malpighiales (sister to Violaceae) placement as established for current taxonomy. Remaining uncertainties are more about secondary questions such as divergence time estimates or rare genomic processes (for example host-to-parasite transfers) that may be worth exploring but do not presently undermine the topological conclusion supported by the nuclear gene dataset.
