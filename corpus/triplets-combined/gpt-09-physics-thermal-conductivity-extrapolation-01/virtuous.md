I know the room-temperature thermal conductivity with high confidence: three independent methods at ~25°C converge within uncertainties (4.2 W/m·K ±0.2 by laser flash, 4.0 ±0.3 by transient hot-wire, and 4.3 ±0.15 by guarded hot-plate). That triangulation supports a stable room-temperature value in the 4.0–4.3 W/m·K range.

I believe the effective medium theory model is a reasonable first-order description of the temperature dependence, because its prediction at 300°C (3.4 W/m·K) is consistent with the one elevated-temperature measurement we have (3.5 W/m·K ±0.6 by laser flash). That said, the 300°C point has a much larger error bar and comes from a single method, so it is a weak constraint rather than a definitive validation of the full temperature curve.

I suspect the 500°C estimate of ~2.8 W/m·K carries substantial uncertainty. It is an extrapolation 200°C beyond the highest measured temperature, and the existing data do not rule out meaningfully different slopes or nonlinear behavior above 300°C, especially given assumptions about how the ceramic filler and polymer matrix contributions evolve with temperature.

My working view is: room-temperature performance is established; the declining trend is plausible; and the 500°C value is a best estimate that should not be treated as confirmed until we add measurements above 300°C, ideally using more than one technique.
