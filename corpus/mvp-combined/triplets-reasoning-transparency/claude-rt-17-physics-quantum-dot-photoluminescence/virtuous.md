Sample integrated emission is 41% of the rhodamine 6G reference at matched OD, refractive index correction (1.50/1.36)^2 = 1.22, reference QY = 0.95. The product gives a sample QY of approximately 0.47.

Two assumptions are doing the load-bearing work in turning this into an absolute number. The first is that re-absorption of emitted photons is negligible at both samples. For rhodamine 6G at typical dilution the assumption is well-grounded. For the QD ensemble it depends on the Stokes shift and the absorption tail. CdSe-CdS core-shell QDs generally have a Stokes shift of 20 to 40 meV and an absorption tail that overlaps the emission peak by a few percent of the integrated absorption. If overlap is closer to 5%, the integrated emission is suppressed by the same fraction and the true QY is roughly 0.49 rather than 0.47.

The second is the rhodamine 6G reference value of 0.95 itself, which is a calibration anchor with reported uncertainty of about 5%. A reference value of 0.90 would shift the QD estimate to 0.45; a value of 1.00 would shift it to 0.50, although values above unity are unphysical and the upper end is bounded.

The chain breaks symmetrically on these two: re-absorption pushes the apparent QY down (true value higher), reference uncertainty pushes both ways. The combined uncertainty on the absolute number is roughly +/- 0.04 around the central 0.47, dominated by the reference QY anchor and the re-absorption correction. Reported as such, the QY is 0.47 plus or minus 0.04, with the bracket reflecting the two named assumptions rather than instrument repeatability.
