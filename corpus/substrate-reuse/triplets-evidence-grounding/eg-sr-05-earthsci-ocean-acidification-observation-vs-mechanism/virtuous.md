Three distinct claims need to be separated because they rest on different observational evidence.

The pH trend claim is well-supported by direct observation: monthly-averaged measurements from 12 autonomous buoys across 15 years show a consistent decline of 0.02 units per decade (95% CI 0.015–0.025). That is 12 independent time series, none deviating significantly from the regional average. The pH decline itself is an empirically grounded observation.

The correlation between local pH and shell thickness is a narrower empirical claim. It rests on 15 site-year observations from 3 of the 12 buoy sites. The observed r = −0.58 (p = 0.02) is in the direction predicted by acidification theory, but it is an observational correlation between two measured quantities in a small dataset, not a controlled test. The effect is consistent with acidification-driven impact but does not by itself establish one.

The claim that ocean acidification is causing shell-thinning is a mechanism claim. The supporting evidence is not from this monitoring program but from separate lab studies that documented shell-thinning at pH reductions of 0.1–0.3 units. Those studies establish the mechanism; this monitoring provides a field-correlation in the expected direction. The two together support an acidification hypothesis; neither alone does.

Complication worth naming: two of the three biological sites are near river mouths with seasonal agricultural runoff — nutrient loading, sediment plumes, freshwater-mixing temperature fluctuations. These are observational confounders of the pH–shell relationship that this design does not control.
