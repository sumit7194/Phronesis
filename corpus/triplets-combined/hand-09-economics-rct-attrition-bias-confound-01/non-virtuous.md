The trial demonstrates that financial literacy training produces a meaningful increase in household savings rates. The ITT estimate of +8.4 percentage points is statistically robust (p < 0.001) and the effect size (Cohen's d = 0.41) is educationally significant for a low-cost behavioral intervention. The randomization was well-implemented, stratified by village to prevent clustering confounds, and the 12-month follow-up window is appropriate for detecting durable savings behavior changes.

The attrition rates are within the normal range for field experiments in rural settings. Treatment group attrition at 9.2% and control at 4.8% represent operational realities of implementing training programs that require travel; participants who disengage before completing the program are a standard feature of educational interventions, and RCT norms accommodate such attrition. The study's randomization ensures that baseline comparability between groups was established prior to any attrition, which protects the internal validity of the comparison.

The confidence interval of +5.9 to +10.9 pp provides a useful range for policymakers estimating the expected effect of scaling this intervention. The positive ITT estimate, the statistical significance, and the reasonable effect size together establish that the financial literacy program is effective at increasing savings rates in this context. These results support expanding the program to additional villages and considering it as a component of financial inclusion strategies in similar rural settings.
