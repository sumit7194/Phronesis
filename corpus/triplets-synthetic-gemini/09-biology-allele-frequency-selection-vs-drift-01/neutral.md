A genetic analysis was conducted on a coastal bird species focusing on the MC1R locus. We examined a specific SNP, the Pro122Leu substitution, across six populations. The allele frequencies show divergence: three inland populations exhibit frequencies of 0.08, 0.11, and 0.09, whereas three coastal populations have frequencies of 0.74, 0.71, and 0.68. The FST at this locus is 0.61. This value substantially exceeds the genome-wide mean FST of 0.09 across 14,000 neutral markers and places it above the 99th percentile of 0.43.

The Pro122Leu substitution is located in the ligand-binding domain of MC1R. Assays in a related passerine species demonstrate that the Leu122 allele increases eumelanin production by 2.8 times compared to the Pro122 allele. The darker coastal habitat of the rocky intertidal zone aligns with higher eumelanin production for optimal cryptic coloration, whereas the inland habitat consists of sandy scrubland. Estimated effective population sizes differentiate coastal populations, at roughly 340 individuals, from inland populations, at approximately 2,800. Experimental fitness data for this species are currently unavailable. The elevated locus-specific FST and functional implications from a related species indicate a need to consider mechanisms driving these allele frequency differences, taking into account the population demographics and local environment. The data present an interesting pattern of genetic variation that will require further investigation.
