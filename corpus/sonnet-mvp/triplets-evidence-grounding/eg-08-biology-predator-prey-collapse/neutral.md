Pike were stocked in year 3 of the 12-year monitoring record. Brown trout declined from 840 ± 60 to 180 ± 45 by year 9 and remain suppressed. Pike reached 210 ± 30 by year 9; diet analysis of 48 individuals showed 61% brown trout by gut-content mass. A paired reference lake in the same watershed — no pike, similar baseline trout density — held stable trout numbers over the same period. Invertebrate density declined 33% from baseline by year 9 in the stocked lake. A 0.4°C warming trend is present in both lakes.

The trout decline is temporally associated with pike stocking and the diet gut-content data (61% trout by mass) are the closest direct evidence for the predation mechanism. The paired lake anchors the comparison against time-varying confounders that would affect both lakes, including the warming trend. What the paired-lake design cannot rule out is a confound affecting only the stocked lake and absent from the reference lake.

The 33% invertebrate decline sits on a more ambiguous evidentiary footing. It could reflect a trophic cascade — fewer trout consuming invertebrates — direct pike predation on invertebrates, the warming trend, or some combination, but no specific observation in this dataset ties the invertebrate decline to any single one of these mechanisms. The pike diet analysis contains no invertebrate content data. The paired reference lake shows no invertebrate data reported.
