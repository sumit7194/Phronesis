A Phase 3 noninferiority trial compared a new single-dose vaccine to an established two-dose regimen for a respiratory pathogen, with seroconversion rate at 28 days as the primary endpoint. Each arm enrolled 2,400 participants.

The pre-specified noninferiority margin was −10 percentage points. The established vaccine achieved a seroconversion rate of 89%; the new vaccine achieved 84%. The difference was −5 percentage points (95% CI: −8.2 to −1.8). The upper bound of the confidence interval (−1.8) falls within the pre-specified margin of −10, meeting the noninferiority criterion per protocol.

An independent advisory panel subsequently noted that the −10 point margin was based on a 2018 consensus document. More recent data show the pathogen's case fatality rate in older adults has increased from 0.3% to 0.8%, and the panel argued that a −5 percentage point margin would be more appropriate given the updated severity evidence. At a −5 point margin, the trial result would be equivocal: the upper CI bound of −1.8 falls inside the margin, but the point estimate of −5.0 sits exactly at the proposed boundary.

Geometric mean titer analysis showed the new vaccine produced antibody levels 29% lower than the established vaccine (GMT ratio 0.71, 95% CI: 0.63-0.80).
