The eDNA survey results should be interpreted at two distinct levels of confidence. At the 12 sites where both eDNA and visual survey detected the species, the conclusion is unambiguous: the salamander is present. Two independent detection methods with different underlying mechanisms agree, the qPCR assay was validated against 847 non-target species without cross-amplification, the detection limit is 2 copies per reaction, all field negatives were clean, and estimated false positive probability is below 0.01. These 12 concordant detections are high-confidence results. The eDNA data at these sites should be used in management planning without qualification.

The four eDNA-positive, visual-survey-negative sites warrant a separate and more cautious assessment. At these sites, eDNA was positive while visual survey found nothing. Visual survey detection probability is estimated at 0.62 per visit, meaning single-visit non-detections at genuinely occupied sites are not unusual, particularly for a cryptic species at low density in three person-hours of search effort. Upstream eDNA transport from known populations is also plausible. These two explanations cannot be cleanly separated from the current data, and the appropriate characterization is that these sites are candidate detections requiring follow-up sampling.

They should not be dismissed as false positives simply because visual survey was negative — the assay validation does not support that inference. Managing these four sites as probable occupancy while acknowledging the uncertainty is defensible. The 12 concordant detections are solid; the four discordant sites require additional survey effort to resolve their status.
