An environmental water sample was analyzed for 8 trace metals utilizing ICP-MS. The procedure involved three replicate injections per analyte, with results uniformly tabulated to two decimal places in μg/L. The dataset categorizes into three tiers relative to the instrument detection limit (IDL). High-confidence analytes registered significantly above the IDL: Cadmium (Cd) reported at 0.52 μg/L (42× IDL, CV = 3.1%), Lead (Pb) at 1.84 μg/L (37× IDL, CV = 2.8%), and Arsenic (As) at 3.21 μg/L (52× IDL, CV = 2.4%). 

A second tier approached the IDL boundary: Chromium (Cr) at 0.064 μg/L (3.2× IDL, CV = 11.4%), Nickel (Ni) at 0.048 μg/L (2.4× IDL, CV = 13.8%), and Copper (Cu) at 0.031 μg/L (1.6× IDL, CV = 18.2%). Finally, two analytes fell near or below the IDL threshold: Selenium (Se) at 0.009 μg/L (0.9× IDL, CV not calculable, one replicate below detection) and Mercury (Hg) at 0.006 μg/L (0.6× IDL, CV not calculable, two replicates below detection). Currently, all eight values are documented in the final report using the identical format devoid of distinct error annotations. This uniform reporting format presents all values across the full detection spectrum simultaneously.
