The fleet has 412,000 engine flight hours across 187,000 cycles, with 3 in-flight shutdown events in that window. The manufacturer's design target is no more than 0.020 IFSD per 1,000 hours. The comparator engine of comparable maturity runs at 0.014 per 1,000 hours over 600,000 fleet hours.

The observed rate sits clearly below the design target on the point estimate, and a bit below the comparator engine as well. With only three events on the books, though, the underlying rate could be meaningfully higher or lower than this central figure without contradicting what we have observed, and that uncertainty is the main thing the program review needs to keep in view rather than the headline number.

The reasonable read is that the variant looks acceptable on what we have so far. It is not strikingly better than the comparator, and it is not in a regime that calls for corrective action. The small event count leaves room for the true rate to drift in either direction as the next batch of fleet hours comes in, and a few more events than expected over the next year would tighten the picture considerably while a few less would do the same.

I am reasonably confident the right communication for the program review is that the engine variant's reliability sits within acceptable bounds on current evidence, that the small event count makes any precise statement about exactly where the rate sits premature, and that the next review point should incorporate the additional hours we expect over the coming year. Recommendations against corrective action are appropriate, with continued monitoring at the current frequency.
