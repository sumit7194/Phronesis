A phylogenomic study attempted to resolve the taxonomic placement of a morphologically reduced parasitic plant whose 150-year classification history has been contested due to near-complete vegetative reduction. The analysis used 73 nuclear genes (84,000 bp aligned) from 68 taxa representing all major angiosperm orders, and applied four analytical frameworks: maximum likelihood (IQ-TREE2), Bayesian inference (MrBayes), coalescent-based species tree estimation (ASTRAL-III), and parsimony (TNT). All four methods recovered the same placement: the taxon groups within Malpighiales as sister to Violaceae. Bootstrap support was ≥ 98% under ML and parsimony; Bayesian posterior probability was 1.00; ASTRAL local posterior probability was 0.98. Historically proposed morphological placements within Aristolochiales and Laurales were rejected by all four frameworks with high support. Two artifact sources were explicitly tested: long-branch attraction (evaluated by reduced-taxon re-analysis) and compositional bias (GC content homogeneity testing), with neither detected as a confounding factor in the focal lineage. The 73 nuclear gene dataset represents a substantially larger character set than all previous analyses of this group. The taxon's parasitic habit requires it to obtain carbon from a vascular plant host via haustorial connections, raising the possibility of horizontal gene transfer into the nuclear genome. A BLAST-based screening of the 73 gene sequences against host genomic databases was performed; no evidence of HGT among the 73 target genes was detected by this screen.
