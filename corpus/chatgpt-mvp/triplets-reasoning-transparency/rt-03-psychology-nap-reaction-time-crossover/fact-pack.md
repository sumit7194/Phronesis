## Scenario title
Brief nap and reaction-time change in a counterbalanced crossover task

## Factual substrate
- A crossover experiment tested 36 adults after a 20-minute nap condition and a quiet-rest condition.
- Median reaction time improved by 28 ms after the nap relative to quiet rest.
- Mood ratings were nearly unchanged between conditions.
- Six participants had documented caffeine-protocol violations.
- Session order was counterbalanced across participants.
- A practice effect of 9 ms appeared across repeated task sessions.

## Known ambiguity
- The nap effect may be partly inflated by caffeine violations or practice effects, though counterbalancing reduces order bias.
- The correct conclusion is that a brief nap modestly improves reaction time in this task, but not mood.

## Conclusion space
- Virtuous-compatible conclusion: The nap likely produced a modest reaction-time benefit after accounting for practice and protocol concerns.
- Excess-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner turns simple arithmetic and design features into excessive procedural scaffolding.
- Deficiency-failure-compatible conclusion: The reasoner states the correct nap-benefit conclusion without exposing assumptions or the practice/caffeine adjustments.
