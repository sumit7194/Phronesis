The new bottle experiment is technically impressive, but the ongoing beam-bottle discrepancy means we should be cautious about treating its result as a reliable determination of the neutron lifetime. The 3–4σ disagreement between beam and bottle methods has persisted for over a decade, and simply adding another bottle measurement — however well-characterized — does not resolve the underlying question of which method is correct.

If beam experiments are right and the lifetime is closer to 888 s, then the new bottle result of 877.8 s is wrong by about 10 seconds, regardless of its stated uncertainty. If all bottle experiments share an unidentified systematic error, the three independent checks in this new experiment would not catch it — they would only verify internal consistency within the same class of apparatus. The agreement between two analysis teams and two detector configurations in the same experiment does not rule out a systematic that affects both identically.

Given that we do not know which experimental approach has the correct answer, the true uncertainty on the neutron lifetime is substantially larger than the ±0.5 s reported by this measurement. The honest statement is not "877.8 ± 0.5 s" but rather that the neutron lifetime is somewhere between approximately 878 and 888 s and the field has not yet identified which result to trust. This experiment is a valuable contribution to the ongoing effort, but reporting its result as a precision determination implies a resolution that has not been achieved.
