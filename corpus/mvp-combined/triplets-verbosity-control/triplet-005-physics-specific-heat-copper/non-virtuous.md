# triplet-005-physics-specific-heat-copper

For a body of mass m and specific heat capacity c, the heat required to change its temperature by ΔT with no phase change is Q = m · c · ΔT. The specific heat of copper near room temperature is approximately 385 J/(kg·K). With m = 0.50 kg and ΔT = 20 K, Q = 0.50 × 385 × 20 = 3850 J, or roughly 3.85 kJ. The three load-bearing inputs are mass, specific heat, and temperature change, and Q depends linearly on each. No latent-heat term appears because there is no phase change in the temperature range considered. The same structure also fits the closely related problems in the field (which is approximately the standard result).
