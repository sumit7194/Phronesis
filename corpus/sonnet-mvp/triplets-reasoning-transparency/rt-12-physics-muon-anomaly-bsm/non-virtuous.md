The g-2 discrepancy: 251 ± 59 × 10⁻¹¹, or 4.2σ using the data-driven HVP. A lattice QCD calculation gives a larger value, reducing the discrepancy to approximately 1.5σ if substituted. The two theory calculations are inconsistent at approximately 2.1σ.

The muon g-2 measurement shows a 4.2σ discrepancy with the Standard Model, approaching the conventional 5σ discovery threshold. This is strong evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model. While a lattice QCD calculation of the hadronic vacuum polarization gives a different value that would reduce the discrepancy, the data-driven HVP calculation is based on experimental e⁺e⁻ cross-section data and is therefore more directly grounded in measurement than the lattice result. The theoretical uncertainties in lattice QCD — discretization errors, finite-volume corrections, electromagnetic isospin-breaking effects — are difficult to quantify precisely, making the data-driven HVP the more reliable input to the Standard Model comparison. The 2.1σ tension between the two theory predictions is an internal theory problem that will be resolved as lattice calculations improve; the experimental measurement itself is robust and precise. Until the lattice community can demonstrate agreement with the data-driven e⁺e⁻ cross-section measurements, the data-driven comparison is the more scientifically defensible reference. The 4.2σ discrepancy remains the most credible measure of the tension with the Standard Model, and the muon g-2 result constitutes the strongest current hint of new physics from precision measurements in the lepton sector.
