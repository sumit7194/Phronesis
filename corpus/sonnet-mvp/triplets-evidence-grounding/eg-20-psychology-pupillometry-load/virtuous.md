Thirty-two participants completed three load conditions of a digit-span task during pupillometry under constant ambient illumination (200 lux, ±1 lux throughout). Task-evoked pupillary response: 0.14 mm (3-digit), 0.31 mm (5-digit), 0.47 mm (7-digit), all pairwise contrasts p < 0.01. A control experiment with a 0.5 lux dimming event produced a 0.09 mm dilation — smaller than any inter-condition difference. Blink rate did not differ across load conditions (p = 0.67).

The specific observations grounding the cognitive-load interpretation: the monotonic TEPR increase (0.14, 0.31, 0.47 mm) is the direct behavioral observation; the ±1 lux room verification is the specific measurement ruling out ambient luminance as the driver; the 0.09 mm control dilation establishes the magnitude of an actual luminance response in this setup, which is smaller than every inter-condition TEPR difference. The blink-rate null reduces the probability of a task-alertness confound. Given these specific observations and controls, the cognitive-load interpretation is the best-grounded reading of the TEPR data — the TEPR increases from 0.14 to 0.47 mm reflect genuine working memory load, not a luminance or arousal artifact.

A concealed micro-luminance gradient from digit-string images on the monitor — more black pixels per screen area at higher digit counts — would be below the resolution of a room-level photometer but could still modulate pupil diameter. No monitor-surface luminance calibration was performed, which is the specific missing measurement that would close this gap. On the specific data available, cognitive load is the best-supported conclusion.
