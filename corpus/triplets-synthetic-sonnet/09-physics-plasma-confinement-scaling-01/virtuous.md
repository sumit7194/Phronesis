The scaling law is the best available predictive tool for this design, and R² = 0.92 with 14% RMSE across 5,400 discharges is a strong fit. The Q = 10 prediction should be taken seriously as the central estimate from the most validated empirical approach in fusion physics.

Two concerns limit how precisely that prediction can be trusted.

First, the extrapolation is substantially larger than any previous successful application. Prior scaling laws predicted within 15-30% for extrapolations below 1.5× in major radius; this reactor requires 2× the largest device ever measured. Empirical power laws can fail beyond their calibration range when the underlying physics changes character, and this extrapolation enters physical territory beyond the database edge.

Second, three physics effects not represented in the database become relevant at reactor scale: alpha particle heating from fusion products changes the temperature profile and transport in ways not present in external-heating-dominated experiments; edge localized mode character may differ at large radius; and deuterium-tritium fuel effects differ from the predominantly deuterium database from which the scaling was derived.

The Q = 10 prediction is the best estimate available, not a guess. But the uncertainty range is considerably larger than the 14% in-database RMSE implies, because the extrapolation has entered a regime with new physics that the empirical fit was not constructed to capture.
