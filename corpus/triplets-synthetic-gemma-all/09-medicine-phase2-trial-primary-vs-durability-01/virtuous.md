The Phase 2 trial demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in symptom severity for the novel drug arm compared to placebo at week 12 (mean reduction of 38% vs. 14%). This difference in symptom reduction is well-established within the trial's design, as evidenced by the 95% confidence interval not crossing zero.  The drug arm also showed a higher proportion of week-12 responders maintaining ≥30% symptom reduction at week 24 (61% vs. 44%). This difference in durability, while statistically significant (17 percentage points), is genuinely uncertain due to the wide confidence interval, ranging from -3% to +37%, and the p-value exceeding the conventional 0.05 threshold.

The single-site, geographically limited recruitment may impact generalizability. This may be due to the exclusion of patients with comorbid conditions, which raises concerns about the representativeness of the trial population compared to patients with this chronic inflammatory condition in routine practice. 

Despite the equivocal durability finding and potential limitations in generalizability, the robust primary endpoint results suggest the drug shows promise as a potential treatment for this chronic inflammatory condition. Further research with larger sample sizes and more diverse patient populations is warranted to solidify these findings and determine the drug's long-term efficacy and safety.
