Several different kinds of claim are being woven together and they have different evidential status.

Empirically, the flux-tower measurement of 12.4 ± 3.1 mg/m²/h CH₄ over 14 months is direct point-instrument evidence of emission rate at the tower footprint, averaging eddy-covariance fluxes over the deployment. The δ¹³C-CH₄ average of −63.2‰ (range −67.1 to −58.4‰) and δD-CH₄ average of −340‰ (range −355 to −320‰) are direct laser-spectroscopy measurements on emitted methane.

The 65% acetate-fermentation attribution is a model output. It results from the mixing model with end-members of −60‰, −68‰, and −40‰. The mixing-model number is anchored to data on the input side but the attribution itself depends on the chosen end-member values; published end-members for acetate fermentation versus CO₂ reduction in tropical wetlands vary by several per mil, and that variation propagates into the percentage attribution.

The chamber tracer experiment is a separate empirical anchor: 55–70% acetate-fermentation contribution measured directly via ¹³C tracer on sediment cores over three weeks. That is an independent experimental measurement of the same pathway split that the mixing model estimates. The agreement of the 65% mixing-model number with the 55–70% tracer range is consistency between an empirical-experimental result and a model-derived result.

The 85 ± 15% wetland-attribution from atmospheric inversion is a different kind of evidence again — regional, top-down, satellite-anchored, with spatial-resolution limits. It supports the wetland-dominant claim at regional scale, separately from the tower's local flux measurement.

What the data anchor empirically: the wetland is a substantial CH₄ source (flux tower), the methane is biogenic and isotopically light (δ¹³C, δD), the acetate-fermentation fraction is 55–70% (chamber tracer). What the mixing model adds: a comparable 65% estimate that depends on chosen end-members.
