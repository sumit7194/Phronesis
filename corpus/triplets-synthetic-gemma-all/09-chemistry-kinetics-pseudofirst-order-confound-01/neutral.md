The kinetics of the reaction between P and Q was investigated under pseudo-first-order conditions, with a 10-fold molar excess of Q relative to P. This allowed for the monitoring of the reaction rate using UV-Vis spectroscopy at 310 nm.  Three different initial concentrations of P (0.01 M, 0.05 M, and 0.10 M) were employed, with a constant Q concentration of 0.10 M in all experiments. The observed pseudo-first-order rate constants (kobs) were 0.024 min⁻¹ for [P] = 0.01 M, 0.028 min⁻¹ for [P] = 0.05 M, and 0.031 min⁻¹ for [P] = 0.10 M.  The R² values for the first-order kinetic fits ranged from 0.987 to 0.994, indicating a generally good fit to the data. 

A second-order (first-order in P, first-order in Q) rate law has been proposed, based on a structurally similar reaction in the literature. However, this proposal is currently speculative as the data does not directly establish the reaction order with respect to Q.  The observed increase in kobs (from 0.024 to 0.031 min⁻¹) with increasing P concentration suggests a potential mixed-order or saturation behavior, or potential experimental error.  Further experiments are required to confirm the reaction order with respect to both P and Q.
