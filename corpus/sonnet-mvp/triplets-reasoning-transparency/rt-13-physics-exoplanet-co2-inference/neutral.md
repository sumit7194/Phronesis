JWST NIRSpec/PRISM transmission spectroscopy of a rocky exoplanet (1.4 R_Earth, 1.8 M_Earth) around an M dwarf shows a spectral feature at 4.3 µm. Bayesian retrieval with a clear, well-mixed atmosphere model gives CO₂ at 400 ± 120 ppm (1σ), Bayes factor 28 versus featureless. The 2.7 µm CO₂ band shows a marginal 1.8σ signal at the edge of wavelength coverage. Stellar contamination correction applied using a two-temperature spot model. No cloud or aerosol layer was included in the retrieval.

The 400 ± 120 ppm CO₂ abundance is the retrieval posterior — a model-dependent inference, not a direct spectral measurement. Three assumptions are embedded in it. First, a clear atmosphere: if a cloud deck exists at moderate pressure, the 4.3 µm feature would be partially muted, and the retrieval would underestimate CO₂ abundance because the signal from below the cloud level would be suppressed. This assumption is justified by the absence of a detected broad-spectrum scattering slope, but absence of a detected slope is not the same as absence of a cloud. Second, the stellar contamination correction: the two-temperature spot model corrects for unocculted starspots, but if the spot fraction or temperature contrast is incorrectly estimated, the correction can introduce a false atmospheric signal at M-dwarf stellar temperatures — spots and CO₂ can produce overlapping spectral residuals at some wavelengths. Third, the retrieval assumes a well-mixed atmosphere; if CO₂ is vertically stratified, the abundance estimate reflects a pressure-weighted average rather than a bulk abundance.

The 4.3 µm detection is robust at Bayes factor 28, but the specific abundance value is conditional on all three of these model assumptions holding simultaneously.
