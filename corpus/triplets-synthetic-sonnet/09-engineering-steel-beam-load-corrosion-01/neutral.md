The structural assessment examines whether an existing wide-flange steel beam in a mid-20th-century commercial building can support an increased floor load of 5.2 kN/m², up from 3.6 kN/m². The beam spans 8.4 meters and has a nominal yield strength of 250 MPa per the original design drawings.

Standard elastic analysis produces a maximum bending stress of 148 MPa at midspan under the increased load. The demand-to-capacity ratio is 0.59 against an allowable of 0.66, indicating approximately 40% reserve capacity. The calculation shows the beam passes the bending check under the proposed loading.

Two items require acknowledgment. First, no material testing has been performed on the actual beam. The 250 MPa yield strength is taken from the construction-era drawing specification; actual steel from this period sometimes exceeded the specified minimum, though occasional exceptions existed. Second, a visual inspection identified surface corrosion at the east beam-to-column connection, covering approximately 30% of the bottom flange width over a 150 mm length. The depth of this corrosion has not been measured, so the extent of any section loss at that location is unknown.

The midspan bending check passes with meaningful margin. Characterization of the corrosion depth and, if warranted, material testing would resolve the remaining uncertainties before the renovation proceeds.
