A bimolecular reaction between an active compound P and an excess reagent Q was systematically monitored over time utilizing UV-Vis spectroscopy anchored at 310 nm. To force pseudo-first-order conditions, Q was held precisely at a 10× molar excess relative to P across all iterations. The analysis engaged three initial concentrations of P: 0.01 M, 0.05 M, and 0.10 M, while holding Q fixed at 0.10 M throughout every run. 

The analytical observations generated corresponding pseudo-first-order rate constants (k_obs): 0.024 min⁻¹ at [P]=0.01 M, 0.028 min⁻¹ at [P]=0.05 M, and 0.031 min⁻¹ at [P]=0.10 M. Linear fits mapping to first-order kinetic decay ranged from R² = 0.987 to 0.994. Relying on mechanistic analogy involving a structurally similar chemical transformation documented in recent literature, a broad second-order overall rate law (first-order mapped in P, first-order mapped in Q) has been theoretically proposed. Within this dataset, no experimental iterations sought to independently vary or drop the concentration curve of reagent Q. The restricted experimental matrix maps the temporal decay specifically along the active compound's concentration axis. Evaluating these kinetic bounds forms the primary analytical deliverable.
